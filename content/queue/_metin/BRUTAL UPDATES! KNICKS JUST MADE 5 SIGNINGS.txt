THE HONEST CAVEAT — BECAUSE WE ALWAYS DO IT

Now let's pump the brakes exactly as much as the situation deserves — no more, no less. Dexter Henry made the critical distinction on the NY Got Game podcast, and it's the right one: being medically CLEARED and being back in peak competitive form are two very different things. A shooting wrist isn't a knee — every catch, every dribble, every follow-through runs through it. The muscle memory, the touch on that signature mid-range pull-up, the confidence to absorb contact on drives and finish through fouls — those only come back through repetition and REAL game situations, and no surgeon can sign off on rhythm. So here's the realistic frame: expect the timing to take a few weeks. Expect a couple of preseason nights where the ball comes off his hand a little flat. Expect Mike Brown to manage his October minutes like the treasure they are. And don't panic when any of it happens — that's not a setback, that's the process.

But also remember exactly WHO we're talking about, because the bar this man returns to is absurd. During the title run — the run that ended fifty-three years of waiting — Brunson averaged 28 points, 6 assists, 3.2 rebounds, and 1.2 steals a game on 46.5% shooting and 36.3% from deep. He ranked top-five in the league in win shares, posted a staggering plus-minus north of twelve, and led ALL postseason players with 38 total clutch points. Thirty-eight clutch points — the most of anyone, on the biggest stage, with five decades of franchise weight on his shoulders. That's not a player who needs to rediscover who he is. That's a killer who needs three weeks of reps. And the beauty of THIS roster is that it can afford him every one of those weeks: KAT's playmaking hub, the OG-Bridges wing wall, Hart's chaos, the deepest bench in the conference. If the captain needs until Thanksgiving to be fully HIM, this team can bank wins in the meantime. The margin exists precisely because the front office built it.

THE OG ANUNOBY ALL-STAR CASE — IT'S REAL

Now to the story I've been waiting to make a full segment, because the analysts are finally catching up to what we watched in June: OG Anunoby is on the verge of his first career ALL-STAR selection — and the case is nearly airtight. And here's the beautiful part: the argument starts with a SACRIFICE. Remember the Karl-Anthony Towns transformation we've celebrated all summer? The numbers tell the story with brutal clarity: KAT took 13.8 shots a game in the regular season — second on the roster — then deliberately dialed it back to 9.8 in the playoffs, FOURTH on the team, morphing into a true playmaking hub at the center position. He stopped demanding shots and started manufacturing them.

And nobody ate better from that buffet than OG. Regular season: a solid 16.7 points a game. Playoffs, with the cleaner role and extra touches: 20.1 a night as the team's second-leading scorer — catch-and-shoot threes, attacking closeouts, punishing transition, finishing THROUGH rim protectors. A complete, mature offensive arsenal, unlocked at exactly the right time. Project that role over a full 82, and the classic knock on OG's All-Star résumé — "great defender, but a 16-point scorer" — simply evaporates. A 20-a-night OG changes the entire ballot math.

Because the defense, folks, has never needed an argument. The percentiles read like a cheat code: 95th percentile in isolation defense. 96th — NINETY-SIXTH — in matchup difficulty, meaning he takes the hardest assignment on the floor virtually every single night. 90th in positional versatility. Opposing coaches literally scheme AROUND him — design entire game plans to keep their best player out of his zip code. That's a level of defensive gravity maybe five players on Earth generate. Pair it with twenty points a game on a defending champion with a top seed, and I dare a voter to leave him off.

And the precedent is sitting right there: Cleveland sent THREE All-Stars — Garland, Mitchell, and Mobley — to the same game just two seasons ago. Brunson and KAT have made the last two together. Adding OG wouldn't be charity; it would simply be the ballot catching up to reality: this is the deepest collection of star-level talent in the Eastern Conference, and it just proved it with a parade. At 29, OG is squarely in his prime window. Twenty and a lockdown every night — the conversation takes care of itself. Three Knicks in the All-Star Game. Say it out loud. It's been DECADES.

One more layer on this, because it connects to a thread we pulled weeks ago: remember our video on OG's contract next to the league's other big deals, when critics were calling his number an overpay? Follow the arc. First the ring. Then the Finals defensive masterclass. Now a 20-point postseason platform and a live All-Star candidacy at the exact moment KAT's evolution hands him more runway than he's ever had in his career. If OG makes that team in February, the "overpay" conversation doesn't just die — it flips into one of the best value contracts among wings in basketball, exactly as we argued when nobody wanted to hear it. And there's a hungrier layer too: OG has never been an All-Star. Never. Nine seasons of doing the dirtiest, least-celebrated job in the sport at the highest level, watching lesser defenders make rosters off raw scoring. You think that man isn't walking into camp with February circled? The quiet ones always are.

THE ROYAL RUMBLE GETS ODDS

And now the segment for everyone following our Exhibit 9 coverage, because the camp battle we christened the Royal Rumble just got official ODDS — analyst percentages on all five names fighting for, realistically, two open seats. Quick table-setting for the new folks: the Knicks carry 13 standard contracts, two open spots, second-apron handcuffs, and five non-guaranteed vets — every one of them waivable before January 1st with zero cap consequence. Here's how the smart money handicaps it.

BRUCE BROWN: 60% — the clear frontrunner, and can I just say... WE CALLED IT. Our prediction the day the class assembled was Brown wins the spot, and every data point since backs it: all EIGHTY-TWO games played last season in Denver — an iron man in year eight — 7.9 points, 3.9 boards, 2.1 assists on 47.5% shooting and a sizzling 38.5% from three. And the pedigree: a legitimate rotation piece on the 2023 title team, 11.5 a night on 48% shooting during that championship season. Per Steve Popper, the front office has admired him for YEARS — the Josh Hart comparison isn't lazy, it's literal. Sixty percent honestly feels LOW.

DREW EUBANKS: 50% — and pay attention, because this one carries new information. The nine-year vet, undrafted out of Oregon State, is a pick-and-roll specialist with a career 60.3% field goal mark — efficient rim-running, real rim protection, exactly the interior species this roster has starved for since the Robinson departure. But here's the detail the report surfaced: Eubanks is coming off SURGERY — a torn UCL in his left thumb, which cut short a 42-game, 11-start season in Sacramento. The health of that thumb is officially a camp storyline: if he's moving well, he's the strongest big-man option on the shelf and a near-lock for a seat; if the hand betrays him in the paint, the door swings wide open for the man below.

JAMES WISEMAN: 40% — the redemption swing, and the odds respect the opportunity more than the résumé. Six seasons, only 152 games — the knee year, the torn Achilles, the whole cruel catalog we covered in his file. But two things keep the former No. 2 pick alive in this race: the Knicks NEED a third center, and — the wrinkle I love — Mike Brown personally coached Wiseman for his first two Golden State seasons. The head coach knows exactly what's in the toolbox and exactly what isn't: elite rim-finishing on 56% career shooting, real size... alongside well-documented struggles as a screener and defender. This isn't a blind flyer; it's an informed one. Forty percent for a 25-year-old 7-footer with a coach who believes? I'll take those odds.

JOHN KONCHAR: 40% — and yes, correction window open again: the man we once called "the signed 14th man" is officially handicapped as a coin flip, which confirms the Exhibit 9 reveal we issued in the nuclear-signing video. The scoring pops nobody's eyes — he's never averaged more than 5.1 — but the profile is pure Knicks DNA: multi-positional defense, 4.3 boards from the wing, 2.1 assists, 35% career from deep, and defensive tape from Memphis and Utah that reportedly has evaluators genuinely excited. His problem isn't his game; it's the traffic jam — he's fighting Bruce Brown for the same job description, and Brown has a ring.

OCHAI AGBAJI: 20% — the longshot, exactly as we projected in his file. The talent flashed loudly in that breakout Toronto season — 40% from the field, 39% from three, 10.4 a night — but last year's slide to under 27% from deep across Toronto and Brooklyn is the whole case against. No positional need pulls him forward, so his path runs entirely through his jumper: shoot the leather off the ball for three weeks or wave goodbye. Simple as camp math gets.
