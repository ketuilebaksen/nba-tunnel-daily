## THE RULES THAT FORCE THE KNIFE

Here's the setup. Every NBA team can carry a maximum of 15 players on standard contracts during the regular season, plus three two-way players who can move between the NBA team and the G League. In training camp, teams can carry more — up to 21 — which is why camps are full of Exhibit 9 and Exhibit 10 deals, invite guys, and young players fighting for one shot. But on the day before the regular season starts, the league's cut-down deadline hits, and every roster has to be legal. Everybody above the limit has to go.

For the Knicks, that deadline falls right before ring night against Philadelphia on October 20th. That's twelve days from now. So in the next week and a half, Leon Rose and Mike Brown have to decide who's in, who's out, and who gets sent to Westchester.

And here's the important wrinkle we've talked about before: the Knicks have been seriously considering whether to fill the 15th roster spot at all. Keeping it open saves money under the second apron, and it leaves a door open to add a prorated contract in January. Remember that word — January — because it's going to come back in a big way later in this video.

## THE CUT THAT'S BASICALLY ALREADY DECIDED

Here's something a lot of fans don't realize. Some cuts happen not because a player failed, but because the cut is part of the plan. That's how Exhibit 10 contracts work. A team signs a young player to an Exhibit 10 deal in the summer. He comes to camp, learns the system, and then — before the season — he's waived. If he then signs with the team's G League affiliate, which for the Knicks is Westchester, he receives a bonus, and the Knicks keep his G League rights.

So when you see the headline "Knicks waive young player," don't panic right away. In many cases, that's actually the plan working. The kid stays in the organization, develops in Westchester, and stays one phone call away. Dante, who's been on an Exhibit 10 while recovering from his ACL injury, fits that path perfectly. It's a "cut" on the transaction wire. In reality, it's a development decision.

## THE CUT THAT COULD ACTUALLY SHOCK PEOPLE

Now, the real drama. The real shock would be if the Knicks cut a young player most fans expect to make the team. And the most realistic version of that scenario is the 15th spot.

Picture it like this. Wiseman has been the camp favorite. But imagine the front office decides to leave the 15th spot open — not because Wiseman isn't good enough, but because of money, flexibility, and the January calendar. In that world, Wiseman doesn't make the roster even though he did everything right. That's the definition of a shock cut. It's not about performance. It's about the business.

Or flip it. Imagine Wiseman wins the spot, and a young wing like Agbaji gets cut instead because Mike Brown wants more size behind his two starting bigs. Either way, a young player walks out of that building with his bags, and the reason won't be found in a box score. It'll be found in a spreadsheet. And that spreadsheet has Karl-Anthony Towns' name at the top.

## KAT CONNECTION NUMBER ONE: EVERY DOLLAR UNDER THE APRON

Let's connect the dots, starting with money. The Knicks have drawn a hard line at the second apron. James Dolan has made it clear: this team is not crossing it. That's the exact reason the KAT negotiations are stuck. The Knicks are holding at around four years and $200 million; KAT believes he's worth more.

Now think about what that means for cut day. When a team is pressed right against the apron, even a minimum contract matters. Filling the 15th spot with a veteran on a minimum deal costs real money — and real room under that line. Leaving it open, or filling it with the cheapest possible young player, gives the front office breathing room. And breathing room matters, because the Knicks are trying to protect every last dollar of flexibility for the biggest decision of the season: what to do with Karl-Anthony Towns.

So here's the uncomfortable truth. The young player who gets cut this month may be losing his spot partly because of a negotiation he has nothing to do with. That's how tight this cap sheet is. When the front office tells KAT they can't go higher, cut day is the proof that they're not just saying it. They're counting pennies on the end of the bench too.

## KAT CONNECTION NUMBER TWO: THE OPEN SPOT IS A TRADE TOOL

This is the part almost nobody is talking about — and it's the reason this video exists.

Remember what Ian Begley said: if the two sides don't move, you get to January and you have to think about the pivot. You can't lose him in free agency for nothing. Now, let's think about how a KAT trade would actually work. KAT makes about $57 million this season. Under the trade rules, the Knicks can't take back one player at a tiny salary — they'd need to take back a lot of salary in return. And in almost every realistic scenario, that means taking back MORE than one player. Two players. Maybe three.

And here's where the roster spot matters. If the Knicks trade one player and receive two or three, they need room on the roster to fit them. If every one of the 15 spots is full, they'd have to cut somebody else — and pay that guy's salary — just to make the trade legal. But if they leave the 15th spot open now? That's one free slot already waiting.

So follow the logic. Keeping that spot open in October isn't just about saving money. It's insurance. It keeps the front office ready for ANY big move in January, including the one nobody in New York wants to think about. To be clear, this is NBA Tunnel analysis — no report has said the Knicks are keeping the spot open for a KAT trade. But when you put Begley's January timeline next to the Knicks' 15th-spot debate, the timing lines up too perfectly to ignore. Same month. Same flexibility. Same front office that traded Quickley for OG and Randle for KAT.

## KAT CONNECTION NUMBER THREE: WHO PROTECTS HIS MINUTES

The third connection is on the court. The young big man who wins this camp battle isn't just the 15th man. He's the insurance policy behind KAT and Mitchell Robinson. And that matters more than usual this year.

Mitch's health is always a question in January and February. If he misses time, KAT slides into heavier center minutes — more bumping, more rebounding battles, more wear and tear on the body during a contract year. And remember, the betting market has KAT at +500 to lead the entire NBA in rebounding this season, second only to Nikola Jokić. If he's chasing that crown while carrying a bigger load, the backup big becomes important for his health and his numbers.

That's why the choice between Wiseman, Eubanks, and Bradley is bigger than it looks. Pick the right young big, and KAT gets real rest and the Knicks stay dangerous when he sits. Pick wrong, and the drop-off when KAT goes to the bench becomes a problem — one that would show up in losses, and maybe even at the negotiating table. Every minute that backup plays well is a minute that keeps KAT fresh for the spring. And a fresh KAT in April is a KAT whose value keeps climbing.

## WHY DADIET ISN'T THE ANSWER — AND WHY THAT MATTERS

Some fans will see "Knicks cut young player" and immediately think of Pacôme Dadiet. Let's clear that up. Dadiet is on a guaranteed rookie-scale deal worth about $2.9 million. Cutting him wouldn't magically remove that money from the books — guaranteed salary still counts when you waive a player. So there's no financial benefit in releasing him, and the Knicks have already shown they'd rather hold him than give him away. Remember, they turned down a Dadiet-for-Diabaté type of offer, and Stefan Bondy has reported he doesn't carry much trade value right now.

But here's the bigger point: Dadiet is the perfect example of why this roster is so tight. He's a young player with a guaranteed salary who might not play much this season, and that $2.9 million sits under the same apron line that the KAT negotiations are hitting. Every guaranteed dollar on the end of the bench is a dollar that can't go to KAT. That's not Dadiet's fault — it's the math of a championship roster in the second-apron era.

## WHAT THE CUT WILL TELL US ABOUT THE KNICKS' PLAN

So when the cut finally comes, here's how to read it, Tunnel fam.

If the Knicks fill all 15 spots, including a young big like Wiseman, the message is: we're all in on this season, we want maximum depth for the title defense, and we're not preparing for a big January shake-up. That's a good sign for anyone who wants this core to stay together.

If the Knicks leave the 15th spot open, the message is more complicated: we're protecting every dollar, we want flexibility, and we're keeping our options alive. It doesn't mean a KAT trade is coming. It means the front office wants to be ready if it has to happen.

And if a young player most people expected to make it — Wiseman being the biggest example — gets cut while a veteran stays, the message is: this is about winning now, and experience beats upside when you're defending a title.

Any one of those three outcomes tells us something about how Leon Rose sees the next four months. That's why this cut matters more than any other cut in recent Knicks history. It's a small transaction with a big signal.

## WHAT KAT SEES FROM THE LOCKER ROOM

Let's look at cut day from one more angle: Karl-Anthony Towns' own seat in that locker room. KAT was once the young guy everyone was watching — a number one overall pick carrying a franchise at 20 years old. He knows what it's like to have your future decided by people in an office. Now he's a champion, a veteran, and in the middle of his own business fight with the same front office.

So when a young player gets cut this month, KAT is going to see something very familiar. He's going to see a front office making a decision based on numbers, flexibility, and the apron — not just on effort or loyalty. That's the same lens being used on his contract. And that's why this cut, even if it's a fringe name, carries emotional weight inside the building. Players notice how teams treat the guys at the end of the bench. It tells them how they'll be treated when it's their turn.

And there's a positive way to read it too. If the Knicks keep a young big like Wiseman and give him real minutes, that's the front office investing in KAT's support system. It says: we want you healthy, we want you fresh, and we're building the depth around you to defend this title. Small moves send big messages, and KAT will be reading every one of them.
