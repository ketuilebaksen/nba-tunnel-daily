## THE GAME: A TALE OF TWO HALVES

**The first half was a clinic.** The Knicks came out on fire, shooting better than 70% from the field and from three in the first quarter. They led 65–50 at halftime and pushed the lead to 19 early in the third.

**The second half was a grind.** Then everything slowed down. The Knicks scored just 12 points in the fourth quarter, shot three-of-18 in the period, and didn't score at all over the final three minutes and 26 seconds. Minnesota outscored them 49–37 after the break.

**But the hole was too deep.** The Timberwolves never got over the hump, and the Knicks escaped with a 102–99 win.

**The close numbers:** The Knicks shot 45% to Minnesota's 44%, and won the rebounding battle 40–39. Jonathan Kuminga led all scorers with 23 for the Wolves, who fell to 11–5.

This was a game with a playoff feel — a strong team, a hostile building, a tight finish — and the Knicks handled it.

## KAT'S HOMECOMING AND HIS NEW RECORD

**The welcome:** Towns spent nine seasons in Minnesota before being traded to New York in 2024. Before tip-off, the Target Center crowd gave him a loud, warm welcome. That's a special moment for a player who gave that franchise so much.

**The performance:** Then he went to work. Seventeen of his 22 points came in the first half — finishing five times at the rim and knocking down a couple of threes.

**The line:** 22 points, 12 rebounds, four assists, and three blocks in 32 minutes.

**The record:** That was his 15th straight double-double — extending the new Knicks franchise record he set when he passed Willis Reed. Fifteen games, fifteen double-doubles. Every single night of the season.

**The one bad moment:** Not everything was perfect. With about three minutes left and the Knicks up 11, Towns reacted as if Rudy Gobert had hit him hard. The referees called an offensive foul on Gobert, but Chris Finch challenged, and the crowd's reaction suggested KAT had sold the contact. The call was overturned, and Minnesota turned that sequence into three points from the free-throw line and the possession that followed. That's the kind of play that can swing a close game the wrong way. A lesson for a star who's otherwise been nearly flawless.

## BRUNSON'S STRANGE NIGHT

**The scare:** Just three minutes into the game, LaMelo Ball hit Brunson in the face, and the captain headed to the locker room.

**The return:** He came back — but his shot didn't, at first. At halftime, he had just seven points on two-of-eight shooting.

**The takeover:** In the third quarter, Brunson took over. He scored 14 of New York's 25 points in the period, hitting tough shots every time Minnesota threatened to make a run.

**The final line:** 21 points and seven assists on eight-of-20 shooting, including four-of-five from three.

Towns and Brunson each took 20 shots. When the Knicks needed someone to carry the offense, their two stars did it. And OG Anunoby added 15 points, as five Knicks scored in double figures.

## THE HIDDEN HERO: THE VIDEO ROOM

And here's a story you won't see in the box score. The Knicks have a new weapon in their video room: Ed Flynn, the staffer who handles replay challenges. Flynn joined the team after three years as a video coordinator with the New Orleans Pelicans, and he's now won nine of his 11 challenges this season.

**The big one:** In the fourth quarter, LaMelo Ball was credited with an and-one after contact with Josh Hart. But replay showed an elbow to Hart's throat. The Knicks challenged with about a tenth of a second left in the challenge window — and won.

**The impact:** Minnesota then went nearly two minutes without scoring, a stretch that helped protect the lead.

Nine of 11 is elite. And think about the pressure: deciding in seconds, with a tenth of a second left in the window, whether to spend a challenge on the road in a tight game. He got it right. In close games — and the Knicks keep playing them — a sharp challenge staff is worth a win or two over a season. Shout-out to Ed Flynn.

## THE FINISH

The final minutes were tense. Ayo Dosunmu hit a three with 2:34 left to cut the lead to three. On the next trip, he hit another one that would have tied the game — but it didn't count, because a shot-clock violation was called first. After that, nobody on either team scored again.

The Knicks leaned on their defense, got the stops they needed, and escaped.

**Why the fourth quarter dried up:** Part of it was likely Minnesota's defense tightening up, with Rudy Gobert anchoring the paint. Part of it was the Knicks settling for tough jumpers instead of moving the ball the way they did in the first half, when they were hitting over 70%. And part of it is fatigue at the end of a long road trip: Indiana, two in Toronto, then Minnesota.

**The takeaway:** The offense needs to be cleaner in fourth quarters going forward. Three-of-18 and no points in the final 3:26 won't beat the East's best. But the stars delivered, the bench contributed, and even the video room earned a share of the credit.

## THE BACKUP GUARDS — A STRENGTH, NOT A LOGJAM

Before we get to the center question, we have to talk about the guards — because the win in Minnesota added fuel to one of the biggest debates around the Knicks right now.

**Tyler Kolek:** When Brunson had to leave in the first quarter, Kolek stepped in and handed out five assists in the first quarter alone. He plays like a true backup floor general — looking to get teammates involved before himself — and his assist-to-turnover ratio is among the best in the league. Remember, he's in his third season, eligible for an extension, and came into the year behind Alvarado on the depth chart.

**Jose Alvarado:** Then Mike Brown went a different direction. Looking for a spark, he turned to Alvarado — the second straight game Alvarado showed up in a surprise rotation role. He ended up playing more minutes than Kolek. His box score was quiet: 13 minutes, two points on one-of-three shooting, two rebounds, one assist, and a minus-three. But he was a plus-seven in his first-half minutes and made life hard for LaMelo Ball and Bones Hyland on defense. And Knicks fans remember how big he was in last year's Finals win over San Antonio.

**Landry Shamet:** He came into the game third in the Eastern Conference in plus-minus — and took only one shot. Brown kept shuffling the second unit, and the guards found ways to help even as the minutes moved around.

What's clear is that the guard group isn't a logjam. It's a strength. Behind Brunson, the Knicks have:
- A passer in Kolek
- A defensive pest in Alvarado
- A shooter in Shamet
- A scorer in Jordan Clarkson
- A two-way piece in Deuce McBride

Few teams in the league can match that depth.

## THE TRADE DILEMMA

So why is this even a debate? Money and roster needs.

**The rule:** The Knicks are a deep, expensive team, and owner James Dolan wants to stay below the second apron. That means in any trade, New York has to send out at least as much salary as it takes back.

**The idea being floated:** Pair Kolek with Pacôme Dadiet. Together, those two contracts add up to about $5.1 million in matching salary — potentially enough to bring in a backup center.

**But who's actually movable?**
- **Landry Shamet:** Likely off the table. He re-signed this summer on a four-year, $24 million deal, and he's been worth every dollar.
- **Jordan Clarkson:** Doesn't make sense to move. He's been essential in several wins, he's a valued voice in the locker room, and his veteran-minimum contract adds very little salary to any package.
- **Deuce McBride:** The most realistic candidate. He's on an expiring contract, and the Knicks may struggle to keep him next summer without crossing the second apron. HoopsHype's Michael Scott has reported that Shamet's strong start could make McBride more expendable. And his minutes have been shrinking: 14, 9, and 8 over the last three games.

**The cost of trading Deuce:** It would be real. Many believe he's a better all-around player than both Kolek and Alvarado — a strong shooter and one of the team's best point-of-attack defenders. If Shamet got hurt, Deuce would be the first guard the Knicks leaned on. Moving him would make the roster weaker in two areas that matter a lot in the playoffs: shooting and on-ball defense.

**The two options for Leon Rose:**
- **Option one:** Decide the frontcourt depth problem is serious enough to give up a guard.
- **Option two:** Live with thin backup center minutes and keep a clear strength intact.

Neither is perfect. And that's exactly why the internal answer matters so much.

## THE CENTER QUESTION

The Knicks have won seven of their last nine and sit at 9–6. But the backup center minutes haven't done much to help that run. That's why the calls for a trade keep getting louder.

**Andre Drummond:** He came to New York this summer to replace Mitchell Robinson, who left for Boston. He's still one of the best rebounders in the game and gives the Knicks a physical presence on the glass. But outside of rebounding, his impact has been limited. He's not a strong rim protector at this stage of his career, he hasn't stretched the floor, and his minutes have often been hard to watch. For a team trying to repeat, that's a weak spot opponents can target.

**The new wrinkle:** And then, in Minnesota, something surprising. With under three minutes left in the third quarter, Mikal Bridges found Drummond for a 10-foot jumper — and Drummond knocked it down. A small play on paper. But floor spacing has never been part of his game. Seeing the backup center step out and hit a shot from that range gives the Knicks a look they rarely get from that spot.

## THE ANSWER: FIND OUT ABOUT WISEMAN FIRST

And here's the core argument. Before the front office trades away a guard, it should find out what it has in James Wiseman.

**The case for Wiseman:**
- **The size and athleticism:** He's a 7-footer with length and bounce — exactly the "young, athletic, up-and-coming" profile the Knicks were looking for all summer.
- **The glass:** Per 36 minutes, he's pulling down more offensive rebounds than anyone on the roster — roughly 50% more than Drummond.
- **The shot-blocking:** Remember that four blocks in his first 21 minutes of the season matched the total of starters who had played ten times as many minutes.
- **The finishing:** He dunks over people. The Knicks have had their shots blocked at the rim far more this season than last. A big who finishes above defenders helps fix that.
- **The flashes:** Eight points, eight rebounds, and three blocks in about 10 minutes against Chicago.

**The case for patience:** He's barely played — 26 minutes in the first 12 games. That's not enough to know what he is. And a 26-minute sample shouldn't decide whether the Knicks trade a playoff-tested guard.

**The cost of finding out:** Almost nothing. Wiseman is already on the roster. Giving him a defined backup role for a few weeks — before the trade window opens on December 15th — costs a few minutes, not a rotation player.

**My view:** That's the smart order of operations. Give Wiseman real minutes now. If he answers the question, the Knicks keep their guard depth and save their trade assets. If he doesn't, the front office trades with full information in January.

## WHAT A "WISEMAN AUDITION" SHOULD LOOK LIKE

If Brown does give Wiseman a real look, here's how I'd set it up — my analysis, not a report.

**Defined minutes:** Eight to 12 minutes a night, mostly in the first half, so a few mistakes don't swing a close game. Consistency matters for a young big. Two minutes one night and a DNP the next doesn't tell you anything.

**Defined role:** Screen, roll, rebound, protect the rim. That's it. No face-up jumpers, no forcing post-ups. Simple jobs, done hard.

**Defined partners:** Pair him with Kolek, a pass-first guard who'll find him on rolls and lobs. Kolek has already shown great chemistry with KAT. Give Wiseman the same chance.

**Defined measures:**
- Does he stay vertical and avoid cheap fouls?
- Does he grab offensive rebounds without giving up transition baskets?
- Does he finish at the rim instead of getting blocked?
- Does the defense hold up when he's in drop coverage?

**A timeline:** Run it through December 15th. By then, the Knicks will know whether they need to spend a guard on a center — or whether the answer was already on the bench.

And let's remember Wiseman's story. He was the No. 2 overall pick in 2020. He tore his Achilles. He was reportedly headed overseas before the Knicks gave him this chance. Few players have more reason to grab an opportunity when it comes.

Start at the top of the list. Only move down if you have to.
