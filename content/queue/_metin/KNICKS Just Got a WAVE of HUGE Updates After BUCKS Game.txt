## UPDATE #1: THE GAME — A BLOWOUT WITH NO LETDOWN

**The setup:** On paper, this was a game the Knicks were supposed to win. Milwaukee came in at 1–9, struggling through its first season without Giannis Antetokounmpo and coming off a 20-point loss to Boston. And the Bucks were short-handed — Tyler Herro, their leading scorer at 21.4 points per game, was out with a knee injury.

**The danger:** But here's what worried people going in. This Knicks team has shown a habit of playing down to weaker opponents. Brooklyn, a winless team at the time, led the Knicks after three quarters just over a week ago. And a rematch with Cleveland was waiting the very next night. A slow, sloppy, look-ahead performance wouldn't have been a surprise.

**What actually happened:** The Knicks took care of business. Here's how the quarters went:

| Quarter | Knicks | Bucks | Running score |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 26 | 21 | 26–21 |
| Q2 | 32 | 13 | 58–34 |
| Q3 | 27 | 38 | 85–72 |
| Q4 | 32 | 13 | 117–85 |

**The second quarter** was a defensive clinic. Thirteen points allowed in 12 minutes. The ball was moving, the defense was locked in, and the lead jumped to 24 at halftime.

**The third quarter** was the one blip. The Bucks came out of halftime with more energy, won the quarter 38–27, and briefly made it feel competitive.

**The fourth quarter** ended it. Another 32–13 quarter, the lead ballooned, and Mike Brown emptied the bench in the closing minutes. Two quarters where the Knicks held an NBA team to 13 points. That's championship-level defense.

## UPDATE #2: THE STARS GOT TO REST

**Jalen Brunson:** 23 points in just 27 minutes on seven-of-18 shooting. Not his most efficient night, and he didn't need it to be. The reigning Eastern Conference Player of the Week came in averaging 26.2 points and 5.6 assists, attacked a Milwaukee defense ranked 27th in the league, and then spent a good part of the fourth quarter on the bench. With a back-to-back on the schedule, those saved minutes matter. Remember Mike Brown's comments about the two-minute tracking data — Brunson can handle heavy minutes, but the goal is to protect him for the moments that matter most. A blowout is the perfect chance to do that.

**Karl-Anthony Towns:** Another monster night on the glass — 21 points and 16 rebounds. Another double-double in a season where he's been one of the best rebounders in the entire NBA. And look at the team rebounding: Milwaukee came in ranked sixth in the league in rebounds per game. The Knicks out-rebounded them 59–32. That's a 27-rebound margin. Extra possessions all night long.

**OG Anunoby:** And here's maybe the most encouraging sign of the night. OG has been in a real shooting slump this season — 29.3% from three coming in, and he'd been one of the most concerning trends on this team. Against Milwaukee, he looked far more comfortable. Nineteen points, and he was a big part of the early push that built the lead. If he carries that into the next few games, the Knicks have their dependable third scorer back behind Brunson and Towns.

**The team numbers:**
- **Knicks:** 45% from the field, 17 three-pointers, 26 assists
- **Bucks:** 38% from the field, 20 assists, 16 threes — but nothing inside and crushed on the boards

For Milwaukee, Jaime Jaquez Jr. led the way with 16 points and five rebounds, and Ryan Rollins added 14 and five. But without Herro, the Bucks just didn't have the firepower. Jaquez and Herro both arrived in Milwaukee in the trade that sent Giannis away, and this season has shown how big the gap is for a franchise starting over. Milwaukee drops to 1–10.

## UPDATE #3: THE JOSH HART NEWS — WHY IT'S ACTUALLY GOOD

**The headline:** Josh Hart missed another game.

**The detail that matters:** Before the game, Mike Brown said Hart was out because of an illness — not the ankle. And Brown made it clear Hart would have played if he hadn't been sick.

**Why that's good news:** Hart had been sidelined since the Brooklyn game on November 2nd with right ankle soreness. That ankle was the real concern. Ankle problems can linger for weeks, and they can limit a player even after he returns. The fact that an illness — not the ankle — kept him out on Tuesday strongly suggests the ankle is finally behind him. And an illness is usually a short-term problem.

**The timeline:** If he recovers quickly, Hart could be back as soon as Wednesday night against Cleveland.

**The numbers:** Before he was shut down, Hart was averaging 8.6 points, 7.3 rebounds, and 3.9 assists while shooting just 38.6% from the field — his weakest numbers since he arrived in New York. Part of that may have been the ankle all along. Once he's fully healthy, the expectation is that his production moves back toward normal.

And let's be fair to Hart: playing through an ankle problem for seven games while still grabbing seven-plus rebounds a night says a lot about his toughness. That's exactly who he is.

**The Tunnel verdict:** Another absence sounds bad. But "out with an illness, would have played otherwise" is one of the best injury updates the Knicks have had all season.

## UPDATE #4: LANDRY SHAMET, STARTER — THE NUMBERS ARE REAL

While Hart's been out, Landry Shamet has stepped into the starting lineup as Brunson's main backcourt partner. And the results have been better than almost anyone expected.

**His stretch as a starter heading into Tuesday:**
- 12.7 points per game
- 52.2% from the field
- 42.1% from three
- 1.3 assists and 1.3 rebounds per game

Those aren't flashy all-around numbers. But his shooting has been exactly what this team needed, especially with Bridges and OG both struggling from deep.

**The team results:** The Knicks were 3–0 in those games before Tuesday. Shamet started again against Milwaukee, scored in double figures, and the Knicks cruised. That makes it four straight wins with Shamet in the starting lineup.

**The advanced numbers with Shamet on the floor:**
- **Offense:** 119.2 points per 100 possessions — 82nd percentile
- **Defense:** 107.5 points allowed per 100 possessions — 91st percentile
- **Opponent effective field goal percentage:** just 48.8%

That defensive number surprised me. Shamet isn't known as a stopper, but the units he's been part of have been excellent on both ends.

**The fair caveat:** The Knicks shouldn't expect Shamet to replace Hart long term. Hart is central to this team's identity — the guard who crashes the glass like a big man, guards multiple positions, and plays with relentless energy. He was a huge part of bringing a championship back to the Garden. Shamet is a great short-term answer, but what Hart brings is very hard to replace.

**The big question:** When Hart returns, does Shamet go back to the bench? Almost certainly. But his minutes shouldn't drop much. He's earned a real role.

## UPDATE #5: THE BENCH KEEPS DELIVERING

This stretch really shows the depth of this roster, and the Bucks blowout put it on full display.

**Tyler Kolek:** The third-year backup point guard came into the game averaging 14.9 assists per 36 minutes — a number that shows how well he runs an offense when he's on the floor. Against Milwaukee, he played 15 minutes and put up 12 points, three rebounds, four assists, and a blocked shot. He scored, he set up teammates, and he defended. That's everything you want from a backup point guard. And remember, he's held that job even since Jose Alvarado got healthy — Alvarado didn't play at all in Cleveland.

**Andre Drummond:** In limited minutes, he grabbed double-digit rebounds and gave the Knicks a physical presence inside while Towns rested. He's leading the NBA in offensive rebounds per 36 minutes among players with at least 100 minutes. The unsung hero keeps doing unsung-hero things.

**Deuce McBride:** Steady, reliable minutes in the backcourt. The second unit kept the pressure on and never let Milwaukee back in.

**Pacôme Dadiet:** And here's a fun one. The young Frenchman checked in late with the game decided — and took full advantage. He scored on several straight possessions in the fourth quarter: a three-pointer, a jumper, and a layup plus a free throw. It was a short burst, but it was a reminder of the potential the Knicks once saw in him. Remember, his fourth-year option was just declined. Nights like this build confidence — and maybe a little trade value too.

**The bigger lesson:** The Knicks have several low-cost players who can step in, play their role, and help win. Over an 82-game season with injuries, illnesses, and back-to-backs, that matters. It gives Mike Brown options. Depth helps a team survive the regular season.

## UPDATE #6: THE KAT CONTRACT — THE REAL REASON FOR THE STANDOFF

And now the biggest long-term question in New York. On the court, Karl-Anthony Towns is playing at an elite level — Tuesday's 21 and 16 was just the latest example. Off the court, his future is unresolved.

**The reported numbers:** According to reports, the Knicks have held firm with offers in the range of $45 to $50 million per year. As we covered last video, the Post's Stefan Bondy reported that Towns turned down a four-year, roughly $200 million offer this past summer — about $77 million less than his max.

**The obvious explanation: the second apron.** It's the strictest spending limit in the collective bargaining agreement. Teams above it lose trade flexibility, can't use certain contract exceptions, and can even see future draft picks frozen. James Dolan has been reluctant to cross it, and plenty of fans have criticized that approach.

**But here's the deeper explanation.** Even without the second apron, the Knicks front office would likely hold a similar price. The real concern is how big contracts for star players age over time.

## THE AGING-CONTRACT PROBLEM

Look around the league this season. There are 15 players aged 30 or older earning more than $45 million. The list includes Stephen Curry, Nikola Jokić, Giannis Antetokounmpo, Anthony Davis, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown, Devin Booker, Jimmy Butler, Paul George, Kawhi Leonard, Donovan Mitchell, Zach LaVine, Pascal Siakam, Domantas Sabonis — and Karl-Anthony Towns himself.

Now ask a simple question: how many of those contracts would bring back positive value in a trade right now?

According to the analysis, only about five: Jokić, Curry, Giannis, Siakam, and Towns. Kawhi could be part of that group, but he's dealing with an injury. Jaylen Brown has a reasonable case — but Boston traded him this summer and got what's been described as a modest return. That tells you a lot about how teams view expensive players once they pass 30.

**The numbers keep climbing:**
- Donovan Mitchell signed a four-year, $276 million extension — and it's already seen as one of the toughest contracts in the league to move.
- Stephen Curry's extension is worth $116 million over two years.
- Giannis becomes eligible for a four-year, $276 million deal starting January 6th.
- Nikola Jokić is in line for a five-year, roughly $357 million max in the 2027 offseason.

**The team-building trend:** Front offices have also become wary of pairing two stars on near-max deals. Boston is often used as the example of how expensive that model gets and how quickly it forces tough decisions.

## WHAT THAT MEANS FOR THE KNICKS

For the Knicks, this is the core tension. Brunson becomes eligible for his own huge extension next summer — projected at about $286.5 million over four years. If they also pay Towns near the max, they'd have two near-max players heading into their 30s, plus Bridges' $150 million deal and OG's long-term contract, all under a second apron they refuse to cross.

So the Knicks' position isn't just "Dolan is cheap." It's a bet that paying a 30-plus big man near the max for four more years carries real risk. And the fact that Towns is one of the few over-30 stars whose contract still has positive trade value cuts both ways: it means he's worth a lot — and it means the Knicks know exactly what he'd bring back if it ever came to that.

**My view:** Both sides have a point. Towns has earned a big raise with his play. The Knicks have a legitimate long-term concern. The most likely landing spot is somewhere in the middle — above their $45 to $50 million range, below the max. That's my analysis, not a report.

## THE WINDOW KEEPS MOVING

One more thing about the contract. The longer this goes, the harder it can get. Every double-double raises KAT's value. Every big contract around the league — like Jalen Duren's reported four-year, $200 million extension in Detroit — gives his camp another comparison. And with Brunson's extension window opening next summer, the Knicks may want KAT's deal settled before they start that conversation. Waiting has a cost on both sides. That's why this standoff is worth watching even while the team is winning.

The turnaround keeps going. The Knicks opened 0–3 — the first defending champion to start that way since the 2011–12 Mavericks — and they've won six of seven since.
