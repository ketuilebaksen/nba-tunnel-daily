## THE HONEST FRAMING

Let me be clear up front. We could not find any credible report that the Knicks and the Washington Wizards are discussing a Karl-Anthony Towns for Anthony Davis trade. We could not find Shams saying "this trade is going to happen." What we did find is a real Shams comment about KAT and Josh Hart as possible trade candidates, a hypothetical trade proposal from a sports website, and a lot of noise built on top of both. Let's separate the pieces.

## WHAT'S REAL: SHAMS ON "NBA TODAY"

Here's the real Shams quote, and it matters. In early October, on ESPN's NBA Today, Shams said Towns and Hart could become trade candidates this season if they don't sign extensions. He noted that Hart "has already hinted" at things heating up "as this gets closer to February," and said "the temperature could rise if Karl-Anthony Towns and Hart himself are not extended." And then the key line: "So far, these sides are not close."

That's a real warning. It's the same theme Ian Begley raised with his "January pivot" logic — you can't lose a star for nothing in free agency. But notice what Shams did NOT say. He didn't name Anthony Davis. He didn't name Washington. He didn't say a trade is going to happen. He said the pressure could rise if nothing changes.

## WHERE THE KAT-FOR-AD IDEA ACTUALLY CAME FROM

So where did "Davis for Towns" come from? From a hypothetical. On October 8th, Adam Taylor of Heavy — republished on Yahoo Sports — wrote a proposed trade: the Knicks send Towns to Washington, and the Wizards send Davis to New York. He presented it as his own idea: "a potential trade package could look something like this." The Wizards as a possible landing spot for KAT came from Andrew Peters of Bleacher Report, who listed teams that could chase Towns if he and the Knicks can't agree.

That's it. A columnist's trade idea. Not a report of talks, not an offer, not a phone call. And to be fair, writers propose trades every day — that's part of the fun of this league. The problem is when a proposal gets dressed up as breaking news.

## WHAT WE COULD NOT CONFIRM

Here's the list of claims in the viral version that we couldn't verify anywhere in mainstream reporting:

**"Shams confirmed the conversation is back on the table."** Couldn't find it.

**"This trade is going to happen."** Couldn't find it. And that's not how Shams usually talks about trades that haven't happened.

**"Jake Fischer reported in February 2026 that the Wizards called the Knicks about Towns."** Couldn't confirm it. The Heavy article that proposed the deal doesn't mention any Wizards phone call — in February or ever.

**"KAT said last night, 'We're not that Knick team yet.'"** Couldn't confirm that quote. And it doesn't match what KAT actually told ESPN's Malika Andrews this week, when he said he's willing to take less than the max because "I want this team to stay together."

**Davis's contract "renewal postponed until approximately 20 games."** Couldn't confirm.

So until a credible insider says it plainly, treat this one as unconfirmed — and treat the "it's going to happen" part as false.

## WHY THE IDEA IS TEMPTING ON PAPER

Now, let's be fair to the idea itself, because there's a reason it went viral. On paper, it's a fascinating swap.

**The salaries line up.** KAT makes about $57 million this season with a $61.1 million player option for 2027–28. Davis is in a similar range, reportedly around $58.5 million. In trade terms, that's close enough that the salary-matching math is simple. That's the "Parent Trap" angle — two lookalike contracts swapping places.

**The résumé is real.** Davis is a 10-time All-Star and won a title in 2020. He knows what the last step of a championship run feels like.

**The need is real.** The Knicks lost Mitchell Robinson — their rim protector — to Boston. Davis is one of the best rim protectors of his generation. He'd give Jalen Brunson a vertical lob partner in the pick-and-roll, the thing that made Brunson and Mitch one of the league's top duos. And remember the preseason: in his first game as a Wizard, Davis scored 16 points with seven rebounds in under 17 minutes, and he won his matchup with KAT that night.

So yes, on a napkin, it looks like a direct solution to a direct problem. That's why people are talking about it.

## WHY IT'S A BAD IDEA IN REALITY

But here's where the napkin falls apart.

**Age and health.** Davis is 33 and has a long injury history. KAT turns 31 on November 15th. Trading a younger star for an older one with more injury risk, in the middle of a title defense, is a huge gamble. Any team making that move has to price in that history honestly.

**KAT is the hub.** The Knicks' championship run turned on making KAT the offensive hub — high-post passes, dribble handoffs, spacing the floor so Brunson can attack. Davis is a different type of player. He doesn't stretch the floor the way KAT does. You'd be solving one problem — rim protection — by creating another: spacing and playmaking around Brunson.

**Washington's timeline.** The Wizards have Trae Young and AJ Dybantsa, the number one overall pick. Would a team built around a teenage rookie want to commit huge money to a 31-year-old big on a new long-term deal? That's not obvious at all.

**The insiders don't see a trade coming.** Jake Fischer has reported that KAT is likely to decline his player option and that extension talks probably won't resume until after the trade deadline. If talks resume after the deadline, the Knicks aren't planning to trade him this season. Daily Knicks pointed out that the Knicks kept Mitchell Robinson past the deadline even knowing he'd likely leave, and argued KAT's extension drama won't lead to a trade. And The Sporting News' Jon Conahan wrote that Towns is unlikely to be traded, citing Sean Deveney: "Is there an offer that would get the Knicks to the table? Probably not."

**KAT wants to stay.** And maybe most important: KAT just told ESPN that "nowhere in my mind a max was even thought of," and that keeping this team together is his priority. Jalen Brunson's response: "Sign a contract." That's not a relationship heading toward a blockbuster swap.

## THE FIT, POSSESSION BY POSSESSION

Let's play it out on the court, because this is where the idea really gets tested.

**On offense with KAT:** Brunson comes off a high screen. KAT pops to the three-point line, and his defender has to choose — stay home on a 40-percent-type shooter, or help on Brunson. Either way, the floor is open. KAT can also catch at the top of the key and run dribble handoffs, which is how the Knicks' offense flowed during the title run.

**On offense with Davis:** Brunson comes off the same screen. Davis rolls hard to the rim — a real lob threat, which the Knicks haven't had since Mitch left. That's a plus. But Davis's man can sag into the paint without paying the same price, and Brunson loses the open driving lanes that KAT's spacing creates. Add Josh Hart, who isn't a high-volume shooter, and the floor can get crowded quickly.

**On defense with KAT:** This is KAT's weakness. Teams attack him and try to get him in foul trouble, like Philadelphia did on opening night.

**On defense with Davis:** This is Davis's strength. He protects the rim, switches onto smaller players, and changes shots. The Knicks' defense would almost certainly get better.

So the swap trades offensive spacing for defensive protection. For some teams, that's a great trade. For a team whose title offense was built around KAT's unique skill set, it's a big risk.

## WHAT ABOUT THE REST OF THE STARTING FIVE?

A trade doesn't happen in a vacuum, so think about the other four starters. Jalen Brunson thrives with space — KAT gives him that. OG Anunoby has been crashing the offensive glass at a career-high rate and would benefit from a rim-running big drawing attention. Mikal Bridges needs driving lanes too. And Josh Hart, the heart of the team and one of the league's best rebounding guards, isn't a high-volume shooter — which means a lineup of Hart plus a non-spacing big could make life harder for Brunson. KAT's shooting is part of what lets Hart do Hart things. Swap KAT for Davis, and the Knicks would have to rethink their spacing across the entire starting group.

## WHAT THE KNICKS COULD DO INSTEAD

So if the Knicks want more rim protection without trading KAT, what are the realistic options?

**Develop the internal option.** James Wiseman, the 6-foot-11 former number two pick, earned the final roster spot because Mike Brown values his "vertical threat at the rim, the vertical threat defensively." Getting him a few minutes each half would also protect Andre Drummond from the heavy load that wore him down against Philly.

**Use the trade tools after December 15th.** Pacôme Dadiet's roughly $3 million salary, paired with draft compensation, could be enough for a young big like Moussa Diabaté, according to Daily Knicks. Combine Dadiet's salary with Deuce McBride's, and the Knicks could bring back a player making just over $6.9 million.

**Watch the young bigs on cheap deals.** We've covered names like Ryan Kalkbrenner in Charlotte before — the kind of rim protector who fits under the apron. And remember, Bobby Marks said a Kel'el Ware trade is very hard to see.

None of those are blockbuster headlines. But they're the kind of moves that actually fit this roster and this front office.

## THE APRON MATH OF A SWAP

And there's a money wrinkle too. The salaries are close, but not identical — Davis is reportedly about $1.5 million higher. The Knicks are only around $3.2 million below the second apron, and teams near that line have strict rules about taking back more salary than they send out. Even a "lookalike" swap would need careful accounting. And then there's next season: KAT's $61.1 million option versus Davis's own contract down the road. A trade that looks simple in 30 seconds can get complicated very quickly once the cap experts get involved.

## IF WASHINGTON DID CALL — SAYING NO WAS RIGHT

Let's play out the viral claim one more way. Suppose, hypothetically, Washington had called about KAT last February. The viral video itself admits the Knicks said no — and then won the championship. That's the best possible proof that saying no was the right call. The Knicks were building toward a title, KAT was a huge part of it, and they got it done.

And the same logic holds now. The Knicks are the defending champions. Their core is intact except for Mitch. KAT has publicly said he wants to stay and will take less. Brunson wants him signed. You don't blow that up in October because of one tough opening-night loss.

## THE CONTEXT: WHY RUMORS SPREAD AFTER A LOSS

And there's a reason this rumor caught fire this week. The Knicks lost the opener to Philadelphia, 106–100. KAT picked up early fouls and played only 24 minutes. The latest championship odds slid the Knicks to plus-1100, behind the Sixers. When fans are nervous, blockbuster rumors spread faster.

But remember what Mike Brown's team showed last season: the Knicks rallied from double-digit deficits four times on the way to the title. One loss doesn't define a champion. And it definitely doesn't justify trading the second-best player on the roster.

## THE REAL "PRICE" OF KAT

So if KAT isn't the price of Anthony Davis, what is the real price? It's the price of keeping him.

Brian Windhorst has warned that if the Knicks pay KAT close to what he wants, Josh Hart and Deuce McBride would likely be moved because of the second apron. The executives Stefan Bondy talked to put KAT's market value at about $40 to $50 million a year — right around the Knicks' roughly $200 million offer. KAT is eligible for about $276 million. A middle-ground deal — something like a four-year contract starting near his max of about $61.6 million and declining 8 percent a year — would total roughly $218 million.

That's the real trade-off this season: not KAT for Davis, but KAT's next contract versus the depth around him. And the decision will likely come after the trade deadline, not before.

## A QUICK WORD ON VIRAL RUMORS

We did a full checklist on spotting fake trade rumors in our Cason Wallace fact-check video, so I'll keep this short. When a video tells you an insider said a trade "is going to happen," ask three questions. Can you find the insider's actual words? Do the details match what other reporters are saying? And does the trade fit what the team has already told us about its plans? This rumor fails all three. A real Shams quote about pressure got blended with a columnist's hypothetical and turned into a "confirmed" blockbuster.

## WHAT TO ACTUALLY WATCH

So what should Knicks fans actually keep an eye on?

**KAT's foul trouble.** In the opener, he picked up his second foul at 3:15 and played just 24 minutes. Fixing that is more important than any trade rumor.

**The December 15th window.** That's when most offseason signings become tradable and the market wakes up. If the Knicks make a move for size, the realistic tools are Pacôme Dadiet's contract, Deuce McBride's expiring deal, and second-round picks — not KAT.

**The trade deadline in February.** If the Knicks and KAT are still far apart and nothing changes, Shams' "temperature could rise" warning becomes real. But all the reporting so far points to the Knicks keeping him and negotiating after.

**The extension talks.** KAT says he'll take less. The math says a deal around $218 to $230 million is possible. That's the storyline that matters.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. KAT for Anthony Davis is a fun napkin trade, and I understand why it caught fire — the salaries match and the Knicks really do miss Mitch's rim protection. But there's no credible report of talks, the "this trade is going to happen" quote couldn't be verified, and every serious indicator — KAT's own words, Brunson's words, Fischer's timeline, the Robinson precedent — points the other way. I expect KAT to finish this season as a Knick and sign an extension in the $218 to $230 million range after the deadline. Anthony Davis stays in Washington. And if the Knicks add size this season, it'll come from the Dadiet-and-picks toolbox or from Wiseman's development — not from trading their second star.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's the fact-check. Real: Shams said on NBA Today that KAT and Josh Hart could become trade candidates if they aren't extended, that the temperature could rise toward February, and that the sides are not close. Real: Heavy's Adam Taylor proposed a hypothetical KAT-for-Anthony Davis trade, and Bleacher Report's Andrew Peters listed Washington among possible KAT destinations. Unconfirmed: Shams saying the talks are "back on the table" or that "this trade is going to happen," a February 2026 Wizards phone call, and a KAT quote saying "we're not that Knick team yet." The idea is tempting on paper — similar salaries, a 10-time All-Star rim protector — but Davis is 33 with a long injury history, KAT is the offensive hub, and the insiders expect KAT to stay. The real price of KAT is his next contract — and what it could cost the Knicks in Hart and Deuce.
