## VOICE ONE: KAT — "I'M TWO FOR ONE NOW"

Let's start with the player whose name has been attached to almost nothing but contract headlines lately. Karl-Anthony Towns. And this week, he said something that deserves a lot more attention than it got.

Here's the quote: "The only thing that changes is the promise that we made to New York when I got here — to be that missing piece and be part of the team that finally brings the championship home. When I say it changes the promise: I'm two for one now. I want to try to get two."

Then he kept going. He said the team has been having great practices, and that hopefully they can unify and the continuity from last year carries over. "Nothing diverts us from each other." He said they're always leaning into their on-court experience and having fun. And then the part that should excite every Knicks fan: last year, the team was learning Mike Brown's new system. This year, he said, camp has been "a lot more of just re-emphasizing and tweaking and being ahead of the curve, so we won't have to relearn his offense. We'll just know it. It's just who we are. It's in our DNA." And he finished by saying the team is starting off in a much better place than he expected last year.

**What it really means:** Read that again — "nothing diverts us from each other." That's a man in the middle of a contract standoff telling you, directly, that the business isn't going to break the locker room. And "two for one now" is a beautiful way to frame it. When KAT arrived, the promise was one title. He delivered. Now he's raising his own bar. That's not a player with one foot out the door. That's a player chasing a legacy.

And the system point matters too. Last season, the Knicks didn't even lean on the KAT-as-hub offense during the regular season — they discovered it in the playoffs. This year, it's the starting point. "It's in our DNA" means the Knicks start this season where they finished the last one. That's a scary thought for the rest of the league.

## VOICE TWO: MIKE BROWN ON KAT — "I TRULY LOVE THE WAY HE'S BEEN PRACTICING"

And the praise went both ways. According to James L. Edwards III of The Athletic, KAT said the coaches and players talked openly this week and called it a "mindful practice." He said it was really cool for Mike Brown to talk about everyone being human. "I got to give flowers to Mike. It was an amazing job by him."

Then Mike Brown returned the favor. "I truly love the way he's been practicing," Brown said. He added that KAT is in great shape and has been helping the young guys.

**What it really means:** This is the quote I'd show anybody who thinks the contract situation is poisoning the building. A head coach doesn't go out of his way to praise a player's practice habits, his conditioning, AND his leadership with young guys unless he means it. And remember, this is the same coach who stood in front of the whole camp and said nobody outside the starting five is safe. Brown isn't handing out compliments for free right now.

And think about the "helping the young guys" part. KAT is doing that while his own future is unresolved. That's what leaders do. You can disagree with his contract number — that's a business debate. But you can't question how he's carrying himself in the gym.

## VOICE THREE: MIKE BROWN ON BRUCE BROWN — "IT WAS TEXTBOOK"

Next voice, and this one has real roster implications. Mike Brown on Bruce Brown, the veteran on a tryout deal fighting for a job.

"It's great that you're able to get vets like him into camp, because you get to know them. You never know what to expect if you don't really know somebody. You get to know their personalities just as much as you get to know their game. He's been doing a nice job."

Then he went further, talking about a group Bruce played with a few nights earlier: those guys made a couple of mistakes, but they played hard — not just defensively, but moving the ball quickly. "Bruce, he was great on the ball. It was textbook. Watching him still do those things, knowing he's fighting for a spot — it's a lot of fun to see."

**What it really means:** "Textbook." Coaches don't use that word lightly. And it lines up perfectly with everything we've covered — Bruce Brown rated as the second-best screen navigator on this roster last season behind only Jose Alvarado, and against Washington, he defended well enough that the Wizards started passing out of his matchup.

But here's the catch, and it's the same one we laid out in our trial video: with only one roster spot available under the apron, and three centers in the mix, Bruce Brown probably needs a SECOND spot to open. And that leads straight to the next piece of reporting.

James L. Edwards also wrote that Pacôme Dadiet is the youngest of the group but hasn't shown much to date, and that he wouldn't be surprised if New York ends up trading him in a salary dump — if there are any takers — to open up an extra roster spot for one of its veterans in camp. So the chain reaction we mapped in our Buy, Sell, Hold video is still very much alive: move Dadiet's $2.98 million, attach a second-round pick, and open the door for Bruce Brown. Keep an eye on that this week.

## VOICE FOUR: THE NUMBER — BRUNSON'S PROJECTED $290 MILLION

The fourth voice isn't a quote. It's a number. And depending on how you look at it, it's either great news or a little scary.

According to reporting from The Athletic and Yahoo Sports, The Athletic's Fred Katz believes Jalen Brunson can get an extension worth around $290 million over four years — a deal that would begin in the 2028–29 season. All signs point to the NBA Finals MVP earning a massive raise.

**What it really means:** Let's start with the emotional side. Jalen Brunson took less money to come to New York and then signed an extension that left money on the table so this front office could build a champion. It worked. He won Finals MVP. He delivered the first title since 1973. If anyone in this league has earned a huge payday, it's him. He deserves every penny.

Now the honest side — because this channel always gives you both. That number sits right next to everything else on the Knicks' books. KAT's extension. Hart's future. Deuce's next deal. OG's contract. And Dolan's second-apron line. Fred Katz is the same reporter who broke down how much of the cap KAT and Brunson take up together — and a $290 million Brunson deal down the road means the squeeze on the rest of this roster only gets tighter. That's why every negotiation happening right now is connected. When you hear the front office holding firm with KAT, part of the reason is that they KNOW this Brunson number is coming. Good news? Absolutely. But it's good news with a long shadow.

## VOICE FIVE: JORDAN CLARKSON — THE ROLE AND THE WARNING TO MITCH

The fifth voice is one of my favorites of the week. Jordan Clarkson on his role first: "It's something that I've fallen in love with. I love playing my role, playing for this team, playing for each other. And I think that was the biggest thing last year that I learned — just trying to be comfortable and know the system. This year, a lot more — come here, provide a spark, and keep trying to do the small things that win games."

Then he talked about Mitchell Robinson, and you could feel it. "That was my dog, man." He said that on every road trip, Mitch's locker was next to his, and they'd go eat together most of the time on the road. He said Mitch was probably the closest guy to him on the team, because he already knew Josh, OG, and Jalen before — but last year he got to know Mitch and became close with his family. "It's definitely tough, but it's the business of basketball. I hope he does great things in Boston. But when we see him, we've got to whoop him." And then right back to love: "That's my dog, man. I love that dude. Forever."

**What it really means:** Two things. First, Clarkson's quote about his role is exactly what a contender needs to hear from a former Sixth Man of the Year. He's not asking for more shots. He's not complaining about minutes. He's talking about "the small things that win games." On a team where Mike Brown just told everyone outside the starting five to fight for minutes, that's the right mindset.

Second, the Mitch part is pure Knicks energy. Love for your guy, and zero mercy when he's on the other side. And remember — Boston plays the Knicks in its second game of the season. Mitch's first trip into the fight with his old teammates is coming almost immediately. Brunson already joked that Mitch won't be his friend four nights a year. Now Clarkson has added his own warning. That game is going to be emotional, loud, and physical.

## VOICE SIX: MIKAL BRIDGES — "I'VE GOT A PHONE"

The sixth voice belongs to the player who gets more scrutiny than almost anyone on this roster. Mikal Bridges addressed the noise directly.

"I've got a phone. Even if I don't want to see it or anything, it's always there. That's just life. People talk about anything, and that's all right. People can think however they want to think. I just know that I'm going to work hard and try to be the best teammate that I can be, and the best player that I can be, and continue to try to help this team win as much as I can."

**What it really means:** Bridges knows what people say. The trade price with Brooklyn. The questions about avoiding contact on his drives. The debate about whether his playoff physicality was real. And his answer isn't defensive — it's calm. He sees it, he accepts it, and he goes back to work. And notice: that lines up with what we've seen on the court this preseason. He's driven straight to the basket several times instead of settling, and he's said attacking more is a focus. The best response to criticism is the one you give with your game. That's what he's doing.

## VOICE SEVEN: HART AND BRUNSON ON DRUMMOND

The seventh voice is actually two voices — and they're both about the man replacing Mitchell Robinson in the rotation.

Jalen Brunson on Andre Drummond's three-point shooting: he said that when Drummond made three threes in a game last year, "I remember that I was questioning myself, like, where did this come from? That's what happens when you work on your game."

And Josh Hart: "It's tough to replace someone like Mitchell Robinson. Obviously, an amazing rebounder, an amazing defensive player. Drum gives you a different look. Offensively, a way better passer than I thought he was. He's extremely light on his feet. Obviously, can knock down shots."

**What it really means:** When your two most respected voices in the locker room — the captain and the heart of the team — both go out of their way to praise the new center, that matters. Hart's line about passing is especially interesting, because it matches what we saw against Washington: four assists in 20 minutes, from a player not known as a passer. And "light on his feet" speaks directly to the biggest fear about Drummond coming in — pick-and-roll defense. Through two games, he's held up better than many expected.

One honest note from this channel, though: the three-point shot is a nice bonus, but Drummond's value lives near the basket. He shot 35.6 percent from deep in his career year. We'd still rather see him crashing the glass than falling in love with the corner three. Let Shamet and OG handle that.

## VOICE EIGHT: SGA — "I'M [BLEEP]ED"

And now the voice from outside the building — and it's a great one. The reigning NBA MVP, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, apparently did NOT enjoy watching the Knicks win the championship.

According to his trainer, Nem Ilić, in a recent interview, it didn't fully hit SGA until New York won Game 5. Ilić said: "Literally, as the buzzer was about to sound, he texted me and said something like, 'I'm [bleep]ed. I can't believe I'm not there right now.'" The trainer explained that they were on vacation around the time Games 1 and 2 started and watched together, but he didn't get that same feeling from SGA until the series was actually over. Then it hit him: the Thunder had lost, and New York had won the title. Ilić added that SGA isn't a guy who makes excuses — it didn't work out the way he hoped, but that's part of an NBA season. So he knew he had to go back to the drawing board and get better.

**What it really means:** Honestly? This is a compliment to the Knicks. The best player in the world watched New York lift the trophy and couldn't stand it. And you know what — get in line. San Antonio wasn't happy. Plenty of fans around the league weren't happy. The Knicks won a championship that a lot of people didn't expect, and now every contender is chasing them.

And I respect SGA for it. That's the competitiveness you'd expect from an MVP. He's angry, he's motivated, and he's coming back better. But remember how the West played out last spring: Oklahoma City didn't get past San Antonio. The Spurs did — and then the Knicks beat the Spurs in five. If SGA wants to settle it, there's only one place to do it: the NBA Finals. Thunder versus Knicks. SGA versus Brunson. If that matchup happens, it'll be epic. And this channel will be ready.

## VOICE NINE: THE MARKET — THE CHAMPS AT +1000

And the final voice is the betting market, which apparently didn't get the memo about who won last June. Here are the latest odds to win the NBA Finals this season:

Oklahoma City Thunder: +240. San Antonio Spurs: +280. Then the Philadelphia 76ers and the New York Knicks, tied at +1000. Boston Celtics: +1400. Toronto Raptors: +2200. Cleveland Cavaliers: +2500. And rounding out the list, the Pistons, Nuggets, Timberwolves, and Rockets, all tied at +2700.

**What it really means:** The defending champions — with nearly the entire title roster back — are sitting at +1000, behind two Western teams, and TIED with a Sixers team that just rebuilt itself around LeBron James and Jaylen Brown. The Spurs team the Knicks beat in five games is listed ahead of them. Think about that.

Some of it makes sense. The East got a lot stronger this summer — Philly, Miami with Giannis, Boston with Paul George and Mitch, Toronto with Kawhi, Washington with Anthony Davis. And the market hates uncertainty, so the KAT contract situation probably isn't helping. But this is the kind of disrespect that a locker room hangs on the wall. Remember what KAT said: "I'm two for one now. I want to try to get two." The market says that's a long shot. The players say it's the plan.

## THE OTHER SIDE OF THE LEDGER

Now, this channel always keeps it honest, so let's not pretend everything is perfect. KAT still doesn't have an extension, and the two sides are still far apart. Josh Hart's future is still uncertain. Deuce McBride's name keeps coming up in trade conversations. Brunson's massive number is coming down the road. And under this CBA, keeping a championship core together is the hardest thing in the sport. There are real questions about this team's future. We've covered them, and we'll keep covering them.

But here's the thing: it's easy to get lost in the negative. When you only read the contract headlines, you'd think this team was falling apart. When you listen to the voices inside the building, you hear the opposite. A star who says nothing diverts them from each other. A coach who praises that star's leadership. A bench scorer who's fallen in love with his role. A wing who handles criticism with maturity. The captain and the heart of the team believing in the new center. And a reigning MVP who's still mad that they won. The good and the bad can both be true at the same time. And right now, the good is louder than people realize.
