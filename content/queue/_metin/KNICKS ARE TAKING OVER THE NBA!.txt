## THE STREAK

Quick context, because this run has been something. After an 0–3 start — the first defending champion to do that since the 2011–12 Mavericks — the Knicks are now 11–6. They've won five straight: two in Toronto, one in Minnesota, then Portland and Indiana at the Garden this weekend. Since November began, they've owned one of the best records in the league.

Now let's watch the double feature.

## FEATURE #1: SATURDAY — LIFE WITHOUT KAT

**The setup:** Towns sat out with an illness — his first missed game of the season. OG Anunoby and Jordan Clarkson were also out. So the Knicks were short three key rotation players against Portland.

**The ending:** You know how it ended. Mikal Bridges buried a three-pointer at the final horn to give the Knicks a 97–96 win at the Garden. After everything Bridges has gone through this season — the slump, the trade rumors, the fourth-quarter benchings — that's a huge moment. Two games after hitting the dagger in Toronto, he hit a game-winner at home. The slump is officially in the rearview.

**But the path there was rough.** Before that shot, the game nearly slipped away.

## BRIDGES' REDEMPTION ARC

Let's stay on that shot for a moment, because it caps one of the best mini-stories of the season.

**Two weeks ago:** Bridges was averaging around six points on about 37% shooting and under 28% from three. Fans were calling for him to be benched. Trade proposals were everywhere — OKC, the Clippers, Milwaukee. Stephen A. Smith brought up the five first-round picks on First Take. Mike Brown was benching him in fourth quarters.

**Last Sunday in Toronto:** Five points, but a team-high plus-15 thanks to fourth-quarter defense — four rebounds, a block, and a steal.

**Last Tuesday in Toronto:** 17 points on seven-of-11 and the dagger three with 36 seconds left.

**Saturday at the Garden:** The game-winning three at the horn.

That's a player who kept working, kept defending, and kept believing in his shot. Brown told him to lean into his strengths — running, cutting, rebounding, driving — and stop forcing it. He did. And now he's hitting the biggest shots of the season.

This is exactly why the Knicks shouldn't sell low on proven players. Slumps end. And when they do, you want that player wearing your jersey.

## THE DRUMMOND STORY

With Towns out, Andre Drummond got his first start of the season.

**The good start:** Early on, it looked like the right call. He opened with a putback dunk, a steal, a block, and three rebounds. He even knocked down his first three-pointer as a Knick. When Mike Brown subbed him out, the Knicks led 18–11 and the Garden was buzzing.

**The rough second half:** In the third quarter, Drummond missed three point-blank attempts in about 40 seconds. Those are shots a center has to finish, especially in a close game. In his 10 minutes after halftime, the Knicks were outscored by 10.

**The controversial play:** With the game tied and under five seconds left, Drummond switched onto Portland forward Deni Avdija and fouled him. Avdija calmly made both free throws to put the Blazers in front. Josh Hart looked surprised by the switch, and longtime broadcaster Walt Frazier questioned the decision on the MSG broadcast. Without Bridges' buzzer-beater, that would have been the defining moment of the night — and not in a good way.

**To be fair:** Drummond wasn't the only problem. The offense struggled badly after halftime — the Knicks scored just 28 second-half points before Bridges' winner. Without Towns, OG, and Clarkson, there just weren't enough reliable options. Brunson, Hart, and Landry Shamet all had to play at least 35 minutes just to keep the Knicks in it.

## THE DRUMMOND NUMBERS

Here's the context on Drummond:
- He's 33 years old and in his 15th NBA season.
- He's a four-time rebounding champion, and his work on the glass is still a real asset.
- But his finishing has dropped off. Through 16 games, he's shooting 51.1% at the rim — the lowest mark of his career.

For a big man whose main offensive job is finishing around the basket, that's a red flag.

**The fair view:** The issue isn't that Drummond can't help. Saturday's first quarter showed what he still brings in short bursts — energy, rebounding, a physical presence that changes the tone. The concern is longer stretches and high-pressure moments late in close games. That switch onto Avdija is a good example: late-game defense depends on communication and quick decisions, and the fact that Hart seemed caught off guard suggests the Knicks weren't on the same page. Those are details a champion can't afford to miss in April and May.

**What Brown can do:** Shorter minutes, simpler assignments, and a clear plan on defensive switches. Use Drummond for what he's great at — rebounding and energy — and protect him from the rest.

## BRUNSON TIES A KNICKS LEGEND

And here's the history from Saturday night. Brunson finished with 30 points, seven rebounds, and five assists. That was his 33rd game as a Knick with at least 30 points, five rebounds, and five assists — tying Hall of Famer Richie Guerin and one other player for the most such games in franchise history.

One more, and the record belongs to Brunson alone.

Think about the names in Knicks history. Walt Frazier. Patrick Ewing. Willis Reed. Bernard King. Carmelo Anthony. And Brunson is about to stand alone in a category that measures all-around dominance. That's how much he's done in New York in such a short time.

## FEATURE #2: SUNDAY — REVENGE ON INDIANA

**The stakes:** Just nine days earlier, the Pacers beat the Knicks 106–104 in Indianapolis in an NBA Cup game that effectively ended New York's hopes of defending its Cup title. Brunson scored 33 with 11 assists that night, but Indiana held on. Sunday was the chance to answer back.

**The context:** It was the second night of a back-to-back, after an exhausting finish against Portland. OG Anunoby sat out with left calf tightness, and Jordan Clarkson remained out with a foot injury. Indiana was without Tyrese Haliburton (hamstring) and T.J. McConnell (hamstring). But the Pacers still had Pascal Siakam and Andrew Nembhard — the same core that beat the Knicks nine days earlier.

**The return:** KAT was cleared to play after missing Saturday. And it showed.

## SUNDAY, QUARTER BY QUARTER

**First quarter:** A tight battle. Siakam, who was listed as questionable with a sore hip, showed no signs of trouble and scored Indiana's final 11 points of the quarter. It ended tied at 26.

**Second quarter:** The Pacers went on a 10–0 run, but the Knicks answered right away with 10 straight of their own. New York led 55–51 at the half.

**First-half leaders:**
- **Towns:** 14 points, seven rebounds, and three assists
- **Landry Shamet:** 12 points on five-of-seven shooting
- **Brunson:** 10 points

**One sequence summed up the team effort:** Tyler Kolek went to the line for a free throw, and moments later Towns found Shamet cutting for an easy layup. Ball movement, trust, and execution.

**Third quarter:** This is where New York took control, outscoring Indiana 34–27, with Brunson and Shamet keeping the offense moving.

**Fourth quarter:** The Pacers made one last push and won the period 28–24, but Brunson and Josh Hart closed the door down the stretch.

**Final:** Knicks 113, Pacers 106. Indiana fell to 5–12 and lost its fourth straight.

## BRUNSON THE CLOSER

Brunson's recent form explains a lot of this surge.

**This month:**
- **27.6 points** per game
- **6.3 assists** per game
- **3.3 rebounds** per game
- **46.3%** from three

**For the full season:** 26.8 points and 5.9 assists per game.

**In crunch time** — the final five minutes of a game within five points — Brunson leads the NBA this season with **32 points, 10 assists, and five made threes**. When the Knicks need a basket late, everyone in the building knows where the ball is going, and he still delivers.

That's a huge change from the start of the season. Brunson had wrist surgery in the offseason, and the rust showed early. Now he looks fully healthy, and the Knicks look like a different team because of it.

**The personal subplot:** Pacers head coach Rick Carlisle coached Brunson for three seasons in Dallas. Few coaches in the league know his game better. And still, Indiana has struggled to find an answer for him, especially at the Garden. Last season, Brunson averaged 31.3 points, 8.0 assists, and 5.6 rebounds in three regular-season games against the Pacers.

## SHAMET STEPS UP AGAIN

And Landry Shamet deserves credit, too. With so many rotation players out, he stepped into a bigger role and gave the Knicks a reliable second scoring option. Twelve points on five-of-seven in the first half alone, and 35-plus minutes on Saturday. Remember, he barely made the roster a year ago. Now he's one of the most trusted players on the team, and a real Sixth Man of the Year candidate.

## THE DOUBLE FEATURE'S REAL LESSON: KAT'S VALUE

Watch the two games back-to-back, and one thing jumps off the screen: when Towns isn't on the floor, the Knicks look like a completely different team, especially on defense.

**For years,** Towns carried a reputation as an elite offensive big who was a step behind on the other end. **This season,** the numbers tell a different story:
- He leads the league in **defensive rebounding rate** — no player grabs a higher share of the available misses on his end.
- He's averaging a **league-best 13.9 rebounds** per game, plus **21.3 points**.
- He opened the season with a record-setting run of double-doubles.

**And here's the stat that should get people's attention:** With Towns on the floor, opponents take **5.4% fewer of their shots at the rim** and **2.8% fewer from floater range**. According to Daily Knicks, the only other player in the league who matches both of those marks is **Victor Wembanyama**.

Think about that. Karl-Anthony Towns, once seen as a defensive weakness, is in the same defensive category as Wemby.

## WHAT HAPPENED WITHOUT HIM

Saturday showed what happens when that presence disappears.

- **Portland took 42.8% of its shots at the rim** — way above the league average of about 31%.
- **With Towns playing this season,** the Knicks have allowed only 29.3% of opponents' shots to come at the rim.
- **Donovan Clingan** went a perfect six-of-six at the rim and grabbed nine rebounds, including three that led directly to second chances.

Clingan is exactly the kind of physical big who punishes a team without its starting center. And opponents who usually think twice before driving into the paint suddenly had no reason to hesitate.

**Then Sunday:** Towns returned, led the Knicks with 14 points and seven rebounds by halftime, and the paint got crowded again. Two games, back-to-back, gave a perfect before-and-after picture of what he means to this team.

## THE CONTRACT ANGLE

And that brings us to the business side.

**The situation:** KAT's extension talks are still unresolved. The Knicks have reportedly offered in the range of $45 to $50 million per year, and the New York Post reported he turned down about $200 million over four years this summer. The Knicks have been careful about the second apron, the threshold that brings heavy restrictions on trades, signings, and future draft flexibility.

**The leverage shift:** The more Towns proves he's essential on both ends, the harder it is for the Knicks to use the apron as a negotiating tool. If the team truly believes it can't win at the highest level without him, it can't afford to play hardball.

**From KAT's side:** The timing couldn't be better. He's putting together one of the best all-around seasons of his career — and the Knicks just played a game that showed exactly how much they struggle without him. Every strong defensive night strengthens his case. Every game he misses does the same in a different way.

**His agent doesn't need to make a long argument. The film from Saturday and Sunday makes it for him.**

**The balance:** That doesn't mean a deal has to happen right away. NBA contract talks take time, and both sides have priorities. The Knicks want to keep the core together while protecting long-term flexibility. Towns wants to be paid like the cornerstone he's become. Somewhere between those goals, a deal has to be found.

## THE BACKUP CENTER QUESTION — AGAIN

And this weekend put the backup center debate right back in focus. When a player as important as Towns sits out, the drop-off to the next man up shouldn't feel this steep.

The Knicks rely on Towns to rebound, protect the paint, and space the floor at the same time. A backup center doesn't need to do all of that — but he does need to hold his ground for meaningful stretches without the game flipping. On Saturday, the second half showed how quickly things can change when that doesn't happen.

The good news? The problem showed up in a win. Towns was back the next night, and the urgency passed. But the larger question hasn't gone away. Injuries, illness, and foul trouble are part of every long season, and the Knicks will need a backup big they can trust when those moments come. That's why fans keep calling for one more dependable center before the deadline — and why the James Wiseman question is still worth exploring.

## THE INJURY WATCH

A quick health check heading into the week:
- **OG Anunoby:** Left calf tightness. Keep an eye on it — calf issues can linger if rushed.
- **Jordan Clarkson:** Out with a foot injury.
- **Karl-Anthony Towns:** Back after the illness.

The Knicks won both games this weekend without OG and Clarkson. That says a lot about the depth — and about the stars carrying the load. Getting OG and Clarkson back healthy would make this streak even scarier.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. Brunson breaks Richie Guerin's record with his 34th 30-5-5 game as a Knick within the next week — and he does it at the Garden. The win streak reaches seven before it ends. Drummond's minutes shrink a bit, with James Wiseman getting a longer look as the backup. And on KAT: this weekend was the most convincing evidence yet that he's the Knicks' most irreplaceable player. I think it pushes the front office closer to his asking range. A deal still probably waits until the offseason, but the Knicks can't let this get away.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. The Knicks have won five straight and are 11–6. On Saturday, without Towns, OG, and Clarkson, they beat Portland 97–96 on a Mikal Bridges buzzer-beater after Andre Drummond's tough second half and a costly late switch-and-foul on Deni Avdija. Brunson's 30-7-5 night tied Richie Guerin's franchise record with 33 such games. On Sunday, with Towns back, they got revenge on Indiana, 113–106, on the second night of a back-to-back. Brunson leads the NBA in crunch-time points, assists, and threes, and is shooting 46.3% from three in November. And the weekend proved Towns' value: he leads the league in rebounding and defensive rebounding rate, and only he and Victor Wembanyama reduce opponent rim and floater attempts at that rate — while Portland feasted at the rim without him. That's leverage for his next contract.
