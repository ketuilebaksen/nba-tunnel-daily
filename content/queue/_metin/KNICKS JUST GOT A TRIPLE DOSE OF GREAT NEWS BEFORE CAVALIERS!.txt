## FIRST, THE LATEST

Quick update before we dive in. Since a lot of this news broke, the Knicks went out on Tuesday and crushed the Milwaukee Bucks 117–85. Brunson scored 23 in just 27 minutes, Karl-Anthony Towns had 21 points and 16 rebounds, OG Anunoby finally found some rhythm with 19 points, and the Knicks out-rebounded Milwaukee 59–32. They held the Bucks to 13 points in two separate quarters. That's four straight wins and six of the last seven.

So everything you're about to hear just got a little more convincing.

## GOOD NEWS #1: THE CAPTAIN IS PLAYER OF THE WEEK

We covered this in detail already, so here's the quick version. The NBA named Jalen Brunson the Eastern Conference Player of the Week for Week 3, after he led the Knicks to a 3–0 week.

**His week:**
- 27.7 points per game
- 6.3 assists per game
- 42.9% from three
- Only 0.7 turnovers per game

That assist-to-turnover ratio is absurd for a player who carries this much of the offense. And the Western Conference winner? Luka Dončić. Two former Dallas teammates, both winning weekly awards, neither one in a Mavericks jersey anymore. Dallas fans, we're sorry. Kind of.

And honestly, this award could have gone to Karl-Anthony Towns too. Both of them played phenomenal basketball. Which brings us to good news number two.

## GOOD NEWS #2: KAT THE REBOUNDER

Towns is now leading the NBA in defensive rebounding percentage at 32.3%. That's the highest mark of his career.

Think about what that means. Every missed shot by the other team, KAT is grabbing about a third of the available defensive rebounds when he's on the floor. That ends possessions. That stops second chances. And it starts the break.

Remember why this matters. Mitchell Robinson left for Boston this summer, and Towns admitted he had to raise his rebounding effort because of it. He said, "With Mitch gone, I had to pick up the effort, especially the rebounding effort." He hasn't just picked it up — he's leading the league.

And on Tuesday against Milwaukee, he grabbed 16 more. A Bucks team that came in ranked sixth in the league in rebounding got destroyed on the glass by 27.

## GOOD NEWS #3: KAT THE DEFENDER

And this is the one that might surprise people the most. A few years ago, when you talked about Karl-Anthony Towns on defense, he was often called the weakest link. Lazy. A target for opposing offenses. That reputation followed him for years.

Not anymore.

According to the New York Post, the Knicks are shutting down opponents at a top-tier rate this season. They obviously have OG Anunoby, a premier and versatile individual defender. But in today's NBA, where ball handlers hunt switches and favorable matchups through screens, a defense is often only as good as its weakest link. And Towns has been far from weak.

Just like during his strong playoff run, KAT has been active off the ball, committed, and clearly in great shape after cutting weight this summer.

## WHAT MIKE BROWN SAID ABOUT KAT

Mike Brown challenged Towns on defense — and the results really started kicking in during last season's playoffs. Here's what Brown said:

"He's got a tremendous feel. He's a smart dude when it comes to a lot of things, but especially when it comes to the game of basketball. His feel is at a high level. And so, you couple that with the job that Brendan O'Connor and Darren Erman are doing, it's easy to see where he is, because everything we've asked him to do, he's tried to do. So, if you're extremely coachable, you've got a great feel, and you've got two guys in Brendan O'Connor and Darren Erman that are teaching you the game defensively at a really high level, you'll see the results we're seeing now."

That's a big shout-out to the assistant coaches too. Defensive development doesn't happen by accident.

## WHAT BRUNSON SAID ABOUT KAT

And the captain had his back too:

"It's his willingness to do what's best for the team, to continue to get better. I think we've all been challenged by Mike in some way, shape or form, and he's stepped up to the plate with that, and he's been great."

That's the captain publicly praising the guy whose contract situation is the biggest storyline in New York. That tells you everything about how the locker room sees Towns. He's not a distraction. He's a pillar.

## THE CONTRACT ANGLE

And yes, we have to mention it. Every one of these good-news stories makes KAT's contract standoff more interesting. The Knicks reportedly offered in the $45 to $50 million per year range, and the Post's Stefan Bondy reported he turned down a four-year, roughly $200 million offer this summer.

Now he's leading the league in defensive rebounding percentage, he's become a legit defender, and his coach and captain are praising him publicly. The longer this goes, the stronger KAT's case gets. That's the reality.

## GOOD NEWS #4 (THE BONUS): THE TEAM DEFENSE

We said triple dose, but here's a bonus one. The Knicks defense as a whole is finally looking like a championship defense again.

**According to the New York Post's numbers, three weeks into the season:**
- The Knicks were allowing 104.2 points per game — third-best in the league
- They ranked seventh in defensive rating, points allowed per 100 possessions — the same spot they finished last season
- They had held opponents to 105 points or fewer in five of six games

And that was before Tuesday. Add the 85 points they allowed to Milwaukee, and it's now six times in seven games. Not coincidentally, that's also six wins in seven games.

**The fair caveat:** Part of the points-per-game number comes from pace. The Knicks play at the slowest pace in the NBA, which means fewer possessions — and fewer points — for both teams. That's why the defensive rating is the better measure. And seventh in defensive rating is still very good.

**Why it matters:** Mike Brown's teams win with defense. When the shots aren't falling — and with Bridges and OG struggling, they often haven't been — the defense keeps the Knicks in games. That's what's happening during this win streak.

## BAD NEWS #1: MIKAL BRIDGES' DEFENSE

Now the harder part. According to The Athletic's James L. Edwards III and Fred Katz, Mikal Bridges' point-of-attack defense hasn't been nearly as bad as his offense — but it hasn't been good either.

That's a big deal, because defense has always been Bridges' calling card. In crunch time, when Mike Brown needs somebody to slow down the other team's best perimeter player, he's been turning to Josh Hart — or even Landry Shamet — instead of Bridges.

**The minutes tell the story:** Bridges has logged 22 minutes or fewer in four of the first nine games. For his career, he averages 33 minutes per game. That's a huge drop.

**The offense:** He's been hesitating on wide-open threes, which is not like him. Usually, when Bridges is open, he lets it fly without thinking. Now there's hesitation, and the confidence just isn't there. He's had bad spells since joining the Knicks, but by many accounts, this is the worst.

**What happens next?** I don't think Brown takes Bridges out of the starting lineup unless the Knicks really start to slide. A move like that can damage a veteran's confidence even more. But expect a short leash, with Shamet, Clarkson, and Deuce McBride ready to take his minutes when he's off.

**The bright spots:** In Cleveland on Saturday, Bridges was 4-of-13, but he was aggressive — six rebounds, four assists, and two blocks. Aggression usually comes before the shot returns. I don't need him to be the old Bridges overnight. I just need him to be more efficient, more consistent, and to stop hesitating.

## BAD NEWS #2: OG'S CHAOTIC DRIVES

And then there's OG. According to Fred Katz, OG Anunoby's drives to the hoop have been chaotic, often ending in frantic finishes under the basket.

**The numbers:**
- 56% at the rim
- Only one made shot between the restricted area and the three-point line all season
- Just 31% on corner threes — an area of the court where he normally roasts teams
- Under 40% from the field and under 30% from three

That's a big step down from the player who helped win a championship.

**But here's the context that matters:** OG did not forget how to shoot. He hit 49% of his threes during last spring's playoffs. Behind the scenes, he's obsessive about his mechanics. This is a nine-game slump — one that could, and probably will, end soon.

**And it might be ending already.** Against Milwaukee on Tuesday, he looked far more comfortable, scoring 19 points and helping build the early lead. One game isn't a trend. But it's a start.

**The bright side:** Even while the jumper has been off, OG has been incredibly aggressive. He's averaging about 6.2 free-throw attempts per game — way up from his career average of 2.1 — and making them at an elite rate. He's still the Knicks' best perimeter defender. Once the shot returns and you pair it with that aggression, the rebounding, and the defense, he's going to look like an All-Star again.

## WHAT THE KNICKS CAN DO ABOUT THE WINGS

So if Bridges and OG are both struggling, what can Mike Brown actually do about it? Here are a few ideas — my analysis, not reporting.

**Simplify OG's role.** We've talked about this before. Analysts have pointed out that OG's expanded offensive role this season — more touches and more drives — hasn't produced efficient results. The fix might be to go back to what made him great: catch-and-go attacks, attacking closeouts, open threes in the flow of the offense, and less one-on-one creation. Let Brunson and Towns create. Let OG finish.

**Get Bridges easy looks early.** When a shooter is hesitating, the best cure is a few easy ones. Run a set for him in the first quarter. Get him a cut to the rim, a transition layup, a trip to the free-throw line. Confidence usually comes from the simple shots first.

**Lean on the hot hands late.** In crunch time, ride the players who are producing. Shamet, Clarkson, and Deuce have all won their minutes during this stretch. That's not a punishment for Bridges. It's just smart basketball.

**Get Hart back.** The single biggest fix is Josh Hart returning. He takes the toughest perimeter assignment, which takes pressure off Bridges and OG defensively, and he's the best glue player on the roster.

**Give Diawara a look.** Mohamed Diawara has been impressive lately — a plus-16 in Cleveland and eight points, six rebounds, four assists, and two blocks against Chicago. At 6-foot-9 with defensive versatility, he's an internal option if the wings keep struggling.

The good news is that none of these require a trade. It's all internal, and it's all fixable.

## TONIGHT: CLEVELAND AT THE GARDEN

Now the rematch. The Cavaliers come to the Garden tonight for the second game of the back-to-back. Saturday, the Knicks won 109–97 in Cleveland behind Brunson's 31 points and nine assists and KAT's 29 points, 13 rebounds, and zero turnovers.

And the blowout against Milwaukee was the perfect setup. The starters got extra rest in the fourth quarter, so they should have fresh legs tonight.

Here's what I'm watching:

- **Josh Hart's return.** He missed Tuesday with an illness, not the ankle — and Brown said he would have played otherwise. If he's back, the defense gets even better.
- **KAT early and often.** When Towns is involved early in the offense, he stays engaged for 48 minutes. The Knicks need him attacking from the start.
- **Bridges' aggressiveness.** Can he build on his aggressive night in Cleveland?
- **OG's follow-up.** Was Tuesday the start of the bounce-back?
- **Cleveland's response.** The Cavs just lost on their own floor. Donovan Mitchell and James Harden will be looking for payback.
- **The defense.** Can the Knicks hold a good offense under 105 for the seventh time in eight games?

## THE CAVS MATCHUP: KEYS TO A SWEEP OF THE MINI-SERIES

Cleveland is a very different test than Milwaukee. This is a team the Knicks swept in last year's Eastern Conference Finals, including that wild Game 1 comeback from 22 down in the fourth quarter. And the Cavs have a backcourt that can score on anybody — Donovan Mitchell came into Saturday averaging over 22 a game, and James Harden can pick a defense apart.

So here are the keys:

- **Contain Mitchell.** If Hart is back, he gets the assignment. If not, it's OG with help from Deuce.
- **Win the glass again.** The Knicks dominated the boards against Milwaukee. KAT and Drummond have to do it again against Cleveland's bigs.
- **Protect the ball.** Turnovers have been an issue all season. Harden and Mitchell turn every mistake into points.
- **Start strong.** This team has had too many slow starts. Against a playoff-caliber opponent on the second night of a back-to-back, you can't spot them double digits.

Win tonight, and the Knicks take both games of this mini-series against a team they'll likely be fighting for seeding all season.

## WHY THIS STRETCH MATTERS

Let's zoom out. The Knicks opened 0–3 and heard all the noise about a championship hangover. Since then: six wins in seven games, a Player of the Week award, a center playing the best two-way basketball of his career, and a defense back to its championship level.

And they've done it without Bridges or OG at their best, and without Josh Hart for the last three games. That's what should excite you most. This team hasn't hit its ceiling yet. When the wings start making shots, and Hart is healthy, this is a dangerous team again.
