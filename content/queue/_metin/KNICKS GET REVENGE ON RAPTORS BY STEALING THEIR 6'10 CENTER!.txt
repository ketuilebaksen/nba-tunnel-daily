## TUESDAY: THE 40-POINT WAKE-UP CALL

Let's set the stage. Tuesday in Toronto was the second night of a back-to-back, after the Knicks' starters had played Minnesota at the Garden. So Mike Brown rested his main guys and sent out a lineup made up mostly of reserves and roster hopefuls. The Raptors took full advantage, 138–98.

Was it a real measuring stick? Not really. It was a bench showcase in a game the betting market expected the Knicks to lose. But it still stung. A 40-point loss to a conference rival, with Knicks fans watching, two days before the final dress rehearsal. And the one bright spot was Wiseman — starting, playing real minutes, and putting up 18 and 10. None of the other camp bigs came close to that.

## THURSDAY: THE CHAMPIONS SHOW UP

Then came Thursday, and the difference was obvious from the opening tip. Mike Brown sent out a starting five of Jalen Brunson, Mikal Bridges, OG Anunoby, Mohamed Diawara, and Karl-Anthony Towns — and the Knicks played like a team ready for opening night.

**The first quarter:** Deuce McBride closed the period with three straight three-pointers to give New York a 35–32 lead. And Towns grabbed six rebounds in the first quarter alone.

**The middle:** The Knicks led 63–58 at halftime and 82–77 after three quarters.

**The finish:** A tight fourth quarter against a legit Toronto roster, and the Knicks held on for their first preseason win. They put up 122 points, finished the exhibition schedule 1–4, and walked into opening-night week with some confidence back.

Is a preseason win a big deal? On its own, no. But it was the first time all fall that this team looked like itself. And after a 40-point loss two days earlier, the response mattered.

## THREE THINGS THAT STOOD OUT

**One: Deuce answered on the court.** All fall, Deuce McBride's name has been floating in trade conversations, and he struggled with his shot early in the preseason. Then, in the dress rehearsal, he hit three straight threes to close the first quarter. That's the Deuce we know — an elite defensive backup who can catch fire. Whatever the front office decides about his future, he's reminding everyone what he's worth.

**Two: KAT was fully locked in.** Six rebounds in the first quarter. Active on the glass from the opening tip. This came days after the Knicks shut down extension talks and KAT said he was "very disappointed." Nothing about Thursday suggested a player who's checked out. That's the KAT who promised, "We intend for it to stay in New York."

**Three: Mohamed Diawara in the starting lineup.** This is a detail worth noticing. The second-round pick started alongside Brunson, Bridges, OG, and KAT in the dress rehearsal. We've talked about how the Knicks gave Diawara a contract and how he's moved ahead of Pacôme Dadiet in the pecking order. Starting him in the final tune-up tells you the coaching staff trusts him. Remember that chase-down block on Kentavious Caldwell-Pope in the opener? He's earning real looks.

## THE TORONTO GHOSTS: QUICKLEY AND RJ

And there's a storyline in this matchup that every Knicks fan should appreciate. Look at Toronto's roster: Immanuel Quickley and RJ Barrett. Two former Knicks. The two players at the center of the trade that brought OG Anunoby to New York back in December 2023.

Think about that. OG, the player the Knicks got in that deal, started Thursday night against the two guys they gave up. And OG is now a champion who's been living at the free-throw line all preseason. That trade is one of the biggest reasons the Knicks have a banner going up on Tuesday. Toronto also now has Kawhi Leonard back home, plus Scottie Barnes and Jakob Poeltl. They're a real team. Beating them in the finale with the starters on the floor was a nice reminder of how this championship was built.

## CUT DAY CAME EARLY: EUBANKS, BRADLEY, AGBAJI

Now to the biggest roster news of the week, because hours before the finale, the competition for the final roster spots got turned upside down.

**Drew Eubanks: torn pectoral muscle.** According to ESPN's Shams Charania, Eubanks suffered a torn pec and will be sidelined for an extended period, and the Knicks are releasing him. That's brutal. Eubanks was one of the main candidates for the reserve center job, Ian Begley had backed him as the safe choice all camp, and he was already coming back from thumb surgery. On this channel, we wish him a full and fast recovery. He was a pro through this entire camp.

**Tony Bradley: waived.** Bradley had suffered a hamstring injury in the previous preseason game, and Shams reported the Knicks are waiving him too.

**Ochai Agbaji: waived.** The wing who needed his shot to fall never quite found it this preseason. He's out as well.

So with those three gone, only two training-camp invitees remain: James Wiseman and Bruce Brown.

## WHAT THE SURVIVORS TELL US

**James Wiseman.** He was already the favorite for the third-center role behind KAT and Drummond, and now he has no direct competition at his position. That 18-and-10 night in Toronto may have settled it for good. But here's the important detail: Wiseman is still on a training-camp contract and hasn't been signed to a standard deal yet. That could happen in the next few days. Until it does, there's still a small chance the Knicks find a center they like more — through a trade, or from the wave of players other teams release before the regular season. Given how tough the trade market has been for New York, that seems unlikely. And Wiseman offers more upside than most of the bigs who'll hit waivers this week.

**Bruce Brown.** This is the more revealing one. Brown isn't a center, so keeping him isn't about filling the frontcourt need. One theory was that the Knicks just wanted one more look at him in the finale. But they could've done the same with Agbaji — and chose to release Agbaji instead. Since Agbaji was competing for a similar wing role, choosing Brown over him strongly suggests the Knicks have actual plans to keep Bruce Brown on the main roster. And it's easy to see why: a champion from Denver's title team, a defender Mike Brown called "textbook" on the ball, and a player Jalen Brunson says brings a lot of things that can help.

And if the Knicks really want both Wiseman and Bruce Brown, we're back to the math we've covered all fall: one veteran minimum fits under the second apron. Two need a little more room. Which is why the Pacôme Dadiet salary dump is still the move to watch before Monday.

## THE 6'10" STEAL: TREY JEMISON

And now, the second kind of revenge. With Eubanks out with a torn pec and Bradley gone, look at the Knicks' center depth: Karl-Anthony Towns, Andre Drummond, and — once he's officially signed — James Wiseman. That's it. Three centers, and the third has played very little NBA basketball over the last two years.

Now look at the two-way contracts: Tyler Nickel, Kevin McCullar Jr., and Jaden Akins. Two wings and a guard. Zero bigs.

And on October 14th, the Toronto Raptors waived Trey Jemison III. A 6-foot-10, 270-pound center. A player who spent last season with the Knicks on a two-way deal and was part of the championship team. A player Mike Brown coached and the Westchester staff developed — averaging 8.0 points, 8.4 rebounds, 3.0 assists, and 1.4 blocks in the G League. The Knicks' former big man, cut loose by the same team that just lost to New York at the Garden.

The Knicks wouldn't be stealing him in the literal sense — he's a free agent once he clears waivers. But it's a pretty sweet way to rub it in: Toronto lets him go, and the champs take him back. Even outlets covering the team are saying the Knicks should be all over a Jemison reunion.

## WHY THE STEAL MAKES EVEN MORE SENSE NOW

A few days ago, Jemison was a "nice idea." After the Eubanks injury, he's close to a necessity. Here's why.

**The depth hole got deeper.** The Knicks lost two of their three camp centers in one day. If Wiseman's body doesn't hold up, or KAT and Drummond both run into trouble on the same night, there's no backup plan on the roster.

**Two-way eligibility.** Veterans like Eubanks and Bradley could never have gone on a two-way deal because of their years of service. Jemison can — he was just on one in Toronto.

**Zero apron impact.** A two-way contract doesn't touch the second-apron math that's choking every other Knicks decision. No salary dump needed. No trade needed.

**Familiarity.** He knows the system, the coaches, the Westchester staff, and the locker room. Zero learning curve.

The only cost: one of the current two-way players would have to go. And as we laid out in our Jemison video, Jaden Akins — a good young defender, but a guard on a roster loaded with guards — is the most logical swap.

And there's a clock. Back in 2024, New Orleans claimed Jemison's two-way contract right off waivers, and the Knicks, as champions, are last in line. If they want him, they have to be ready the moment he's available.

## KAT: CARMELO SPEAKS

Now let's talk about the biggest storyline of the week — Karl-Anthony Towns. As we covered, the Knicks decided to shut down extension talks until later in the season. The two sides had spent months negotiating without getting close, and the front office chose to end it early — before Monday's October 19th deadline — to keep the focus on the title defense.

On paper, that logic makes sense. But one of the most famous names in franchise history thinks the Knicks handled it badly. Carmelo Anthony addressed it on his podcast, "7PM in Brooklyn." And Melo has been a vocal supporter of the Knicks — even of James Dolan. He didn't criticize the decision itself. His problem was the communication. KAT said he was hearing the news for the first time, and Melo said the Knicks dropped the ball on that one — that the organization should never have let a moment like that play out in public, and that the way the conversation ended was the real mistake.

And I think that's fair. KAT was arguably the most important piece of last season's championship run. Learning about a major decision on your future from outside the organization is exactly the kind of thing that leaves a player feeling disrespected. KAT has said openly that he's disappointed. And ironically, the attempt to avoid distractions might have created more of them.

## THE SUITORS: CHICAGO AND THE CLIPPERS

And here's the new wrinkle. According to Stefan Bondy of the New York Post, an anonymous NBA executive said the Chicago Bulls and the Los Angeles Clippers are viewed as possible suitors for Towns if he were to become available in a trade.

Let's be careful with that. It's one anonymous executive's view of possible suitors — not a report that either team has called, and not a report that the Knicks are shopping him. But it adds another layer to a story that's been growing all fall: Shams' report that a trade could become possible without an extension, Begley's January pivot, Bleacher Report's Cleveland idea, and now Chicago and the Clippers.

And here's a fun scheduling note: the Knicks play in Chicago on October 28th. Just eight days into the season. You can bet that game will come with plenty of questions.

## KAT'S LEVERAGE AND THE MONDAY DEADLINE

Here's why this situation is so delicate. The money gap is huge: KAT is eligible for a max extension of about $276 million, and the Knicks are reportedly offering around $200 million over four years — a difference of more than $70 million.

And KAT holds real leverage. The last year of his contract is a $61 million player option. If he declines it, he could become a free agent as early as next summer — and then the Knicks wouldn't be negotiating alone. Other teams with cap space could make offers, and a bidding war would be very likely. That would make it much harder and much more expensive for New York to keep him.

So the situation now has two possible exits: a trade during the season, or free agency next summer if the relationship doesn't improve. And his option decision is due Monday — the same day as roster cutdowns, one day before the banner.

But here's what matters most to me: on Thursday, nothing about KAT's game looked like a player with one foot out the door. Six first-quarter rebounds. Locked in. Helping win a game the team needed for its confidence. For Leon Rose and the front office, the challenge now is balancing both sides — protecting their financial flexibility while protecting the relationship with a player who helped bring a championship back to New York. How they manage that balance could define this entire season.

## MY CALL

Here's my prediction for the next five days. Wiseman gets signed to a standard deal for the 14th spot. The Knicks find a way to keep Bruce Brown — most likely with a Dadiet salary dump. And I'd put real odds on the Knicks bringing back Trey Jemison on a two-way, especially after losing Eubanks to a torn pec. That's the revenge I want: beat the Raptors on the court, then take back the big man they let go. And on KAT: I still believe this ends with him in New York. But the Knicks need to repair the communication, fast — because the noise isn't going away on its own.
