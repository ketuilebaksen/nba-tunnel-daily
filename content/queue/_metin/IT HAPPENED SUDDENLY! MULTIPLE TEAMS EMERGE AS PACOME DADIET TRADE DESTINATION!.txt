THE SETUP: ONE SEAT UNLESS THE DOMINO FALLS

And spare a thought for the human side of this before we grade spreadsheets, because it deserves saying every time: Pacôme Dadiet did nothing wrong here. He's 21, he worked, he waited, and the only crime on his file is being a developmental project on a roster with zero developmental minutes. Wherever this domino lands him — a rebuild in Chicago, a fresh start in Brooklyn, wherever — he leaves with a championship ring, a New York education, and our genuine hope that the league sees what the Garden never had room to showcase. Business is business, but this channel doesn't do coldness toward its own. Good luck, kid — just not against us.

Quick refresher for anyone joining late, because everything today hangs on math we've mapped for weeks. Five veterans — Bruce Brown, Konchar, Eubanks, Wiseman, Agbaji — are competing for what is currently ONE roster spot. The front office wants TWO: a wing AND a center out of that camp class. The only path from one to two runs through a salary dump: move Dadiet's $2.9 million off the books, stay under the second apron, sign both winners. That's the plan, that's been the plan, and per Yahoo Sports, Dan Favale just scoured the league and shortlisted the five franchises with the cap room and open seats to make it happen: the Hornets, Bulls, Grizzlies, Clippers, and Nets. Let's grade them, one by one — because they are NOT created equal.

CROSSING OFF: CHARLOTTE AND MEMPHIS

Start with the two names I'm drawing a line through. The HORNETS, on paper, look plausible — over $20 million under the tax, trade exceptions in the back pocket, a rebuild mentality after the LaMelo move; Favale figures a second-rounder or two gets it done. But here's the cold water, and it's been cold all summer: Charlotte has shown zero appetite for doing business with New York — all offseason long, every inquiry, frost. And they JUST made their trade, shipping Finney-Smith to Atlanta for Hield and Nembhard. Teams that just re-shuffled eighteen guaranteed contracts rarely rush back to the table within days. Cross them off — for the dump, anyway. Hold that thought, because there's an asterisk coming.

And the GRIZZLIES eliminate themselves with simple arithmetic: they're living the exact opposite problem. Memphis has MORE players than seats right now — a roster crunch of their own, guys they genuinely don't want to waive, and a hard fifteen-man limit bearing down. A team agonizing over which of its own players to keep does not import someone else's salary problem as a favor. Cross them off without ceremony.

THE REAL THREE: BULLS, CLIPPERS, NETS

Now the live wires. CHICAGO: the quietly interesting one. The Bulls hold an actual open roster spot, and they're deep in a reset under new lead executive Bryson Graham — a rebuild that should be collecting exactly this profile: a 21-year-old former first-rounder with tools. And here's the detail that changes the price tag: Dadiet's rookie deal carries a $5.3 million TEAM OPTION for next season. Translation: whoever trades for him isn't marrying him — evaluate for a year, keep him if he pops, decline if he doesn't. For a rebuilding team, that's a free look at a lottery-adjacent talent. And for New York, that interest is leverage: a team that actually WANTS the player pays you in convenience — fewer second-rounders out the door than you'd burn bribing a team planning to waive him on arrival. If Chicago sees a project worth 20 minutes a night in a lost season, this is the deal where everybody genuinely wins — including, honestly, Dadiet himself, who'd finally get the runway we've said all along he deserves.

THE CLIPPERS: the desperate one — and Tunnel fam, you know this story, because we broke down the hammer when it dropped. Los Angeles was stripped of FIVE future first-round picks for their role in the salary-cap circumvention scandal around Kawhi Leonard. Five firsts. Gone. That franchise's draft cupboard is a crime scene, and a team that cannot rebuild through the draft will do nearly anything to restock even the loose change. Favale's read: they might take Dadiet for as little as a future SECOND, just to hold a young asset and a pick. It's grim, it's transactional, and it works fine for New York — though let's note the dark comedy of the same Clippers team we flagged as the KAT free-agency predator now also being the league's designated salvage yard. Keep both files open on them.

And THE NETS: the cleanest one — Favale's "most ideal partner," and mine too. Brooklyn still has its ENTIRE $9.4 million room exception sitting unused. Absorbing $2.9 million into that is genuinely nothing — no salary matching, no roster gymnastics, no drama. They're rebuilding, they collect young fliers professionally, and — the geographic cherry — the whole transaction happens across the river. We flagged the Nets as the natural landing spot back in the master-plan video, and the shortlist just confirmed it. If I'm setting odds: Nets favorite, Bulls the value play, Clippers the fallback. Any of the three completes the plan.

One tactical note on timing, because it's the part fans underrate: Leon Rose is in no rush, and that's the whole game. Every one of these three teams knows New York wants this done — but New York knows Chicago wants a free look at a first-round talent, Brooklyn has an exception burning a hole in its pocket, and the Clippers need draft capital like oxygen. Three motivated buyers for one asset is called an AUCTION, folks, even when the asset is a salary dump. Expect Rose to let them bid against each other right up until the roster deadline forces ink. The difference between a rushed dump and a patient one might only be one extra second-rounder saved — but this front office has built an entire empire on exactly those margins.

THE HARD LINE: DEUCE IS NOT A SALARY DUMP

One more time for the people in the back, because the alternative scenario keeps circulating: if the Knicks somehow refused to move Dadiet, the only other contract that opens a seat is Miles McBride's — and NO. The reporting agrees, the logic agrees, and this channel has drawn this line in permanent marker: Deuce McBride does not get salary-dumped. Ever. A dump means paying someone to take your player; Deuce is a player teams would pay FOR — arguably the best pure trade asset on this roster not named Brunson. If he moves at all — and the extension talks we covered in the plot-twist video say that's no longer the default — it happens at the February deadline, for something SUBSTANTIAL: a real center, a difference-maker, a return worthy of the chip. You don't burn a winning lottery ticket to make change for the parking meter. Dadiet's contract opens the seat. Deuce's value wins a trade. Different tools, different jobs, and this front office knows the difference better than anyone.
