## THE HONEST HEADLINE

Quick clarity, the NBA Tunnel way. There was no "bombshell decision" from Tyler Kolek. He didn't sign anything, demand anything, or announce anything. The "decision" is the one he forced on the court — with his play, he made it very hard for Mike Brown to give the backup point guard job to anyone else. That's the real story, and it's a great one.

## THE GAME IN 60 SECONDS

Quick recap of Tuesday night at the Garden. The Knicks beat the Bucks 117–85, a 32-point win that pushed them to 6–4 and extended the win streak to four.

- **The second quarter:** The Knicks held Milwaukee to 13 points. The game was basically over by halftime.
- **The third quarter:** The one sloppy stretch. The Bucks finally started hitting shots, especially from three, and cut a 26-point lead down to 11.
- **The fourth quarter:** Killer instinct. The Knicks held Milwaukee to 13 points again and slammed the door.

Two 13-point quarters. The Bucks held well under 100 points. And the Knicks dominated the boards all night.

## WHAT MIKE BROWN SAID

Here's Brown after the game, and he was fired up about the defense:

"Pretty efficient physicality on the defensive end. Pretty high level. Did it the right way without fouling, which was a lot of fun to watch. The point of attack defense with Landry Shamet and Deuce McBride especially on the ball was unbelievable. Deuce was our defensive player of the game. Could have gone to Landry as well. Just a fantastic job at the point of attack, screening and pick and rolls, too. They had 13-point quarters — two 13-point quarters — to shoot 38% and only give up seven offensive rebounds. Heck of a job."

That's a coach who loves what he's seeing, and who's happy to spread the credit around. And now let's go through the statements.

## STATEMENT #1: TYLER KOLEK — "THE JOB IS MINE"

**The line:**
- 12 points
- 5-of-6 from the field
- 2 three-pointers
- 4 assists
- 1 block
- Plus-23 in 15 minutes

In my opinion, that's his best performance of the season. And that's saying something, because he's had a great stretch — including that run of 21 assists and zero turnovers over three games while Jose Alvarado was hurt.

**The highlight:** He blocked a shot on defense, then went straight down the floor and scored. That's a two-way play from a guy who was once seen mostly as a passer.

**The growth:** Every game, Kolek seems to add something new. A new way of attacking. A new defensive habit. Better shot selection. He's hitting open threes. He's making great passes to OG, to KAT, to Mohamed Diawara, to everybody on the floor. And when he's running the offense, players move more. They cut, they relocate, they stay in constant motion — because they know if they get open, Kolek will find them.

**The defense:** This might be the biggest change. Kolek's defense has never been his calling card, but right now it looks better than it ever has in his career. He's competing, holding his ground, and making plays.

**The numbers:** Coming into the Bucks game, he was averaging 14.9 assists per 36 minutes. That's elite playmaking when he's on the floor.

## THE ALVARADO QUESTION

So what does this mean for Jose Alvarado? Here's what Mike Brown said:

"It's not anything negative right now on Jose Alvarado or even Tyler Kolek when he's out. They've just got to be ready to play when their number's called. And both of them are trying to do that."

That's a coach being diplomatic — and fair. Alvarado isn't in trouble. He's a beloved part of last year's championship team, and his pressure defense is still valuable. But the reality is clear: Kolek has the backup point guard job right now. Alvarado didn't play at all in Cleveland on Saturday.

Some people say Kolek "stole" the job. I'd put it differently. The job was Alvarado's to start the season. Kolek earned it — through his play, his work ethic, and how much better the Knicks look when he's on the floor. Nobody snuck anything. He just kept getting better.

**The trade angle:** And remember what we covered in the Trade Calculator video. If Kolek holds the job, Alvarado — who makes about $4.4 million and becomes trade-eligible on December 15th — could become a real piece in a deal. Kolek's emergence doesn't just change the rotation. It changes what the front office can do.

## HOW FAR KOLEK HAS COME

Let's appreciate the journey for a second, because it makes this even better.

A year ago, Kolek was a deep-bench guard on a championship team. During last season's playoff run, the only games where he played at least 10 minutes were blowouts. When this season started, he was behind Alvarado on the depth chart, and his future in New York wasn't locked in. Extension talks with several young players had been tabled. There were even questions about whether he'd be a trade chip.

Then Alvarado sprained his ankle against Orlando, and Kolek got his chance. Nine assists and zero turnovers that night, even on a 0-for-4 shooting game. Then nine points, nine assists, three of four from three, and a viral behind-the-back pass to KAT in Chicago. Then he kept the job after Alvarado came back. And now this: his best game of the season, in a 32-point win.

That's not luck. That's a player who stayed ready for a long time and grabbed his moment the second it arrived. Mike Brown has said only five spots in the rotation are locked and that minutes are earned. Kolek earned his.

## WHAT KOLEK NEEDS TO KEEP IT

To be fair, holding a job is harder than winning it. Here's what Kolek has to keep doing:

- **Protect the ball.** Turnovers have been a concern in his past. His assist-to-turnover ratio this season has been elite. That has to stay.
- **Keep hitting open threes.** If defenses have to respect his shot, everything else opens up.
- **Hold up on defense against bigger, faster guards.** The league's best backcourts will test him, especially in the playoffs.
- **Stay consistent.** One great game is a statement. Ten great games is a role.

If he does those four things, the backup point guard job is his for the rest of the season.

## STATEMENT #2: DEUCE McBRIDE — "DON'T FORGET ABOUT ME"

Here's the stat that jumped off the page: **plus-39 in 25 minutes.**

- 8 points
- 3-of-6 from the field
- 2 threes
- 3 assists
- Mike Brown's defensive player of the game

After a rough start to the season — 15 minutes or fewer in three of the first five games and a cold shooting stretch — Deuce is back to being Deuce. Point-of-attack defense, timely threes, and winning minutes. That plus-39 is the best on the team by a mile.

And it matters for the trade conversation too. A Deuce who's playing like this is a lot more valuable to keep than to trade.

## STATEMENT #3: ANDRE DRUMMOND — "THE GLASS IS MINE"

- 14 rebounds in about 15 and a half minutes
- 5 of them offensive

Brown's praise went beyond rebounding: "He was also really, really good defensively in the pick-and-roll situations. His P&R defense is getting better and better each time he steps on the floor. His communication is great. He impacted the game at a pretty high level."

That's a big deal, because pick-and-roll defense has been the main knock on Drummond. If he's improving there while leading the league in offensive rebounds per 36 minutes, the Knicks' backup center "problem" looks a lot smaller than it did two weeks ago.

## STATEMENT #4: KARL-ANTHONY TOWNS — "PAY ME"

**The line against Milwaukee:** 21 points, 16 rebounds, three assists, two steals, and strong defense. He didn't shoot it great, but he attacked, drove, took contact, and lived at the free-throw line. Milwaukee's Myles Turner had no answer.

**The record chase:** That was KAT's 10th straight double-double to start the season. The franchise record for the longest double-double streak to open a season belongs to Willis Reed — at 11. So Towns is one away from tying a Knicks legend.

His reaction when he heard? "Shout out to Willis Reed, man. I didn't know that." That tells you everything. He's not chasing stats. He's just playing.

**Mike Brown on KAT's rebounding:** "He just has a nose or a knack for the ball. You know, he doesn't jump real high, but he's long and he understands angles. KAT's in that category when it comes to rebounding. We emphasize it quite a bit. And we'll tell guys, you've got to put bodies on bodies. So if our smalls are putting bodies on bodies, it's a one-on-one contest for KAT, and he's going to win most of the time."

**The improvement, by the numbers (via Ian Begley):**

| Through 10 games | This season | Last season |
|---|---|---|
| Field-goal % | 54% | 48% |
| Three-point % | 39% | 33% |
| Fouls per game | 2.8 | 3.7 |

Better shooting. Better spacing. Fewer fouls — which means more time on the floor. When asked whether he's moving better, Towns kept it simple: "I don't know. Just another year with this team. Just experience. Just another year and a better understanding of what head coach Mike Brown wants and what I can do to help in this system."

**The message:** Every one of these games sends the same message to the front office. Towns has been the Knicks' most consistent player all season. His contract standoff is still unresolved — the Knicks reportedly offered in the $45 to $50 million per year range, and he turned down about $200 million over four years this summer, per the New York Post. But the longer he plays like this, the harder it is to justify holding firm. A lot of Knicks fans are saying it loud: pay the man.

## STATEMENT #5: OG ANUNOBY — "I'M BACK"

And here's the one Knicks fans have been waiting for.

- 19 points
- 6-of-12 from the field
- 5-of-8 from three
- 3 rebounds in 29 minutes

Five threes. After shooting under 30% from deep through the first nine games — including just 31% on corner threes, an area where he usually roasts teams — OG finally found his stroke.

We kept saying it: OG didn't forget how to shoot. He hit 49% of his threes during last spring's playoffs. He's obsessive about his mechanics. Slumps end. And now pair that shooting with what he's already been doing — attacking the rim, drawing contact, getting to the line at a career-high rate — and you have the version of OG that looks like an All-Star.

## THE BRUNSON NOTE

And the captain? Brunson led the Knicks with 23 points, four rebounds, and two assists, shooting 38.9% from the field. Not his best shooting night, and he'll want to move the ball more. But he was the top scorer, and more importantly, he got to rest a big chunk of the fourth quarter on the front end of a back-to-back. That's a win too.

## THE TEAM IS TOP 10 EVERYWHERE

And here's the big-picture stat, via Ian Begley. Through 10 games:

- **Offensive rating:** 9th
- **Defensive rating:** 6th
- **Net rating:** 6th
- **Offensive rebounding percentage:** 1st in the NBA
- **Overall rebounding percentage:** 1st in the NBA
- **Defensive rebounding percentage:** 2nd in the NBA

Remember where this team was after the 0–3 start? Near the bottom of the league in offensive rating, scoring, and field-goal percentage. Now they're top 10 on both ends and the best rebounding team in the league — without Mitchell Robinson.

Brown gave credit to assistants Brendan O'Connor and Darren Erman for the defensive improvement, and credited both Towns and Drummond for getting better as pick-and-roll defenders. That's coaching, development, and effort all coming together.

## THE BENCH IS A WEAPON

Let's give the whole second unit its flowers. Kolek, Deuce, Drummond, Jordan Clarkson, Landry Shamet, Mo Diawara — whoever comes off the bench, it's like a switch flips. They understand their roles, play within the flow, defend, move the ball, and keep the lead when the starters sit. Clarkson can play one minute or 15 minutes and still make an impact.

That's what a championship bench looks like. And remember, most of these guys are on small contracts. For a team pressed right up against the second apron, getting this much production from cheap players is huge. And it's a big reason the Knicks can survive nights when the stars aren't at their best.

## THE WILLIS REED CONNECTION

Let's take a moment on the record, because it's a special one. Willis Reed is Knicks royalty — the captain of the franchise's championship teams in 1970 and 1973, the man who limped out of the tunnel in Game 7 of the 1970 Finals and inspired one of the most famous moments in NBA history. Until last June, those were the only two titles in franchise history.

Now Karl-Anthony Towns, a key piece of the team that won the franchise's first championship in 53 years, is on the verge of matching one of Reed's records. A center, chasing a Knicks legend at center, in a season where he's defending a title. You can't write it better.

And it adds to the contract debate. When your player starts showing up next to Willis Reed in the record books, it gets a lot harder to tell fans you won't pay him.

## THE KNICKS' IDENTITY IS BACK

Step back and look at what this team looks like right now. Elite rebounding. A top-six defense. A deep bench that wins its minutes. A captain who doesn't have to carry everything. A center playing the best two-way basketball of his career. And a wing who just found his three-point shot again.

That's the identity that won a championship. Tough, physical, unselfish, and deep. After the 0–3 start, a lot of people wondered if it was gone. It's back.

## WHAT'S NEXT

The Knicks went right back to work on the second night of the back-to-back, hosting Cleveland in a rematch of Saturday's 109–97 win. Next on the schedule: a trip to Indiana and back-to-back road games in Toronto.

And here's the storyline to watch: KAT's next double-double ties Willis Reed's franchise record. Two more and he owns it outright.

Deuce stays a Knick, because a plus-39 defensive player of the game isn't someone you trade away during a title defense. There was no "bombshell decision" — just Tyler Kolek playing so well that the backup point guard job is clearly his: 12 points on 5-of-6 shooting, two threes, four assists, a block, and a plus-23 in 15 minutes in a 117–85 rout of Milwaukee.
