## THE HONEST FRAMING

Quick note on the title, because this channel always keeps it straight. "Six huge signings" doesn't mean six brand-new players were signed this week. It means six contract decisions that define this roster heading into opening night: four signings the Knicks made this offseason, and two that are expected to come before Tuesday but are NOT official yet. We'll be clear about which is which.

## THE FINALE: THE CHAMPS LOOKED LIKE THE CHAMPS

Let's set the scene. Two nights earlier in Toronto, the Raptors ran a reserve-heavy Knicks lineup out of the building in a blowout. That night, the Knicks leaned on backup bigs at center. On Thursday, the main frontcourt was back — Karl-Anthony Towns and Andre Drummond handling the middle — and the difference was obvious from the opening tip.

KAT looked like the do-everything center who carried this team through last spring's title run. He took the shots he wanted, controlled the glass, and finished just one assist short of a triple-double. In his final tune-up before the games matter, he played with total confidence in his role — days after the Knicks shut down extension talks and with his player option decision due on Monday.

And what stood out most was how comfortable both bigs looked attacking Toronto center Jakob Poeltl. KAT and Drummond were willing to put the ball on the floor, face up, and go right at Poeltl in space. That's a mismatch the Knicks expect to exploit a lot this season. There's been a lot of talk this offseason about the search for a third center — and that conversation isn't over — but Thursday was a reminder: having two reliable bigs like Towns and Drummond already gives New York more at the position than most teams in the league, including some potential playoff opponents.

## THE HART SCARE

There was one small scare. Josh Hart was a late scratch with a sore right ankle. Based on what's known so far, it doesn't appear serious. If Thursday had been the regular-season opener, there's a good chance he would've played. With nearly five full days off before Philadelphia comes to town, there was no reason to take any risk with one of the team's most important players. Mike Brown started Mohamed Diawara in Hart's place — and that brings us to our scorecard.

## SIGNING ONE: ANDRE DRUMMOND — GRADE: A

**Status: Done.** The Knicks signed Drummond this summer after Mitchell Robinson left for Boston. Fans were split: one group believed he could replicate Mitch's offensive rebounding, the other worried about his finishing and his pick-and-roll defense.

**The preseason evidence:** He just keeps answering the doubters. In the finale, he put up 14 points, eight rebounds, and four assists in just 15 minutes. Earlier in the preseason, he had three blocks in Philadelphia — including one on a corner jumper — and nearly a triple-double against Washington with eight points, nine rebounds, four assists, and two steals. Josh Hart said he's "a way better passer than I thought" and "extremely light on his feet." Jalen Brunson praised his work on his three-point shot.

**The grade:** A. The concern about his pick-and-roll defense hasn't shown up. His rebounding is exactly as advertised. And his passing gives the second unit a new dimension. The one thing to watch: finishing around the rim, where he's not the explosive leaper Mitch was. But as the backup center on a championship team? He's been everything the Knicks hoped for.

## SIGNING TWO: LANDRY SHAMET — GRADE: A-MINUS

**Status: Done.** Shamet came to New York on an Exhibit 9 camp deal in September 2024 with no guarantees. This summer, as a champion, he earned a new four-year, $24 million contract.

**The preseason evidence:** Through his first two games, he had 23 points in just 27 minutes, shooting eight-of-15 from the field and five-of-nine from three. Ian Begley pointed out how remarkable it is that a player who spent the last two preseasons fighting just to make the team stood out as one of New York's most impressive performers this fall.

**The grade:** A-minus. The questions coming in were health, defense, and whether the shooting would hold up — he's only finished a season above 38 percent from three four times in his career. So far, he's answered all three. The minus is just the sample size: preseason hot streaks cool down. But if Shamet even comes close to this level, he and Deuce could split sixth-man duties all season.

## SIGNING THREE: JOSE ALVARADO — GRADE: INCOMPLETE

**Status: Done.** The Knicks brought Alvarado back on a new contract this summer. And honestly, he's the signing whose preseason raises the most questions.

**The preseason evidence:** Across four appearances, Alvarado shot just 36 percent from the field — nine-of-25 — and three-of-10 from three, in just under 60 total minutes. The most concerning number: nine made field goals and seven turnovers. He barely produced more buckets than giveaways.

And the on-court numbers before the finale were ugly. With Alvarado playing, the Knicks posted an offensive rating of 79.2 and a defensive rating of 120.8 — a net rating of minus 41.7. The defense was about the same with or without him, which says more about how loose the whole team has been in exhibition games. But the offense jumped to 103.4 points per 100 possessions without him.

There were still flashes of the player Knicks fans love. Against Minnesota, he jumped a ball-handler in the backcourt, ripped the ball away, and finished with a layup — the team's account celebrated it as "Grand Theft Alvarado."

**The grade:** Incomplete — and here's why I'm not panicking. There's real context.

## THE ALVARADO-KAT KEY

First, Alvarado missed the preseason opener with a knee contusion, which cost him early reps. Second, preseason basketball is messy — coaches test lineups and players rotate at unusual times. And third — this is the big one — he spent almost all of his minutes in lineups that looked nothing like the ones he thrived in last season.

Last season, Alvarado did his best work next to Karl-Anthony Towns. This preseason, they barely shared the floor. Against Washington on October 8th, they played together for just 73 seconds — and in that short stretch, the ball moved well, the Knicks outscored Washington by three, Jordan Clarkson hit a three, and KAT finished a smooth finger roll. Against Minnesota, their time together lasted two possessions.

Now look at last season's numbers. After Alvarado arrived in New York, the Knicks were outscored by 1.9 points per 100 possessions in his 332 non-garbage-time possessions WITHOUT Towns. But with KAT on the floor, the Knicks posted a net rating of plus 8.8 across 528 regular-season possessions. And in the playoffs? Plus 32.5. A center who can create and score opens up the floor and lets Alvarado play to his strengths — pressure, cutting, quick decisions.

This preseason, he mostly played with centers he'd never shared the court with before: Drummond, whose style is very different from KAT's, and Drew Eubanks, who has since suffered a torn pectoral muscle and been waived. So the fix might be simple: get Alvarado back on the floor with KAT.

## ALVARADO HAS BEEN HERE BEFORE

And remember, this isn't the first time Alvarado has gone cold. Last season, he had a breakout night in Philadelphia — eight three-pointers and five steals in a blowout win. Over the next 14 games, he made just five of 34 threes — 14.7 percent — and the slump pushed him out of Mike Brown's rotation. But he didn't stay out for long. When the playoffs arrived, he earned his minutes back and delivered several big moments during the championship run.

So he's already proven he can climb out of a slump. And minutes won't come easily — Brunson leads the backcourt, with Deuce, Shamet, and Clarkson all in the mix. To hold his spot, Alvarado has to bring the defensive energy and cut the turnovers. But here's a fun detail: the opener is against Philadelphia — the same team he torched with eight threes last season.

## SIGNING FOUR: MOHAMED DIAWARA — GRADE: B-PLUS

**Status: Done.** The Knicks gave Diawara, a second-round pick, a contract — and in doing so, they signaled that they believe in him, even ahead of former first-rounder Pacôme Dadiet.

**The preseason evidence:** He made his mark in the opener with a chase-down block on Kentavious Caldwell-Pope. He started the second Toronto game in place of Josh Hart, getting a valuable look with the first unit alongside Brunson, Bridges, OG, and KAT. When a coach puts a young player with the starters in the final dress rehearsal, that's trust.

**The grade:** B-plus. He's not going to crack the regular rotation on a full-strength team right away. But he's the kind of young, cheap, athletic forward a team pressed against the second apron desperately needs. On a roster full of veterans, Diawara might be the best development story of the fall.

## SIGNING FIVE: JAMES WISEMAN — STATUS: EXPECTED, NOT OFFICIAL

**Status: Pending.** This is one of the two signings that hasn't happened yet. Wiseman is still on a non-guaranteed training-camp deal and hasn't been signed to a standard contract.

**The case:** On Thursday, the Knicks waived Ochai Agbaji and centers Tony Bradley and Drew Eubanks — Eubanks after a torn pectoral muscle that will keep him out indefinitely. That leaves Wiseman as the last camp center standing. He's 25, a former number two overall pick, and he has a supporter in Mike Brown, who worked with him as an assistant in Golden State. His best night came Tuesday in Toronto, when he started and posted 18 points and 10 rebounds. With only KAT and Drummond as established centers, the Knicks have made adding a big a priority — which gives Wiseman the inside track.

**The projection:** I'd be surprised if he isn't signed before Tuesday. The smart structure would be a standard minimum that isn't fully guaranteed until early January — upside with a safety net, given his injury history.

## SIGNING SIX: BRUCE BROWN — STATUS: THE BIGGEST OPEN QUESTION

**Status: Pending — and complicated.** Bruce Brown, a key piece of Denver's 2023 championship team, has real backers inside the organization. According to Ian Begley, there's strong support for keeping him. Mike Brown called his on-ball defense "textbook." Jalen Brunson said he brings a lot of things that can help.

**The problem:** money. James Dolan has made it clear the Knicks won't go over the second apron. Right now, the Knicks have room to keep just ONE veteran on a minimum deal while staying below that line. Keeping both Wiseman and Bruce Brown would likely require a trade that sends out salary — and Pacôme Dadiet and Tyler Kolek have been named as the most logical trade candidates.

**The mixed signal:** Here's what made Thursday interesting. With Hart out, there was a natural chance to drop Bruce Brown into the starting group and see how he fit next to the team's best players in a regular-season-like setting. Instead, he didn't play a single minute. That opportunity went to Diawara — and to Dadiet, who got extended run alongside the core.

How do you read that? There are two ways. One: the Knicks already know what Bruce Brown is and didn't need to see more. Two: giving Dadiet extended minutes with the starters could be a showcase — letting other teams see him in a good light before a possible trade. That second one is NBA Tunnel speculation, clearly labeled. But either way, Brown's status remains one of the biggest open questions facing the front office in the next few days.

## WHY KOLEK SHOULD NOT BE THE TRADE PIECE

And let me say it again, because Thursday made the case even stronger: Tyler Kolek should not be the player the Knicks trade to make room. He had five points and five assists without a turnover in the opener. And on Thursday, he hit the shot that won the game in front of a roaring Garden crowd. For a young guard trying to carve out a bigger role, a moment like that matters. He's cheap, he's productive, and he's exactly the type of player a team stuck under the apron needs to keep. If a trade happens, Dadiet is the logical piece. Kolek stays.

## THE BONUS WATCH: A TWO-WAY BIG

One more thing on the roster checklist. With Eubanks and Bradley gone, the Knicks have only KAT, Drummond, and — once he's signed — Wiseman at center. And their three two-way players — Tyler Nickel, Kevin McCullar Jr., and Jaden Akins — are all perimeter players. Toronto waived former Knick Trey Jemison on October 14th, and he's eligible for a two-way deal that wouldn't touch the apron. Not a signing yet. But keep an eye on it.

## THE CHAMPAGNE STILL IN THE BUILDING

And let me end the scorecard with the moment that tied the whole night together. Back in 2016, Stephen Curry returned to Cleveland for the first time since the Warriors had won a title on that floor and joked that the locker room still smelled like champagne. The Knicks didn't celebrate their title at home — they popped the bottles in San Antonio after finishing off the Spurs in five. But Thursday night, when Kolek's shot sat on the rim and dropped, it felt like some of that championship energy is still hanging around the Garden.

And here's the history: Tuesday will be the first time in 53 years that the Knicks start a season as defending champions. The Garden has waited a long time for a night like this. Rings. Banner. Celebration first. Then the work starts all over again.

## THE FINAL SCORECARD

**Andre Drummond — A.** Fourteen, eight, and four in 15 minutes in the finale. Better than advertised.

**Landry Shamet — A-minus.** Twenty-three points in 27 minutes early, five-of-nine from three. The $24 million deal looks smart.

**Jose Alvarado — Incomplete.** Nine-of-25, seven turnovers, minus-41.7 net rating — but almost no minutes next to KAT, where he posted plus 8.8 in the regular season and plus 32.5 in the playoffs.

**Mohamed Diawara — B-plus.** Starter in the dress rehearsal. A real development story.

**James Wiseman — Pending.** The last camp center standing after Eubanks' injury and the Bradley cut. Expected to be signed.

**Bruce Brown — Pending and uncertain.** Strong internal support, but he didn't play Thursday, and keeping him requires a trade — likely involving Dadiet.
