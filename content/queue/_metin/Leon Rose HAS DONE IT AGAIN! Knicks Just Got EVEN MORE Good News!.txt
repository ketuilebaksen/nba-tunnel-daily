## GREEN LIGHT ONE: ZERO INJURIES

Let's start with the news every Knicks fan wanted to hear. Josh Hart missed the preseason finale with a sore right ankle, and for a couple of days, people held their breath. Then Mike Brown said he didn't think the ankle would impact Hart's status for opening night. And according to the team's injury report, the Knicks listed zero injuries for the opener.

Zero. That means Hart is good to go, and the entire roster is fully healthy and available for ring night. For a defending champion starting an 82-game marathon in the toughest division in basketball, you couldn't ask for a better starting line.

And Brown added a detail that explains something we talked about last week. He said he started Mohamed Diawara in the finale because he wanted to see how Diawara would guard Kawhi Leonard and Scottie Barnes if he got the opportunity. So that wasn't just about Bruce Brown sitting — it was a coach testing a young defender against two of the best forwards in the East. That's a smart use of a preseason game.

## GREEN LIGHT TWO: MIKE BROWN EXPLAINS THE WISEMAN CALL

The second green light is about the final roster decision — and this is where the title comes from. Leon Rose's front office chose James Wiseman over Bruce Brown for the final spot. Now Mike Brown has explained the thinking, and it makes total sense.

Here's what he said: when you have a 25-year-old with the length, athleticism, and talent of James Wiseman — because KAT isn't athletic in that way, and Andre once was and still has some of it — you look for guys who could have, should have, and at his age, maybe still can. That vertical threat at the rim, the vertical threat defensively, how well he runs. "You welcome that at his size and age and athleticism."

That's the clearest explanation we've had. The Knicks needed a big all offseason, especially after Mitchell Robinson left for Boston. They didn't just need any big — they needed the specific thing Mitch provided: a vertical threat above the rim on both ends. KAT is a skilled hub, not a lob-catcher. Drummond is a bruising rebounder and a better passer than people thought, but he's not the high flyer he once was. Wiseman is the only one who brings that dimension. And Brown's "could have, should have, maybe still can" line tells you everything about how he sees him: a former number two pick who still has the chance to become what people expected.

So yes — Leon Rose has done it again. Not with a blockbuster, but with a cheap, upside bet that fills the exact hole his team needed filled. That's how this front office built a champion.

## GREEN LIGHT THREE: DEUCE'S AGENT LEAVES THE DOOR OPEN

The third green light is about Deuce McBride — and it's better news than it first appeared. We covered some of Mark Bartelstein's comments last week, but the full quote has more.

Bartelstein said they'd spoken to the Knicks throughout the summer and that Deuce, like any player in his position, would love an extension. "I think he's earned it. He's been a huge part of where that organization has gone over the last three or four years. He's been their sixth man."

Then he revealed something a lot of fans didn't fully know: how hurt Deuce played last postseason. He said Deuce had abdominal surgery that caused him to miss a couple of months, came back before he was really right, and played anyway because he didn't want to miss the opportunity to be there for his teammates. Remember how Deuce struggled offensively in the Finals against San Antonio? Now you know why.

And here's the key part: "It takes two sides to get a deal done. If the Knicks aren't ready to do extensions right now, then we just got to buckle up and go have a great season. He'll be a free agent this summer, but we'll see. We've got a great relationship with President Leon Rose and the front office. There may be opportunities during the season — maybe in the near future. Who knows where we'll be able to have those conversations."

That's a big deal. It's not an agent burning bridges. It's an agent saying: we're not mad, we have a great relationship with Leon Rose, and we can revisit this during the season. So even though Deuce is fourth in the payday line, an extension is not ruled out. A healthy Deuce, playing for a contract, with a door still open — that's a green light.

## GREEN LIGHT FOUR: LEBRON CROWNS OG

And now the quote that's going to make every Knicks fan smile. LeBron James — who'll be standing on the other side of the court tonight — said this about OG Anunoby and the NBA Finals:

"OG, I think, was the MVP of the team in the Finals. Obviously, Brunson became the MVP, but it's all the small things. The chase-down block on Fox, the tip-in game-winner in Game 4 of the NBA Finals, continuing to knock down threes. We knew Jalen Brunson was the head of the snake, but I think OG was the most steady person for that team."

That's enormous praise from one of the greatest players of all time. And it lines up with what we covered in our last video: OG's Game 4 performance was one of the signature moments of the Finals, the night he made Victor Wembanyama look uncomfortable on the biggest stage.

Is LeBron wrong? Honestly, he has a case. But here's why Brunson as Finals MVP is still perfectly right: in Game 5, with everything on the line, Brunson dropped 45 points — playing on a wrist that needed surgery this summer. That's the kind of performance that wins MVPs. The beautiful part is that the Knicks had two players worthy of the award. Whether it was Brunson or OG, the result is the same: the Knicks are the defending champions.

And notice the bonus green light here: OG is coming into this season playing with new aggression, crashing the offensive glass at a rate we've never seen from him. The guy LeBron called the steadiest player on a title team might be getting even better.

## GREEN LIGHT FIVE: BRUNSON SETS THE TONE

The fifth green light comes straight from the captain. Jalen Brunson on opening night: "Every year is a new year to attack something. When people say we're defending the title, that's one way to look at it. It's something that we've won. We've put it in our past, and now we're just trying to attack another."

That's championship DNA. Not "defending." Attacking.

And on the fans seeing the banner raised tonight: "They deserve it. This is something that's been brewing for a long time. To share this moment with them is definitely going to be special."

Fifty-three years. Brunson knows exactly what tonight means to this city.

## GREEN LIGHT SIX: HART TURNS THE PAGE

Josh Hart, to the New York Post's Jared Schwartz: "It'll be cool. Obviously, getting a ring, hanging the banner will be dope. I think we're excited about that, but also excited to kind of move on from it. You get tired of hearing the congrats. The stories are nice at times, but we've got to fully turn the page and realize that's not the team that we are right now. We have to put the work into being a team that's in a similar position next year."

That's exactly the mindset you want. Celebrate tonight, then get back to work. Hart has been the emotional heart of this team for years, and he's telling everyone: last season is over. And remember — no team has repeated in eight years. Hart's said that's partly because of the second apron and this CBA. He knows how hard the road is, and he's not letting anyone get comfortable.

## GREEN LIGHT SEVEN: MIKE BROWN ON THE SIXERS — "A LITTLE MORE JUICE"

The seventh green light is the coach's take on tonight's matchup. Mike Brown was asked whether Knicks-Sixers feels bigger than just a regular-season game, and his answer was refreshingly honest.

In coach talk, he said, you want to say every game is the same — when you step on the floor, no matter who's in front of you, you give the same focus, energy, and effort to the next possession. "But because of the talk, because of who LeBron is — he's a large figure, not just in life, but in the NBA. You also got to look at the guys they have on the team, the guys they've added, the potential of that team, and what we accomplished and what we're trying to accomplish." All those storylines, he said, keep getting pressed on you. "It gives you a little bit more juice, a little bit more energy because of all the stuff around it."

And on whether it'll become a rivalry: "I don't know where we're going to be and where they're going to be... Could it be right now? Yeah, it's a fun story."

That's a coach who isn't pretending. He knows tonight is special. And he's going to use that energy.

## GREEN LIGHT EIGHT: THE DRUMMOND TRANSFORMATION

And the eighth green light might be my favorite story of the entire preseason. Andre Drummond came into camp in the best shape we've seen in years — leaner and more explosive. And he explained exactly how it happened.

He said when he first signed, he felt like he had to come in "guns blazing" because this team had just won. He started feeling anxiety. It trickled into his workouts — if he missed a shot, he'd get frustrated, thinking about whether he'd play, whether something would go wrong. So he decided to shut that off and focus on what he could control: his workouts and getting in shape.

Then the phone rang. Mike Brown called him first. And Drummond asked him flat out: what do you want from me this summer? What am I working toward? He said he does well with direction — "if you tell me what you need me to do, I will do it at 150 percent, every single time." He didn't want to work on something all summer and then show up and find out it wasn't what the coach wanted. So Brown was super transparent — about playing time, about his role. And his main message was simple: be in shape. Run.

And look at the results. Three blocks in Philadelphia, including one on a corner jumper. A near triple-double against Washington. Fourteen points, eight rebounds, and four assists in just 15 minutes in the finale. Hart calling him "a way better passer than I thought" and "extremely light on his feet." That's what happens when a veteran gets clear direction and commits to it. And it's another feather in Leon Rose's cap: the signing that fans were split on in July looks like one of the smartest moves of the offseason.

## THE ONE RED LIGHT: KEL'EL WARE

To keep it real, here's the one piece of news that isn't good — at least for fans who dreamed about Kel'el Ware. As we covered before, Bobby Marks said on Knicks Fan TV that it's hard for him to see Milwaukee doing anything with Ware — let alone trading him to New York. Even if the Bucks made him available, they'd likely want multiple first-round picks and young players, and the Knicks can't trade a first before 2033.

Honestly, it was always a long shot. Ware is exactly the type of center the Knicks have been searching for, but a young, cheap, productive big is the last thing a rebuilding team wants to give away. Maybe down the line. Not now.

And here's the thing: it's okay. Look at the green lights. A healthy roster. A rim-running third center the coach believes in. A rejuvenated Drummond. OG playing with new aggression. Deuce's door still open. This roster is good enough to get back to the Finals. That's the goal every single year — and the Knicks have it.

## WHY "LEON ROSE HAS DONE IT AGAIN" FITS TONIGHT

Step back and look at what Rose's front office did this offseason under the tightest financial limits in the league. They lost their longest-tenured player to a rival and couldn't match because of ownership's apron line. They responded by signing Drummond, who's now in career-best shape. They found Wiseman, a former number two pick, for a minimum. They re-signed Shamet, who was one of the best players of the preseason. They kept Dadiet and Kolek to preserve trade flexibility. They kept a strong relationship with Deuce's camp even while pausing talks. And they walk into ring night with zero injuries.

Not every decision has been perfect — the way the extension pause was communicated to KAT drew real criticism, including from Carmelo Anthony. But on the basketball side, the roster Rose built is ready.

## THE OPPONENT: WHAT PHILLY BRINGS TO THE PARTY

Let's not pretend tonight is a soft landing. The Sixers came into this season loaded. They added two former Finals MVPs: LeBron James, entering his 24th season and turning 42 in December, and Jaylen Brown, a 6-foot-8 wing with explosive athleticism. They still have Tyrese Maxey, who averaged 28.3 points and 6.6 assists last season. And they have Joel Embiid, who — when healthy — few teams can handle.

But the Knicks have history on their side. Last spring, they swept Philadelphia in the playoffs, and Embiid struggled badly on defense in that series. Jose Alvarado once torched the Sixers with eight threes and five steals in a single game. And Philly will be without Ariel Hukporti, the former Knick who suffered a season-ending Achilles injury in the preseason opener — against New York.

The betting market has the Knicks as East favorites at plus-360, with Philadelphia right behind at plus-400. So tonight isn't just a ceremony. It's a statement game between the two favorites in the conference.

## THE 53-YEAR CONTEXT

And before the ball goes up, take a second to appreciate what tonight actually is. The last time the Knicks started a season as defending champions was in 1973. Generations of Knicks fans grew up without ever seeing this. Patrick Ewing and Walt Frazier traveled with the team and were in San Antonio for the Game 5 clincher. Former Knicks — Stephon Marbury, Larry Johnson, John Starks — packed the seats behind the bench during the run, cheering like fans. The Knicks popped the champagne in San Antonio, not at home. Tonight, the Garden finally gets its moment.

That's why Brunson said the fans deserve it. That's why Hart said it'll be "dope" to see the banner. And that's why, for at least one night, the contract noise, the apron math, and the trade rumors can wait.
