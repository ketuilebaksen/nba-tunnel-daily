FRONT ONE: THE NEXT SHAMET SWEEPSTAKES

Here's the frame that makes this camp special. Most Exhibit 9 stories end in silence — released before opening night, forgotten by Thanksgiving. But every few years, one sticks and changes everything. Alfonzo McKinnie signed one with Golden State in 2018 and played 72 games. Gary Payton II walked the same path three years later and ended up a champion. And this franchise owns the gold standard: two years ago, a camp invite named Landry Shamet made the team, became a folk hero, and shot his way into the championship story. That's why five non-guaranteed vets in New York get more attention than they'd get anywhere else on Earth — because in THIS building, the fourteenth man sometimes ends up in the parade.

And before we handicap it, appreciate WHY the Shamet archetype keeps recurring in this specific building, because it's not luck. Camp-invite success stories need three ingredients: a system that magnifies one elite skill, a coaching staff secure enough to play the cheap guy over the drafted guy, and a locker room that folds outsiders in fast. New York runs all three at maximum. The five-out machine turns any competent specialist into a weapon. Mike Brown demonstrably plays whoever earns it — ask the two-way carousel from the title run. And this locker room made a folk hero out of an undrafted Georgian, a Finals hero out of a camp shooter, and a family member out of a two-way wing. The Exhibit 9 lottery hits here more often than anywhere else because the building is DESIGNED to cash those tickets.

So who's next? The fresh analysis walks the five files with colder eyes than we've seen yet — and lands somewhere interesting. Wiseman: rarest upside, but the injuries and the production say what they say. Eubanks: high floor, smart passer, solid interior defender — but a below-average rebounder for a big, with a hard ceiling. Agbaji: the wing-defender profile fits, but four pro seasons and only ONE above 36% from deep. And then the eyebrow-raiser — a genuinely critical read on BRUCE BROWN, our longtime favorite: the durability halo is newer than it looks (he missed real time in the seasons before the iron-man year), and the analysis argues his Denver play last season was worse than the box score dressed it up to be. Which leaves... JOHN KONCHAR as the crowned favorite. The most sensible bet, they call him. And the case is honest on both ends: the three-ball hasn't cooperated since 2022 — and even then he barely took them — but everything else points north: a +2.0 defensive box plus-minus, elite per-36 steal and block rates, rebounding way above his position, connective passing with THREE career triple-doubles. A smaller-scale Josh Hart, with the size to play the style. Sound familiar? It's the exact scouting file we published the day he signed.

Now, where do I land with our Bruce Brown prediction on the line? I'll say it straight: I hear the critique, and I'm not moving — yet. The ring, the versatility, the years of front-office admiration still make Brown my pick for the seat. But here's the honest nuance today's analysis gets right: Brown wins the RESUME battle; Konchar might win the FIT battle. This roster doesn't need another connector who needs the ball — it needs the defense-rebounding-cutting profile that works next to anyone. If camp is close, fit beats résumé. Consider my confidence officially trimmed from bold to firm. And there's a ripple worth repeating from the analysis: the roster math still expects New York to move Pacôme Dadiet — or, and hear this name re-enter the chamber, TYLER KOLEK — to open the second seat. Kolek's name appearing in trade speculation again, after his summer aggression mission and that payroll-value confession we covered? File it. The domino still hasn't fallen, and now it has two possible faces. And whoever survives this gauntlet lands squarely in Mohamed Diawara's runway — the internal competition never, ever sleeps here.

FRONT TWO: DETROIT'S $200 MILLION MELTDOWN

Now to the schadenfreude section — except it's not even guilty pleasure, it's strategic analysis. Jalen Duren — yes, the same Duren from our four-team blockbuster fantasy earlier this summer — has REJECTED the Pistons' latest extension offer. And the number, per Shams: five years, TWO HUNDRED MILLION dollars. Turned it down. He's now openly considering the qualifying offer route — $9.6 million for one year, then unrestricted free agency. And here's the part that should terrify Detroit: the math doesn't justify it. Run it out — QO plus a max deal elsewhere projects to roughly $198 million over five years. That's LESS than what's on the table right now. When a 22-year-old walks away from guaranteed generational money to bet on an uncertain market, the spreadsheet isn't the story. The RELATIONSHIP is. Something is broken in that building, folks, and everyone in the league can smell it.

Play out the branches, because every single one blooms for New York. Branch one: the standoff hardens into a sign-and-trade — Detroit, having already moved Isaiah Stewart, gets thinner at center while negotiating from public weakness, with thirty front offices circling like it's a yard sale. Branch two, the nightmare: he signs the QO, and the Pistons spend an entire season with a walking trade rumor at center — every loss a referendum, every road presser about his future, a locker room marinating in uncertainty. As a rival fan? Chef's kiss. Branch three, still most likely: they eventually agree — and inherit different problems. The scar tissue from a negotiation this ugly doesn't dissolve at the signing ceremony. And the cap sheet it creates is brutal: at around $40 million a year for Duren, Detroit would owe north of $120 MILLION in 2027-28 to just three players — Duren, Cade Cunningham, and Ausar Thompson — two-thirds of a cap tied up in a trio with exactly ONE established star. Remember, this is a core that nearly got bounced by ORLANDO last spring. That's not a dynasty foundation; that's a ceiling with a mortgage.

And can we take one second to appreciate the second-apron era's cruel comedy here? Detroit was supposed to be the team WITHOUT New York's money problems — young core, cheap contracts, picks in the drawer, all runway. Yet here we are in September: the champs, allegedly strangled by the apron, have their entire core signed, sacrificing, and publicly bored of praise... while the flexible young challenger can't get its own 22-year-old to take two hundred million. Cap space doesn't build culture, folks. The Brunson discount, the KAT sacrifice, the Hart tone-setting — that's infrastructure no spreadsheet shows, and it's why the "aging expensive champs versus hungry cheap kids" narrative keeps missing. The kids have to actually WANT to stay together first.

The honest caveat, as always: Detroit still punches. The physicality travels, the defense disrupts, and those +850 East odds we covered aren't charity — matchups with them will stay ugly all season. But zoom out to the takeaway that matters for the title defense: one of the teams everyone penciled in as a primary East challenger is walking into October with more questions than answers, a quiet offseason behind it, and its young center's agent on line one. Meanwhile the champs' biggest September drama is... which glue guy wins a camp battle. The contrast IS the story. The East gauntlet is real — but one of its gates just got a little rusty.

FRONT THREE: THE DIABATÉ DOOR SWINGS OPEN

And now the part that had me pacing, because our longest-running trade target just came back into play. The Hornets traded Dorian Finney-Smith to Atlanta for Buddy Hield and Ryan Nembhard — a one-for-two swap that leaves Charlotte with EIGHTEEN players on guaranteed money. The limit is fifteen. Three players must go before opening night — waived, traded, or both — and every guaranteed dollar they waive still gets paid, which means Charlotte's front office has every incentive to answer the phone instead of eating money. And who's been calling since July? Per Marc Stein's earlier reporting, the Knicks already asked about MOUSSA DIABATÉ once this offseason. The file from our deep-dive still glows: 24 years old, $2.5 million — pocket change — a rim-finishing, offensive-glass-pounding, switchable young big who checks every doctrine box this channel has recited all summer: young, cheap, hungry, evolving. And now the kicker: he's in a CONTRACT year, on a team that just drafted young frontcourt help and traded for Naz Reid, that moved on from LaMelo and clearly isn't chasing wins. If Charlotte doesn't plan to pay him next summer — and rebuilding teams rarely pay backup bigs — trading him NOW beats losing him for nothing. We've heard that logic somewhere before, haven't we, Tunnel fam?

So let's talk structure, because the math needs a third dance partner and this is where it gets fun. New York can't absorb Charlotte's roster crunch directly — there aren't enough expendable salaries to send two bodies back for one. The play is the classic three-team grease job: bring in a club with open roster spots, pay them in second-round picks to absorb contracts, and let everyone walk away happy. New York's war chest of extra seconds can cover Dadiet's $2.9 million landing softly elsewhere AND help Charlotte offload a smaller deal — a Nembhard at $2.2, a Connaughton at $3.8, maybe a young flyer if the Hornets have moved on. There's even enough sweetener to help them move something bigger if that's the price of doing business. As for Diabaté's cost: the seconds and a pick SWAP should be on the table without hesitation — a 24-year-old rotation big at that salary is worth an aggressive package of the future's loose change. The precious stuff — the distant firsts — stays in the vault.

One firm line from me, though, because the analysis floated a name that made my eye twitch: the suggestion that Deuce McBride could headline a Diabaté package "if the Knicks don't plan to extend him." Absolutely not — and the reporting agrees with me now. As we broke down in the plot-twist video: the cap forecast moved, the sides are in regular contact, and the extension is the stated perfect-world outcome. You do not spend your best trade chip on a BACKUP center trade — Deuce's price is a difference-maker or nothing. Diabaté is a seconds-and-swaps purchase, not a Deuce purchase. Keep the tiers straight, and keep the chip polished.
