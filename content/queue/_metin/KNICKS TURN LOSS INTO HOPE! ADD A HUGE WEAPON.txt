## ACCOUNTABILITY FIRST

On this channel, we own our calls. I predicted a ring-night win. I was wrong. The Sixers came into the Garden on the biggest night in recent Knicks history and took it. And to be clear about the title: the Knicks didn't sign anyone new after this game. ## THE NIGHT: HOW IT SLIPPED AWAY

The pregame ceremony lasted about 30 minutes. There had been some doubt about Josh Hart, but the full title-winning starting five took the floor together: Jalen Brunson, Hart, Mikal Bridges, OG Anunoby, and Karl-Anthony Towns.

And for most of the night, it was the Knicks' game. New York held the lead through most of the first three quarters and went into halftime up 58–54. The Knicks never trailed by more than nine points. Remember, this is a team that rallied from double-digit deficits four times on the way to the title in June. This one never felt out of reach.

But the night turned late in the third quarter. Philadelphia closed the period on an 11–0 run, capped by a Tyrese Maxey three at the horn. The Knicks fought back and twice cut the deficit to a single point in the final three minutes — but they never retook the lead. Brunson airballed a three in the closing seconds, and LeBron James sealed it at the free-throw line.

## THE BOX SCORE TRUTHS

**Philadelphia:** Maxey led all scorers with 28 points. Jaylen Brown, in his first game with the Sixers after coming over from Boston, added 19 points, eight rebounds, and six assists. LeBron, also making his Sixers debut, finished with 14 points and nine rebounds.

**New York:** Brunson scored a team-high 21 points, but needed 19 shots to get there, making only six. As a team, the Knicks shot 37.2 percent from the field and 33 percent from three.

And here's the frustrating part: Philadelphia didn't shoot well either. The Sixers made just 41.9 percent of their shots and only 27.5 percent from three. On a night when the other team shoots like that, the champions usually win. So why didn't they?

## THE REAL STORY: 3 MINUTES AND 15 SECONDS

It took Karl-Anthony Towns just three minutes and 15 seconds to change the shape of the night. That's when he was whistled for his second personal foul, forcing Mike Brown to go to his bench far earlier than he'd planned. By halftime, KAT had three fouls and had played barely six minutes. He still finished with 19 points — but he logged only 24 minutes in a game the Knicks lost by six.

Having your starting center available for only half the game can easily be the difference between a win and a loss. And that's exactly how it played out.

The early whistles weren't a total surprise. Foul trouble has followed KAT throughout his career, and the Sixers know that better than anyone. During last spring's playoff series, Philadelphia attacked him repeatedly, looking to draw contact and pull him off the floor. On Tuesday, they picked up right where they left off. KAT is also closing in on three straight seasons as the NBA's leader in offensive fouls — many of them on drives into the paint.

## THE BLUEPRINT PROBLEM — AND THE FIX

This isn't a minor issue for the reigning champions. Every opponent is circling the Knicks on the schedule this season, and they now have a clear blueprint: force KAT into early fouls, and New York's best big man spends long stretches on the bench. That makes it hard for him to find a rhythm — and even harder for the Knicks to play their way.

The fix? Lean more on his passing from the top of the key, where he can create for teammates without bulldozing into a crowded paint. That's the KAT-as-hub offense that won them a championship — high-post passes, dribble handoffs, Brunson attacking off his screens. That part of his game wasn't on display Tuesday. It needs to be on Friday.

And there's a financial angle too. KAT is heading toward what could be the last major contract of his career. If foul trouble becomes a season-long storyline and drags down his numbers, teams will factor that in. The incentive to fix this has never been greater.

## THE HOPE: THE WEAPON THEY ADDED THIS SUMMER

Now the part that turns this loss into hope. When KAT went to the bench three minutes in, Mike Brown turned to the veteran New York signed this summer to replace Mitchell Robinson's minutes: Andre Drummond. And for one half of basketball, Drummond was everything the Knicks could have dreamed of.

The Mount Vernon, New York native made his regular-season debut in orange and blue just minutes into the game, and he immediately grabbed the Garden's attention. He blocked a Joel Embiid mid-range jumper. He battled the Sixers' star in the post. And according to the team, he recorded the third-fastest double-double by any Knick in the play-by-play era, which goes back to the 1996–97 season.

His first-half line: in just over 17 minutes, 10 points, 12 rebounds, three assists, a steal, and two blocks. His plus-seven was the best mark on the team. And he grabbed 26.1 percent of New York's missed shots by himself. That's elite offensive rebounding — the exact thing the Knicks lost when Mitch left.

For a fan base that mostly knew him by reputation, it was a spectacular introduction. That's the weapon. That's what Leon Rose added this summer. And remember why he looks like this: Drummond told us that Mike Brown called him early in the offseason, and when Drummond asked what the coach wanted, the answer was simple — be in shape, run. He came in leaner and more explosive, and it showed.

## THE WARNING SIGN

But here's the honest part. In the second half, the picture changed. Drummond had more fouls and turnovers than made field goals — he made just one. He wasn't moving with the same energy, and his finishes around the rim lost the craft he'd shown earlier.

That's the warning. Drummond was signed to be a backup. On Tuesday, because of KAT's foul trouble, he was asked to do far more — to battle Embiid for long stretches, to carry the frontcourt, to play starter-level minutes in the first game of the season. He's a 33-year-old veteran. Asking him to be Mitch and KAT at the same time isn't a sustainable plan.

So the hope is real: when Drummond is fresh, he's a weapon. The lesson is also real: the Knicks need to protect his minutes.

## THE OTHER BRIGHT SPOT: ALVARADO

There was one more clear bright spot on the bench. Jose Alvarado — acquired at last season's trade deadline and brought back this summer — did exactly what he's here to do. Eight points, including two threes, plus three rebounds, three assists, and two steals. He pressured the ball, created extra possessions, and gave New York a jolt of energy every time he checked in.

After a rough preseason, that's a great sign. And it means Tyler Kolek, who sits behind Alvarado on the depth chart, probably won't get an opening soon. There's no reason for Mike Brown to change anything there.

## THE CONCERNS ON THE BENCH

Now the tougher part. The rest of the bench was a mixed bag at best.

**Deuce McBride** had a night to forget: one-of-five from the floor, with little positive impact. His defense is a real asset, but it can only carry him so far when his shot disappears — and that's becoming a pattern. His offense went missing during last season's playoff run, again in the preseason, and now in the opener. He's pointed to his core-muscle surgery as the reason, but that procedure is now months behind him. And Mike Brown seems to be adjusting already: Deuce played only 14 minutes. On an expiring contract, with Brian Windhorst already naming him as a likely casualty of the apron, this is a stretch Deuce needs to turn around quickly.

**Landry Shamet** led the reserves with 25 minutes, but his shooting — the main reason he's in the rotation — didn't show up. Two-of-seven from the field, one-of-four from three, and zero-of-two at the line. After a red-hot preseason, one cold night is not a crisis. But the bench needs him.

**Jordan Clarkson** played only eight minutes, which suggests he doesn't have a firm grip on a big role either.

## THE OPENING FOR DIAWARA

And that could open a door for Mohamed Diawara. The young forward didn't play Tuesday. But Mike Brown started him in the preseason finale specifically to see how he'd guard Kawhi Leonard and Scottie Barnes. With the starters only a few months removed from a long, demanding Finals run, Brown can't afford a bench that gives him so little. If the veterans keep stumbling through the first week, don't be surprised if Diawara gets his chance.

## THE CENTER PECKING ORDER — AND WISEMAN'S PATH

Here's the biggest roster takeaway. When KAT went to the bench, Brown leaned heavily on Drummond. The only other option was James Wiseman — and Brown clearly wasn't ready to throw him into a matchup with Joel Embiid in the first game of the season. Wiseman didn't play a single minute.

That hesitation makes sense. Wiseman's road to New York wasn't smooth — at the start of the offseason, he was reportedly preparing to continue his career in Europe because of a lack of NBA interest. And during camp, Brown said the bigs brought in were meant to be ready contributors, not development projects. On opening night, his choices told a slightly different story.

But here's why Wiseman might have come out ahead anyway. If KAT keeps getting targeted for early fouls, the third center on this roster is going to get more chances than a typical end-of-bench reserve. And if Drummond can't hold up under heavy minutes — like we saw in the second half — the pecking order behind KAT may not stay settled for long. Wiseman needs patience. But a path to the rotation clearly exists.

And if the answer on Wiseman isn't enough to support KAT and Drummond, Leon Rose and the front office will have to start working the phones. Remember, they kept Pacôme Dadiet's contract specifically to preserve trade flexibility for exactly this type of need.

## HOW WE GOT HERE

It's worth remembering how thin this frontcourt became. Last summer — the Knicks' first free agency as champions — got off to a rough start. Mitchell Robinson signed with Boston. Then Philadelphia, a division rival, took advantage of New York's refusal to go beyond the second apron and offered Ariel Hukporti enough money to pull him away. In a matter of weeks, the Knicks lost two centers who gave them reliable minutes during the title run. Drummond and Wiseman are the replacements. Tuesday showed both the promise and the limits of that plan.

## PERSPECTIVE

Let's keep it in perspective. This was one game. The Knicks won the title four months ago. They led for most of the night. KAT played only 24 minutes. Brunson shot six-of-19. And they still had chances in the final minutes. No sweeping conclusions should come from one loss.

But repeating as champion in today's NBA leaves very little room for error — especially in an East where Philadelphia just added LeBron James and Jaylen Brown. The Knicks need to learn from this one fast.

## NEXT UP: BOSTON AND MITCH

And the next test comes quickly. The Knicks visit the Boston Celtics on Friday, October 23rd — and Mitchell Robinson will face his former team for the first time. A living reminder of exactly what New York gave up at center. For Drummond, it's a chance to prove the first half wasn't a fluke. For KAT, it's a chance to stay out of foul trouble. For Wiseman, it might be the night he gets his first minutes. And for Brunson, it's a chance to bounce back the way he always does.

## THE BRUNSON BOUNCE-BACK FACTOR

Let's talk about the captain for a second, because one line in the box score will get a lot of attention: six-of-19, and an airball in the closing seconds. On a night when the whole building was emotional — the ring, the banner, 53 years of waiting — it's understandable if the shooting wasn't sharp.

But remember who this is. In Game 5 of the Finals, with everything on the line, Brunson dropped 45 points on a wrist that needed surgery this summer. All preseason, he looked like the least of the Knicks' worries — attacking the rim, hitting floaters, pulling up from midrange, and showing zero hesitation with that surgically repaired shooting hand. Bad shooting nights happen to everybody. What matters is the response. And Jalen Brunson's responses tend to be loud.

## THE GAME PLAN FOR BOSTON

So what should the Knicks do differently on Friday? Here's my game plan.

**Keep KAT on the floor.** Boston now has two starter-level rim protectors in Mitchell Robinson and Neemias Queta. Driving into that crowd is exactly how KAT picks up offensive fouls. Run the offense through him at the top of the key — handoffs, high-post passes, pick-and-pop threes — and make Boston's bigs come out to him.

**Win the glass war.** This is the matchup everyone will watch: the Knicks without Mitch against Mitch himself. Drummond's first half showed he can own the offensive boards. OG has been crashing the glass at a rate we've never seen from him. If the Knicks win the rebounding battle against their old center, that's a statement.

**Manage Drummond's minutes.** Don't make him carry starter minutes again. Keep him fresh so the first-half version shows up for the whole night. And if that means giving Wiseman a few minutes, this is a good time to find out what he can do.

**Pair Alvarado with KAT.** Last season, the Knicks were plus-8.8 per 100 possessions with Alvarado and KAT together in the regular season, and plus-32.5 in the playoffs. Alvarado was the best bench player in the opener. Put him back with his best partner.

**Expect Tatum.** Jayson Tatum is back from his Achilles tear, and Paul George is playing a supporting role. Boston will be motivated, the building will be loud, and the Celtics would love nothing more than to send the champs to 0–2. This is a big early test.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. The Knicks bounce back in Boston. KAT plays more from the top of the key, stays on the floor, and the offense looks like the playoff version again. Drummond's minutes get managed, and we see a few minutes of Wiseman. Brunson responds with a big night. And by the end of the first week, the opener will look like what it was: one tough night on an emotional evening — with a real silver lining in Andre Drummond.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. After a 30-minute ring and banner ceremony, the Knicks lost the opener to Philadelphia, 106–100, after leading 58–54 at halftime and giving up an 11–0 run to close the third quarter. Maxey scored 28, Jaylen Brown added 19, eight, and six, and LeBron had 14 and nine. Brunson scored 21 on six-of-19 shooting, and the Knicks shot 37.2 percent. The biggest factor: KAT picked up his second foul at 3:15 and played just 24 minutes. The hope: Andre Drummond's first half — 10 points, 12 rebounds, three assists, two blocks in 17 minutes, the third-fastest Knicks double-double in the play-by-play era — though he faded badly in the second half. Alvarado gave the bench energy; Deuce, Shamet, and Clarkson struggled; Diawara and Wiseman could get chances. Next up: Boston and Mitch on Friday.
