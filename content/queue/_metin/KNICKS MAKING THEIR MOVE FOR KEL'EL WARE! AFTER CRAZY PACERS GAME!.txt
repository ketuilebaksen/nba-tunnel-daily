## THE PACERS GAME

**The result:** Pacers 106, Knicks 104.

**The hole:** The Knicks fell behind by 14 — another slow stretch that put them in chase mode on the road.

**The comeback:** They fought all the way back and had a real chance to win it at the end. It didn't fall their way, but the fight was there.

**The stars:**
- **Jalen Brunson:** 33 points and 11 assists. The reigning East Player of the Week keeps doing it.
- **Karl-Anthony Towns:** 23 points and 15 rebounds.

**The KAT history note:** Here's a big one. After the Bucks game, Towns was at 10 straight double-doubles to open the season — one away from tying Willis Reed's franchise record of 11. Then he went 21 and 11 against Cleveland, and 23 and 15 against Indiana. By our count, that's 12 straight. That means KAT has passed Willis Reed for the longest double-double streak to start a season in Knicks history. A loss doesn't erase that. That's a special achievement, and it puts KAT's name right next to a true Knicks legend.

**The record:** The Knicks are 6–6, with two games in Toronto next, then a trip to Minnesota and a home game against Portland.

## WHAT THE PACERS LOSS TELLS US

Let's be honest about what this game means — and what it doesn't.

**The bad:** Another early hole. The Knicks have now fallen behind by double digits in a lot of their games this season — Detroit, Chicago, Philadelphia, Cleveland twice, and now Indiana. Against weaker teams, they've been able to climb out. Against good teams on the road, it's a much harder fight. That's the biggest fixable problem on this team.

**The good:** They didn't fold. Down 14 on the road, a lot of teams would accept the loss and move on to Toronto. The Knicks fought back and had a real chance at the end. That's the champion's DNA.

**The pattern:** Two straight losses — Cleveland at home and Indiana on the road — after a four-game win streak. That's not panic territory. It's a reminder that this East is deep, and that the Knicks can't keep spotting good teams a lead.

**The fix:** Better first quarters. Brunson and Towns are carrying their weight — 33 and 11, 23 and 15. The rest of the starters need to match that energy from the opening tip, especially Mikal Bridges and OG Anunoby. And Josh Hart getting back to full strength would help the defense set the tone early.

## THE BRUNSON-TOWNS ENGINE

One more thing from Indiana. Look at the two-man production: 56 points, 26 rebounds, and well over a dozen assists between Brunson and Towns. That's an elite duo carrying the offense night after night.

And it's exactly why the reporting says both are viewed as close to untouchable. No matter what happens with the trade market, the Knicks are building around the Brunson-Towns engine. Every bench move — Kolek, Alvarado, Clarkson, Deuce — is about finding the right pieces to support those two.

## TYLER KOLEK: FROM QUESTION MARK TO ASSET

Now the most interesting story on the roster. A few weeks ago, Tyler Kolek's future in New York looked uncertain. He's 25, his role had gone up and down, and it seemed unlikely the Knicks would pick up his $2.2 million club option for the 2027–28 season.

Then Jose Alvarado went down with an ankle injury. Kolek stepped into the backup point guard role, and he hasn't looked back.

**The surface numbers:** 3.4 points and 4.9 assists per game on 42.9% shooting. Not the kind of numbers that make highlight shows.

**The deeper numbers — and this is where it gets wild:**
- Among players with at least 100 minutes this season, Kolek **leads the entire NBA in assist-to-turnover ratio**.
- Before Friday's game, he ranked **third on the team in plus-minus at plus-61**.
- In the 115 minutes he played, the Knicks had a **net rating of plus-27.6**. In the 413 minutes without him? Just **plus-0.9**.

That's the profile of a player who makes the team better simply by being on the floor. He doesn't force shots. He doesn't give the ball away. He keeps the offense organized when Brunson sits.

**In Indiana:** six rebounds, a three, and zero turnovers. Again. Steady, mistake-free basketball — exactly what a team built to win now needs.

## KOLEK'S THREE PATHS

According to Knicks On SI, Kolek's rise is opening several new paths for the front office before the February trade deadline. Let's go through them.

## PATH A: TRADE ALVARADO, PROMOTE KOLEK

**The idea:** Alvarado is on a three-year, roughly $14 million deal. Moving him would open more minutes for Kolek permanently and free up some money, which matters a lot for a team that has only about $89,000 of room, according to ESPN's Bobby Marks.

**What the money could do:** That extra room could help the Knicks add depth on the wing or at center — the two spots where they could use one more reliable piece.

**The catch:** Alvarado can't be traded until December 15th. And he's a beloved part of the championship team. His full-court pressure and chaos don't show up in Kolek's game.

**My grade:** 🟡 The most logical path, but an emotional one.

## PATH B: KEEP KOLEK AND MAKE HIM PART OF THE LONG-TERM PLAN

**The idea:** Deuce McBride is heading toward free agency next summer. If Alvarado ends up somewhere else, Kolek could quickly go from fringe player to priority. A cheap, efficient backup point guard who knows the system and has earned the coaching staff's trust is exactly the kind of piece contenders try to lock up early.

**The money:** Picking up that $2.2 million club option — or even working out an extension — would be a bargain for a player with these numbers.

**My grade:** ✅ The safest and smartest long-term path.

## PATH C: USE KOLEK AS A TRADE CHIP

**The idea:** His strong play has turned him into a real trade asset. If the Knicks decide to make a bigger move, Kolek could be packaged with Pacôme Dadiet and some of their 12 second-round picks to bring back a player who fills a more pressing need.

**The interest:** Kolek has reportedly started drawing interest from other teams — including Milwaukee, the city where he played his college basketball at Marquette.

**The irony:** A few weeks ago, nobody would have listed Kolek as an asset that could help land a meaningful upgrade. Now he's part of that conversation.

**My grade:** 🔴 Only for a clear upgrade. Players this efficient on contracts this small are rare. Don't sell them cheap.

## THE HONEST LIMITS ON KOLEK

To be fair, there are real limits to his game. He's not one of the most athletic guards in the league, and he lacks size — which may cap how highly other teams value him. Bigger, faster guards can test him on defense, especially in the playoffs.

But the Knicks aren't asking him to be a star. They're asking him to run the second unit, protect the ball, and keep the team on track. So far, he's done that as well as almost anyone in the league.

And there's a culture lesson here too. Kolek didn't complain when his minutes were limited. He stayed ready. When his moment came, he grabbed it. That's the kind of professionalism coaching staffs remember — and it's a big reason the conversation around him flipped so fast, from "will he stay?" to "how do the Knicks best use him?"

## JORDAN CLARKSON'S MYSTERY MINUTES

Kolek isn't the only reserve guard getting attention. Jordan Clarkson's role has created even more questions.

**The puzzle:** Clarkson played in nine of the Knicks' first 10 games — and he didn't finish a single one with a negative plus-minus. Yet his minutes have gone up and down. And in the recent home loss to Cleveland, a very winnable game, he didn't get on the floor at all. Instead, young forward Mo Diawara played 11 minutes.

For a lot of fans, that was hard to understand. You've got a guy who's positive every night, and he doesn't play in a close game?

## THE EXPLANATION: BROWN IS COACHING FOR JUNE

According to Knicks On SI, there's likely a clear explanation. Mike Brown doesn't seem to be treating every regular-season game as a test that must be passed at any cost. He appears to be separating long-term evaluation from the result of a single night.

In other words, Brown is using the early part of the season to learn what each player can do — trying combinations, seeing who can handle which role. That approach might cost a game here or there. But it's built for April, May, and June, not November.

That makes sense for a coach who just won a championship. Brown knows a title roster needs more than a fixed rotation. Injuries happen. Matchups change. A playoff series can turn on a player who was barely used in the regular season. Learning about Diawara now, while keeping Clarkson fresh, gives the Knicks more answers later.

It also fits the two-minute tracking data Brown described — the staff is measuring everyone constantly. This isn't random. It's research.

## WHY CLARKSON STILL MATTERS — A LOT

And make no mistake, Clarkson brings things this roster needs.

- **The scoring:** He's a former Sixth Man of the Year and one of the most productive bench scorers of his generation. He can create his own shot and change the pace of a game in just a few minutes.
- **The rebounding:** He's been one of the Knicks' top offensive rebounders — on the best rebounding team in the NBA.
- **The pressure:** He's one of the few players on the roster who will pick up opposing ball handlers near full court. That wears teams down and fits the energy the Knicks want from the second unit.

Knicks On SI even compared his role to a hybrid of Josh Hart and Jose Alvarado — a guard who scraps for loose balls, chases on defense, and still gives you offense. That's a rare mix from a player who arrived mostly known as a scorer.

## WHY CLARKSON'S VERSATILITY IS INSURANCE

And here's why that versatility could become even more important. If injuries hit, or if a trade reshapes the backcourt, Clarkson can cover for several different players:

- **Landry Shamet's** minutes as a shooter
- **Deuce McBride's** minutes as a defender
- **Tyler Kolek's** minutes as a secondary ball handler

Remember: Deuce is expected to be a free agent next summer, and Kolek is drawing trade interest. If either one is moved, Clarkson suddenly becomes a much bigger part of the plan.

## WHERE THE CORE STANDS

Here's the important context for all of this trade talk. According to the reporting, the core looks set:
- **Mikal Bridges** is considered unlikely to be traded, despite all the speculation
- **Jalen Brunson** and **Karl-Anthony Towns** are viewed as close to untouchable

So any changes will most likely happen around the edges of the rotation — exactly where Kolek, Alvarado, Deuce, Clarkson, and Dadiet live.

## SO… WHAT ABOUT KEL'EL WARE?

Let's come back to the title, because a lot of you are going to ask. Does a Kel'el Ware-type move even make sense?

**The profile:** Ware is a young, athletic seven-footer, a 2024 first-round pick, still on his rookie-scale deal. That's exactly the type the Knicks were looking for all offseason after Mitchell Robinson left — young, athletic, and up-and-coming.

**The fit:** A rim-running, shot-blocking big next to KAT would address the Knicks' biggest playoff question: rim protection behind Towns. Andre Drummond leads the league in offensive rebounds per 36 minutes, but rim protection is still the weak spot.

**The problem:** A young, productive big on a rookie deal is exactly the kind of player teams don't give away. The Knicks would almost certainly have to send out real value — think Kolek, Dadiet, and second-round picks, or Alvarado once he's eligible — while still matching salary under the second apron.

**The reality check:** Again, we didn't find any reporting that the Knicks are pursuing Ware. So this is a "fun idea," not a "move."

**My verdict:** The *type* of player makes total sense. The specific player is a long shot. If the Knicks make a frontcourt move, it'll likely be cheaper and quieter.

## THE BIGGER PICTURE: DEPTH IS NEVER A WEAKNESS

For Knicks fans, all of this is a good problem to have. Depth at point guard is never a weakness. Finding a player who produces this much value on such a small contract gives Leon Rose and Mike Brown more options than they had at the start of the season.

Think about where the Knicks were in October. Alvarado was the clear backup point guard. Kolek was a question mark. Deuce was struggling and losing minutes. Clarkson was a scoring specialist. Now Kolek leads the league in assist-to-turnover ratio, Deuce has found his shot, Clarkson has become a Hart-Alvarado hybrid, and Diawara is showing he can handle real minutes.

That's a lot of moving parts — and a lot of trade flexibility. The Knicks have until the February deadline to decide how to shape the bench. Until then, Brown seems comfortable moving pieces around, testing young players, and learning what works. It might cost a game or two in November. But if it pays off in May, nobody will remember.
