## THE HONEST PART FIRST

Let's be clear about one thing right away, because this channel doesn't play games. The Knicks did NOT sign a new 6-foot-8 guard from outside the organization. The surprise is the decision itself: instead of keeping Bruce Brown, the Knicks chose to hold on to Pacôme Dadiet — their 6-foot-8 young guard — and the reason is all about what his contract could do in a trade. The only "signing" on the books is James Wiseman, who's now set to make the roster. With that clear, let's dig in.

## THE MOVE

Here's what happened. According to Shams, the Knicks waived Bruce Brown, locking in the roster just days before the title defense begins Tuesday night against LeBron James and the Philadelphia 76ers at the Garden.

That settles the final spot. James Wiseman, the former number two overall pick, is now set to open the season as the team's third center behind Karl-Anthony Towns and Andre Drummond. He joined the Knicks on an Exhibit 9 training-camp deal and, across four preseason games, averaged 8.0 points and 4.3 rebounds — enough to convince Mike Brown he deserved the job. Earlier in the week, the Knicks had already cleared out the rest of the veteran camp group: Drew Eubanks after his torn pectoral muscle, plus Tony Bradley and Ochai Agbaji.

Bottom line: there will be no other veteran signing before opening night. The roster is set.

## IT WASN'T AN EASY CALL

Let's give Bruce Brown his respect, because he earned it. He won a championship with Denver in 2023. He brings toughness and versatility. Mike Brown called his on-ball defense "textbook." Ian Begley reported strong support for him inside the organization. Jalen Brunson said he brings a lot that can help. The Knicks reportedly wanted to keep him.

But the only way to do that was to open another roster spot — and that meant moving either Dadiet or Tyler Kolek. Moving Dadiet would likely have required a salary dump, costing the Knicks draft assets just to get rid of his contract. And Kolek is one of the ball-handlers the coaching staff likes to have available. Losing either one would have created a ripple effect far beyond the last seat on the bench.

So Bruce Brown is now an unrestricted free agent, free to sign anywhere. And honestly? He'll land somewhere quickly. A champion who can defend multiple positions doesn't stay unsigned for long. On this channel, we wish him nothing but the best.

## WHY THIS DECISION IS SMARTER THAN IT LOOKS: THE TRADE WINDOW

Here's the part most fans miss — and it's the real reason this move matters.

Under NBA rules, players who sign as free agents can't be traded until three months after signing, or December 15th — whichever comes later. That means most of the players the Knicks signed this offseason are frozen in place until mid-December.

So who CAN the Knicks trade right now? According to the reporting, only three non-starters: Pacôme Dadiet, Tyler Kolek, and Deuce McBride. That's it.

Now imagine the Knicks had moved Dadiet or Kolek to make room for Bruce Brown. That list would have shrunk from three to two. And Brown himself couldn't have been traded for months. In other words, the Knicks would have locked themselves into two months of doing almost nothing on the trade market — right as the season starts and their weaknesses start to show.

By keeping Dadiet and Kolek, Leon Rose kept every early-season door open.

## THE SALARY MATH THAT MAKES DADIET THE KEY

And here's where the 6-foot-8 guard becomes the most interesting contract on the roster.

Dadiet earns roughly $3 million this season. Bruce Brown would have counted about $2.5 million on the books. That half-million difference sounds tiny. But for a team living right up against the second apron, it's huge — because teams above or near that line can't take back more salary in a trade than they send out. Every dollar going out determines how much can come back.

**The Diabaté scenario.** According to Daily Knicks, Dadiet's salary is enough — paired with draft compensation — to make a deal work for a player like Moussa Diabaté, the Charlotte big man linked to New York for months. Kolek's salary, which sits under $2.3 million, wouldn't get the job done on its own. Neither would Brown's.

**The consolidation scenario.** Combine Dadiet's salary with Deuce McBride's, and the Knicks could bring back a player making just over $6.9 million. With Brown in Dadiet's place, that ceiling would've dropped to about $6.4 million. And since Brown couldn't have been traded right away, the Knicks would've been stuck pairing Deuce with Kolek — which only reaches about $6.25 million.

So by keeping Dadiet, the Knicks preserved the biggest possible trade package they can build before December 15th. That's the surprise. Not a new player — a contract that unlocks options.

## THE "CHANGE EVERYTHING" PART

So how could a 6-foot-8 guard who's barely played change everything? Not necessarily by what he does on the court — but by what his contract makes possible.

Think about the Knicks' biggest weaknesses heading into the season. The center rotation behind Towns is still thin — Drummond and a 25-year-old Wiseman who's played very little over the last two years. Deuce is heading into a contract year after an uneven preseason. And the team has almost no breathing room under the apron. If any of those issues flares up in November, Dadiet's contract — alone or paired with Deuce's — is the tool that could bring in a rotation big like Diabaté, or another player making up to about $6.9 million.

And don't forget: Dadiet is still only 21. He's been buried on this roster, but he got extended run alongside the starters in the preseason finale. Maybe he's a trade chip. Maybe he finally earns minutes. Either way, the Knicks kept their options open — and that's exactly what a smart front office does.

## WILL A TRADE HAPPEN SOON?

Let's keep expectations realistic. None of this guarantees a trade is coming soon. Most teams wait until mid-December, when a much larger group of players becomes eligible to be moved — and the Knicks could easily follow that same pattern. The Knicks made no announcement about their trade plans, and they didn't need to. The decision itself sends the message: this is a front office that won a championship in June and still wants to keep every door open as it tries to do it again.

## WISEMAN'S FRESH START

And let's take a moment for the winner of the camp battle. James Wiseman is 25 years old. He spent the previous two seasons in the Indiana Pacers organization, where an Achilles tear derailed everything. Now he gets a fresh start with a contender, a clear role as the third center, and a head coach who's reportedly a fan of his game — Mike Brown, who worked with him in Golden State.

His preseason highlight was an 18-point, 10-rebound night in Toronto. His job now is simple: be ready when KAT gets in foul trouble, when Drummond needs rest, and when the schedule gets heavy. Run the floor. Catch lobs. Protect the rim. Give Jalen Brunson a rim-runner like the one he lost when Mitchell Robinson went to Boston.

## THE CISSÉ TWIST — AND A CORRECTION

Now, while the Knicks finalized their roster, a story from this summer came roaring back — and it involves Dallas.

According to Jake Fischer of The Stein Line, Moussa Cissé is now at risk of losing his spot as the Mavericks try to keep veteran big man Dwight Powell on their 15-man roster. Remember the history: this summer, the Knicks signed Cissé to an offer sheet — a modest two-year deal worth about $4.7 million total. Dallas matched. Then Dallas kept him past October 1st, which made his salary fully guaranteed. If they waive him now, they'll be paying a player to leave just months after going out of their way to keep him.

**And here's the correction.** In our Cissé video last week, we laid out a five-step path for Cissé to end up in New York if Dallas released him. According to this latest reporting, that door is actually closed: because the Knicks signed him to an offer sheet that Dallas matched, NBA rules prevent the Knicks from acquiring him for one full year. Whether the Mavericks waive him or trade him, New York reportedly can't be the team that picks him up. So I want to own that — the Cissé-to-New-York scenario we described is off the table this season. Thank you to everyone who keeps this channel accurate.

## DID DALLAS MATCH JUST TO BLOCK THE KNICKS?

Which raises an uncomfortable question: did Dallas match the offer sheet just to keep Cissé away from the Knicks?

Here's how restricted free agency works. When a player signs an offer sheet, his current team has the right to match — but matching is a choice, not an obligation. Dallas could have let Cissé walk to New York and saved a roster spot. Instead, they absorbed his deal even though they already had several bigs, and it was never clear how the 24-year-old fit their plans. Now his spot is in danger. It's fair to wonder if the decision was about something other than basketball.

And there's plenty of history to fuel the theory. In 2022, the Knicks signed Jalen Brunson away from Dallas in free agency — one of the most lopsided outcomes in recent NBA history. Brunson became the face of the franchise and, last June, the NBA Finals MVP as the Knicks won their first title in 53 years. That's not the kind of loss a franchise easily forgets.

To be fair, the Mavericks look very different now. Mark Cuban is no longer the owner, and Nico Harrison is no longer running basketball operations. Patrick Dumont owns the team, and Masai Ujiri has taken over the front office. But as Daily Knicks pointed out, someone inside that organization was likely around for the Brunson departure.

It might not even be about Brunson. It could be about Jason Kidd. Before the Knicks hired Mike Brown in the summer of 2025, New York aggressively pursued Kidd, who was coaching Dallas at the time. The Mavericks responded by giving Kidd a contract extension — and later, after Ujiri arrived, Dumont moved on from Kidd anyway, meaning Dallas paid for a coach it didn't keep.

And then there's Ujiri himself, whose relationship with the Knicks has never been warm — remember, when the Knicks filed a lawsuit accusing the Raptors of using stolen team secrets, Ujiri brushed it off with open sarcasm.

None of this proves Dallas matched out of spite. There are real basketball reasons to keep a young center on a cheap contract, and plans change after training camp. But the chain of events makes the theory hard to ignore.

## WHY KNICKS FANS CAN SMILE ABOUT IT

For the Knicks, the stakes are low. Missing out on Cissé won't decide whether New York repeats. Cissé would have been a natural backup big, filling exactly the role the Knicks spent all of camp trying to settle — but they found their answer in Wiseman.

And there's a lighter side to this. Nearly half a decade after the Brunson signing, after changes in ownership, the front office, and the coaching staff, the Knicks might still be on the Mavericks' minds. That's a reminder of how far this franchise has come. Back in 2022, New York was a team trying to convince a star to believe in its project. Today, it's the team everyone else measures itself against.

## ONE MORE DEADLINE: TOMORROW

And don't forget — tomorrow, Monday, October 19th, is Karl-Anthony Towns' player option deadline. He has to decide whether to decline his $61 million option for next season. Declining keeps in-season extension talks possible but makes him an expiring contract; keeping it locks him in for next year and cools the trade talk. With the roster now set and the trade window narrowed to three movable non-starters, KAT's choice will shape this front office's entire approach to the season.

## WHAT THE ROTATION LOOKS LIKE NOW

With the roster locked, let's sketch the picture heading into Tuesday.

**The starters:** Jalen Brunson, Mikal Bridges, OG Anunoby, Josh Hart, and Karl-Anthony Towns — the same five that won the title, the same five Mike Brown named in front of the whole camp as the guys who are "going to play." Hart's sore right ankle kept him out of the finale, but it doesn't appear serious, and he had nearly five full days to rest.

**The core bench:** Deuce McBride, Landry Shamet, Jordan Clarkson, Jose Alvarado, and Andre Drummond. Shamet was one of the best performers of the preseason. Deuce closed the finale with three straight threes. Alvarado needs to find his rhythm — and the fix might be more minutes next to KAT. Drummond has been better than advertised.

**The development and depth group:** Tyler Kolek, Pacôme Dadiet, Mohamed Diawara, and now James Wiseman. Kolek hit the shot that won the finale. Diawara started that game in Hart's place. Dadiet got extended run with the core. Wiseman is the third center.

**The 15th spot:** Empty — and it's likely to stay that way, because of the second apron. That's the one opening that a trade involving Dadiet's salary could create room around later.

## THE TWO-WAY QUESTION STILL STANDS

One more note: waiving Bruce Brown settles the standard roster, but it doesn't settle the two-way spots. Those are a separate decision, and they don't count against the salary cap. The Knicks' three two-way players — Tyler Nickel, Kevin McCullar Jr., and Jaden Akins — are all perimeter players. With Eubanks and Bradley gone, there's still a case for adding a two-way big for insurance. Former Knick Trey Jemison was waived by Toronto this week and still has two-way eligibility, though Philadelphia, Sacramento, and the Lakers could also be in the mix. If the Knicks want a fourth center for an 82-game grind, that's the path that doesn't touch the apron.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. Cutting Bruce Brown hurts in the short term — he would've helped this bench. But keeping Dadiet and Kolek was the smart, long-game decision. Wiseman earned the spot. And I think Dadiet's contract gets used before the trade deadline — possibly paired with Deuce or with second-round picks — to land a rotation big. Not this week. Maybe not until December. But Leon Rose just made sure that when the moment comes, he'll have the tools ready.
