## WHAT THE KNICKS ACTUALLY "CONFIRMED"

Let's start with the facts, the NBA Tunnel way. The news comes from Shams Charania. The Knicks plan to decline Pacôme Dadiet's fourth-year team option for the 2027–28 season, worth about $5.37 million. The deadline for rookie-scale option decisions is October 31st, so this lands right on time.

It's a real decision and a real report. But "shocks everyone"? Not quite. Back in July, Knicks outlets were already writing that there was basically no way New York would pick up this option. The surprise isn't the decision. It's the timing. It came now, before he was traded, which a lot of people expected to happen first.

## MYTH VS. FACT

There's a lot of noise out there, so let's clear it up.

**MYTH: "Dadiet is off the team."**
**FACT:** He's still a Knick. Declining the option affects the 2027–28 season, not this one. He's under contract for this season at about $2.98 million and remains on the roster. Nothing changes on the court tonight.

**MYTH: "The Knicks are done with him forever."**
**FACT:** Shams noted that both sides could still discuss a reunion when he hits free agency in June. There's one catch. Under league rules, when a team declines a rookie-scale option, it can't re-sign that player for more than the declined option amount in the first year. So if he comes back, it would be at a modest number.

**MYTH: "This kills his trade value."**
**FACT:** It might actually make him easier to trade. His contract is now effectively an expiring deal, about $3 million that comes off the books next summer. For a team trying to match salaries in a deal, that's useful. We've already talked about how Dadiet's salary could pair with Deuce's expiring contract to bring back a player after December 15th. That's still on the table.

**MYTH: "The Knicks wasted a first-round pick."**
**FACT:** That's more complicated, and we'll dig in below. Dadiet signed a reduced rookie deal when he was drafted, below the normal scale for his slot. From day one, he was a cheap swing, not a franchise-defining bet.

## THE NUMBERS — HONESTLY

Let's be fair and complete. Dadiet was the 25th overall pick in 2024, a young French wing from Germany's top league. Per New York Basketball on X, his career averages are 1.6 points, 1.0 rebound, and 0.4 assists, on 32.9 percent shooting from the field and 25.5 percent from three. And this season? He's played one minute.

Those numbers aren't good. There's no way around that. The three-point shot was always the swing skill. A 6-foot-8 wing who can defend multiple spots and hit corner threes is valuable in any system. But the shot hasn't come along at the NBA level. He also dealt with an injury during Summer League, which cost him valuable development time this offseason.

Now, context matters. Those career numbers come from tiny samples, mostly garbage-time minutes on a contender. The Knicks have been winning at the highest level since he arrived, and contenders don't hand rotation minutes to raw 20-year-olds. He spent a lot of his time in Westchester. A small sample on a champion is a hard way to judge any young player.

If Dadiet becomes one of them, good for him.

## WHY THE KNICKS DID IT: THE MONEY

So why decline now? Shams pointed directly to it: salary space for next season. And with this roster, every dollar matters.

- **Karl-Anthony Towns:** Extension talks were tabled before the season. He has a big player option and wants a big number. According to Brett Siegel's reporting, his camp is looking for something in the $225 to $240 million range.
- **Jalen Brunson:** Starting next July, he's eligible for an extension projected around $286.5 million over four years.
- **Josh Hart:** He has a team option for next season, another decision on the horizon.
- **The apron:** Owner James Dolan has shown he doesn't want to go into the second apron, and the Knicks are already operating close to the line.

So about $5.4 million in 2027–28 isn't small change. It could go toward a free agent, a re-signing, or more room on one of those big extensions. Paying $5.4 million to a player outside the rotation would make that harder.

## THE TRADE THAT NEVER HAPPENED

This also connects to what happened over the offseason. There were reports last summer that the Knicks were shopping Dadiet, first in deals for other players, and later to clear room so they could keep both Malcolm Brogdon and Landry Shamet. Some Knicks podcasters have claimed that teams wanted multiple second-round picks just to take on his contract. We haven't seen that confirmed by a national insider, so take it with a grain of salt. But the bottom line is clear: no trade happened, and Dadiet stayed.

That's actually the most interesting part of this decision. Declining the option doesn't close the trade door. It may open it wider. Teams are much more willing to take on a cheap expiring contract than a player with a future team option attached. If the Knicks want to make a move after December 15th, Dadiet is now a cleaner trade piece.

## THE BIGGER STORY: THE KNICKS DON'T TRUST FIRST-ROUND PICKS

This might be the most important part of the news. New York Basketball on X pointed out a fascinating fact: Dadiet is the only first-round pick the Knicks have kept in the last five years. Think about that.

The pattern is everywhere:

- **2024:** The Knicks took Dadiet at No. 25 on a reduced deal and traded up into the second round for Tyler Kolek at No. 34.
- **2025:** They brought in Mohamed Diawara in the second round and signed him to a four-year contract.
- **2026:** They moved back from No. 24 to No. 25, took Sergio de Larrea, and immediately flipped him to Dallas for two future second-round picks and more.

The theory is simple. First-round picks come with guaranteed rookie-scale contracts, salary that eats into your cap and apron space. Second-round picks are far cheaper and more flexible. And the Knicks seem to believe their scouting is good enough to find value later in the draft.

## THE SECOND-ROUND RECEIPTS

Look at the results, and it's hard to argue with them.

**Tyler Kolek:** The 34th pick in 2024. Right now, he's the backup point guard with 21 assists and zero turnovers over three games, including that viral behind-the-back pass to KAT in Chicago.

**Mohamed Diawara:** A second-rounder who passed Dadiet in the rotation. As a rookie last season, he played more than twice as many minutes as Kolek did. He's now on a four-year deal.

**Dadiet:** The first-rounder, who has played one minute this season.

The second-rounders beat the first-rounder. That's not a knock on Dadiet. It's a sign that this front office knows what it's doing in the late picks. Leon Rose and cap expert Brock Aller have built a system: cheap contracts, smart scouting, and assets that keep the championship core together.

Is there a downside? Sure. Some fans have grown frustrated watching the Knicks trade down or out year after year, hoping to see the team develop its own young talent. If you never keep first-round picks, you never get the cheap, rising player who becomes a star on a rookie deal. That's the trade-off. But with a title banner hanging in the Garden, it's hard to argue with the results.

## WHAT A FIRST-ROUND PICK REALLY COSTS

To see why the Knicks think this way, it helps to know how rookie contracts work. A first-round pick signs a rookie-scale contract. The first two years are guaranteed, and the team holds options for years three and four. The salary is set by draft slot, so higher picks cost more. Every one of those dollars counts against the cap and the aprons.

A second-round pick is different. There's no scale. Teams can sign second-rounders to minimum deals, two-way contracts, or longer, cheaper contracts with flexible terms. That's how the Knicks locked up Diawara for four years at a low cost.

For most teams, that difference doesn't matter much. For a champion sitting near the apron, it matters a lot. A late first-round contract might cost about $1 to $2 million more per year than a minimum deal. That may not sound like much, but when you're $3.2 million under the apron, it can decide whether you can sign a 15th man or keep a veteran. The Knicks know that better than anyone. They still can't fill that 15th roster spot because of the apron.

So when the Knicks trade down or out of the first round, it's not that they don't value young talent. They value cap flexibility more, and they think they can find the talent anyway.

## WHAT THE DADIET EXPERIMENT TAUGHT THE KNICKS

Here's the thing: Dadiet was already an attempt to get around this problem. He signed that reduced rookie deal, below the normal scale for his draft slot, which basically gave the Knicks a first-round talent at a discount. It was a creative idea, and it made sense at the time.

But it didn't solve everything. The team still had to guarantee two years, still had to decide on the third-year option, and still had to find minutes for a raw prospect on a contending roster. Last year, the Knicks picked up his third-year option. Now they're declining the fourth. Even at a discount, a raw first-round prospect is hard to develop on a team chasing titles.

That might be why the 2026 draft looked the way it did. Instead of keeping de Larrea at No. 25, the Knicks turned the pick into future second-rounders. Lesson learned: a high-ceiling prospect on a guaranteed deal fits a rebuilding team better than a champion. Give those players to Dallas, and keep the picks you can use as trade currency.

## WHAT TO WATCH FROM DADIET THIS SEASON

If he stays, here's what I'll be watching. This is what would actually change his market.

- **The corner three.** It's the swing skill. If he starts hitting catch-and-shoot corner threes at a respectable rate in Westchester, teams will notice.
- **Defensive versatility.** At 6-foot-8 with real length, he can guard multiple positions. A strong G League defensive season would show he can be a 3-and-D prospect, even if the "3" comes later.
- **Health.** After the Summer League injury, staying on the floor and stacking reps is the first priority.
- **Decision-making.** Quick, simple reads, the kind of connector play that OG has been growing into. Young wings who make the right pass get minutes.

None of this is about the Knicks rotation right now. It's about his next contract. He's 21 and about to be an unrestricted free agent, and a good year can change his career path.

## WHAT DADIET'S SEASON LOOKS LIKE NOW

What happens next for him? A few scenarios, and this is my analysis.

**One: he's traded before the deadline.** Most likely, in my view. His expiring salary helps match money in a bigger deal, and the receiving team gets a free look at a young wing.

**Two: he stays and grinds in Westchester.** If no deal comes, he finishes the season with the Knicks, gets G League minutes, and auditions for his next contract. A strong G League season could earn him a real NBA opportunity next summer.

**Three: a late-season role.** Injuries happen. If the Knicks get hit at the wing, Dadiet could see a few real minutes. If the shot is falling, that changes his market.

**Four: a modest reunion.** Unlikely, but Shams left the door open. It would have to be at a number below his declined option.

Either way, this season is a showcase for Pacôme Dadiet. The pressure is on, and sometimes that's exactly what brings out a young player's best.
