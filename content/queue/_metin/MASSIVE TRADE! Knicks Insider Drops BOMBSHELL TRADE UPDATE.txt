The title says a Knicks insider dropped a bombshell trade update. Here’s what actually happened. SNY’s Ian Begley, who covers the Knicks as closely as anyone, talked through the Knicks’ trade landscape. He made some smart points about Tyler Kolek, Andre Drummond, Mikal Bridges and Karl-Anthony Towns. And at one point, he wondered out loud whether struggling New Orleans might eventually make players like Trey Murphy III and Herb Jones available. That’s it. He didn’t report that the Knicks are going after Murphy. He didn’t report any talks. The video turned a passing thought into a bombshell. So today, let’s go through what Begley actually said, what it really means, and whether a Trey Murphy trade even makes sense for New York.

Let’s start with Kolek, because that’s where Begley started. He framed it as a question. In the big picture, is Tyler Kolek solidifying his role, or is he increasing his trade value? And then he connected it to Andre Drummond. He said Drummond has been good, but he doesn’t know if Drummond is the guy who’s going to be there in April. And if that’s the case, Kolek could be part of a deal to bring in a backup center.

That’s a really sharp point, and it’s not a contradiction. Kolek has been excellent. When Jose Alvarado was out with his ankle sprain, Kolek had twenty-five assists and only two turnovers in fifty-seven minutes, according to SNY. Against Milwaukee, he scored twelve on five of six shooting with four assists in fifteen minutes. Sports Illustrated noted his assist-to-turnover ratio for the season was thirty-six to four. He’s taken over a lot of the backup point guard minutes. That makes him more important to the Knicks. But it also makes him more attractive to other teams. Both things can be true at once. The better he plays, the more the Knicks want to keep him, and the more other teams want to take him.

And here’s the history. Back in October, Marc Stein reported that the Knicks weren’t shopping Kolek, and that they’d only move him for a coveted center. So Begley’s comment fits right into what we already knew. Kolek isn’t on the block. But if the right big man becomes available, he’s one of the few young, cheap players the Knicks could use. He makes about two point three million dollars this season, with a team option for next season. Cheap, good and young is exactly what other teams want.

But let’s do the math on a Kolek trade, because it shows why he’d almost certainly be part of a bigger package. Kolek makes about two point three million dollars. Above the first apron, the Knicks can’t take back more than they send out. So Kolek alone could only bring back a player making about two point three million or less. That’s not much of a center. Even Charles Bassey of the Warriors, whose cap number is about two point four five million, would be about one hundred fifty-three thousand dollars too much for Kolek by himself. If you pair Kolek with Pacôme Dadiet’s expiring contract, you get to about five point three million. In a two-for-one, after the Knicks sign a minimum player to refill the roster, the incoming center could make somewhere between about three and a half and four and three-quarter million, depending on whether the refill is a veteran or rookie minimum. Add Miles McBride instead of Dadiet, and the number goes up a little more. So the realistic version of Begley’s idea is Kolek plus another small salary, maybe a couple of second-round picks, for a solid backup big. Not a star. A role player who protects the rim.

Now, Drummond. Begley’s line was that Drummond has been good, but he’s not sure he’s the April answer. I think that’s fair. Drummond has been better lately. Mike Brown named him the defensive player of the game in the first Cleveland win. He had fourteen rebounds in sixteen minutes against Milwaukee. His rebounding has been fantastic. But in a playoff series against a team with elite size, the Knicks may need more rim protection behind Towns. ESPN said the same thing last week: center is the clearest spot on the roster to improve.

Then Begley talked about the starters. He said if the Knicks want to trade one of their starters, Mikal Bridges is the easiest deal to make. He said Bridges has value around the league because of what he’s done over his career. Teams would take this slow start into account, but they know what he’s capable of, and that he’s played different roles.

That’s an important point, and it’s more balanced than the video makes it sound. Begley isn’t saying the Knicks will trade Bridges. He’s saying that if they ever traded a starter, Bridges would be the easiest one to move. Those are very different statements, and the difference matters. And he’s also pushing back on the idea that Bridges has no value. Teams remember that he started all nineteen playoff games last season, shot about fifty-six percent, and scored fourteen with four assists in the Finals clincher. A slump in November doesn’t erase that.

Then Begley talked about Towns, and this is the part I want to slow down on. He said Towns is playing at an extremely high level. But he said the tricky thing is the extension. If you get into January and there’s no extension, what do you do? He said if you’re the Knicks, you at least have to look at the trade scenarios for Towns and know what you could get back. Do your homework.

Now, here’s the important detail the video skips. The deadline for veteran extensions on multi-year contracts passed on October nineteenth. So the Knicks can’t simply extend Towns in January. The only way a new deal could happen during the season is if Towns declines his sixty-one million dollar player option as part of it. That’s rare. So realistically, the decision about Towns’ future happens next summer, when he decides on that option. Begley’s point is that the Knicks should be prepared. That’s just good management. Knowing your options is not the same as using them.

And the video says Towns could be a free agent this coming offseason. That’s only true if he declines his player option. He could also pick it up and stay under contract for next season at sixty-one million. Or he could decline it and sign a new long-term deal with the Knicks. The Knicks hold his Bird rights, which means they can re-sign him even though they’re over the cap. And back in October, Stefan Bondy quoted an executive who said the best outside free agent offers would likely be around forty-five to fifty million dollars a year. The Knicks’ original offer was about two hundred million over four years, so about fifty a year. So the Knicks are in a stronger position than the scary version of this story suggests.

And just look at how Towns is playing while all this talk goes on. Twenty-seven and twenty against Detroit. Twenty-two and twelve in the Cup game in Philadelphia. Twenty-nine and thirteen in Cleveland. Another double-double against Milwaukee. He’s been the most consistent Knick all season, and he’s chasing Willis Reed’s franchise record for consecutive double-doubles to start a season. A player doing that isn’t someone you trade because of a calendar date. He’s someone you find a way to pay. Begley’s point about doing your homework is fair. But homework is not a decision, and I don’t think the Knicks are anywhere near that decision.

The video also says losing Towns for nothing would be poor asset management after losing Mitchell Robinson. I understand that feeling. But Robinson left because the Knicks couldn’t pay him what Boston did, three years and about forty-seven million, without breaking James Dolan’s second-apron rule. Towns is a different situation. He’s a star, the Knicks have Bird rights, and both sides have reasons to keep talking. I’d be surprised if this ends with Towns walking away for nothing.

Now let’s talk about the Josh Hart quote, because the video twists it. The video says Hart is basically hinting that a trade is needed. That’s not what he said. Hart was talking about minutes and roles. He said there are different ways to look at it. If guys have it going, cool, good. Or you can look at it like, maybe something else has to change. And then he said there are going to be good days and bad days with that, but whatever gives the Knicks the best chance to win, that’s what matters.

That’s a player talking about a crowded rotation. Remember, Mike Brown said recently that it’s hard to find minutes for Bridges, and even for Hart at times, because Landry Shamet and Miles McBride are playing so well. Hart was responding to that reality. Change, in his answer, means rotation changes. It doesn’t mean he’s asking for a trade. The video says logic indicates he meant a trade, but that’s the video’s guess, not Hart’s words.

Now, the so-called bombshell. Begley said New Orleans is in a bad way right now. And he wondered whether, if they keep going down this path, players like Trey Murphy III and Herb Jones could become available. That’s an observation about the Pelicans. It’s not a report connecting them to the Knicks. Insiders say things like this about struggling teams all the time. It’s a heads-up about the market, not a sign of talks. If you treated every one of those comments as a trade report, the Knicks would be linked to half the league every single week.

But let’s take the idea seriously, because it’s a fun one. The video’s cleanest version is Bridges for Trey Murphy, maybe with some picks added.

Who is Trey Murphy? He’s a six-eight wing with a long wingspan. He can shoot from deep, he can defend multiple positions, and when he’s healthy, he can change a game on both ends. He’s younger than Bridges, and he’s on a long-term deal. The video says he may be hurt right now. I haven’t confirmed that, so treat it as the video’s version.

Now the math. Murphy is on a contract that pays him in the high twenties this season, which is less than Bridges’ thirty-three and a half million. Because the Knicks would be taking back less salary than they send out, that part works for New York under the first-apron rule. The harder part is New Orleans. The Pelicans would be taking on more salary this season and a contract that keeps rising through twenty twenty-nine thirty. A struggling team usually wants to cut long-term money, not add it.

And then there are the picks. The video says the Knicks might have to add first-round picks or pick swaps. Here’s the problem. The Knicks only have one tradable first-round pick, in twenty thirty-three. They owe their twenty twenty-seven, twenty twenty-nine and twenty thirty-one firsts to Brooklyn from the Bridges trade, and they also gave Brooklyn a pick swap. So the Knicks don’t have much to sweeten a deal like this. They do have about twelve second-round picks, according to ESPN. But for a player like Murphy, seconds alone usually aren’t enough.

So could a Bridges for Murphy trade work? On paper, the salaries can work for the Knicks. In reality, New Orleans would need to want Bridges, and the Knicks would need enough extra value to get the Pelicans to say yes. That’s a lot of ifs. And nobody has reported that either team is thinking about it.

The video also floats Herb Jones, maybe in a deal with Josh Hart. Jones is one of the best perimeter defenders in the league. The video says his new contract extension kicks in soon at around twenty million a year, which would be close to Hart’s salary of about twenty point nine million. I’d treat those numbers as the video’s version. But even if they line up, trading Hart would be a huge decision. Hart is part of the Villanova core with Brunson and Bridges. He’s one of the team’s toughest players and best rebounders. Trading him for another defender doesn’t obviously make the Knicks better.

And let’s remember what the Knicks’ actual need is. It’s not another wing. The Knicks already have Hart, Anunoby, Bridges, Shamet, McBride, Clarkson and Diawara. The need is a backup center. The video says Murphy’s size and wingspan check the box for adding more size. I don’t really agree. A six-eight wing is not a rim protector. If the Knicks are going to use their limited trade assets, they should be aiming at the spot ESPN and Begley both pointed to: center.

The video also makes a fair point about Leon Rose. When the Knicks needed a change a few years ago, Rose traded RJ Barrett and Immanuel Quickley to Toronto for OG Anunoby. That trade changed the franchise. So yes, Rose has shown he’ll make a big move when he thinks it’s necessary. But that move happened because the Knicks had young players and assets that Toronto wanted. Today, the Knicks have far fewer assets, a hard line under the second apron, and a championship core. Rose’s history tells you he’s willing to be bold. The current roster tells you it’s a lot harder to be bold now.

Let me also mention the Pelicans situation, because the video lists a lot of drama there. It says Dejounte Murray is on the trade block and wants to be traded, that Zion Williamson has been calling out the team, and that Herb Jones doesn’t like his role. I haven’t confirmed all of that, so treat it as the video’s version. But if New Orleans really is heading toward a sell-off, a lot of teams will be calling, and many of them will have more picks and young players than the Knicks do.

And let’s be clear about why an insider like Begley would even mention the Pelicans. It’s not because he heard something. It’s because that’s part of his job. When a team is struggling, everyone around the league starts to think about which of its players could become available. Begley is thinking out loud for Knicks fans: if New Orleans becomes a seller, here are names that would interest a lot of teams. That’s useful context. But if Begley had heard that the Knicks were calling about Trey Murphy, he would have said that directly. He didn’t.

There’s also a timing issue. The trade deadline is February eleventh. The Knicks can’t sign a new player until February fourteenth, according to ESPN, because of how little room they have under the second apron. That makes big two-for-one or three-for-one trades very hard to pull off early in the season, because the Knicks can only stay below fourteen players for two weeks at a time. So even if a Pelicans sell-off happened, the Knicks would be working with tight timing and tight math. That’s another reason a big swing like this is much more likely in the summer than at the deadline.

So where does this leave us? Let me sum up what Begley actually said, in plain terms.

On Kolek: he’s playing well, which makes him both more important and more valuable in a possible trade for a backup center.

On Drummond: he’s been good, but he may not be the answer in April.

On Bridges: if the Knicks ever trade a starter, he’s the easiest to move, and he still has real value around the league.

On Towns: he’s playing at a very high level, and the Knicks should know their options if there’s no long-term deal. But remember, the real decision point is next summer.

On New Orleans: they’re struggling, and if it continues, players like Trey Murphy and Herb Jones could become available. That’s a market observation, not a Knicks report.

And my take is this. The most realistic Knicks trade this season is still a smaller one, probably for a backup center, using pieces like Kolek, McBride, Dadiet, and second-round picks. A big move for a player like Trey Murphy would be a much bigger swing, and the Knicks don’t have many of the assets that kind of trade usually requires. If it ever happens, it’s more likely to be in the offseason than at the deadline.

The video also says that if the Knicks aren’t playing well in January, Leon Rose will make a move. That could be true. But right now, the Knicks are six and five, they’ve won six of their last eight, Brunson has been playing great, and Towns is chasing a Willis Reed record for consecutive double-doubles to start a season. This isn’t a team in crisis. It’s a team still figuring out its rotation. That’s a very different situation, and it calls for a very different response.

So what should the Knicks actually do between now and January? Keep playing the hot hands, like Brown has been doing. Keep giving Bridges time to shoot his way out of the slump. Keep watching the center market, especially once December fifteenth arrives and players like Drummond, Alvarado and Clarkson become trade-eligible. And keep the Towns conversation for the summer, when the rules actually allow a new deal. That’s not a bombshell plan. It’s just the smart one.

A more honest title would be something like, Ian Begley on Kolek, Bridges, Towns and the Pelicans: what he really said. Or, the Trey Murphy idea, explained, and why it’s harder than it sounds.
