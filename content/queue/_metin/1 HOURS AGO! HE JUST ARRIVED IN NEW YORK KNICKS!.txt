## THE TORONTO SWEEP, IN TWO GAMES

**Sunday:** The Knicks erased a 13-point Raptors lead and won 112–108 in overtime.

**Tuesday:** They erased another 13-point lead and won 111–102.

Two games, two comebacks from 13 down, two wins. The Knicks have now beaten Toronto 15 straight times, a streak that goes back almost four full years. And the Raptors have now lost five in a row, falling to 5–9.

After the back-to-back losses to Cleveland and Indiana, this is exactly the response you want. The Knicks are 8–6, and this sweep is the high point of the young season.

## TUESDAY NIGHT: THE TIMELINE

**The first half hole:** Toronto built a lead of as many as 13 in the first half. Late in the second quarter, the Knicks were down 48–35.

**The run:** Then everything changed. The Knicks closed the half on a huge run and went into the locker room up 54–50.

**The third-quarter scare:** Toronto came out firing from deep, and the Knicks trailed 68–64.

**The Alvarado answer:** Mike Brown went to Alvarado. He changed the game with pressure, playmaking, and a floater that capped a 10–2 Knicks run.

**The fourth-quarter test:** Toronto put together a 9–0 run, and Ja'Kobe Walter stole the ball from Brunson and dunked it to give the Raptors a 95–93 lead with 5:04 left.

**The finish:** The Knicks stayed calm. Brunson answered with a three-pointer with 1:25 left, the biggest shot of the night, and New York pulled away as Toronto was forced to foul. A tight game turned into a nine-point win.

## THE CAPTAIN AND THE BIG MAN

**Jalen Brunson:** A game-high 31 points, including eight in the fourth quarter and that dagger three with 1:25 left. One turnover led to the Walter dunk, and he answered it with the shot that won the game. That's who he is.

**Karl-Anthony Towns:** 16 points, 16 rebounds, and eight assists. A little bit of everything. His double-double streak to open the season — the one that already passed Willis Reed for the Knicks record — keeps rolling. And those eight assists show again how much the offense runs through him when defenses load up on Brunson.

## JOSH HART'S HIDDEN MASTERPIECE

Now, the player who actually swung this game. On the stat sheet, Josh Hart's night didn't look like much. After three quarters, he had just five points and three rebounds, the lowest totals of any Knicks starter.

But numbers don't tell the story with Hart. Look at what he did during that second-quarter run, when the Knicks turned a 48–35 hole into a 54–50 halftime lead:
- **He knocked down a three.**
- **He stole the ball and found Landry Shamet for another three.**
- **He set up Towns for a dunk.**
- **He hit a pull-up jumper right over former Knick Immanuel Quickley.**

That's the kind of stretch that changes the energy of an entire game. And it was Hart's best performance since he returned from the ankle soreness and the illness that kept him out.

Remember when Hart said after the Philly loss, "We all feel the fatigue. We have to fight through it"? This is what fighting through it looks like. He doesn't need 20 points. He needs four plays in four minutes, and that's what he gave them.

## THE DECISION: ALVARADO OVER KOLEK

Now here's the moment worth noticing. When the Knicks trailed 68–64 in the third quarter, Mike Brown didn't go to Tyler Kolek. He called on Jose Alvarado as the first guard off the bench, taking Hart's spot on the floor.

**And Alvarado made an immediate difference:**
- His defensive pressure helped force Toronto into missed threes.
- He created good looks for his teammates.
- He found Brunson for a three.
- He capped a 10–2 Knicks run with a floater.

That's Grand Theft Alvarado. Full-court pressure, deflections, chaos, and suddenly the momentum is yours.

## WHY BROWN MADE THAT CALL

This decision says a lot about where the Knicks are right now.

**The backstory:** Alvarado missed seven of the team's first 11 games because of the ankle injury and coach's decisions. During that stretch, Kolek took over the backup point guard minutes and played great. He leads the NBA in assist-to-turnover ratio among players with at least 100 minutes.

**The matchup logic:**
- **Sunday's game** was slower and more physical. Kolek's steady, organized style fit well.
- **Tuesday's game** was faster, with more scoring. Alvarado's pressure and energy were the better option.

**The bonus:** Playing Alvarado next to Brunson keeps the top scorer on the floor while taking some ball-handling work off his shoulders. That's a big deal for a player whose minutes Brown is carefully managing with that two-minute tracking data.

**The big picture:** A year ago, the Knicks didn't have a backup point guard who was truly ready for big NBA minutes. Now they have two. And Brown can pick based on the matchup.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE TRADE TALK

And this matters for everything we've been talking about. In recent videos, we've covered how Kolek's rise could make Alvarado a trade candidate after December 15th, and how SNY's Ian Begley even suggested Kolek could be part of a deal for a backup center.

Tuesday night complicates all of that. If both guards are valuable in different matchups, the Knicks have a reason to keep both. Two different tools for two different kinds of games is exactly what a contender wants in May.

That doesn't mean nobody gets traded. But it means the Knicks don't have to move one of them just to clear room. They can wait for the right deal.

## KOLEK VS. ALVARADO: A MATCHUP GUIDE

Since Brown now has two backup point guards, let's build a quick cheat sheet for which one fits which game. This is my analysis, based on how they've played.

**Go with Kolek when:**
- The game is slower and half-court heavy
- The Knicks need organization and ball security (he leads the NBA in assist-to-turnover ratio)
- The second unit needs a pass-first creator to find shooters and cutters
- KAT is on the floor and the pick-and-roll is working

**Go with Alvarado when:**
- The game is fast and high-scoring
- The Knicks need a spark on defense, with full-court pressure and deflections
- The opponent's guards are turnover-prone
- Brunson is on the floor and needs someone to take some ball-handling off him

**Play both when:**
- The Knicks need a guard-heavy, high-energy lineup to chase a lead or flip momentum

That's the luxury Mike Brown has now. In October, it looked like one of these guys might be pushed out. In November, they've become complementary pieces.

That's the kind of matchup that gives the Knicks' best action trouble. Fifteen straight wins is great, but May is different.

As one observer put it: that's what champions do. When the shots don't fall, you win with effort, rebounding, and toughness. That's how this team won a title, and that's how it's climbing back now.

## THE MIKAL BRIDGES STARTING DEBATE

Now, even after a big win, one question keeps coming back. Fourteen games into the season, the debate over whether Mikal Bridges should stay in the starting lineup is getting louder.

**The fan side:** More and more fans want a change, pointing to his struggles over the first weeks of the season.

**Mike Brown's side:** So far, he hasn't given in. He's made it clear he sees that kind of move as a panic decision, and he doesn't want to make one this early in a title defense.

## THE JOSH HART PRECEDENT

But there's a recent example that shows Brown will change his mind when the situation calls for it, and that example is Josh Hart.

**Last season's debate:** Many people believed Hart should be in the starting five. He was playing well next to Brunson, Towns, and OG Anunoby, and the group looked strong whenever it shared the floor.

**Brown waited:** It wasn't until the end of November that he finally made the switch.

**The reason matters:** Brown didn't change the lineup only because fans asked for it. Part of the decision came from adversity, since Anunoby was dealing with hamstring issues at the time. The staff saw a problem and adjusted.

That pattern shows the Knicks aren't stuck in one plan. When something goes wrong, they respond.

## WHAT'S DIFFERENT THIS TIME

**No injury trigger.** This time, there's no injury forcing the issue. It's purely about performance. Bridges hasn't played at his usual level, and the pressure grows with every quiet night.

**A different kind of decision.** Unlike last year, Brown would be making a change based only on how a player is performing. That's harder, especially with a player the Knicks gave up five first-round picks to get.

**The middle ground Brown is already using:** The Knicks have kept Bridges in the starting lineup, but they haven't kept him on the floor when games are decided. Brown has benched him in several fourth quarters this season. That's a quiet compromise: he keeps the starter's role and protects his confidence, while the closing lineup goes to whoever's hot.

## MY TAKE ON BRIDGES

Here's where I land, and this is my analysis.

**Keep him starting for now.** Brown is right that an early lineup change can look like panic, and it can shake a player's confidence even more. The current system, starting Bridges and closing with the hot hands, is working. The Knicks just won two straight.

**But set a soft deadline.** Last year, Brown made the Hart change at the end of November. If Bridges is still struggling by then, a move to the bench, even a temporary one, becomes reasonable. Think of it like a reset, not a demotion.

**Who would replace him?** The most natural option might be Shamet, who's been excellent as a fill-in starter and posted a plus-26 in Toronto. Or Deuce McBride, if Brown wants more defense.

**What Bridges needs to do:** Brown has already told him. Lean into his strengths: running, cutting, rebounding, and driving. The jump shot will follow.

## WHY THE FOURTH-QUARTER BENCHINGS MATTER

Let's take one more second on that fourth-quarter detail, because it's the most important clue in the whole Bridges debate. Starting lineups get the headlines. Closing lineups win games. And Brown has shown he'll close with whoever is playing best that night, whether it's Shamet, Deuce, Hart, or Alvarado.

That means the starting spot is almost symbolic right now. Bridges keeps the title, the routine, and the respect. But the minutes that matter go to whoever earns them. If Bridges wants those closing minutes back, the path is clear: defend at a high level, attack the rim, and make the simple plays. Once he does, Brown has every reason to put him back on the floor when it counts.
