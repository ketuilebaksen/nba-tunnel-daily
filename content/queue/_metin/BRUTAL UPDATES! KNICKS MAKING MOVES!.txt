THE SIGNING: IGOR MILIČIĆ JR. TO AN EXHIBIT 10

The news, via Stefan Bondy of the New York Post and Polish outlet Interia Sport: the Knicks have signed free agent forward Igor Miličić Jr. to an Exhibit 10 — the classic training camp deal we've been predicting would start flying this month. The résumé: 6'10", 225 pounds, just 24 years old, a member of the Polish national team, and a genuinely well-traveled basketball education — one year at Virginia, two at Charlotte, and a senior season at Tennessee where he started 37 games and produced 9.4 points, 6.7 rebounds, and 1.9 assists in 25 minutes a night for one of the best programs in America.

Then came the pro path — and here's where our rivalry subplot kicks in. Miličić went undrafted in 2025 and signed an Exhibit 10 with the SIXERS, who waived him before the season and stashed him with the Delaware Blue Coats, their G League affiliate: 45 games, 6.9 points, 5 boards in about 19 minutes a night on a 40/28/76 shooting line. Modest numbers — G League development numbers. And now, after Philadelphia spent this offseason raiding OUR roster — Hukporti, Dillon Jones — the champs just reached into THEIR developmental system and walked out with the most intriguing young big they had. Petty? Maybe a little. Satisfying? Absolutely.

WHY HE CHOSE NEW YORK — AND WHY THAT MATTERS

Now the detail I love most in this story: out of every team he could have signed with, Miličić chose the Knicks — and per the reporting, the reasons were strategic. One: New York's THREE open two-way slots, the exact vacancies we've been mapping for weeks. Every other contender has their two-ways filled; the champs deliberately kept the shelf empty, and now that patience is functioning as a RECRUITING TOOL. A young player looks at New York and sees something no other roster offers: real, winnable jobs. Two: he sees it as the best developmental opportunity for his career — and honestly, who can argue? The organization that turned Diawara from a late second-rounder into a trusted piece, that built Hukporti into a player Philly paid for, that runs the Westchester lab we just covered with the Mascari trade... that's where a hungry 24-year-old bets on himself. The pipeline reputation is now doing the front office's work for it. That's what winning a championship with homegrown development buys you.

And the timing of his audition tape could not be better. Playing for Poland in the World Cup qualifiers just weeks ago, Miličić went for 18 points and 8 rebounds against GERMANY — a legitimate international power — then followed it with 13 points, 6 assists, and 4 steals against Israel. Those aren't empty-gym numbers; that's production against grown men in meaningful international basketball, with his father — a longtime professional coach overseas — having drilled the IQ into him since childhood. The kid arrives sharp, confident, and battle-tested.

And appreciate the basketball bloodline for a second, because it shapes everything about how this kid plays. His father, Igor Miličić Sr., is a respected professional coach in Europe — the man currently steering the Polish national program — which means Junior didn't just grow up around basketball; he grew up inside film sessions, scouting reports, and professional locker rooms. That's why the IQ pops off the screen: the early shot-fake reads, the timing on cuts, the willingness to make the extra pass. Coaches' sons process the game differently — they see it from the clipboard side before they ever star in it. It's the same quality that made Brunson, another coach's son, the smartest player of his generation. New York clearly has a type.

THE SCOUTING FILE: WHAT HE DOES WELL

Let's do the honest breakdown, because this profile is genuinely fun. Start with the calling card: TRANSITION. At 6'10", Miličić gallops the floor faster than almost any opposing big — an elite lane-filler who can even handle the ball himself on the break. For a Mike Brown team that wants to run off every rebound, that's instant utility. Then the brain: high basketball IQ, refined by a lifetime around professional coaching. He passes exceptionally well for his size, especially in the open floor, with real court vision. His off-ball movement is advanced — great timing on backdoor cuts, clever baseline hesitations that generate point-blank looks. And unlike traditional bigs, he attacks closeouts comfortably: catch, shot-fake, one dribble, into the teeth of the defense. Add rotational rim protection and — the skill that probably sealed this signing — legitimate REBOUNDING instincts, and you have a Swiss Army forward whose toolkit checks nearly every box on the front office's stated wishlist: size, speed, versatility, glass work.

THE SCOUTING FILE: THE HONEST FLAWS

Now the other side, because we don't do puff pieces here. The issues live on defense, and they're specific. One: perimeter lateral speed. He's agile FOR A BIG, but isolated on an island against elite NBA guards in the pick-and-roll, he can get blown by — that's the gap between international bigs and NBA speed, and it's real. Two: post strength. At 225 pounds he can struggle to hold ground against true NBA bulk; stronger centers will move him. Three — and this is the most correctable one: closeout discipline. The kid BITES on pump fakes. Sees a shot coming, leaps at it, and suddenly his man is open or he's out of position chasing a rebound that hasn't happened yet. He'll even abandon his own assignment to contest a nearby shooter — admirable instinct, terrible geometry. Teams that scout him will hunt exactly that. But here's the context that keeps me optimistic: every one of these flaws is either physical development (strength comes with NBA weight rooms) or habit correction (closeout discipline is COACHING) — and he's 24, in the best development program in basketball. If he were flawless, he wouldn't be available on an Exhibit 10. The flaws are the price tag. The tools are the bet.

And don't dismiss the "waived by Philly" line as a red flag — read it as a data point about ROLES. The Sixers cut him last fall because their books were a mess and their camp was a circus; the Blue Coats then gave him 19 minutes a night in a system designed around other prospects. None of that measures what he is; it measures where he was. The G League is full of players whose numbers say "meh" because their role said "wait your turn." What the Knicks' evaluators clearly weighed instead was the full body of evidence: the SEC production at Tennessee, the international performances against real competition, the physical tools you can't teach. When development staffs trust their own eyes over another team's depth chart, that's when steals happen — ask everyone who passed on the undrafted guys now earning NBA paychecks.

Picture where he'd actually slot if the development hits, too. The frontcourt behind the stars is exactly where this roster is thinnest long-term: Diawara owns the young-forward runway, but behind him? Air. A 6'10" transition weapon who can pass, cut, and rebound is the perfect complement to that room — Diawara the defensive tone-setter, Miličić the connective playmaker, two 24-and-under bigs developing side by side on sub-minimum money while the champs chase banners above them. That's how sustainable contenders are built in the apron era, and it's why a "minor" Exhibit 10 headline deserves thirteen minutes of your day.
