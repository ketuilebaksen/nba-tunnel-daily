## THE HONEST HEADLINE

Quick clarity, the NBA Tunnel way. There's no trade, and no reported talks between the Knicks and the Bucks. What we have are two real pieces of reporting — Siegel on the Bucks and Clippers being open for business, and Begley on Kolek and the center spot — plus a lot of fan and analyst speculation about Kel'el Ware. Important detail: Siegel's report did not mention Ware by name. So connecting Ware to the Knicks is speculation. We'll keep that line clear.

## THE CONTEXT: THE KNICKS STILL WANT A CENTER

Start with the need. Coming off that frustrating 106–104 loss in Indiana — the one where the Pacers grabbed 17 offensive rebounds and KAT's buzzer-beater rimmed out — the backup center question is louder than ever.

Andre Drummond has done a solid job in a lot of ways. He leads the NBA in offensive rebounds per 36 minutes among players with 100-plus minutes, and Mike Brown has praised his improving pick-and-roll defense. But there are nights when the defense isn't there and the offense isn't there. James Wiseman has barely played — 26 minutes in 12 games.

And here's the key reporting, from Ian Begley:

"If you just look at the landscape and Andre Drummond and where things are — look, Drummond has been good, but I don't know if he's going to be the guy who's going to be there come April. If that's the case, Kolek could be in some kind of deal to bring in a backup center."

That's a significant comment from one of the most plugged-in Knicks reporters out there. It tells us two things. First, the Knicks are still looking at the center position — something they've tried to upgrade since the offseason, even after signing Drummond. Second, Tyler Kolek might be part of the answer — not by playing center, obviously, but as a trade piece.

## BEGLEY'S BIG QUESTION: SOLIDIFYING OR SELLING HIGH?

So let's sit with Begley's question for a second: is Kolek solidifying his role, or increasing his trade value? Honestly, the answer might be both.

**The case for "solidifying":**
- Among players with at least 100 minutes, Kolek leads the NBA in assist-to-turnover ratio.
- The Knicks have been dramatically better with him on the floor — a net rating around plus-27 in his minutes, compared to around plus-1 without him, per the numbers we covered recently.
- He's taken the backup point guard job from Jose Alvarado.
- He's cheap — under $2.3 million this season, with a roughly $2.2 million club option for 2027–28.

**The case for "trade value":**
- A cheap, efficient young guard is exactly what rebuilding teams want.
- He's reportedly drawn interest from teams including Milwaukee — where he played his college ball at Marquette.
- He's not super athletic and lacks size, which caps his ceiling in some people's eyes.
- The Knicks are deep at guard — Brunson, Deuce McBride, Landry Shamet, Jordan Clarkson, and Alvarado.

**My read:** Kolek is more valuable to the Knicks than to anyone else — but he's also one of the only cheap, appealing pieces they have. If the right big becomes available, he's going to be in the conversation.

## THE SIEGEL REPORT: MILWAUKEE AND LA ARE OPEN

Here's the full picture from Brett Siegel:
- **The Clippers and Bucks** are expected to be open to hearing offers on virtually any of their players entering the deadline.
- **Milwaukee** has been trying to move **Myles Turner** and **Kyle Kuzma** since the offseason started.
- **Milwaukee** has *not* been receptive to calls about **Jaime Jaquez Jr.** or **Ryan Rollins**.

Remember the context. The Bucks are in their first season after trading Giannis Antetokounmpo, and they've had a brutal start — they were 1–10 when the Knicks beat them 117–85 at the Garden. They're clearly in rebuild mode, looking for picks and young players.

## THE PHONE CALL SIMULATOR

So let's imagine Leon Rose dials Milwaukee. What does each part of that call sound like?

## ASK #1: "WHAT ABOUT MYLES TURNER?"

**The pitch:** He's an experienced, shot-blocking, floor-spacing center — exactly the profile the Knicks want behind KAT, or next to him.

**Milwaukee's answer:** "Sure, he's available. We've been trying to move him since the summer."

**The problem:** The money. Turner is on a big contract. And the Knicks have only about $89,000 of room under their hard spending line, according to ESPN's Bobby Marks — they can't take back more salary than they send out. To match Turner's salary, they'd need to send out a starter-level contract.

**And the fit concern:** The night the Knicks beat Milwaukee, Turner was a non-factor, and KAT dominated him. Fans watching that game didn't see a player who'd move the needle.

**Verdict:** ❌ It just doesn't work financially.

## ASK #2: "WHAT ABOUT KYLE KUZMA?"

**The pitch:** A versatile forward who could add size and scoring on the wing.

**Milwaukee's answer:** "Available. We've been shopping him."

**The problem:** Same as Turner — a big contract, and the Knicks can't absorb it. Plus, he doesn't solve the center problem.

**Verdict:** ❌ Wrong position, wrong price, wrong timing.

## ASK #3: "WHAT ABOUT JAIME JAQUEZ JR. OR RYAN ROLLINS?"

**Milwaukee's answer:** "Not interested. We're keeping them."

That's straight from the reporting. And it's a really important clue for the next ask.

**Verdict:** ❌ Completely off the table for now.

## ASK #4: "WHAT ABOUT KEL'EL WARE?"

And now the one everyone's excited about.

**Why fans love the idea:** Ware is young, still developing, and checks a lot of boxes for New York. He blocks shots, protects the rim, and has shown he can step out and hit threes. That's exactly the profile the Knicks were reportedly looking for all offseason after Mitchell Robinson left: young, athletic, and up-and-coming.

**The lineup dream:** Imagine the options:
- A double-big lineup with KAT and Ware
- Ware and Drummond together in bigger matchups
- Or simply: when KAT sits, Ware comes in — a similar skill set, so the offense doesn't have to change

**The "change of scenery" argument:** Some fans will say Ware hasn't lived up to expectations. Maybe. But a young player on a rebuilding team in his situation isn't always showing his best. Put him on a contender in New York with a defined role, and he could grow.

**Milwaukee's likely answer:** "Probably not."

Here's why. Remember the clue from Ask #3? Milwaukee isn't even taking calls on Jaquez or Rollins — their young pieces. A young, cheap, developing big man is exactly the type of player a rebuilding team wants to keep, not sell. And Siegel's report didn't single out Ware as available. "Virtually any player" is a general statement, not a price tag.

**And if they would listen?** The Knicks would have to send real value. Fans have floated packages including some combination of:
- Second-round picks — the Knicks have 12 of them
- Pacôme Dadiet's expiring contract
- Deuce McBride
- Tyler Kolek
- Jose Alvarado, after December 15th

That's where Begley's Kolek comment becomes really interesting.

**Verdict:** 🟡 Worth a phone call. Very unlikely to happen — but if Milwaukee ever changes its mind, the Knicks should be first in line.

## ASK #5: "OKAY — WHAT ABOUT THE CLIPPERS?"

Remember, Siegel said the Clippers are open for business too. And we've already covered the Clippers angle on this channel:
- **Yanic Konan Niederhäuser:** a young, 6-foot-11 rim-runner on a sub-$3 million deal with team options. Analysts have floated him as a fit, and his dunk rate mirrors what Mitchell Robinson gave the Knicks.
- **Max Strus and Derrick Jones Jr.:** the "two weapons" from a Bleacher Report proposal for Mikal Bridges.

The Clippers' draft future is badly compromised, which gives them a reason to sell. And Niederhäuser's salary is the kind that could match with Dadiet's roughly $3 million.

**Verdict:** 🟡 More realistic than Ware, at least on paper. Still no reported talks.

## THE CAP MATH, ONE MORE TIME

Here's why all of this comes back to the same place. With only about $89,000 of room:
- The Knicks **can't sign** anyone new until around February 14th, when prorated minimum contracts become small enough to fit.
- They **can't take back more salary** than they send out in any trade.
- They **can't sign** waived players who were earning $15 million or more.

So realistic trade targets are players earning a few million dollars at most, unless the Knicks send out a big contract. That's why the Turner and Kuzma asks fail immediately — and why young, cheap bigs like Ware and Niederhäuser keep coming up.

## WHAT A REALISTIC KNICKS OFFER LOOKS LIKE

If I'm Leon Rose, here's the type of package I'd put together for a young big — my analysis, not reporting:

**Package A — the small one:** Dadiet plus two or three second-round picks. That matches salary with a cheap big and costs the rotation nothing.

**Package B — the medium one:** Dadiet plus Kolek plus seconds. More attractive to a rebuilding team, because Kolek is a young, cheap, productive guard. But it costs the Knicks their best backup point guard.

**Package C — the big one:** Add Deuce or, after December 15th, Alvarado. Now you're giving up real rotation depth.

**My preference:** Start with Package A. Only go to Package B if the big man is clearly better than what the Knicks have. Avoid Package C unless it's a game-changing player.

## THE KOLEK DILEMMA, UP CLOSE

Let's slow down on the Kolek part, because it's the most painful piece of any realistic deal.

**What the Knicks would lose:** The backup point guard who leads the NBA in assist-to-turnover ratio. The guy who ran a lineup without Brunson or Towns against Milwaukee and won his minutes. The player who had 12 points on five-of-six shooting, four assists, a block, and a plus-23 in just 15 minutes in that Bucks game. A cheap contract that's a bargain through next season if they pick up his option.

**What happens to the rotation:** If Kolek goes, Alvarado likely becomes the backup point guard again. That's not a disaster — Alvarado was a key part of the title team, and his full-court pressure is real. But the offense loses its most organized bench playmaker. Clarkson can handle the ball in spurts, and Deuce can defend, but neither runs a second unit the way Kolek does.

**The trade-off:** Is a young backup center worth more to the Knicks than a young backup point guard? For the playoffs, maybe. Rim protection matters a lot in May. But you'd be solving one problem by reopening another.

**My view:** If the Knicks trade Kolek, it has to be for a big who's clearly better than Drummond — not just younger. Anything less, and it's a lateral move.

## THE ALVARADO ALTERNATIVE

And here's the twist. Instead of Kolek, what about Alvarado?

- He's on a three-year, roughly $14 million deal — more salary to match, which could open the door to a slightly better player.
- He becomes trade-eligible on December 15th.
- Kolek has taken his backup point guard role, and Alvarado didn't play at all in the first Cleveland game.

For a rebuilding team, Alvarado is less appealing than Kolek — he's older and more expensive. But for the Knicks, moving Alvarado costs less on the court. That's why some analysts see Alvarado, not Kolek, as the more likely trade piece. Begley's comment about Kolek is a reminder that the front office is open to both.

## WHY THIS MATTERS RIGHT NOW

Let's tie it back to the Pacers loss. Indiana — one of the worst rebounding teams in the league — beat the Knicks on the glass and at the free-throw line. Cleveland took 25 free throws to New York's 11 the game before. The Knicks have been playing smaller than they are.

Josh Hart's return will help. More two-big lineups with KAT and Drummond could help. But if the Knicks want a real answer for April, May, and June — when the toughest frontcourts in the East show up — a young rim protector would make a big difference. That's why the front office is still looking, and why Begley says Kolek could be the price.

## THE OTHER OPTION: DO NOTHING (FOR NOW)

To be fair, there's also a case for patience.

- **Drummond is improving.** His pick-and-roll defense has gotten better every week, according to Mike Brown.
- **Wiseman has flashed.** He grabs more offensive rebounds per 36 minutes than anyone on the roster.
- **The free-agent route.** We talked about Lawson Lovering, the 23-year-old 7-footer the Lakers waived. A two-way signing like that costs nothing in salary.
- **The calendar.** Most trades won't happen until after December 15th anyway, and the best deals often come closer to the February deadline, when sellers get desperate.

So the Knicks don't have to panic. They have time to make the right call — literally.

And there's one more reason to be patient: the market will only get bigger. Right now, the Bucks and Clippers are the teams openly listening. By January, more teams will fall out of the race, and more useful bigs will hit the market. Teams that move too early often overpay. Teams that wait for the right seller — and have flexible pieces like Dadiet's expiring deal and a stack of second-round picks — usually get better value. The Knicks are built to be that second kind of team.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. Leon Rose calls Milwaukee — every good front office checks in on a team that says everything is available. But Kel'el Ware doesn't become a Knick. The Bucks keep their young pieces, just like they're doing with Jaquez and Rollins. The Knicks' eventual frontcourt move is smaller — Dadiet plus second-round picks for a cheap big, maybe from the Clippers. Kolek stays a Knick through the deadline, because his value to New York is bigger than his value in a trade. And Drummond is still in the rotation in April — maybe not as the starter's only backup, but still playing.

Here's where it stands. There's no trade and no reported talks with Milwaukee.
