## THE HONEST HEADLINE

Quick clarity, the NBA Tunnel way. The Knicks haven't acquired a center. Ian Begley named two names to watch, but he also said, "Are they looking to upgrade at the backup center position? I don't know." So this is monitoring, not negotiating. Let's go through it properly.

## STOCK REPORT: THE TRADE MARKET

### 📈 JAKOB POELTL — THE TRADITIONAL ANSWER

**What Begley said:** "I've heard that Jakob Poeltl has drawn interest."

**What he brings:** The veteran center offers rebounding, screening, and interior defense — qualities the Knicks could use behind Karl-Anthony Towns. In other words, he's the closest thing on the market to a Mitchell Robinson replacement.

**Why he fits:** Remember the Favale analysis we covered last video. When Brunson plays without Towns, the Knicks' offense drops to about 1.12 points per possession, partly because the backup center spot hasn't provided good screening or finishing. Poeltl is one of the best screeners in the league. That's exactly the kind of help Brunson needs.

**The catch:** Poeltl is a starting-caliber center on a significant contract. With only about $89,000 of room under the second apron, according to ESPN's Bobby Marks, the Knicks would have to send out a lot of salary to match. That's not a Dadiet-plus-picks deal. And the "drawn interest" line means the Knicks wouldn't be the only team calling.

### 📈 MORITZ WAGNER — THE MODERN ANSWER

**What Begley said:** "I'm sure that they will get phone calls on Wagner." Phone calls, plural. The Knicks wouldn't be the only team asking.

**What he brings:** The Brooklyn veteran can stretch the floor and give the Knicks another scoring option off the bench. His ability to space the court could complement New York's perimeter-heavy offense, although his defensive limitations would need consideration.

**Why he fits:** Again, the Favale problem. Drummond doesn't shoot threes, so defenses crowd the paint and sit in Brunson's driving lanes. A big who can pull his defender out to the arc changes that.

**The timing:** Wagner just signed with Brooklyn this offseason, so he becomes trade-eligible on December 15th — the same date as a lot of other players around the league.

### BEGLEY'S CAUTION

And here's the most honest part. Begley himself said: "I'm not saying that either of those two would be a perfect fit for the Knicks."

That's important. This is a market question, not a solution.

**The simple way to think about it:**
- **Poeltl** protects the rim. He's a Robinson-style replacement.
- **Wagner** stretches the floor. He's a Drummond upgrade on offense.

They solve completely different problems.

## STOCK REPORT: THE BACKCOURT

### 📈 JOSE ALVARADO — SOARING

Alvarado scored 21 points on 80% shooting against Cleveland and led the team in scoring as the backup point guard, including five threes. He stripped Donovan Mitchell in the fourth quarter and helped turn an 85–82 deficit into a 121–102 win. After missing seven of the team's first 11 games because of injury and coach's decisions, he's back — and better than ever.

And here's the analysis that ties it together: Alvarado has been efficient and game-changing since returning, and the Knicks keep proving their backcourt depth outweighs their frontcourt depth. Alvarado's resurgence could be exactly what helps the Knicks make a move for a more complete roster.

### 📉 DEUCE McBRIDE — FALLING

And that's where it gets uncomfortable. Deuce is a lethal shooter and a versatile defender, but his numbers and health have been trending downward.

**The before:** Last season, Deuce exploded offensively before undergoing surgery to repair a core injury. Before the injury, he was putting up 13 points in 27 minutes per game, shooting an elite 42% from three.

**The after:** In the 41 games since his return, including the playoffs, his usage and production have dropped. He's averaging about six points in 17 minutes per game in that span.

**The new concern:** Deuce was sidelined with left adductor tightness for the win over Cleveland. The New York Post's Stefan Bondy noted that the injury raises concern because of its relation to the sports hernia surgery he had this past February.

That's the part that worries people. It might be bad luck. It might be a pattern. Either way, it affects his value.

**The trade angle:** Deuce is on an expiring contract, and the Knicks may struggle to keep him next summer without crossing the second apron. With Alvarado, Kolek, Shamet, and Clarkson all in the backcourt, Deuce has become the most logical trade chip if the Knicks go after a center.

**The emotional side:** Deuce is the longest-tenured Knick. He came up through Westchester. He won a championship in New York. It's hard to imagine him being moved. But the math keeps pointing that way.

**My view:** The Knicks shouldn't trade him just because Alvarado had one big night. But if the right big man becomes available after December 15th, Deuce is the most likely piece to go. That's my analysis, not a report.

## STOCK REPORT: THE FRONTCOURT

### 📈📈 JAMES WISEMAN — BIGGEST RISER

Here's the story that might make a trade unnecessary.

**The line against Cleveland:** In about six minutes, Wiseman grabbed seven rebounds — six of them offensive — and scored six points on five shots.

**The reaction:** As Empire Sports Media put it, that's a ridiculous rate to expect anyone to repeat. But it's also a pretty good advertisement for keeping a center on the floor who makes the other team finish a stop. That's the whole value of an offensive rebounder.

**The highlight:** An athletic tip-in off a Jalen Brunson miss that was initially ruled offensive interference. After review, the basket counted.

**The energy:** Daily Knicks described him as a ball of energy on both ends of the floor — different from Drummond, who gives great effort on the glass but at times looks sluggish when moving his feet.

**The context:** Drummond left the Cleveland game with a head injury. Wiseman got the minutes, and he produced. And this comes after weeks of us asking whether Wiseman deserved a real look.

### 📉 ANDRE DRUMMOND — UNDER PRESSURE

**The situation:** Drummond is 33. Wiseman is 25. Drummond left with a head injury. And the numbers from the Favale analysis are tough: Brunson shoots 38.8% on twos with Drummond on the floor and 48.2% when he sits.

**The precedent:** Daily Knicks pointed to the Kolek-Alvarado story. Alvarado missed four games with an ankle sprain. Kolek took the job and kept it. Now Brown is using all three point guards based on matchups. Could the same thing happen at center?

**The honest read:** As Daily Knicks put it, assuming Drummond doesn't miss time, Brown probably won't drop him to third string yet. But Wednesday certainly opened up the conversation. Key word: yet.

## THE WISEMAN TRADE-OFF

Let's be fair, because there's a real trade-off. Offensive rebounds or transition defense — you can't always have both from your third-string center.

The Knicks play Miami next, and as Empire Sports Media noted, the Heat can find a mismatch quickly with Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo before a defense is set. Wiseman doesn't have to erase either one. But he does have to get back before Miami can put a smaller Knick under the rim. And he can't go hunting every offensive rebound if it means he's the last one back.

The best line from that analysis: Wednesday didn't settle how much Wiseman should play. But it made a better case that the Knicks can give him one regular shift without needing a blowout or an injury first.

One regular shift. Not a starting job — a shift.

## STOCK REPORT: THE TWO-WAYS

### 📈 TYLER NICKEL — SURPRISE RISER

And here's the one nobody expected. Tyler Nickel made his NBA debut against Cleveland, and Daily Knicks broke down how his first minute told the whole story.

**The entrance:** He checked in at the 8:33 mark of the third quarter and was immediately tasked with chasing Cavs sharpshooter Sam Merrill.

**The foul:** He picked up a foul fighting for a rebound. And as Daily Knicks put it, everyone associated with the Knicks should love that. Fouls aren't always bad. In the right circumstances, they show effort and aggression.

**The next possession:** He tracked Merrill away from the ball. Nickel was preparing for a Jarrett Allen screen when Merrill tried to beat him with a back cut — and Nickel stayed with it.

**Why it matters:** Remember what the Westchester scouting report said about Nickel: great catch-and-shoot ability, no hesitation, but he needs to improve defensively. His first NBA minute was all about defense, effort, and attention to detail. That's exactly what Mike Brown wants to see.

### ➡️ DALTON KNECHT — HOLDING

Knecht's stock isn't falling — he just dropped 35 in Westchester and had a strong NBA debut against Chicago. But the "60 seconds that put Nickel ahead of Knecht" storyline is about the same thing: defense. Knecht's shooting is NBA-ready. His defense is still the question. Nickel just showed the defensive effort that earns a coach's trust. That's a healthy competition for the Knicks' two-way minutes.

## POELTL VS. WAGNER: HEAD-TO-HEAD

Let's put the two names side by side, based on the way Begley and the analysis described them.

| | Jakob Poeltl | Moritz Wagner |
|---|---|---|
| **Style** | Traditional center | Modern stretch big |
| **Biggest strength** | Rebounding, screening, interior defense | Floor spacing, bench scoring |
| **Biggest concern** | Salary and competition from other teams | Defensive limitations |
| **Fixes which Knicks problem?** | Rim protection, Brunson's screening | Spacing for Brunson's drives |
| **Fits next to KAT?** | Yes, as a defensive anchor in big lineups | Less so, since both prefer the perimeter |
| **Trade timing** | Depends on his team's direction | Eligible Dec. 15 |

**My read:** If the Knicks are building for May and June, Poeltl's profile matters more. Playoff basketball is physical, and rim protection wins series. But if the goal is to fix the regular-season problem of Brunson playing without Towns, Wagner's floor spacing directly attacks it. The ideal answer might be a player who does a little of both — and that's why names like Karlo Matković, a 37% career three-point shooter who can also finish above the rim, keep coming up.

## WHAT A REALISTIC PACKAGE LOOKS LIKE

Let's talk about the actual mechanics, because the cap is the whole story. My analysis, not a report:

**The tools:**
- Pacôme Dadiet's roughly $3 million expiring deal
- Deuce McBride's roughly $3.9 million expiring deal
- Jose Alvarado's roughly $4.4 million deal, trade-eligible Dec. 15
- A stack of second-round picks — reportedly 12 of them
- One tradable first-round pick, in 2033

**The rule:** With about $89,000 of room, the Knicks can't take back more salary than they send out.

**What that buys:**
- **Dadiet plus seconds** gets you a minimum-level big.
- **Dadiet plus Deuce** — about $6.9 million — gets you a mid-sized backup big.
- **Dadiet, Deuce, and Alvarado** — about $11.3 million — gets you into more meaningful contracts.
- **A starting-caliber center** almost certainly requires a bigger salary, like Josh Hart's.

That's why Wagner-type players are more realistic than Poeltl-type players for this roster. It's not about talent. It's about math.

## A WORD FOR DEUCE

Before we move on, let's take a second for Deuce. Whatever happens at the deadline, he's been one of the most beloved Knicks of this era. A second-round pick who developed in Westchester, became an elite perimeter defender, hit huge shots, and won a championship. He's the longest-tenured Knick on the roster. If he's moved, it won't be because he failed. It'll be because the Knicks' financial reality and their need at center forced a tough choice. And if he stays and gets healthy, he's still a guy who can swing playoff games. Either way, respect.

## WHAT IT ALL MEANS

Put the stock report together, and here's the picture.

**The backcourt is overloaded with talent.** Brunson, Alvarado, Kolek, Shamet, Clarkson, and Deuce — plus Bridges and Hart on the wing. That depth is the Knicks' biggest trade asset.

**The frontcourt is the need.** Behind Towns, the Knicks need either a rim protector, a floor spacer, or both.

**The internal option is rising.** If Wiseman keeps producing, the Knicks might not need to trade for a center at all. Or they could make a smaller move instead of a big one.

**The external options exist.** Poeltl and Wagner are real names with real interest. But the salary math under the second apron is tricky, and other teams will be calling.

**The likely trade chip is Deuce.** Not because he's a bad player — he isn't — but because his contract is expiring, his health is a question, and the Knicks have backcourt depth to cover him.

## WHAT TO WATCH AGAINST MIAMI

The Knicks face the Heat next, and it's a big test for everything we just talked about.

- **Wiseman's minutes:** Does he get a real shift? Can he get back in transition against Giannis and Bam?
- **Drummond's status:** Does the head injury keep him out?
- **The backcourt:** With Deuce dealing with the adductor, does Alvarado keep his bigger role?
- **The streak:** Can the Knicks make it seven straight?

## MY CALL

Here's my take. Wiseman gets a regular shift going forward — even when Drummond returns, the gap between them shrinks. The Knicks make calls on both Wagner and Poeltl after December 15th, but the more realistic target is the cheaper, floor-spacing type — Wagner or a Karlo Matković-style big — because Poeltl's salary is much harder to match. Deuce becomes the centerpiece of whatever trade they make, and I think it happens before the February deadline. Alvarado keeps his bigger role. And Nickel gets a few more NBA minutes in December — his defense-first debut earned it.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands.
