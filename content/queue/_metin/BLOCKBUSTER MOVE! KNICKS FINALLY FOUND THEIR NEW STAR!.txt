## STRAIGHT TALK ABOUT THE TITLE

Clarity first, the NBA Tunnel way. There was no blockbuster move. Nobody was traded and nobody was signed. The "new star" is Tyler Kolek, a player who has been on this roster for two seasons. He's stepped into the backup point guard role and played some of the best basketball of his career. Is he a star? No, and calling him one puts unfair pressure on a young player. Is he a real breakout story the Knicks can build on? Absolutely. That's what this video is about.

## THE FILM ROOM: HOW CHICAGO HAPPENED

Before we get to Kolek, let's rewind the comeback, because some new details make it even better than we first thought.

**The first-half problem.** This was ugly early. The Knicks shot just 37 percent before halftime while Chicago shot 45. New York turned it over 10 times, which led to 12 Bulls points, and Chicago won the paint 28–20. The only reason the Knicks were still in reach was Jalen Brunson, who had 16 points at the half.

**The collapse.** In the middle quarters, Chicago went on a 33–13 run and took an 87–68 lead late in the third. On the second night of a back-to-back, after a late finish the night before, this had all the signs of a "tired legs" loss.

**The answer.** Then the Knicks closed the third quarter on a 22–7 run and entered the fourth down just six. Kolek was right in the middle of that run, and we'll get to him in a minute.

**The closer.** Brunson took over. He scored 14 points in the final six minutes and 51 seconds. In about a minute and a half, he hit a 26-foot three, a turnaround jumper, and another deep three, and that pushed the lead to 127–116 with 3:17 left.

**The scare.** Chicago didn't go away. A Jalen Smith three made it a one-possession game in the final minute. Norman Powell, who led the Bulls with 30 points, got a look to tie it with 10 seconds left. He missed. OG Anunoby sealed it with two free throws with 1.7 seconds on the clock. Final: 133–127.

Chicago got strong games from Josh Giddey (10 points, 11 assists) and Jalen Smith (17 points, 11 rebounds, three blocks). But the champs found a way.

## THE BRUNSON FOOTNOTE YOU SHOULD KNOW

We already celebrated Brunson's season-high 38 points on 13-of-25 shooting, with six-of-10 from three and seven assists in 35 minutes. But here's a detail that says everything about him. Mike Brown pointed to the late finish the night before, and Brunson still showed up early to get shots up. On a back-to-back. After a rough first week. Coming off surgery on his left wrist in July.

The pull-up three, his signature shot, looked like itself again. That's not luck. That's a Finals MVP grinding through a slump until it breaks.

## THE BENCH, ROUND TWO

We talked about the bench last video, but a couple of new details deserve credit.

**Jordan Clarkson** finished with the best plus-minus on the team, and it wasn't just scoring. He made key defensive plays late, including timely steals down the stretch. That's a side of his game Knicks fans haven't seen much.

**Landry Shamet** added 14 points and five assists in 32 minutes. He also threw down a dunk that lit up the visiting bench. On a night when the starters looked tired, the second unit brought the energy.

## THE TWO CONCERNS

Now the other side. The Knicks have won two straight, but two important players are still struggling. They can't keep winning around them forever.

**Concern one: Mikal Bridges.** Outside of one strong half against Detroit, Bridges has looked hesitant on offense. The low point came in Boston, where he went zero-for-eight from the field in the 107–103 loss. It's early, and one rough week isn't enough to judge a veteran with his track record. But the Knicks need his scoring and his confidence back.

**Concern two: Deuce McBride.** He played only 14 minutes in the opener against Philadelphia. He's now played 15 minutes or fewer in three of the first five games, which rarely happened last season. And his three-point shot, one of his best weapons over the last three years, hasn't been falling. That creates a chicken-or-egg question for Mike Brown. Is the shooting slump costing him minutes? Or are the reduced minutes making it harder for him to find a rhythm?

Here's why this one stands out. Last video, we talked about the idea of trading Deuce to Oklahoma City. A smaller role and a cold shot don't help his trade value, and they don't help the Knicks either. Either way, New York needs the confident shooter and tough defender he's been. To defend a championship, they need more from both of these guys.

## THE ALVARADO SILENCE

Now to the question hanging over the backup point guard spot: Jose Alvarado's health. The latest isn't exactly reassuring.

Alvarado has missed three straight games with a left ankle sprain: Sunday's loss to Orlando, Tuesday's win over Detroit, and Wednesday's comeback in Chicago. Before that, he played in the first two games, averaging about 13.5 minutes.

The issue isn't just that he's out. It's how little the team has said. The Knicks announced the absence, but they didn't mention the injury during or after the game in which he apparently got hurt. There's been no timeline and no grade for the sprain. And before the Chicago game, Mike Brown said he had no update, only that Tyler Kolek is the backup point guard for now.

To be fair, this isn't new. The Knicks have been very quiet about injuries for years. They keep details private and let things play out. But this long after the injury, the silence feels different.

## WHY THE SILENCE IS RAISING EYEBROWS

Here's the logic, and this is analysis, not inside information. Ankle sprains are graded by severity. Almost a week after Alvarado last played, the medical staff almost certainly knows what kind of sprain it is. The swelling should have come down, and the team should have a good idea of the timeline. If it were a minor tweak, it would be easy to call him day-to-day and say he'll be back soon. The fact that nothing like that has come out is what's making people nervous.

There's also recent context. Several players around the league this season have had injuries that looked small at first and then turned into something longer. When a pattern like that shows up, fans and reporters naturally start to wonder.

Let me be fair: it's entirely possible this is nothing serious. Alvarado could be back in the next game or two, and this story could disappear. I'm not saying it's bad news. I'm saying we don't know, and the Knicks aren't telling us.

## WHAT ALVARADO BRINGS

So what does Alvarado mean to this team? He's a Brooklyn native who came to the Knicks in a trade with New Orleans in February. He quickly became an important part of the rotation and played a real role in the run to the 2026 championship, the franchise's first in 53 years. He re-signed in the offseason, a clear sign of how much the front office values him.

His identity is pressure:

- **Full-court defense.** He picks up ball handlers the length of the floor and fights through screens.
- **The little things.** He gets deflections, dives for loose balls, takes charges, and brings pure energy, the stuff that doesn't always show up in the box score.
- **Grand Theft Alvarado.** There's a reason for the nickname. He's famous for sneaking up behind opposing guards to steal inbound passes or poke the ball loose when they least expect it.

That kind of disruption is hard to teach, and it's exactly what a team defending a title wants off its bench. In a playoff series, those details swing games.

But there's a flip side. Alvarado isn't a natural playmaker, and he didn't start this season well. His outside shot wasn't reliable, and in his limited minutes before the injury, he didn't make the impact the Knicks hoped for.

## THE OPENING: THE KOLEK FILE

And that's why this absence matters. While Alvarado sits, someone else is getting a real chance to take the job, and coaches notice when a replacement makes the most of an opportunity.

Let's open the Kolek file.

**Exhibit A, Orlando (Sunday):** A little over 15 minutes, nine assists, zero turnovers. He didn't score, going zero-for-four from the field, but his passing and defense stood out on a night when not much else went right. He even caught the eye of one of the Knicks' most famous fans: the morning after, Ben Stiller posted that Kolek had played good defense. A small sign, but people noticed.

**Exhibit B, Detroit (Tuesday):** A quieter night in limited minutes, but he kept protecting the ball. Still zero turnovers.

**Exhibit C, Chicago (Wednesday):** His best game of the young season. In 18 minutes off the bench, he had nine points, nine assists, two rebounds, and zero turnovers. He hit three of four from deep, giving the Knicks a scoring threat they hadn't seen from him yet this year. He finished plus-13, the second-best mark on the team. And then there was the play: a behind-the-back pass to Karl-Anthony Towns that set off the bench and went viral online. Very few backup point guards in this league would even try that pass. Kolek made it look easy.

**The three-game total:** 21 assists and zero turnovers in roughly 44 minutes. For a backup point guard, that's elite ball security.

## WHY KOLEK WORKS

Kolek's value comes down to one thing: he makes his teammates better. When he's on the floor, the ball moves, shooters get clean looks, and big men get easy finishes.

That matters even more for the second unit, which often plays without Brunson or Towns, the team's two best creators. The bench needs someone to organize the offense, and Kolek has done exactly that.

He's also good for the stars when he shares the floor with them. Towns has benefited from his vision in the pick-and-roll. And in the past, Kolek has helped Mikal Bridges find easier looks, which could be big right now. A struggling scorer often just needs the ball delivered on time and on target. Kolek does that.

There's one more piece. Last season he shot 39 percent from three, and now he's three-of-four in his last game. If that shot keeps falling, defenses can't just go under screens and dare him to shoot. That opens up everything else.

## TALE OF THE TAPE: KOLEK VS. ALVARADO

So who should have the job when Alvarado is healthy? Let's compare them honestly. These are my evaluations, not stats.

| | Tyler Kolek | Jose Alvarado |
|---|---|---|
| **Playmaking** | Clear edge. Pass-first point guard who organizes the offense and protects the ball. | Not a natural playmaker. |
| **Shooting** | Shot 39% from three last season vs. Alvarado's 33%. Hot start lately. | Shot hasn't been reliable early this year. |
| **Defense** | Improving and holding his ground, but not his calling card. Worked against Orlando's backcourt. | Clear edge. Full-court pressure, steals, charges, chaos. |
| **Experience** | Limited playoff minutes. | Played real minutes in a championship run. |
| **Trust** | Earning it right now. | Already proven. |

Notice the pattern? They do different things. Kolek runs an offense. Alvarado wrecks the other team's offense. That gives Mike Brown options, and depending on the matchup, he might use both.

## MIKE BROWN'S TRACK RECORD

Mike Brown has a reputation for trusting veterans, but he's also shown he'll play younger guys over established ones when they earn it. Last season, Mo Diawara, Kevin McCullar Jr., and Tyler Kolek all got minutes ahead of more experienced players at different points of the year. Brown rewards production, and he's been clear about that. He's also said only five rotation spots are locked this season.

As of Thursday, the general feeling is that Alvarado still holds the backup point guard spot when he's healthy. But that hold isn't as strong as it was a week ago.

The Knicks have one goal this season: repeat as champions. With that goal, the decision should be simple. The best option plays, no matter how much experience he has or what he did last June. For Alvarado, step one is getting healthy. Step two might be winning his job back, and the guy standing in his way is having the best stretch of his young career.

## WHAT THIS MEANS FOR KOLEK'S FUTURE

Remember two videos ago, when we asked whether Kolek was a rotation piece or a trade chip? This stretch changes the math. If he keeps playing like this, the Knicks have a cost-controlled backup point guard who protects the ball, shoots 39 percent from deep, and makes the stars better. Those players are hard to find, especially for a team pressed up against the apron.

Two things can both be true. His trade value is rising fast, and his value to the Knicks is rising even faster. My take: unless a deadline offer makes the team clearly better right now, you keep the 25-year-old who just put up 21 assists and zero turnovers. And whenever his next contract comes up, these games will be part of the conversation.

## FRIDAY NIGHT: PHILADELPHIA, ROUND TWO

The next test is Friday night in Philadelphia, a rematch of the opening-night loss, 106–100. Then the Knicks come home Monday to face the Brooklyn Nets.

Here are the keys for Friday:

- **Start better.** In the last two games, the Knicks trailed 38–25 after one quarter against Detroit and fell behind by 19 in Chicago. You can't keep spotting good teams leads.
- **Clean up the turnovers.** Ten first-half turnovers in Chicago turned into 12 points. Against a good Philly team, that's a loss.
- **Get Bridges going early.** Run a few sets for him in the first quarter. Confidence comes from easy looks.
- **Let Kolek run the second unit.** If he keeps the bench organized, Brunson can save his legs for the fourth quarter.
- **Win the glass again.** The Knicks won the boards 65–52 in Chicago and 49–45 against Detroit. That's their identity.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. The Knicks win in Philadelphia on Friday and get back to .500 at 3–3, which would match what I said last video. Kolek gets another seven-plus assists. On Alvarado, I think the silence is classic Knicks, not a hidden disaster, and he's back within a week. But when he returns, I think Kolek keeps a real role, either by sharing the backup minutes or by playing next to Alvarado in certain lineups. Brown has seen too much to send him back to the end of the bench. And one more: Bridges breaks out with a 20-point game within the next three games. He's too good to stay this quiet.

- **Chicago:** The Knicks erased a 19-point deficit, trailing 87–68 late in the third. They closed the quarter on a 22–7 run, and Brunson scored 14 in the final 6:51 of a 38-point night.

- **Alvarado:** He's missed three straight with a left ankle sprain. There's no timeline and no grade, and Brown says Kolek is the backup point guard for now.
