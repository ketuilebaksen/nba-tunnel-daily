And we close with the story that got me weirdly sentimental this week: James Wiseman's audition, seen through a tradition you might not know this franchise has. For a former No. 2 pick, just MAKING an opening-night roster would be a genuine career milestone at this point; the draft expectations died long ago, replaced by one simple question — can he still carve out an NBA life?

But here's the pattern: New York has quietly spent two decades running a halfway house for unfulfilled lottery picks — and it's produced real stories. Cole Aldrich, 11th pick in 2010, averaging two points across three lost seasons before the Knicks took him in — and in New York he found the only stability of his career: 16 starts, career highs of 5.5 and 5.2, nine double-doubles. Modest? Sure. But a career counted out, revived. Jonathan Bender, the 5th pick in 1999 out of high school, swallowed whole by injuries — three FULL seasons missed — until the Knicks handed him a 2009 comeback: 25 games, a dignified final chapter, a career that got to end on a court instead of a waiting room. Mario Hezonja, 5th pick in 2015, three anonymous years in Orlando before New York gave him a real runway. The pattern is consistent: this market notices whatever you do well, this organization hands you a professional environment, and if there's anything left in the tank, the Garden finds it. Now run that tradition through TODAY'S version of the franchise — the best development program in basketball, the machine that rebuilt Hukporti and polished Diawara — and give it a 25-year-old 7-footer with a coach who already knows him. If any building on Earth resurrects James Wiseman, it's this one. The tradition is undefeated in effort. Camp decides if it claims another one.
