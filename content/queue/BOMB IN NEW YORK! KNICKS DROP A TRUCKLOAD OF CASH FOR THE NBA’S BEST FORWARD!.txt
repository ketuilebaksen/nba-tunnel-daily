## THE WEEK THAT CHANGED THE MOOD

Before the awards, let's recap the week that just happened — Week 3 of the season, November 2nd through November 8th.

**Monday — Knicks 103, Nets 90.** The grinder. Brooklyn led 79–75 after three quarters before the Knicks won the fourth 28–11. Landry Shamet scored 22 off the bench. Brunson had 19 points and six assists in his quietest night of the week.

**Wednesday — Knicks 135, Bulls 105.** The demolition. Eighty-four points in the first half, tying the franchise record. Brunson scored 33 in just 28 minutes, including 22 in the first quarter alone — more than the entire Bulls team. Dalton Knecht made his debut with 10 points and seven rebounds.

**Saturday — Knicks 109, Cavaliers 97.** The statement. The first game in Cleveland since the Knicks swept the Cavs in last year's Eastern Conference Finals. After a slow first quarter, Brunson exploded for 18 points in the second quarter and finished with 31 points, a season-high six rebounds, and nine assists.

Three wins. Five of their last six. A 5–4 record and fifth place in the East. The team that started 0–3 is officially back in the conversation.

## AWARD #1: PLAYER OF THE WEEK — JALEN BRUNSON

This one's official, but let's give it the Tunnel treatment.

**The week:**
- 27.7 points per game
- 6.3 assists per game
- 3.3 rebounds per game
- 48.3% from the field
- 42.9% from three
- Only 0.7 turnovers per game

That last number might be the most impressive one. A primary ball-handler carrying the offense, playing at a high pace, and barely turning it over. That's elite control.

**The context:** Remember where Brunson was a few weeks ago. Through his first six games, he was shooting 39.5% from the field and 30.5% from three — the worst marks of his career — after offseason surgery on his left shooting wrist. People were asking whether the touch would come back. Whether Karl-Anthony Towns should take over in crunch time.

Well, here's the answer. Since that slow start, Brunson has had 38 points in the Chicago comeback, 24 with a go-ahead three in Philly, 19 against Brooklyn, 33 against Chicago, and now 31 and nine in Cleveland. He's averaging 26.2 points per game on the season, which ranks 10th in the NBA.

And remember the two-minute data Mike Brown talked about? The staff tracks players in two-minute intervals, and Brunson's offensive pressure increases as the minutes go on. That's exactly what we saw in Cleveland — a slow first quarter, then 18 in the second. He gets stronger as the game goes.

**The Tunnel verdict:** This is the first weekly award of his new season, and it came at the perfect time. The captain pulled the team out of its early hole. Award well earned.

## AWARD #2: THE "OUCH" AWARD — THE DALLAS MAVERICKS

This one is for the team across the country that has to look at the weekly awards and wince.

**The West's Player of the Week:** Luka Dončić, now with the Los Angeles Lakers. And his week was absurd — 45.0 points, 8.3 assists, and 7.0 rebounds per game in three Lakers wins. He scored at least 40 in every game, including 53 against Portland. He's leading the entire NBA at 40.1 points per game, and the Lakers are 9–2.

**The East's Player of the Week:** Jalen Brunson, captain of the reigning champions.

Both began their careers in Dallas after the 2018 draft. Both spent four seasons together there. And today, neither one is a Maverick.

**The Brunson story:** In 2021, the Mavericks had the chance to sign Brunson to a four-year, $55 million extension. They passed. They passed again. Only after the 2022 trade deadline did Dallas finally offer that same deal — and by then, Brunson believed he was worth much more. He was right. He went to free agency and signed a four-year, $104 million contract with the Knicks. Since then: a regular All-Star, the franchise's first championship in 53 years, and the NBA Finals MVP. It's now seen as one of the biggest free-agent steals in league history.

**The Dončić story:** As painful as that was, the Dončić trade made it look small. Dallas sent its superstar to Los Angeles less than a year after he led the Mavericks to the NBA Finals. In return, they got Anthony Davis, Max Christie, and a 2029 first-round pick. Outside of Lakers fans, many people around the league consider it the worst trade in NBA history. And Patrick Dumont, the controlling owner who approved that deal, is still in charge.

**The silver lining:** Dallas does have Cooper Flagg, the No. 1 overall pick in the 2025 draft and a true franchise cornerstone. But after letting two elite guards slip away, the pressure in Dallas is enormous. They need to build a real contender around Flagg while he's young. Anything less makes the Brunson and Dončić decisions look worse every single season.

**The Tunnel verdict:** Congratulations, Dallas — you win the Ouch Award. And Knicks fans, never forget: this front office believed in Brunson when Dallas wouldn't pay him. That decision changed the direction of the franchise.

## AWARD #3: MOST CONCERNING TREND — OG ANUNOBY'S SLUMP

Now the harder part of the show. Most of the early criticism this season has focused on Mikal Bridges, and some of it's fair — in one loss to Philadelphia, Bridges had just four points in 33 minutes. But focusing only on Bridges misses a bigger concern. OG Anunoby, one of the heroes of last season's championship run, is stuck in a surprising slump of his own.

**The shooting:**
- 29.3% from three on the season
- 3-of-12 from the field and 1-of-9 from three in that same loss to Philadelphia
- 56% at the rim — below what you'd expect from a strong, physical forward
- Just one made shot all season between the restricted area and the three-point line

That last stat tells you how limited his offensive game has become. He's either attacking the basket or shooting threes — and right now, neither is falling at a high rate.

**The defense — and this is the shocker:**
- Opponents are shooting 46.4% when OG is the closest defender
- His defensive rating of 110.5 is the worst among the Knicks' regular rotation players
- He's tied for the fifth-most personal fouls in the entire league, with 35 through nine games

That's a lot of fouls for a player whose defense is usually built on smart positioning and quick hands.

**The team numbers with OG on the floor:**
- Offensive rating in roughly the 44th percentile
- Opponent effective field goal percentage of 54.8%, in the 34th percentile
- Team effective field goal percentage of 50.0%, in the 16th percentile
- A net rating of minus-1.0 for the season

For a starter on a team with championship expectations, those numbers need to get better.

## WHY OG'S SLUMP MATTERS SO MUCH

Here's the bigger picture. The Knicks kept the same starting five that won the title: Brunson, Josh Hart, Bridges, OG, and Towns. That group was built on two-way wings who could defend and knock down open shots.

When Bridges and OG struggle at the same time, the Knicks lose a huge part of what made them so hard to beat. Defenses can crowd Brunson and Towns. The spacing shrinks. And on defense, the perimeter isn't the wall it was last spring.

We've talked about this on the channel before — the analysis showing OG's expanded offensive role this season, with more touches and more drives, hasn't produced the efficiency the Knicks hoped for. The suggestion was to simplify his role: catch-and-go attacks, attacking closeouts, hitting open threes. Not asking him to be a lead creator.

Mike Brown's priority with OG should be defense first. That has always been the foundation of his game. Get the defense back, and the offense usually follows.

## THE OG BRIGHT SPOT

But let's be fair, because there's a real silver lining. Even while his shot isn't falling, OG has been incredibly aggressive.

- He's attempted eight or more free throws in five of the Knicks' first nine games. That matches his total number of eight-plus free throw games for the entire 2025–26 regular season.
- He's averaging 6.2 free throw attempts per game — a massive jump from his career average of 2.1 over 10 NBA seasons.
- He's making 87.5% of them.
- Free throws now account for 33.1% of his total points.

Remember the Bulls blowout? He went to the line 12 times in the first half alone — one shy of his Knicks career high for a full game.

That tells you something important. OG isn't hiding or drifting away from the action. He's attacking, drawing contact, and finding ways to contribute even when the jumper is off. Shooting slumps come and go, and nine games is a small sample. If the three-point shot returns to his normal levels, his numbers should climb fast — especially with the confidence he's showing when he puts the ball on the floor.

**The Tunnel verdict:** Concerning, yes. Panic, no. A 29% three-point shooter who gets to the line six times a game is a player waiting for his shot to come back.

## AWARD #4: UNSUNG HERO — ANDRE DRUMMOND

And now the award that might surprise you most. While the starters work through their issues, the Knicks are getting a real lift from a veteran big man who's been doing the dirty work every single night.

**The backstory:** When Mitchell Robinson left for Boston this offseason, a lot of fans worried. Robinson had been one of the most feared offensive rebounders in the league, and creating extra possessions was a huge part of the Knicks' identity. To replace him, New York signed Andre Drummond to a one-year deal and made him the primary backup behind Towns.

**The numbers:** Three weeks into the season, Drummond isn't just filling that role. On the offensive glass, he's beating Robinson at his own game.
- He ranks 17th in the entire NBA in total offensive rebounds, with 27
- Among all players with at least 100 minutes this season, he leads the league with 8.1 offensive rebounds per 36 minutes

Read that again. Number one in the NBA in offensive rebounds per 36 minutes. In a backup role.

**The comparison:** As we talked about recently, Robinson's offensive rebounding in Boston has dropped from 4.2 per game last season to 1.8 this season as the Celtics have pulled him away from the basket. Meanwhile, Drummond is crashing the glass like it's 2016.

## WHY DRUMMOND'S REBOUNDING MATTERS

Offensive rebounds are one of the best cures for a cold shooting team. And this team has had cold nights — remember the nine-of-38 night from three against Orlando. Every Drummond offensive board is a second chance. Every second chance is a chance to reset the offense, draw a foul, or get an easy putback.

And it fits the "chip on the shoulder" story we covered recently. Daily Knicks described Drummond as a player who spent the summer believing veteran minimum contracts were beneath him — and the market told him otherwise. He came to New York fighting a league-wide perception of his worth. So far, he's answering it in the most Drummond way possible: by grabbing every rebound in sight.

To be fair, his defense has been inconsistent, and there's still a real debate about whether the Knicks need another big. But on the glass, he's doing exactly what this team needed.

**The Tunnel verdict:** Andre Drummond is the Unsung Hero of the week. Nobody's making a highlight video of his rebounding. They should.

## DOES DRUMMOND CHANGE THE CENTER CONVERSATION?

This is a fair question. For weeks, the biggest storyline around the Knicks has been the backup center spot. We've covered the Mitchell Robinson reunion idea, the Jarrett Allen fake rumor, the young Clippers bigs, and the Dadiet-for-a-minimum-big options.

So does Drummond leading the league in offensive rebounds per 36 minutes end that conversation? Not completely. Here's my honest read:

- **What he solves:** Second chances, rebounding, physicality, and extra possessions when the shots aren't falling.
- **What he doesn't solve:** Rim protection and switching on defense — the areas where Robinson made the biggest difference in last year's playoff run.

So the Knicks might not need a new big as badly as some people think. But a rim protector would still be a smart addition after December 15th — especially for the playoffs. Drummond buys them time and makes it less urgent. That's a big deal for a front office with almost no trade assets and only about $370,000 under the second apron.
