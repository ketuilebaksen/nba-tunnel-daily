Down big on the road, a lot of young teams would mail it in. Westchester kept defending, kept moving the ball, and kept fighting until it flipped. ## THE WESTCHESTER SCOUTING BOARD

Let's go player by player. For each one: what he does well, what he needs to work on, and how he could help the Knicks.

## PLAYER CARD: DALTON KNECHT

**Contract:** Two-way with the Knicks (up to 50 NBA games)

**The story so far:** The 17th overall pick in 2024. Waived by the Lakers before the season. The Bucks reportedly wanted draft picks just to take him in a trade. Nobody claimed him on waivers. Then he signed with New York, and Mike Brown — who had him down for "zero minutes" — gave him a gut-feel debut against Chicago: 10 points, seven rebounds, four-of-six shooting, and a putback slam that lit up the Garden.

**Strengths:**
- That three-point shot is already NBA-ready. Thirty-five points with six threes in Westchester is a reminder of what he can do.
- He moves well without the ball, cuts backdoor, and can finish above the rim.

**What he needs:**
- Defense. That's the knock that followed him from Los Angeles, and it's still the area that will decide whether he earns real NBA minutes.

**How he helps the Knicks:** On nights when the shooting goes cold — remember the nine-of-38 night against Orlando — Knecht is an instant-offense option. And he's cheap.

## PLAYER CARD: ROB DILLINGHAM

**Background:** A first-round pick in 2024 and one of the most electric guards in that draft class at Kentucky.

**The debut:** 26 points, seven assists, and two steals in his first game with Westchester.

**Strengths:**
- He's quick, fast, and shifty. He knows how to play.
- His shot was falling in his debut, and he showed real playmaking.

**What he needs:**
- Defense, same as Knecht. At his size, staying in front of NBA guards and fighting through screens will be the test.

**How he could help the Knicks:** A creative, shot-making guard who can run an offense. With Brunson, Kolek, Alvarado, Shamet, Clarkson, and Deuce McBride already in the backcourt, there's no room right now. But long-term, players like Dillingham are exactly the type of cheap guard depth a team under the second apron needs.

**The important caveat:** He's not on an NBA contract with the Knicks. Players on G League deals can be signed by any NBA team. So he isn't "locked in" — he's developing in Westchester for now.

## PLAYER CARD: JADEN AKINS

**Contract:** Two-way with the Knicks

**The big night:** 24 points, six threes, and seven assists during the record-setting 28-point comeback.

**Strengths:**
- He's already a good defender.
- He has a relentless motor.
- He gets into the paint, takes contact, and gets to the line.

**What he's working on:** Shot selection and shooting efficiency. And based on this game, he's making progress. Six threes in one night is a real statement. If he keeps building that, he becomes a 3-and-D guard — exactly what wins in the NBA.

**The plot twist:** Remember a couple of videos ago, when we talked about the Knicks possibly signing 7-foot center Lawson Lovering to a two-way deal? The roster math said it would most likely mean letting go of Akins. Well, nights like this make that decision a lot harder. Akins is playing his way into the Knicks' long-term plans.

## WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE BIG CLUB

Now the most important part: why should Knicks fans care about the G League?

**The cap reality:** The Knicks are right up against the second apron. According to ESPN's Bobby Marks, they have only about $89,000 of room. Owner James Dolan has made it clear he won't cross that line. That means every dollar on the roster matters.

**The coming contracts:** The core is getting more expensive:
- **Jalen Brunson** becomes eligible for a huge extension next summer — projected at about $286.5 million over four years.
- **Karl-Anthony Towns'** extension is still unresolved.
- **Josh Hart** has a team option coming.
- **OG Anunoby** and **Mikal Bridges** are already on big deals.
- **Deuce McBride** is heading toward free agency.

**The solution:** If you can develop cheap players who can come off the bench and contribute, you don't have to pay big money for role players. That leaves more room to keep the stars together.

That's the strategy. Find the right talent. Develop it in Westchester. Bring it up when it's ready. Use the savings on the core.

## THE PROOF IT WORKS

And here's the best part — the Knicks have already done this.

- **Tyler Kolek:** A second-round pick. Now the backup point guard, leading the NBA in assist-to-turnover ratio among players with at least 100 minutes.
- **Mohamed Diawara:** A second-round pick. Now on a four-year deal and playing real minutes, like his plus-16 in Cleveland.
- **Landry Shamet:** Barely made the roster a year ago. Now he's one of the most important players on the team, on a four-year, $24 million deal that looks like a bargain.
- **Dalton Knecht:** Unwanted by the league. Now a two-way player with a real NBA debut and a 35-point G League night.

That's the Leon Rose formula. The Knicks rarely keep first-round picks — Pacôme Dadiet was the only one they'd kept in five years — and they trade down or out on draft night. But they find value in second-rounders, two-way players, and buy-low veterans. The Westchester pipeline is the next step of that same strategy.

## HOW A WESTCHESTER PLAYER BECOMES A KNICK

So what's the actual path from Westchester to the Garden? Here's how it usually works — my breakdown of the process.

**Step one: dominate the G League.** Big numbers get you noticed. But coaches watch the details just as closely: defensive effort, decision-making, and whether you do the little things.

**Step two: earn the call-up.** Two-way players like Knecht, Akins, and Nickel can be brought up to the NBA roster for up to 50 games. That's the bridge. They practice with the big club, travel with them, and get spot minutes when injuries or blowouts open the door — like Knecht's "gut feel" debut against Chicago.

**Step three: win the minutes.** Once you're up, Mike Brown's rule applies: only five rotation spots are locked, and the rest are earned. That's how Kolek took the backup point guard job and how Diawara played his way into real minutes.

**Step four: get the contract.** Eventually, a two-way player can be converted to a standard contract. But for the Knicks right now, that's tricky. With only about $89,000 of room under the second apron, converting a player would likely cost more than they can add. So in the short term, the two-way contract is the vehicle. In the long term — next summer, when contracts reshuffle — that's when these players can get real deals.

**Step five: become the next Kolek.** A cheap, productive rotation player who saves the Knicks millions. That's the dream outcome for everyone.

## THE KNECHT CASE STUDY

Knecht is the perfect example of why this pipeline matters. A month ago, the NBA had basically written him off. The Lakers waived him. The Bucks reportedly wanted picks to take him. Nobody put in a waiver claim.

Since then:
- He made a strong Knicks debut with 10 points and seven rebounds after Brown had him down for "zero minutes."
- He had a quiet night in Cleveland and didn't see the second half — a reminder that the NBA isn't easy.
- And now he's dropping 35 in Westchester with six threes.

That's what development looks like. Ups, downs, and steady work. If he keeps building, especially on defense, he could be the cheapest shooter in the Knicks' playoff rotation.

## WESTCHESTER AS A RECRUITING TOOL

And there's one more angle. A strong G League program attracts talent. When young players see guys like Kolek, Diawara, and Knecht getting real chances in New York, Westchester becomes a place they want to be. That's a quiet advantage for a front office that doesn't spend big on rookies or free agents. It's not flashy, but it's smart, and it compounds over time.

## THE HONEST LIMITS

Let's keep it real, too.

**G League numbers don't always translate.** Thirty-five points in Westchester isn't the same as 35 in the Garden. Plenty of players dominate the G League and never stick in the NBA.

**Defense is the common thread.** Almost every player on this board needs to improve defensively. That's the hardest skill to develop, and it's the one Mike Brown cares about most.

**The minutes aren't there right now.** The Knicks' rotation is deep. Brunson, Shamet, Kolek, Alvarado, Clarkson, Deuce, Bridges, Hart, OG, and Diawara are all ahead of these guys. That's why they're in Westchester.

**Two-way spots are limited.** The Knicks only have so many. If they add a developmental big — like the Lovering idea — somebody has to go.

So this is about the future, not this month. But the future matters for a team trying to win multiple championships.
