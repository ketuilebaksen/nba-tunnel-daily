## THE GAME IN FOUR QUARTERS

**Quarters one and two: the grind.** Brooklyn stayed right with the Knicks. This wasn't a blowout start, and for a winless Nets team, they played loose and confident. For long stretches, Shamet was one of the only Knicks who looked comfortable attacking. But the Nets never built a real cushion. Their largest lead all night was only five points.

**Quarter three: the scare.** This is when it got uncomfortable. Brooklyn outscored the Knicks 35–24 in the third quarter and took a 79–75 lead into the fourth. The Garden got quiet. A winless 0–5 team was 12 minutes away from its first win, against the champs, on their floor.

**Quarter four: the lockdown.** The Knicks won the final quarter 28–11. Eleven points allowed in 12 minutes. Josh Hart set the tone with his energy early in the quarter. The defense forced turnovers and turned them into transition chances. The lead grew to as many as 16 in the final minutes. Final: Knicks 103, Nets 90.

That's the Knicks' 15th straight win over Brooklyn, and it moves them to 3–4.

## THE SECRET WEAPON: LANDRY SHAMET

Let's give the man his flowers.

**The line:**

- **Points:** 22 (team high), in 29 minutes off the bench
- **Field goals:** 6-of-8
- **Three-pointers:** 4-of-6
- **Free throws:** 6-of-6

Sports Illustrated's Knicks coverage gave him an A-plus, and it's hard to argue.

Here's why this performance matters beyond one night. Remember the Orlando game, when the Knicks went nine-of-38 from three? Remember our videos asking where the shooting would come from? We talked about Dalton Knecht in Westchester and Kolek's 39 percent from last season. The answer might have been here all along. Shamet is a career-long sharpshooter, and when he's hot, he spaces the floor and punishes defenses for helping on Brunson and KAT.

And here's the toughness part. Just last week in Chicago, Shamet twisted his right ankle after a dunk in the first half. He played through it there and put up 14 points and five assists. On Monday, he looked fully healthy, attacking closeouts, getting to the line, and hitting threes.

## WHY SHAMET ISN'T REALLY A SECRET

Calling him a "secret weapon" is fun, but the truth is that Shamet has been quietly good all season. Before Monday, he was second on the team in plus-minus at plus-20, behind only Clarkson. He was a plus-22 in the Chicago comeback. He's one of the players who has revived his career under Mike Brown, a coach who gives minutes to whoever earns them.

So he's not new. He's just finally getting the spotlight. And with Bridges struggling, Shamet's role may be about to grow.

**Jalen Brunson:** 19 points and six assists, but on six-of-16 shooting. He went four-of-11 from three and just three-of-six at the line. He was aggressive, which is a good sign, but the efficiency still isn't all the way back.

**Karl-Anthony Towns:** 11 points and 16 rebounds for his seventh straight double-double, with five assists and four turnovers. He shot four-of-11, including one-of-four from three. It wasn't his best shooting night, but he controlled the glass, and the Knicks were plus-13 with him on the floor. Even on an off night, KAT mattered.

## DEBATE NUMBER ONE: WHAT'S GOING ON WITH MIKAL BRIDGES?

Now to the debates, starting with the most uncomfortable one.

**The numbers:** Bridges played only 16 minutes on Monday and went one-for-seven for six points. That came a few days after a career-low 13 minutes in Chicago, and after a zero-for-eight night in Boston.

**The puzzling part:** With several teammates in foul trouble, including Hart, you'd think Bridges would get extra minutes. Instead, he got fewer. That was one of the more surprising decisions of the night.

**The case for sitting him:** Brown rewards production, and right now Shamet, Clarkson, and Deuce are producing while Bridges isn't. A championship coach can't play reputation over results.

**The case against:** You can't win a title without Bridges. He's on a long-term contract, he's an elite defender, and he's been one of the most durable players in the league for years. Fewer minutes could make his rhythm problem worse, not better. It's the same chicken-or-egg question we asked about Deuce.

**My take:** I'd keep Bridges' minutes in the mid-20s, not the teens, and run a few early sets for him to get easy looks. The Knicks need him confident by December, and he won't get there sitting on the bench. But if Shamet keeps shooting like this, Brown has every right to keep riding the hot hand in the fourth quarter.

## DEBATE NUMBER TWO: HOW HARD SHOULD THE KNICKS CHASE HOME COURT?

Monday's win also brought a bigger question back into focus. How much does home-court advantage matter for this team?

**Last season's answer was clear.** The Knicks entered the 2026 playoffs as the third seed in the East and held home-court advantage in every round.

- **First round:** They fell behind the sixth-seeded Atlanta Hawks two games to one, then won four straight.
- **Second round:** They swept the seventh-seeded Philadelphia 76ers.
- **Eastern Conference Finals:** They swept the fourth-seeded Cleveland Cavaliers.
- **NBA Finals:** They beat the San Antonio Spurs in five to win the franchise's first title since 1973.

That extra home game was a real cushion, especially in the Atlanta series.

**This season's start is shakier.** The Knicks are 2–2 at the Garden, with losses to Philadelphia and Orlando and wins over Detroit and Brooklyn. On the road, they're 1–2, with a win in Chicago and losses in Boston and Philadelphia. Four of their first seven games were at home, so they've already let a couple of valuable chances slip.

**The East is tougher.** Talent spread out across the conference this summer. Philly added LeBron James and Jaylen Brown, Boston added Paul George, and Giannis is in Miami. Teams that were a step behind New York last spring have closed the gap. That makes every regular-season win count more in the seeding race.

**The trade-off.** If the Knicks chase a top seed hard, they risk wearing down their stars before April, and Hart has already said the team is feeling fatigue. If they prioritize rest and development, they might open a playoff series on the road. Last year's team handled adversity, but it never had to win a series without the extra home game.

**The counterargument.** A champion shouldn't obsess over seeding. Teams that have won a title carry the confidence to win anywhere. This core has been through every pressure situation imaginable.

**My take:** Chase it, but smartly. A top-four seed is a realistic goal, and the Garden in the playoffs is one of the toughest buildings in basketball for visitors. You don't give that up lightly. But don't burn out the stars to get the one seed. Use the bench, which is exactly what Brown is doing, to win games while saving legs.

## THE YOUNG-PLAYER SQUEEZE

This connects to the development question. So far, Brown has leaned heavily on his veterans. Mo Diawara played just one minute total across the first six games, then got six minutes against Brooklyn. He missed his only shot and grabbed one rebound.

Brown has said that the uneven play of some veterans has limited his ability to give more time to young players like Diawara and Pacôme Dadiet. In other words, he can't comfortably develop the kids while the established rotation is still finding its rhythm.

And the schedule doesn't make it easier:

- **Wednesday:** Chicago at the Garden
- **Saturday:** At Cleveland
- **Then:** Milwaukee and Cleveland again at home, on back-to-back nights
- **Later this month:** At Indiana, then two straight road games in Toronto

A lot of those are Eastern Conference opponents, and head-to-head results can decide tiebreakers in April. A strong November gives the Knicks breathing room. A weak one means spending the winter chasing.

## DEBATE NUMBER THREE: BRUNSON OR KAT IN CLUTCH TIME?

And now the spiciest debate. When the game is on the line, who gets the ball?

For years, the answer has been simple: Jalen Brunson. The reigning Finals MVP, a three-time All-Star, and one of the most trusted closers in basketball. But one Knicks writer argued this week that, at least for now, Karl-Anthony Towns should be the primary option in clutch time until Brunson finds his rhythm. Let's look at the case.

**Brunson's numbers through six games:**

- **Field-goal percentage:** 39.5
- **Three-point percentage:** 30.5
- **The context:** Both are the worst marks of his career.
- **Clutch:** Five-of-13, which is 38.5 percent.

And Monday's six-of-16, with three-of-six at the line, showed that the rust is still there.

**The reason:** Brunson had surgery on his left wrist, his shooting wrist, in the offseason and spent months in a cast. For a player whose game is built on rhythm, footwork, and touch, that takes time to shake off. Touch is often the last thing to come back after a wrist injury.

**KAT's numbers entering Monday:**

- **Averages:** 21.8 points, 13.5 rebounds, and 3.2 assists
- **Field-goal percentage:** 57.5
- **Clutch:** 57.1 percent from the field and 60 percent from three
- **Defensive rating:** 107 points allowed per 100 possessions, on pace for the second-best mark of his career

He also looks quicker and more mobile, with a noticeably slimmer frame. And remember the Philly game, when KAT scored eight straight in the fourth quarter to drag the Knicks back.

## MY VERDICT ON THE CLUTCH DEBATE

Here's where I land. The clutch sample is tiny. Brunson has played about 17 clutch minutes this season. That's not enough to rewrite the hierarchy of a championship team. Brunson has earned that trust on the biggest stage, and Mike Brown isn't going to take the ball out of his hands.

But it doesn't have to be either-or. The smartest answer is to run more late-game action through both of them. Brunson-KAT pick-and-roll forces the defense to choose. Put Brunson as the ball handler and KAT as the screener, and if KAT slips or pops, he's the one taking the shot. If the defense switches, Brunson gets a big man on an island. That's the play that won games last spring, and it uses the hot hand without benching the Finals MVP in crunch time.

And let's be honest: we've already seen what Brunson can do. Fourteen points in the final seven minutes in Chicago. The go-ahead three in Philly. The clutch gene isn't gone. The touch is just catching up.

There's also a contract angle. KAT did not get an extension this offseason despite coming off one of the best seasons of his career and helping win a title. Every strong late-game moment strengthens his case. A bigger clutch role this month would only add to it.

## WHAT THIS WIN MEANS

Let's zoom out. The Knicks are 3–4. Once again, they let an opponent hang around before pulling away late, just like in Chicago. Against better teams, that may not be enough.

But this was also a win that showed the identity: defense, depth, and toughness. When they needed it, they held a team to 11 points in a quarter. The bench outscored the Nets' bench by 15. Hart backed up his words. And a player who isn't a household name, Landry Shamet, carried them for three quarters.

That's how this team won last spring, with everybody contributing.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. The Knicks beat Chicago on Wednesday and get back to .500 at 4–4. Shamet scores in double figures again, and he earns a permanent spot in Brown's closing lineup for now. Bridges has his first 15-point game of the season within the next three games, because a player this good doesn't stay cold forever. And on the clutch debate, Brunson stays the closer, but more of the Knicks' final possessions run through Brunson-KAT pick-and-roll, with KAT finishing more of them.
