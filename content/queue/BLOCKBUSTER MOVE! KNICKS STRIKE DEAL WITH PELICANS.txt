COLUMN TWO: THE FIVE GHOSTS — AND THE JANUARY DOOR

Now to the column written entirely in pencil: five men on Exhibit 9 contracts — Ochai Agbaji, Bruce Brown, Drew Eubanks, James Wiseman, and the newest entry, Tony Bradley. Let's do the mechanics first, because understanding the paper explains the stakes. An Exhibit 9 is basketball's most disposable document: non-guaranteed, waivable before the regular season with ZERO cap penalty, and — the cold-blooded part — if a player suffers a serious injury in camp, the team can release him owing nothing but a one-time payment of $15,000. Fifteen thousand dollars, folks. For an organization tap-dancing on the second apron, that's not a contract — that's a free sample. Five legitimate NBA veterans, auditioning for their careers, on paper the team can shred for the cost of a used sedan. The CBA is a brutal document, and the champs have learned to read it better than anyone.

And the math of the battle sharpened this week with a detail that changes the whole timeline: as things stand, there's room for exactly ONE veteran-minimum contract under the apron — the single golden ticket we've tracked all camp. The second seat still requires the trade. BUT — and here's the new mechanism the analysis surfaced — even without a trade, the Knicks will naturally accrue enough space beneath the second apron to add a FIFTEENTH player in early January. Why? Because minimum contracts prorate: every day of the season that passes, the cost of signing a player for the REST of that season shrinks, until the math that's impossible in October becomes trivial by New Year's. Which means the camp battle's losers aren't necessarily gone forever — they're potentially deferred. A Bruce Brown who narrowly loses the October seat could be back on a prorated deal in January if he's still unsigned, no Dadiet trade required. The door everyone thought was binary — make the team or vanish — actually has a third hinge, and its name is the calendar.
