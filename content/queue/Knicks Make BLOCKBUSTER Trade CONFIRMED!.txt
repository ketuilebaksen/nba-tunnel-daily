## THE HONEST FRAMING: WHAT THIS TRADE ACTUALLY IS

Let's be crystal clear, because the word "trade" can be misleading. This is a G League trade, not an NBA blockbuster. The Westchester Knicks sent the Austin Spurs a 2026 second-round G League draft pick — one Westchester had acquired from the Valley Suns — in exchange for Elfrid Payton's returning player rights.

That's the whole cost. No NBA salary. No roster spot on the Knicks. No Knicks draft pick. Owning his rights simply means Westchester can bring him in if he wants to play in the G League this season — it doesn't guarantee he joins. And according to Heavy, there's been no indication the Knicks plan to sign him to an NBA contract.

So no, this isn't a move that changes the title race. But it's one of the most fascinating storylines of the fall, and it could matter if injuries hit. Let's tell the whole story.

## WHERE IT STARTED: THE 10TH PICK

Let's go all the way back to 2014. Elfrid Payton came out of Louisiana-Lafayette as the 10th overall pick and began his career in Orlando. He made the All-Rookie team and became known for two things: triple-doubles, and that legendary hair.

His triple-double reputation was real. In 2019 with New Orleans, he recorded five triple-doubles in a row — a stretch only a handful of players in NBA history had ever put together. That summer, in 2019, he signed with the Knicks.

## YEAR ONE IN NEW YORK: BETTER THAN YOU REMEMBER

Here's something a lot of fans forget: Payton's first season in New York was honestly fine. He averaged 10 points and 7.2 assists per game. He had a triple-double against Cleveland — 17 points, 11 rebounds, and 15 assists in an overtime win, one of the rare wins that season. A few days later, he set a career high with seven steals. And here's the stat people forget: that season, the Knicks were 4.4 points per 100 possessions better with Payton on the floor than off it.

But the shooting was a problem from day one. He made just 20 percent of his threes and 57 percent of his free throws. And there was some drama too: in January 2020, he received a one-game suspension for shoving Jae Crowder during a loss to Memphis.

After that season, the Knicks waived him in November 2020 — and then re-signed him 10 days later on a one-year deal. And that's when the story got complicated.

## YEAR TWO: THE THIBODEAU ERA AND THE STARTING JOB

The 2020–21 season was Tom Thibodeau's first in New York, and the Knicks became the surprise of the league. In a shortened season, they went 41–31, finished fourth in the East, and made the playoffs for the first time since 2013. That run is widely seen as the turning point that eventually led the franchise to a championship.

And Elfrid Payton started every game he played — 63 starts. But his production didn't match the role. He averaged around 10 points with a lot fewer assists than the year before, and shot just 28 percent from three. And this time, the Knicks were 9.4 points per 100 possessions better with him OFF the floor. That's where Knicks fans turned on him. Why was Payton starting over Derrick Rose? Why wasn't Immanuel Quickley, the breakout rookie, getting more minutes? For much of that season, Knicks Twitter's number one request was simple: take Payton out of the starting lineup.

## THE ROCK-BOTTOM FINISH

And the end of that season got ugly. Over his last 16 games, Payton averaged 4.9 points on 35 percent shooting. In the regular-season finale against Boston, he played 14 minutes and scored zero points. The New York Post's Marc Berman called it rock bottom. Tommy Beer pointed out an even uglier stat: over the Knicks' final five games, Payton had more turnovers — four — than made field goals, made free throws, and made threes combined.

When he was asked about the criticism, he fired back: "I assess how I'm playing by how my team is doing, and we've been winning. So we're in the playoffs."

## THE ATLANTA SERIES

Then came the playoffs against Atlanta. Payton started the first two games, but he played just eight minutes in Game 1 and five in Game 2 — he couldn't stay in front of Trae Young and wasn't efficient anywhere else. For Game 3, Thibodeau finally made the switch. Derrick Rose started, played 39 minutes, and scored 30 points. Rose was the Knicks' best player in that series, trying to carry them, but the Knicks lost in five.

Thibodeau handled it with class. He said, "Elfrid's a critical part of this team. He's had a very good year for us." He added that the team had asked everyone to sacrifice and put the team first — and that Payton had done that. Whatever fans thought of Payton, his coach never threw him under the bus. Not once.

And then it got weird. Payton's mother went on social media and criticized the Knicks for the change. His father told the New York Post he would've liked to see his son play, since he'd started the whole season. Payton averaged about 6.5 minutes in the two playoff games he appeared in. That summer, the Knicks moved on — a move everyone saw coming.

## IT WASN'T ALL ON PAYTON

To be fair, that Atlanta series wasn't lost because of one point guard. Mitchell Robinson — the only player from that roster who would later win a ring with the Knicks — missed most of that season with an injury and didn't play in the series. The Knicks started Nerlens Noel, who couldn't defend the Trae Young–Clint Capela lob game the way Mitch could have. The Knicks kept collapsing, and that led to open threes for Danilo Gallinari, Bogdan Bogdanović, and Kevin Huerter — or Trae floaters when the Knicks dropped, or Capela lobs when they blitzed. The Hawks were the better team. Trae was the better star, and Julius Randle looked exhausted. Payton was one piece of a bigger problem.

But that series changed the franchise. After a couple of misfires — the Kemba Walker and Evan Fournier signings — the Knicks eventually landed the man who changed everything: Jalen Brunson.

## THE LONG ROAD BACK

And Payton? After New York, his road got long. He went to Phoenix for a season, playing about 11 minutes a night, averaging three points, and appearing in two playoff games. Then he was out of the NBA entirely. He played in Puerto Rico. He went to the G League with the Fort Wayne Mad Ants. And in 2023–24, he led the entire G League in assists and made the All-G League third team.

That got him back to the NBA with New Orleans — his home state, which is why Walt "Clyde" Frazier used to call him the "Ragin' Cajun." In November 2024, at 30 years old, he dished out a career-high 21 assists in a game. Then he bounced between New Orleans and Charlotte on 10-day contracts, and was waived again on December 3rd.

Last season, he was with the Austin Spurs in the G League, averaging 6.3 points, six assists, and six rebounds in 29 games. This fall, he signed a training-camp deal with the Sacramento Kings — whose general manager, Scott Perry, was the Knicks executive who originally signed him in New York. And on October 17th, the Kings waived him.

Five NBA teams. Puerto Rico. Multiple G League stops. An NBA camp this fall. A starter at Madison Square Garden just a few years ago, and at 32, still chasing it. Whatever you think of his Knicks years, you have to respect the effort.

## HOW G LEAGUE RIGHTS WORK

Quick explainer, because a lot of fans have never heard of "returning player rights." In the G League, when a player has played for a team, that team can hold onto his rights — meaning if he wants to play in the G League again, he has to play for them. That's why Austin owned Payton's rights after he played there last season. For Westchester to bring him in, they had to acquire those rights, and the price was a G League draft pick. That pick doesn't touch the Knicks' NBA draft picks at all. It's a completely separate system.

So when you see "trade confirmed," think of it as a small transaction between two minor-league affiliates — one that gives Westchester the option to add a veteran if he chooses to play there.

## WHAT PAYTON BRINGS NOW

So what kind of player would Westchester be getting? At 32, Payton isn't the athlete he was in Orlando. But a lot of his strengths still translate to the G League. He's a playmaker first — remember, he led the entire G League in assists just two seasons ago. He rebounds well for a guard — six a game in Austin last season. He has good size for the position and can push the pace in transition. And his feel for the game is the kind of thing young players learn from just by being on the floor with him.

The weakness is the same one that defined his Knicks years: shooting. He's never been a reliable three-point shooter, and that hasn't changed. But in a G League role where his job is to organize the offense and set up younger scorers like Dink Pate and Dalton Knecht, his passing matters a lot more than his jumper.

## HOW FAR THE KNICKS HAVE COME

And honestly, the best part of this story might be what it reminds us about this franchise. In 2021, the Knicks were thrilled just to make the playoffs. The starting point guard was Elfrid Payton. The backup was a 32-year-old Derrick Rose. The team lost in five games to Atlanta, and the city still celebrated, because it had been eight years since the last postseason trip.

Five years later, the Knicks have a championship banner hanging in the Garden. The point guard is Jalen Brunson, the reigning Finals MVP. The bench guards — Alvarado, Deuce, Kolek — would have been starters on that 2021 team. And instead of hoping to make the playoffs, the Knicks are trying to become the first back-to-back champion in years.

Seeing Payton's name pop up again is like opening an old photo album. It reminds you exactly how far the climb has been.

## A LESSON IN PERSISTENCE

And whatever you think of him as a player, there's something worth respecting in Payton's journey. He went from a 10th overall pick and starting point guard to being out of the NBA entirely. He could have quit. Instead, he went to Puerto Rico, then to the G League, led the league in assists, earned his way back, set a career high with 21 assists at age 30, and kept fighting through 10-day contracts and camp invites. Now, at 32, he's still chasing it. That's the kind of mentality you want young players in Westchester to see up close.

## WHY THE KNICKS WOULD WANT HIM AROUND

So why would the Knicks organization bring Payton back now? A few reasons.

**Point-guard insurance.** The Knicks are deep at guard right now: Brunson, Jose Alvarado, Deuce McBride, and Tyler Kolek. But injuries happen. Remember when Brunson turned his ankle in 2025, then Deuce got hurt, and suddenly rookie Tyler Kolek was playing huge minutes? Deuce also played only 41 games last season due to a major injury. If the Knicks ever got that thin again, having a veteran with over 500 NBA games in Westchester — someone who could come up on a 10-day contract — is useful. Not likely. But useful.

**A veteran voice in Westchester.** Westchester is full of young players the Knicks want to develop into cheap contributors — Dink Pate, the 6-foot-8, 20-year-old playmaker who just returned to the organization, and now two-way players like Dalton Knecht, Tyler Nickel, and Jaden Akins who will spend time there. A 32-year-old point guard who led the G League in assists and has NBA playoff experience can mentor those players. That's not a bad idea at all.

**Familiarity.** The organization knows him. Leon Rose arrived in New York while Payton was on the roster — Rose didn't sign him, that was Scott Perry and Steve Mills, but Rose was there for that 2021 playoff run. And remember, this was a G League front office move; every G League team has its own front office. The Knicks' front office surely knew about it, but it doesn't mean Rose personally drove it.

## WHERE HE'D FIT ON THE DEPTH CHART

Let's be realistic about the line in front of him. At point guard, the Knicks have Jalen Brunson as the engine. Behind him, Jose Alvarado — who was the best bench player in the opener with eight points, two threes, three assists, and two steals. Then Deuce McBride, who can handle backup point duties in a pinch even if he's more of a shooting guard. Then Tyler Kolek, the young backup who hit the winning shot in the preseason finale. Landry Shamet and Jordan Clarkson can also handle the ball in short stretches. And on two-way deals, Jaden Akins is another young guard the organization likes.

That's a deep group. So Payton would realistically be the seventh or eighth option at guard for the NBA team — the emergency of the emergency. In Westchester, though, he'd likely be the starting point guard and the organizer of the entire offense. That's the real role.

## WILL HE EVER PLAY FOR THE KNICKS AGAIN?

Honestly? I wouldn't bet on it. The Knicks' NBA roster is full, their 15th spot is blocked by the second apron, and all three two-way spots are taken. For Payton to wear orange and blue again, the Knicks would need a run of guard injuries serious enough to justify a 10-day contract — and even then, they'd likely look at their two-way players first.

But it's still incredible to see him back in the organization in any capacity. Of all the former Knicks point guards who could have returned, Elfrid Payton might be the last one anyone expected.

## THE BIGGER PICTURE: THE WESTCHESTER MACHINE

And this connects to a theme we've been covering all month. The Knicks are building a development machine in Westchester. Dink Pate came back on an Exhibit 10 deal. Dalton Knecht arrived on a two-way after the Lakers waived him. Jaden Akins has impressed the organization. Tyler Nickel is developing as a shooter. Under the second apron, the Knicks need cheap players who can contribute — and Westchester is where those players get built. Adding a veteran point guard to organize that group fits the plan.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. This is a smart, zero-risk depth move disguised as a nostalgia headline. Payton probably never plays another minute for the Knicks — and that's fine. If he joins Westchester, he gives the young guards and wings a steady, experienced leader. And if disaster strikes in the backcourt, the Knicks have a veteran they know who's one phone call away. It's a full-circle moment for a player who's fought for his career every day since he left New York.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. The Westchester Knicks traded a 2026 second-round G League pick — originally from the Valley Suns — to the Austin Spurs for Elfrid Payton's returning player rights. No NBA salary, roster spot, or Knicks draft pick is involved, and there's no indication the Knicks plan to sign him to an NBA deal. Payton, the 10th pick in 2014, had a solid first year in New York in 2019–20, then started every game in 2020–21 before losing his job to Derrick Rose in the Atlanta series. Since then, he's played in Phoenix, Puerto Rico, the G League — where he led the league in assists in 2023–24 — New Orleans, Charlotte, and Austin, and was waived by Sacramento on October 17th. At 32, he could give Westchester a veteran mentor and the Knicks a little point-guard insurance.
