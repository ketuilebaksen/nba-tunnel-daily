## HUNGER LEVEL: MAXIMUM — KARL-ANTHONY TOWNS

For three weeks, the conversation around Towns has been his contract — the tabled extension talks, the reported $225 to $240 million asking range, whether the Knicks should pay him. Meanwhile, he's quietly been the best player on the team, and according to Yahoo Sports' analysis, it hasn't been close.

**KAT through eight games:**
- 19.9 points per game
- A career-high 13.6 rebounds per game
- 3.1 assists per game
- A career-best 42.9% from three on 4.4 attempts per game
- A plus-9.0 net rating, the best among Knicks starters
- A 33.3% defensive rebounding rate, tied for fifth in the entire NBA
- A double-double in every single game — eight for eight

That's not a good start. That's an All-NBA start. And it's happening while Brunson was shaking off rust from his wrist surgery, while Bridges is in the worst slump of his career, and while Hart has been banged up.

## KAT IN HIS OWN WORDS

Here's what Towns said, and I think it's one of the most important quotes of the season so far:

"I came into the season wanting to defend the championship at all costs. The city hasn't seen that trophy here in New York for 53 years, and all of us in this locker room, especially myself, don't intend for it to leave."

That's a player in the middle of a contract standoff saying exactly the right things — and more importantly, playing exactly the right way. No complaining. No drama. Just production.

## THE ROBINSON EFFECT ON KAT

And here's the uncomfortable part. Towns admitted that Mitchell Robinson's departure forced him to change his game:

"With Mitch gone, I had to pick up the effort, especially the rebounding effort. When you have one of the greatest offensive rebounders the NBA has ever seen in Mitchell Robinson, it's a team effort. I just want to do my part."

Think about that. KAT is covering for the hole Robinson left — rebounding at a career-high rate — while his own contract sits unresolved. And as we talked about recently, Robinson's offensive rebounding in Boston has dropped from 4.2 a game to 1.8 as the Celtics pull him away from the rim. So the Knicks lost Mitch's superpower, and KAT has had to absorb part of it.

## DOES KAT'S PLAY HELP OR HURT THE CONTRACT TALKS?

Here's the honest answer: both.

**It helps the relationship.** Towns is proving he's all-in. He's not sulking or holding back. He's leading by example.

**It makes the price go up.** Every double-double, every efficient night from three, every big rebounding game adds to his case. The better he plays, the more he's worth — and the more expensive this gets for a front office that's only about $370,000 under the second apron.

My view: the Knicks need to find a way. You don't let your best player play out a contract standoff during a title defense if you can avoid it. Whether that's a deal during the season or a commitment once the summer comes, KAT has earned the conversation. That's my opinion — not a report.

## HUNGER LEVEL: HIGH — THE CLIPPERS IDEA

Now to the trade idea that keeps coming up. According to Knicks On SI, the Los Angeles Clippers could be a natural trade partner, and the reason is their situation.

**The Clippers' problem:** They're 1–7, and the article describes them as being in one of the worst franchise positions since the post-Kevin Garnett trade Nets. Why? Their draft future is badly compromised:
- They forfeit control of five consecutive first-round picks from 2029 through 2033
- They owe Oklahoma City a pick swap next summer
- They owe their 2028 first to Philadelphia, and their 2029 first too if it isn't top three
- And the newer lottery rules don't reward teams for bottoming out

As the article put it: how can this regime sell hope? By accumulating future draft capital — even if it means moving young players.

**The name to know: Yanic Konan Niederhäuser.**
- **Size:** About 6-foot-11
- **Draft:** The last pick of the 2025 first round
- **Contract:** Less than $3 million this season, with team options for the next two years
- **The style:** He rebounds, blocks shots, and finishes above the rim. Thirty-five percent of his baskets last season were dunks — compare that to Mitchell Robinson's 40%. Twenty-nine of his 64 field goals were throwdowns.

**The second name: Baba Miller.**
- **Age:** 22
- **Size:** About 6-foot-11
- **Draft:** No. 36 overall in 2025
- **The style:** He showed three-point willingness in college and has made 50% of his twos this year — a small sample, but there's skill there.

## DOES THE CLIPPERS IDEA WORK?

**The fit:** The article makes the point that the Spurs, Thunder, Cavaliers, and Celtics are all better or deeper at center than the Knicks. Niederhäuser may never become an Evan Mobley or a Chet Holmgren. But if he lands somewhere around the level of Drummond and Wiseman — with more rim pressure — that helps plug the most concerning hole on a defending champion.

**The cap:** A salary under $3 million can match with Pacôme Dadiet's roughly $3 million expiring deal. That fits under the second apron — exactly the "Tier 1" move we've been talking about.

**The price:** Here's the catch. The Clippers need picks. The Knicks have only one tradable first-round pick, in 2033, plus some second-round picks from the Sergio de Larrea draft-night deal. Would seconds and Dadiet be enough? Maybe — if the Clippers value a cheap expiring contract and future seconds. But young bigs on team-friendly deals are exactly what rebuilding teams usually want to keep.

**My verdict:** Worth a call after December 15th. Not worth the 2033 first. Cheap and young sounds great — until it's January and a young big can't stay on the floor in a tight game. So the Knicks should only do it if the price is truly favorable.

## HUNGER LEVEL: SKY-HIGH — JAMES WISEMAN

And here's the thing: the Knicks might already have an internal version of that fix.

**The before:** Through the first seven games, Wiseman was a DNP four times. In the three games he did play, he logged a total of 10 minutes and didn't make a single basket. We even made a whole video about the "hard truth" facing him.

**The breakout:** Then came Wednesday against Chicago. In about 10 minutes, Wiseman made four baskets, grabbed eight rebounds, and blocked three shots.

**The caveat:** Yes, it came in garbage time of a blowout, and per-36 numbers for a guy playing those minutes can be a trap. But look at this instead.

**The blocks stat that matters:**
- Only two Knicks are blocking at least one shot per game: Dalton Knecht — who has played one game — and Wiseman.
- Four Knicks are tied for the team lead with four total blocks:

| Player | Minutes played | Blocks |
|---|---|---|
| OG Anunoby | 263 | 4 |
| Karl-Anthony Towns | 250 | 4 |
| Andre Drummond | 106 | 4 |
| James Wiseman | 21 | 4 |

Twenty-one minutes. Four blocks. The same total as two starters who have played more than ten times as much.

## WHERE THE KNICKS REALLY MISS MITCH

And here's the most interesting detail from Knicks On SI's analysis. The Knicks are blocking about 4.4 shots a game, which ranks 23rd in the league. But that's not actually where they miss Robinson most.

Where they miss him is finishing over defenders. Last season, Knicks opponents had the seventh-fewest blocks in the league. This season, they have the fourth-most. In other words, Knicks shots are getting rejected at the rim far more often.

Translation: Drummond tends to get blocked around the basket. Wiseman dunks over people. That's a real difference. A big man who finishes above the rim forces defenses to respect the lob, which opens everything else.

**My verdict:** Wiseman has earned a longer look. Not 25 minutes a night — but a real, defined backup role for a few games, especially when KAT is in foul trouble. Let's find out if Wednesday was real.

## HUNGER LEVEL: HIGH — MOHAMED DIAWARA

Now the wing version of the same story. According to Knicks On SI, Mohamed Diawara's latest showing is one the Knicks simply can't ignore.

**The line against Chicago:**
- A season-high 21 minutes, mostly because Josh Hart's ankle acted up
- 8 points, 6 rebounds, 4 assists, and 2 blocks
- 2-of-5 from the field, 1-of-4 from three, and 3-of-4 at the line

**Why the behavior matters more than the stats:** The pattern that has held Diawara back is that he's either too hesitant or trying to do too much. Both get him in trouble. Against Chicago, he found the balance — confident, not second-guessing, but not overreaching — and he was active defensively the whole night. The article said it's the kind of Diawara the Knicks loved last season, and the kind they'll need this season.

**Why it matters for the roster:** Without him, the Knicks are a bit undersized on the wing. OG and Bridges can't play every minute. Landry Shamet and Jordan Clarkson have been great, but they aren't big. Diawara is 6-foot-9 with real defensive versatility. With Hart out, that size matters even more.

**The fair question:** Was this a real step, or just a blowout? One 21-minute night against a rebuilding Bulls team doesn't prove anything. But the upside — a true rotation wing who defends, passes, and can shoot — is high enough that the Knicks have to find out.

And remember, Mike Brown said earlier this season that the team's early struggles forced him to pull back on Diawara's minutes. Now that the Knicks have won three of four, maybe that door reopens.

**My verdict:** If Diawara is real, the Knicks don't need to trade for a wing. That's a huge deal for a team with almost no trade assets.

## HUNGER LEVEL: OFF THE CHARTS — THE CHIP-ON-THE-SHOULDER TRIO

And now the idea that ties it all together. According to Daily Knicks, Dalton Knecht fits right into a new trio of players with something to prove — and it's one of the smartest takes I've read this season.

**The problem every champion faces:** After you win, the hunger fades. The Knicks' starting lineup ranges from 29 to 31 years old. They won. They got their rings. What's left to prove?

**The trio that hasn't won anything yet:**

**Dalton Knecht.** Cut by the Lakers at the end of the offseason. The Bucks reportedly wanted draft picks just to take him in a trade. Nobody claimed him on waivers. He's trying to prove everyone wrong — and he started with 10 points and seven rebounds in his debut after Mike Brown had him down for "zero minutes."

**Andre Drummond.** He spent the summer believing veteran minimum contracts were beneath him. The market told him otherwise. Now he's fighting a league-wide perception of his worth.

**James Wiseman.** He was reportedly set to continue his career overseas before the Knicks' need for a center opened a door. He's fighting to stay in the NBA.

As the article put it, these are exactly the type of players who can help charge up the rest of the Knicks, even through the day-to-day grind of the regular season.

## THE ALVARADO AND CLARKSON BLUEPRINT

The article also makes a comparison that really lands. Last season, Jose Alvarado brought that chip-on-the-shoulder energy and helped keep the locker room humble. Jordan Clarkson's transformation — from a high-volume scorer into a team-first role player — happened as part of that same culture. Now it's Knecht, Drummond, and Wiseman's turn.

That's how a championship culture stays alive. Not through speeches. Through guys who need it more than the guys who already have a ring. Through desperation that rubs off on everyone else in practice, in the locker room, and in garbage time minutes that suddenly feel like playoff minutes.

And honestly, you can add a couple more names to that list: Tyler Kolek, who waited years for a real role and grabbed the backup point guard job; and Diawara, who's trying to prove he's more than a prospect.

## WHY HUNGER BEATS TALENT IN NOVEMBER

Let's be honest about the long grind of an NBA season. In November, the stars are still finding their rhythm. Bridges is in a slump, Brunson is still knocking off the rust, and Hart is dealing with an ankle issue. On nights like that, the difference between a win and a loss often comes from the guys at the end of the bench — the ones who treat every minute like it's Game 7 because, for them, it kind of is.

That's exactly what we saw against Chicago. The Knicks' bench scored 53 points. Wiseman blocked three shots. Diawara played confident, two-way basketball. Knecht threw down a putback slam. None of them were playing for a contract extension or a highlight on a championship banner. They were playing for their careers.

And that energy matters for the stars too. When a starter watches a two-way player dive for a loose ball in a 30-point game, it sends a message. Nobody gets to coast.

## HUNGER LEVEL: RISING — THE STARS

To be fair, the hunger isn't only at the bottom of the roster. Brunson has been showing it in his own way — he came in early to get shots up on the second night of a back-to-back, then dropped 22 in a single quarter against the Bulls. Hart told the world the team was tired and then went out and fought through it. And Towns, as we covered, is playing like a man on a mission. The difference is that the trio's hunger is visible every single possession. That's the spark.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. KAT keeps the double-double streak alive into double digits, and by the end of November, the "Should the Knicks pay him?" debate becomes "How fast can they pay him?" Wiseman gets real backup minutes in at least two of the next four games and keeps blocking shots. Diawara becomes a regular in the rotation while Hart is out — and stays there when Hart returns, in smaller minutes. On the Clippers, I don't expect a deal before December 15th, but if the Knicks make a backup-center move this season, a young, cheap rim-runner like Niederhäuser is exactly the profile. And the big one: the hunger of the chip-on-the-shoulder group is a real reason the Knicks can break that eight-year streak of failed repeats.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. No one "just arrived" — this is about players already on the roster and one trade idea. Karl-Anthony Towns has been the best Knick through eight games: 19.9 points, a career-high 13.6 rebounds, 42.9% from three, a plus-9.0 net rating, and a double-double in every game, all while covering for Mitchell Robinson's departure and playing without a new contract. The Clippers, at 1–7 with their draft future compromised, could be a trade partner, with Yanic Konan Niederhäuser — under $3 million, team options, and a Robinson-like dunk rate — as an analyst-floated target, but there are no reported talks. James Wiseman has four blocks in 21 minutes, and Mohamed Diawara had eight points, six rebounds, four assists, and two blocks in 21 minutes against Chicago. And the chip-on-the-shoulder trio of Knecht, Drummond, and Wiseman might be the fuel that helps this team do what the last eight champions couldn't.
