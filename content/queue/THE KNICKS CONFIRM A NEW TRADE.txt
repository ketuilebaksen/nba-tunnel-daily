## WHERE THINGS STAND BEFORE THE MAILBAG

Quick context before we open the inbox. The Knicks started the season 0–3, the first defending champion to do that since the 2011–12 Mavericks. Then they went on a tear: wins over Detroit and Chicago, a loss in Philadelphia, and then four straight — Brooklyn, a 135–105 rout of Chicago, a 109–97 win in Cleveland, and a 117–85 demolition of Milwaukee. Jalen Brunson was named Eastern Conference Player of the Week. Then Cleveland got its revenge at the Garden on the second night of the back-to-back, and the streak ended.

So the Knicks are 6–5, with a road trip coming up to Indiana and then two straight games in Toronto. Good, not great. And that's exactly why so many of you are asking about trades, rotations, and the roster. Let's open the mailbag.

## QUESTION 1: "DID THE KNICKS ACTUALLY MAKE A TRADE?"

**Short answer: No.**

I know some videos this week are saying the Knicks "confirmed a new trade." They didn't. There's no trade, no signing, and no reported agreement with any team. What's actually happening is a lot of analysis about what the Knicks *could* do before the deadline, and some interesting new details about why their options are so limited. That's what we'll cover today. But if anyone tells you a trade was confirmed — it wasn't.

## QUESTION 2: "IS TYLER KOLEK READY FOR AN EVEN BIGGER ROLE?"

This might be the most interesting question of the week, because Mike Brown just gave us a big clue.

**What happened:** In the blowout win over Milwaukee, Brown closed out the first quarter with a lineup of Tyler Kolek, Landry Shamet, Mohamed Diawara, Deuce McBride, and Andre Drummond. Notice who's missing? Neither Jalen Brunson nor Karl-Anthony Towns was on the floor.

**The result:** In just three minutes, that lineup outscored the Bucks by five points.

**Why it matters:** For much of Brown's time in New York, lineups without both Brunson and Towns have struggled badly. In more than 300 competitive minutes without those two stars, the Knicks have posted a net rating of about minus-6 points per 100 possessions. That's a big hole.

And before the Bucks game, Kolek had played fewer than 10 competitive minutes without Brunson and Towns on the floor. So Brown was clearly experimenting — putting Kolek in charge of a lineup with no stars to lean on.

**The upside:** If Kolek can keep the offense running without relying on the stars, it reduces the physical toll on Brunson and Towns. Remember the two-minute tracking data Brown talked about. Brunson can handle heavy minutes, but the goal is to preserve him for the moments that matter. A Kolek-led unit that holds its own is exactly how you do that over 82 games.

**The chemistry:** Kolek has also shown real chemistry with Towns when they share the floor — that pick-and-roll connection, the behind-the-back pass in Chicago. Giving the Knicks another playmaker makes the offense less predictable.

**The money:** He's earning less than $2.3 million this season. For a team with almost no cap flexibility, a young, productive, affordable point guard is gold. Compare that to what veteran backup point guards cost on the open market, and you see why the front office loves this development.

**The honest caveat:** Three minutes isn't a sample. It's a signal. One good stretch doesn't mean that lineup works all season. But the confidence Brown is showing is a big sign for Kolek's development.

**My answer:** Yes — he's ready for a bigger role, but in doses. Ten to 15 minutes as the backup point guard is his job now. A few minutes running a no-stars lineup is the next test. If he passes that, he becomes one of the most valuable players on the roster relative to his salary.

## QUESTION 3: "WHY CAN'T THE KNICKS JUST SIGN A CENTER?"

This one comes up every single day, so let's explain it once and for all.

**The new number:** According to ESPN's Bobby Marks, the Knicks have only about $89,000 of room under their hard spending line. That's even tighter than the roughly $370,000 that was reported earlier this season. Either way, it's basically nothing.

**What that means:** It prevents the Knicks from signing anyone new until around February 14th. Why then? Because minimum contracts are prorated — the later in the season you sign someone, the less of their salary counts. By mid-February, a minimum deal finally becomes small enough to fit.

**The other restrictions:**
- **The Knicks can't take on more salary in a trade than they send out.** Every trade has to be salary-neutral or cheaper.
- **They can't sign waived players who were earning $15 million or more.** So if a big-name veteran gets bought out, the Knicks are likely shut out.

**The owner:** James Dolan has made it clear he doesn't intend to significantly increase spending. The second apron is a line he won't cross.

**So the short answer:** They can't just sign a center because they literally don't have the room. Any frontcourt addition before February has to come through a trade where they send out at least as much salary as they take back.

## QUESTION 4: "WHAT TRADE ASSETS DO THE KNICKS HAVE?"

Good question, because people assume the cupboard is completely empty. It's not — it's just limited.

**The draft picks:** According to the reporting, the Knicks have one first-round pick and 12 second-round picks available for potential trades. Yes, 12 second-rounders. That's a lot of sweeteners. Some of that came from smart moves like the draft-night Sergio de Larrea deal, when the Knicks picked up extra future seconds.

**The salary pieces:**
- **Pacôme Dadiet** — about $3 million, now on what's effectively an expiring deal after his fourth-year option was declined
- **Deuce McBride** — about $3.9 million, expiring
- **Jose Alvarado** — about $4.4 million, trade-eligible after December 15th
- **Bigger contracts** — Josh Hart, Mikal Bridges, and the core players

**The catch:** Picks help sweeten a deal, but they don't solve the salary problem. The Knicks still need to match money going out with money coming in. So the realistic trades look like this:
- **Small:** Dadiet plus second-round picks for a minimum-level big
- **Medium:** Dadiet plus Alvarado for a player around $7 million
- **Large:** Adding Deuce for a player around $11 million

We broke all of this down in our Trade Calculator video. But the bottom line is the same: lots of picks, very little salary room.

**A bonus point on those 12 second-rounders:** Second-round picks are more useful than they sound. Rebuilding teams love them because they're cheap ways to add young players without guaranteed money. And for a team like the Knicks, they're the main currency for small upgrades. A struggling team that wants to dump a useful veteran might take a couple of seconds plus Dadiet's expiring contract. That's how the Knicks have found value before, and it's how they'll likely find it again.

## QUESTION 5: "IS ANDRE DRUMMOND ENOUGH AT BACKUP CENTER?"

This is the real question behind all the trade talk.

**The case that he is:** Drummond has been better than most people expected. He leads the NBA in offensive rebounds per 36 minutes among players with at least 100 minutes. He grabbed 14 rebounds in about 15 minutes against Milwaukee. And Mike Brown has praised his improving pick-and-roll defense and communication. The Knicks lead the entire league in rebounding percentage, and Drummond is a big part of that.

**The case that he isn't:** In the playoffs, against the East's toughest frontcourts, the Knicks need rim protection and switchability. That's where Mitchell Robinson made the biggest difference last spring. Drummond's defense has improved, but it's still the question mark.

**My answer:** For the regular season, Drummond is enough. For a deep playoff run, the Knicks would love one more big who protects the rim — even a cheap one. That's the move to watch for after December 15th. And James Wiseman's eight points, eight rebounds, and three blocks against Chicago keep him in the picture too.

## QUESTION 6: "SHOULD THE KNICKS TRADE MIKAL BRIDGES?"

The toughest question in the mailbag. Let's be fair and thorough.

**The contract:** Bridges is set to earn about $116.5 million over the next three seasons after this one, including a player option for 2029–30. That's on top of this season's salary. It's a massive commitment.

**The performance:** His start has been the worst of his career — around six points a game, shooting near 30% from the field and under 27% from three through the first stretch, with minutes dropping to 22 or fewer in several games. His point-of-attack defense has slipped too, and Mike Brown has turned to Josh Hart and Landry Shamet in crunch time.

**The trade problem:** According to ESPN's Vincent Goodwill, the Knicks have limited financial flexibility and are pushing some key contract decisions to the offseason. And trading Bridges right now would mean finding a team willing to take on a huge contract from a player who's clearly not at his best. Why would anyone do that without real assurance of what they're getting? That's the hurdle. His trade value is at its lowest.

**The twist:** If Bridges gets back to his best form, the Knicks might not want to trade him at all. They still need a wing who can create, hit big shots, defend, and balance the floor next to Brunson and Towns. A Bridges bounce-back might be the single best "addition" the Knicks could make this season.

**And there are signs:** In Cleveland on November 7th, he was four-of-13, but aggressive — six rebounds, four assists, and two blocks. Aggression usually comes before the shot returns.

**The fan frustration is fair, too.** Watching a player the Knicks gave up five first-round picks for struggle like this is painful. But frustration isn't a trade strategy. The best version of this team still has a confident Mikal Bridges in it.

**My answer:** Don't trade him now. Selling at his lowest value, with a contract this size, is the opposite of smart asset management. The Knicks should focus on getting him right — easy looks early, simpler reads, confidence-building minutes. If he's still struggling in late January, the conversation changes. But not today.

## QUESTION 7: "WHAT ABOUT KAT'S CONTRACT?"

You can't do a Knicks mailbag right now without this one.

**The situation:** Karl-Anthony Towns has been the Knicks' best player through the early season — a double-double machine who's shooting 54% from the floor and 39% from three, with fewer fouls than last year. He's one double-double away from tying Willis Reed's franchise record for the longest double-double streak to open a season.

**The standoff:** His extension talks are still unresolved. The Knicks have reportedly held firm in the $45 to $50 million per year range, and the New York Post's Stefan Bondy reported he turned down about $200 million over four years this summer.

**How it connects to everything else:** This is why the cap numbers matter so much. With about $89,000 of room, no ability to take on salary, and Dolan not wanting to spend more, every decision is connected. Pay KAT more, and there's less room for depth. Keep the bench cheap — like Kolek at under $2.3 million — and there's more room to pay the stars.

**My answer:** The Knicks will find a way to keep him. He's too important, and he's playing too well. But it probably won't happen until the offseason, when the front office can see the full picture — including Brunson's extension window and Josh Hart's team option.

## QUESTION 8: "WHAT'S THE BIGGEST THING TO WATCH NEXT?"

Here's my list for the next couple of weeks:

- **Josh Hart's return.** He missed time with an ankle issue and then an illness. Getting him back changes the defense and the rotation.
- **The no-stars lineups.** Does Brown keep experimenting with Kolek running the show without Brunson and Towns?
- **OG's shooting.** Five threes against Milwaukee. Can he keep it going?
- **Bridges' aggressiveness.** Is he attacking and getting to the line?
- **KAT and Willis Reed.** One more double-double ties the record.
- **December 15th.** That's when the trade market really opens up, and when Alvarado becomes eligible.

## QUESTION 9: "ARE THE KNICKS STILL A REAL CONTENDER?"

Let's end the mailbag with the big-picture question.

**The record:** 6–5 after the four-game win streak ended against Cleveland. Not where anyone expected the defending champs to be, but a lot better than the 0–3 start.

**The projections:** ESPN's projections still have the Knicks finishing around 54–28, with about a 42% chance of reaching the Eastern Conference Finals. That's a contender.

**The numbers:** Through 10 games, the Knicks were top 10 in offensive rating, sixth in defensive rating, sixth in net rating, and first in the NBA in rebounding percentage.

**The concerns:** Bridges and OG need to keep finding their shots — OG did hit five threes against Milwaukee. Josh Hart needs to get fully healthy. And the backup center spot is still a question for the playoffs.

**My answer:** Yes, they're a real contender. Not the favorite — the East got tougher, and the betting markets have the Knicks behind teams like Philadelphia and Boston. But a team with Brunson playing at a Player of the Week level, Towns playing the best two-way basketball of his career, an elite rebounding identity, and a deep, cheap bench? That's a team nobody wants to see in May.

When they do make one, it's small — Dadiet's expiring deal plus a couple of those second-round picks for a backup big who can protect the rim. Kolek becomes a regular part of the no-stars lineups by the end of November, and those lineups stop being a weakness. Bridges stays and slowly climbs back toward his normal level.
