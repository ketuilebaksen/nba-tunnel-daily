## FIRST: WHAT'S REAL AND WHAT ISN'T

**Not real:** A "massive trade." There is no trade involving the Knicks and the Lakers.

**Not real:** A confirmed signing. The Knicks have not signed Lawson Lovering, and nobody has reported talks.

**Real:** The Lakers waived Lovering from his two-way contract earlier this week, and no team claimed him. HoopsHype's Michael Scott reported it on November 10th. And Knicks analysis has pointed to him as a possible fit for New York's backup center need.

So keep it in the "idea" bucket. Now let's build the board.

## WHERE THE KNICKS STAND

Quick context. The Knicks are 6–6 after back-to-back losses — at home to Cleveland, then in Indiana. They've struggled to climb above .500 in the early weeks. Mike Brown is still searching for the right lineup combinations. He's trying to help Mikal Bridges rediscover his confidence during an extended shooting slump. And Josh Hart only just returned after missing time with an ankle injury and then an illness.

The loss also hurt in the NBA Cup. The Knicks are 0–2 in group play, and their hopes of defending the Cup title are hanging by a thread.

But here's the good news: almost every problem we're about to list has a fix.

## PROBLEM #1: THE KNICKS ARE PLAYING SMALLER THAN THEY ARE

**The evidence:** This team prides itself on rebounding. Yet in Indiana, it lost the battle on the glass to a Pacers team that's been one of the worst rebounding clubs in the league.
- Indiana grabbed **17 offensive rebounds**.
- Towns had **15 rebounds** — more than the other four Knicks starters combined, who totaled just **11**.
- Ivica Zubac led Indiana with **13 rebounds**, and the rest of the Pacers starters added **19** more.

That's not a talent problem. That's an effort-and-structure problem. KAT can't win every rebound by himself.

**The solution:**
- **Josh Hart's return.** Hart crashes the glass like a big man. Getting him back to full strength is the single fastest fix.
- **More two-big lineups.** Mike Brown could try more minutes with Towns alongside Andre Drummond when the rebounding battle tilts.
- **Wings who crash.** OG Anunoby and Mikal Bridges both need to contribute on the defensive glass — "bodies on bodies," as Brown likes to say.

## PROBLEM #2: THE KNICKS AREN'T THE MORE PHYSICAL TEAM

**The evidence:** Look at the free-throw line over the last two games.
- **Against Cleveland:** The Cavaliers took **25 free throws**. The Knicks took **11**.
- **In Indiana, first half:** The Pacers shot **20 free throws**. The Knicks shot **3**.

Mike Brown has stressed playing with force all season. In these two games, the Knicks were on the wrong side of that.

**The solution:**
- **Attack the rim more.** OG has been getting to the line at a career-high rate this season — more of that, from more players.
- **Get Bridges driving.** Brown has asked him to lean into his strengths: running, cutting, rebounding, and driving. A downhill Bridges draws fouls.
- **Defend without reaching.** Fewer cheap fouls on the other end means fewer free throws for opponents.

## PROBLEM #3: FRUSTRATION IS BOILING OVER

**The evidence:** Three technical fouls in a game decided by two points.

- Late in the first half, OG appeared to get fouled on a drive with no call. **Mike Brown picked up a rare technical** — and his team responded. Brunson and Tyler Kolek helped cut a 14-point deficit to single digits.
- Early in the second half, down 12, Brown called a quick timeout, said a few words, and let the players handle it. KAT and Bridges each hit a three, Brunson dropped in a floater, and Bridges added a jumper to get within five.
- But in the fourth quarter, with the Knicks down just two, **KAT was hit with a technical** after not getting a call he felt he deserved. Two minutes later, Indiana's lead was back up to 12.

The games the Knicks found ways to win last spring are the games they're finding ways to lose this fall.

**The solution:**
- **Channel it, don't spend it.** Brown's technical worked because it came at the right time and fired the team up. KAT's came at the worst possible time. Champions turn frustration into the next stop, not the next whistle.
- **Leadership from the vets.** Brunson, Hart, and Towns have all been here before. A calm word in a huddle can save a possession — and sometimes a game.

## PROBLEM #4: THE OFFENSE ISN'T ITSELF

**The evidence:** The Knicks have scored more than 100 points in 11 of their 12 games. But look at the split:
- **Three games against Milwaukee and Chicago:** about 128 points per game.
- **The other nine games:** about 104.

For this group, 104 is not a big number.

**The context:** It looks more like a temporary slump than a broken system, given how strong this offense has been in recent seasons. But the schedule is getting tougher, and November losses add up in a deep Eastern Conference.

**The solution:**
- **Better starts.** The Knicks jumped out to an 18–9 lead in Indiana, then gave it all back and fell behind by 14. That pattern — strong start, then a long hole — has shown up too often.
- **Get OG and Bridges going.** When both wings are cold, defenses load up on Brunson and Towns. OG's five-three night against Milwaukee showed what's possible.
- **Keep feeding the hot hands.** Which brings us to Deuce.

## THE SOLUTION THAT'S ALREADY HERE: DEUCE McBRIDE

Before we get to Lovering, let's talk about a solution the Knicks already have. Deuce McBride is sending them a very simple message: you need me.

**The season so far:**
- **19.2 minutes** per game
- **8.0 points** per game
- **44% from three**

**Since the rough night in Chicago** — when he went one-for-seven from deep — Deuce has averaged **9.1 points** and hit **46% of his threes**. Five threes against Cleveland. Three more in Indiana. When the ball swings his way, he's ready.

**The defense is better too.** His defensive rating is **109.6** this season, down from **116.1** last year. It's a small sample, but he's competing at the point of attack, staying in front of ball handlers, and giving Mike Brown a guard he trusts with the second unit.

**The fit:** With Brunson carrying the scoring and Kolek handling backup point guard duties, the Knicks need guards who can play off the ball, space the floor, and defend without needing plays called for them. Deuce does all three. He doesn't demand a big role. He takes what the game gives him — and lately, it's been giving him open threes.

**The energy:** He picks up ball handlers full court, fights through screens, and rarely takes a play off. On flat nights, those effort plays change the tone.

## THE DEUCE TRADE QUESTION

His name has come up in trade talk — which is common for a mid-sized, expiring contract on a contender that might want to make a move. But the case for keeping him gets stronger every week.

**Two kinds of value:** On one side, the draft picks or future assets a Deuce trade might bring. On the other, what he gives the Knicks on the court right now. For a defending champion whose core isn't getting any younger, the second matters more.

**The signal:** Trading a productive rotation player for future picks in the middle of a title defense would send the wrong message. Keeping him says the Knicks are focused on now.

**The caveat:** He's not untouchable. Once Brown has a clearer picture of his rotation, the front office could revisit it if the right deal comes along. But mid-November isn't the time.

Looking back, one of the smartest moves the Knicks made coming into this season might be the one they didn't make: keeping Deuce. It's starting to look like a quiet win.

## PROBLEM #5: THE BACKUP CENTER

And now the problem that ties back to our title. What happens at center when Towns isn't on the floor?

**Andre Drummond:** He was brought in to handle the backup role. He hasn't been overly impressive overall, but he's rebounded better than many expected — and extra possessions matter for an offense that's been stuck. The concern is foot speed, especially late in close games when opponents attack him in space. And if KAT ever missed time, the Knicks would be leaning on Drummond for far more minutes than they'd like.

**James Wiseman:** He's played just 26 minutes in 12 games. His minutes have been inconsistent, but when he plays, he hits the offensive glass. Per 36 minutes, Wiseman grabs more offensive rebounds than anyone on the roster — roughly 50% more than Drummond. But a third center who barely plays leaves the Knicks thin if injuries or foul trouble hit.

For a team with championship expectations, that's not ideal. Especially after a night when Indiana grabbed 17 offensive boards.

## THE CANDIDATE: LAWSON LOVERING

Here's the idea that's been floated.

**Who he is:**
- **Age:** 23
- **Height:** 7 feet, with a 7-foot-5 wingspan
- **Background:** Undrafted out of Utah

**How he became available:** The Lakers signed him to a two-year, two-way deal on October 28th. Less than two weeks later, they moved on and signed center Carson Cooper instead. Lovering played in just two games for the Lakers — about two minutes total. Then they waived him, and no team put in a claim.

**Why the release isn't a red flag:** It says more about the Lakers than about Lovering. They're off to a 10–2 start with a win-now roster. They simply didn't have room to develop a young big.

**His track record:**
- **Memphis, last February:** He signed a 10-day contract and made his NBA debut in Denver — three points, four rebounds, and two steals in 18 minutes.
- **Memphis Hustle, G League:** 30 games, averaging 7.7 points, 7.0 rebounds, and 1.3 blocks in 26.8 minutes.
- **The grind:** Memphis let him go in late September after a training camp deal. He spent a month as a free agent before the Lakers called.

That kind of path is common for undrafted big men — and it often produces players who know their role and will do the dirty work winning teams need.

**His game:** Sturdy and no-frills. He sets screens, crashes the glass, runs the floor, finishes at the rim, and protects the paint. He's not a shooter.

## WHY THE FIT MAKES SENSE

**The job description:** The Knicks mainly want their backup centers to rebound, defend, and leave the outside shooting to others. Drummond showed some three-point range last season, but he's taken just one three this year. Lovering fits that job exactly.

**The style:** Compared with Wiseman, Lovering offers a different kind of upside — less flash, more structure, and a defensive mindset that matches how Mike Brown wants this team to play. For a staff that values effort and physicality — the exact things missing in Indiana — that profile is easy to like.

**The growth:** At 23, he's young enough to develop into a bigger role over the season.

**The money:** This is the key. A two-way contract wouldn't touch the main rotation or the salary picture. For a team with only about $89,000 of room under its hard spending line, according to ESPN's Bobby Marks, that matters more than anything.

## THE COST: A TWO-WAY SPOT

Here's the roster math. The Knicks' two-way spots are full. Signing Lovering would most likely mean letting go of Jaden Akins.

**Would that hurt?** Probably not much. The Knicks are deep at guard — Brunson, Deuce, Kolek, Shamet, Clarkson, and Alvarado. Akins' role isn't essential right now. And Dalton Knecht, who holds another two-way spot, has already shown he can contribute.

**The trade-off:** Swapping a surplus guard for a developmental big addresses a clear need without touching the rotation or the salary. It's the kind of low-risk move contenders make all the time during a season.

## WHAT LOVERING WOULDN'T FIX

To keep it honest, let's be clear about the limits. Lovering isn't a savior.

- **He's unproven.** Two minutes with the Lakers and one 18-minute game with Memphis. That's the NBA résumé.
- **He won't play much at first.** On a two-way deal, he'd likely split time with Westchester, just like Knecht.
- **He doesn't fix the starting lineup's rebounding.** That's on OG, Bridges, Hart, and Brunson helping KAT.
- **He doesn't replace a trade.** If the Knicks want a real rim protector for the playoffs, that's still a December 15th-and-beyond conversation, using pieces like Pacôme Dadiet's expiring contract and some of their 12 second-round picks.

What he would do is give the Knicks a cheap, young, physical body in the pipeline. That's insurance — not an answer. And insurance is exactly what a thin position needs.

## THE ONE BRIGHT SPOT WE CAN'T SKIP

Before the call, let's give credit where it's due. Inside that loss, two stars delivered.

**Jalen Brunson:** 33 points and 11 assists, including 16 in the third quarter alone. After Pacers guard Quenton Jackson got in his ear, Brunson smiled — and then went on a tear that pulled the Knicks right back in.

**Karl-Anthony Towns:** 23 points, 15 rebounds, and five assists — and his 12th straight double-double to open the season, a new Knicks record. He passed Willis Reed. On a frustrating night, that's history worth celebrating.

And for the record: Indiana, playing without Tyrese Haliburton, had all five starters in double figures — Pascal Siakam 19, Aaron Nesmith 18, Kelly Oubre Jr. 16, and Zubac and Andrew Nembhard 12 each. Siakam even missed two free throws with 23 seconds left. The Knicks just couldn't cash in. Both teams shot 39-of-90. The game was decided on the glass and at the line.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. The Knicks add a developmental big on a two-way deal before December — Lovering or a player with that exact profile — and Akins is the one who makes room. Deuce stays through the deadline. The rebounding problem from Indiana gets fixed fast once Hart is fully back. The technical fouls settle down, because this is too veteran a group to keep beating itself. And KAT keeps extending his new franchise double-double record.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. There is no "massive trade" and no confirmed signing. The Lakers waived 7-foot, 23-year-old Lawson Lovering from his two-way deal, and he's been floated as a fit for the Knicks' backup center need — a no-frills screener, rebounder, and rim protector with a 7-foot-5 wingspan who averaged 7.7 points, 7.0 rebounds, and 1.3 blocks in the G League. Signing him would likely cost Jaden Akins' two-way spot, with no salary impact. The 106–104 loss in Indiana exposed four other problems — rebounding, physicality, frustration, and offense — and all of them are fixable. Meanwhile, Deuce McBride is shooting 44% from three with better defense than last season, making the case to keep him stronger every week. And KAT now owns the Knicks record with 12 straight double-doubles to start a season.
