## THE HONEST PART FIRST

Before anything else, let's be clear. Moussa Cissé has NOT been released. Nobody has reported that Dallas has made that decision. What Marc Stein did was raise the question — publicly — of whether the Mavericks might do it. That's a real development, because Stein is one of the most connected reporters in the league and he doesn't throw names around for fun. But this video is about a possibility, not a done deal. Everything about the Knicks' next steps is NBA Tunnel analysis, clearly labeled. With that said, this is a possibility worth understanding — because if it happens, it'll happen fast.

## THE BACKSTORY: THE OFFER SHEET THAT DIDN'T STICK

Quick refresher for anyone who missed our earlier coverage. This summer, Cissé was a restricted free agent. The Knicks liked him so much that they signed him to a two-year offer sheet. And here's how restricted free agency works: when a player signs an offer sheet with a new team, his old team gets the right to match it. Dallas did exactly that, pretty quickly, and Cissé stayed a Maverick.

Then came the next chapter, which we covered in our "ghost clause" video. His deal was partially guaranteed until October 1st. That date was the Mavericks' last easy exit — they could've released him before then without owing the full salary. They didn't. They kept him on the roster past October 1st, and his $2.19 million salary for this season became fully guaranteed. Next season, there's a team option worth about $2.57 million.

So for most people, that was the end of it. Dallas matched in July and guaranteed him in October. Why would they let him go now? And that's exactly the question Marc Stein asked.

## THE STEIN QUESTION: WHY POWELL CHANGES EVERYTHING

Here's the gist of Stein's report. Would the Mavericks really release Moussa Cissé to make room for Dwight Powell — after matching the Knicks' July offer sheet and then letting Cissé's $2.2 million become fully guaranteed by keeping him past October 1st? It all seemed very unlikely — until the Mavericks' coaching staff started gushing over Powell's play in camp.

And that's the key. Dwight Powell isn't just any veteran. He's been in the league for more than a decade, most of it in Dallas. He's still a useful player, but he's also a culture guy — the type of veteran who sets the tone in the locker room, knows every system, and is beloved inside the organization. Think of the role Nick Collison played in Oklahoma City for years. That's Powell in Dallas.

Now look at Dallas' frontcourt. They have Dereck Lively II. They have Daniel Gafford. They have Powell. They drafted another young big this summer. And they still have Cissé. That's a crowded room. If the Mavericks decide to keep Powell, they'd have 16 players for 15 spots — and somebody has to go. The young, cheap backup center who's fifth or sixth in that frontcourt suddenly looks like the odd man out.

Stein also mentioned that Dallas could try to create a roster spot through a trade instead. But he noted that approach could be even harder to pull off this close to opening night — and he's right. With the season a week away, teams aren't eager to take on extra players. A release is simpler.

## STEP ONE: DALLAS HAS TO CHOOSE

So here's the five-step path, starting with the obvious one. Step one: Dallas has to decide it wants Powell more than Cissé.

That's not a crazy choice for a team trying to win now. Powell knows the system, he's trusted by the coaches, and veterans matter in the locker room. Cissé is younger, more athletic, and has more long-term upside — but he's also further down the depth chart in Dallas. If the Mavericks value experience this season, the math could tip toward Powell.

## STEP TWO: THE RELEASE — AND WHO PAYS

Step two: if Dallas releases him, the money matters. Remember, Cissé's $2.19 million is now fully guaranteed. That means if the Mavericks waive him, they still owe him that money. That's a real cost for Dallas — they'd be paying a player not to play for them. That's one of the reasons Stein framed this as surprising. But teams do it all the time when they believe a veteran helps them win more.

## STEP THREE: THE WAIVER WIRE — THE OBSTACLE NOBODY MENTIONS

And now step three — the part most people skip, and it's a big one. When a player is released, he doesn't just become a free agent right away. First, he goes on waivers. For a short period, any team with enough room can claim him and take over his contract. And here's the catch: waiver priority goes in order of the previous season's records — worst teams first.

Who had the best record last season? The team that won the championship. That puts the Knicks at the very back of the line.

So if Cissé hits waivers, a rebuilding team with an open roster spot and cap room could simply claim him — a young, athletic center on a cheap contract with a team option for next year. That's attractive for plenty of teams. And if that happens, the Knicks never get a chance.

## STEP FOUR: CLEARING WAIVERS — THE KNICKS' WINDOW

Step four: if NOBODY claims him, Cissé clears waivers and becomes a free agent. And THAT'S when the Knicks' window opens.

And here's the beautiful part for New York. If he clears waivers, Dallas still pays his guaranteed salary. The Knicks could then sign him to a minimum-type contract — and because of how the rules work, his old team's payment gets offset by what his new team pays him. In plain English: Cissé gets his money either way, and the Knicks get him cheap.

Could the Knicks afford it? This is where the apron comes in. The Knicks are about $3.2 to $3.3 million below the second apron. A minimum contract for a young player like Cissé fits under that line. A waiver claim at his full $2.19 million would technically fit too — but again, the Knicks would be last in line to claim him. So the realistic route is: he clears waivers, then the Knicks sign him as a free agent.

And based on how much they wanted him in July, there's every reason to think they'd be interested. You don't sign a player to an offer sheet unless you really want him.

## STEP FIVE: THE KNICKS HAVE TO MAKE ROOM

Step five, and this is where it gets complicated for New York. Remember the arithmetic from our trial video: the Knicks have 13 guaranteed contracts, they must fill a 14th spot, and the second apron only allows one more veteran-minimum-type player. So if Cissé became available, the Knicks would have to decide: does the one open spot go to Cissé, or to James Wiseman?

Or — and this is the interesting part — do they finally make the Pacôme Dadiet salary dump we've been talking about all fall? Moving Dadiet's $2.98 million would open a second roster spot and give the Knicks room under the apron. Then they could potentially keep Wiseman AND add Cissé — or keep Wiseman and add Bruce Brown, or add Cissé and Bruce Brown. Every option on the board changes the moment Cissé hits the market.

So one decision in Dallas could set off a chain reaction in New York.

## WHY THE KNICKS WANTED HIM IN THE FIRST PLACE

Now let's talk about the player himself, because there's a reason the Knicks went after him in July.

Moussa Cissé is a rim-running, shot-blocking center with real athleticism. He's still on the edge of earning serious NBA minutes, so he's not a finished product. But what he does well, he does REALLY well: block shots, run the floor, crash the offensive glass, and finish around the rim.

Does he add much on offense beyond rim-running and putbacks? No. And that's fine. That's not his job. Some people even argue he's ahead of Wiseman right now as a rim-runner and shot-blocker — that's a debate, but it tells you how highly some evaluators think of his defensive tools.

And remember what we covered in our "Through Brunson's Eyes" video. When Mitchell Robinson left for Boston, the Knicks lost one of the league's best pick-and-roll partnerships — Brunson and Mitch ranked eighth in the NBA over five seasons in points generated as a screen-and-roll duo. What made that work was a big who dives hard and finishes above the rim. Drummond doesn't do that anymore. KAT is too skilled to be boxed into that role. Wiseman is the closest internal option. Cissé is another player who fits that rim-runner profile. He's nowhere near the player Mitch is — let's be clear about that. But he brings the same type of skills: shot-blocking, offensive rebounding, and vertical pressure on the rim.

## HOW HE'D FIT IN NEW YORK

So if he came, how would Mike Brown use him? Realistically, he wouldn't play over Karl-Anthony Towns or Andre Drummond. He'd be depth — interior insurance behind those two.

But Mike Brown likes to experiment with rotations, and there's an interesting idea here. Brown sees Towns as a center, not a power forward. But in certain matchups, a lineup with KAT and a pure rim protector like Cissé could give the Knicks something different: KAT stretching the floor on offense, while Cissé protects the paint on defense. Against teams with huge frontcourts — think Minnesota with Rudy Gobert, or Cleveland with Jarrett Allen and Evan Mobley — that kind of size could come in handy.

The main appeal is simple: rebounding, rim protection, and development. A young big who can grow into a real backup center.

## THE LONG-TERM ANGLE

And that's the real reason this matters beyond this season. Look at the Knicks' center situation down the road. Drummond is on a one-year deal. Wiseman would likely be on a non-guaranteed minimum. The Knicks didn't draft a center this summer, and they don't have first-round picks in two of the next three drafts. Add the second apron squeeze — KAT's unresolved extension, Brunson's projected massive deal down the road, Hart's option, Deuce's future — and the Knicks are going to need cheap, young, productive players more than ever.

Cissé fits that perfectly. If the Knicks landed him, they'd likely try to lock him into a longer, affordable deal — something that keeps them near the apron without crossing it — and develop him into their backup center for the next season or two. That's the kind of quiet move that keeps a contender alive in the second-apron era.

## HEAD-TO-HEAD: CISSÉ VS. WISEMAN

If both players were available for that one roster spot, how would they stack up? Let's run it like a scouting report, category by category — and this is opinion and analysis, not reporting.

**Age and runway:** Both are young bigs with room to grow. Cissé is in his early twenties; Wiseman is 25. Slight edge to Cissé for long-term runway, but neither is a finished product.

**Rim protection:** Edge Cissé. Shot-blocking is the center of his identity. Wiseman has blocked shots in this preseason, but defense has never been his calling card, even with his size.

**Rim-running and lobs:** Close. Wiseman has the bigger frame and real scoring touch — he averaged about 19 points per 36 minutes in Golden State, and more than 46 percent of his made baskets in his last healthy season came on dunks or alley-oops. Cissé is a pure lob-and-putback player. Both fit the role Mitch used to play for Brunson.

**Health and availability:** Wiseman's biggest question. He tore his Achilles in his Indiana debut in 2024 and has played very little since. Cissé doesn't carry that same injury history.

**Familiarity:** Edge Wiseman, by a mile. He's been in Knicks camp for weeks, he knows the system, and he has a relationship with Mike Brown dating back to Golden State. Cissé would be starting from zero a week before opening night.

**Cost and contract:** Both would come cheap. But if Cissé clears waivers, the Knicks could pay him very little while Dallas covers the rest of his guaranteed money. That's an extremely efficient use of the Knicks' tiny cushion under the apron.

Add it up, and you get a real dilemma. Wiseman is the safer bet for opening night because he's already here and already trusted. Cissé might be the better long-term defensive bet. And that's exactly why a Dadiet trade — opening a second spot — would be the dream scenario. You wouldn't have to choose.

## WHAT THIS TELLS YOU ABOUT THE KNICKS' PRIORITIES

Step back for a second and look at every big-man name connected to this team since the summer. Moussa Diabaté in Charlotte. Ryan Kalkbrenner in Charlotte. Kel'el Ware in Milwaukee, even though Bobby Marks says that's very unlikely. Wiseman, Eubanks, and Bradley in camp. And now Cissé again.

What do almost all of them have in common? Size, rim protection, and a cheap contract. That's the pattern. The Knicks know exactly what they lost when Mitchell Robinson signed his three-year, $47.4 million deal with Boston, and they've spent the entire offseason hunting for pieces of what he gave them — without breaking James Dolan's second-apron rule. Cissé isn't a random name. He's the clearest example of the type of player this front office has been chasing all along. They wanted him in July. If he becomes available in October, the interest won't suddenly disappear.

## THE TIMING: WHY THIS ALL HAPPENS THIS WEEK

And the calendar makes this even more dramatic. Every team in the league has to cut down to a legal roster before the regular season starts. So Dallas has to make its Powell-or-Cissé decision within days. Meanwhile, the Knicks have their final preseason games against Toronto — tonight, October 13th, and then October 15th. On October 19th, Karl-Anthony Towns has to decide on his $61 million player option. And on October 20th, the banner goes up against Philadelphia.

That means the Knicks' front office could be juggling a KAT option decision, a final roster spot, a possible Dadiet salary dump, AND a waiver-wire opportunity — all in the same week. If you love roster drama, this is the week.

## THE REALITY CHECK

Now, let's keep our feet on the ground. For this to happen, Dallas has to choose Powell, eat Cissé's guaranteed salary, and then no other team in the league can claim him off waivers — and the Knicks, picking last, can't jump the line. That's a lot of things that have to go right.

And even then, the Knicks would need to decide whether Cissé is worth more than Wiseman — the player who's been winning the third-center battle in camp and has a relationship with Mike Brown. Or they'd have to pull off a Dadiet trade to make room for both.

So is this likely? Honestly, it's a long shot. But Stein raising it publicly means it's on the radar. And if Dallas pulls the trigger, the Knicks front office will be watching that waiver wire very closely.

## MY CALL

Here's my prediction. I think there's a real chance Dallas keeps Powell and lets Cissé go — the coaching staff's praise for Powell is a meaningful signal. But I think another team claims Cissé on waivers before the Knicks ever get the chance. A young, cheap, athletic center with a team option is exactly what rebuilding teams look for. If by some chance he clears, though, I'd expect the Knicks to call immediately — and that could finally be the moment they make the Dadiet move to open up a second spot.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. Marc Stein has raised the possibility that Dallas could release Moussa Cissé to make room for veteran Dwight Powell, who's impressed the Mavericks' coaching staff in camp. The Knicks already tried to sign Cissé this summer with a two-year offer sheet, but Dallas matched, and his $2.19 million salary became fully guaranteed on October 1st, with a $2.57 million team option next year. For Cissé to reach New York, Dallas would have to choose Powell, release him, and he'd have to clear waivers — where the champion Knicks pick last. If he did clear, the Knicks could sign him cheaply under the apron. But they'd still need to choose between him and Wiseman for the 14th spot — or move Dadiet to make room for both. No release has happened yet. But this is one to watch before opening night.
