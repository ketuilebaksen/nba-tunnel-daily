## THE STARTING LINE: WHERE THE ROSTER STANDS

First, a quick snapshot so everybody's on the same page. On Thursday, hours before the finale, the Knicks reshaped the bottom of the roster. Drew Eubanks suffered a torn pectoral muscle and was released. Tony Bradley, dealing with a hamstring injury, was waived. Ochai Agbaji was waived. That leaves two training-camp invitees standing: James Wiseman and Bruce Brown.

The Knicks have 13 guaranteed contracts and must carry at least 14. Under James Dolan's second-apron mandate, they have room for exactly one more veteran minimum. Wiseman is the frontrunner for that spot — but he's still on a training-camp deal and hasn't been signed to a standard contract. And there are no more exhibition games. The coaching staff and front office have seen everything they need to see. Every decision from here on counts.

Now, to the races.

## RACE ONE: THE JEMISON BIDDING WAR

**The runner:** Trey Jemison III. A 6-foot-10 center who spent last season with the Knicks on a two-way deal, was part of the championship organization, and left in free agency for a two-way spot in Toronto in early August.

**What happened:** Toronto waived him on Wednesday — the day before the Raptors lost at the Garden. He'd appeared in only one preseason game for Toronto: six rebounds and an assist in eight minutes against the Clippers. And according to Raptors reporter Blake Murphy, Jemison suffered a hand injury a few games before his release, which may have played a role in the decision. Murphy also noted that Toronto's G League affiliate, Raptors 905, could still be an option for him.

**Why the Knicks are in this race:** Center has been this team's biggest question mark since the summer. Mitchell Robinson left for Boston. Ariel Hukporti left too. That left Karl-Anthony Towns and 15-year veteran Andre Drummond as the main options. Losing Jemison to Toronto in August made the depth thinner. And this week's injuries and cuts in camp shrank the pool of available bigs even more.

**The path:** Jemison still has two-way eligibility, and that's the most realistic route to a reunion. The Knicks' three two-way spots belong to Tyler Nickel, Kevin McCullar Jr., and Jaden Akins — none of them a center. To bring Jemison back, one would have to be released. Financially, that's easy: two-way deals are non-guaranteed, and changing a two-way slot doesn't count against the salary cap. It wouldn't interfere with the main roster decisions or the apron. It's a clean, separate move.

## THE TWIST: THE SIXERS ARE IN THE RACE TOO

And here's where it gets juicy. The Knicks won't be alone. Several teams are still hunting for an extra big. The Sacramento Kings and the Los Angeles Lakers could show interest. But the team that stands out most is the Philadelphia 76ers.

Philly has an open two-way spot. And they need frontcourt help, because Ariel Hukporti — the same Hukporti who was the Knicks' third center last season — is now with the Sixers and suffered a season-ending Achilles injury in the preseason opener... against the Knicks.

Think about how wild that is. The Knicks' opening-night opponent — the team standing between them and a perfect ring night — might be racing them for the same former Knick big man. One former Knicks center goes down in a Sixers uniform, and now the Sixers might come after another former Knicks center to replace him. If Philly grabs Jemison before Tuesday, he could be in the building on banner night wearing the other jersey. That's the kind of storyline you can't write.

## WHAT JEMISON ACTUALLY BRINGS

Let's be honest about the player, because this channel doesn't oversell. Jemison isn't a star, and he isn't an elite rim protector. His strengths are rebounding, setting screens, and playing physically in the paint. He's fairly mobile for his size and plays with real energy. That profile fits perfectly as a third or fourth center who can step in for a few games when needed.

Last season, he moved back and forth between the Knicks and Westchester, and in the G League he put up around eight points and more than eight rebounds a game with over a block per night. Because he was on a two-way deal, he wasn't eligible for the postseason during the title run. But he had useful regular-season moments. Across three NBA seasons, he's appeared in 76 regular-season games for Washington, Memphis, New Orleans, the Lakers, and the Knicks.

And there's a personal connection. He wasn't on the Knicks' summer league team, but he was with the group for its appearance at the ESPYs after the title. He's well-liked inside the organization. For a front office that wants center depth without making a trade, a familiar face who already knows the system is an attractive short-term solution.

## THE TWO-WAY DILEMMA

So who would go? This one is harder than it looks.

**Tyler Nickel** got extended minutes in Tuesday's blowout loss in Toronto and finished with 11 points in 21 minutes — but he made only one of his nine three-point attempts. He's on a two-year two-way deal, which shows the Knicks invested in him as a shooter.

**Kevin McCullar Jr.** is in his third season in the organization, re-signed on a two-way in late September, and the Knicks have shown repeatedly that they believe in him.

**Jaden Akins** is a young guard with strong defensive credentials from Michigan State, but he's a guard on a roster loaded with guards.

None of the three gives the Knicks what Jemison offers: a true center with real NBA minutes on his résumé. And remember the important distinction: the 14th roster spot is a separate decision, likely going to Wiseman. Even if that's filled, a two-way big would still be extra insurance for a long season. Frontcourt injuries happen every year. An 82-game schedule tests every corner of a roster.

**Race one verdict:** If I'm Leon Rose, I move first. Swap Akins for Jemison before Philly, Sacramento, or the Lakers get there. Familiarity, zero apron impact, and a body the Knicks will need sooner than they think.

## RACE TWO: BRUCE BROWN VS. THE APRON

**The runner:** Bruce Brown, a key piece of Denver's 2023 championship team and one of only two camp invitees still standing.

**Why his survival matters:** He's not a center, so keeping him isn't about filling the frontcourt need. One theory was that the Knicks just wanted one more look at him. But they could have kept Agbaji for the same reason — and they cut Agbaji instead. Since Agbaji was competing for a similar wing role, choosing Brown over him is revealing. It suggests the Knicks have real plans for Bruce Brown.

And it's easy to see why. He's a veteran with championship experience who can defend and play multiple positions. He's shown flashes this preseason even without a standout game. Mike Brown has reason to want him — especially since the Knicks' bench has struggled during the exhibition games. A proven player who's delivered on the biggest stage could give the second unit some much-needed energy. Ian Begley has reported strong internal support for keeping him.

**The obstacle:** Money. Dolan's line means the Knicks can't simply sign both Wiseman and Brown. They'd need to trade someone to open a roster spot AND create financial room.

## THE DADIET PROBLEM — AND THE KOLEK QUESTION

**Option one: Pacôme Dadiet.** The most logical candidate. The young forward looks headed for a third straight season without a clear role, and he earns more than the veteran minimum that Wiseman or Brown would sign for. Moving him would solve both problems at once — the roster spot and the money.

But here's the catch: the same things that make Dadiet the obvious candidate also make him hard to trade. A young player who hasn't developed, with a salary above the minimum, isn't something teams line up for — even if the Knicks attach draft picks. Remember the 76ers needed to attach a second-round pick just to get the Clippers to take Johni Broome. The market for these deals exists — Memphis has a little cap space, and teams like Charlotte, New Orleans, the Clippers, and Sacramento have trade exceptions — but it takes a willing partner.

**Option two: Tyler Kolek.** If Dadiet can't be moved, another name could come into focus. Some see Kolek as a third point guard on the depth chart — something of a luxury — with questions about whether he can be a reliable rotation player. Trading him would open the door for a more experienced player like Bruce Brown.

And this is where I'll respectfully push back. Kolek had five points and five assists without a turnover in the opener. And on Thursday, he hit the shot that won the finale in front of the home crowd. He's young, he's cheap, and on a team that's going to be squeezed by the apron for years, cheap productive players are gold. Trading Kolek for a one-year veteran would be short-term thinking.

**Race two verdict:** Dadiet with a second-round pick or two — if a partner exists. If not, I'd rather go into opening night with Wiseman only and revisit Bruce Brown later than give up Kolek. But I think Leon Rose finds a deal. He has a reputation for staying active, and the next few days could bring more than one move.

## RACE THREE: THE KAT CLOCK

**The runner:** Karl-Anthony Towns. And this race has the biggest stakes of all.

**Where it stands:** The Knicks decided to shut down extension talks until later in the season. The two sides spent months negotiating without getting close. The official extension deadline was Monday, October 19th, but instead of letting it drag to that date, the front office ended it early to keep the focus on the title defense. On paper, that logic makes sense — a contract standoff hanging over the first week of the season is exactly the distraction a champion wants to avoid.

**The critique:** Carmelo Anthony addressed it on his podcast, "7PM in Brooklyn." Melo has long been a vocal supporter of the Knicks — and even of James Dolan — and he didn't take issue with the decision itself. His criticism was about communication. KAT described the news as something he heard for the first time, and Melo said the Knicks dropped the ball — that the organization should never have let that play out in public. That's fair. KAT was arguably the most important piece of last season's title run. Ironically, the attempt to avoid distractions may have created more of them.

**The suitors:** According to Stefan Bondy of the New York Post, an anonymous NBA executive said the Chicago Bulls and the Los Angeles Clippers are viewed as possible suitors if KAT were to become available in a trade. To be clear: that's one executive's view, not a report of trade talks. But Chicago is on the Knicks' early schedule — a road game on October 28th.

**The leverage:** The gap is more than $70 million — a max extension worth about $276 million versus a Knicks offer around $200 million over four years. And KAT's final year is a player option, which means he could become a free agent as early as next summer. Then other teams with cap space could make offers, and a bidding war would be very likely. That's why the situation now has two possible exits: a trade during the season, or free agency next summer if the relationship doesn't improve.

**The on-court answer:** And yet, on Thursday, KAT looked like anything but a player who's checked out. Six rebounds in the first quarter alone, active on the glass from the opening tip, and doing his part in a win the team needed for its confidence.

**Race three verdict:** This race won't finish by Tuesday. It might not finish by Christmas. But the front office's challenge is clear: protect its financial flexibility while protecting the relationship with the player who helped bring a title home. How they balance that could define the whole season.

## THE FINALE, QUICKLY

Before we wrap, a quick look back at the 122–120 win, because it showed what this team looks like at full strength. Mike Brown started Brunson, Bridges, OG, Mohamed Diawara, and KAT. Deuce McBride closed the first quarter with three straight threes for a 35–32 lead. The Knicks led 63–58 at the half and 82–77 after three, then held on in a tight fourth against a Toronto team featuring Kawhi Leonard, Scottie Barnes, Jakob Poeltl — and two former Knicks, RJ Barrett and Immanuel Quickley, the players who went to Toronto in the trade that brought OG to New York.

After a blowout loss in Toronto two nights earlier with mostly reserves, the champions finally looked like themselves.

## WHAT THE FINAL ROSTER WILL TELL US

When the dust settles on Monday, the shape of this roster will say a lot about how the front office sees the title defense. Here are the three most likely outcomes — and what each one means.

**Outcome A: Wiseman only.** The Knicks sign Wiseman, release Bruce Brown, and don't make a trade. Message: the apron line is absolute, and the front office won't spend assets to add depth at the end of the bench. Safe, disciplined — but a little thin on the wing if injuries hit.

**Outcome B: Wiseman plus Bruce Brown, via a Dadiet trade.** Message: the front office is willing to use its stash of second-round picks to maximize this season. It's the "go for it" version — more experience, more defense, and a clear signal that the Knicks know their championship window is right now.

**Outcome C: A surprise.** Leon Rose has a reputation for staying active. Maybe a different center shows up through a trade or from the wave of players other teams release this weekend. Maybe a bigger deal emerges. The Knicks could sign Wiseman alone, find a trade that keeps both camp players, or bring in someone new entirely.

And layered on top of all three: the two-way decision. If the Knicks bring back Jemison, it tells you they're serious about frontcourt insurance. If they let Philly or someone else grab him, it tells you they trust KAT, Drummond, and Wiseman to carry the load.

Whatever happens, we'll know by opening night. And this channel will break it all down the moment it does.
