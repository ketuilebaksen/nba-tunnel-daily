## THE LATEST MOVE: KNECHT IS OFFICIAL

According to ESPN's Shams Charania, former Lakers wing Dalton Knecht has agreed to sign with the Knicks on a two-way contract. Ian Begley confirmed it and added the key details: the Knicks are waiving Kevin McCullar Jr. to make room, they were aggressive on Knecht once he cleared waivers following his release by the Lakers, and the club is clearly betting on the 25-year-old's upside and potential.

Here's the quick statistical snapshot from Begley's report. Last season with the Lakers, Knecht appeared in 54 games, averaging 4.2 points on 45.5 percent shooting and 34.2 percent from three, with 1.4 rebounds in 10.2 minutes per game. As a rookie, he played much more: 78 games with 16 starts, averaging 9.1 points on 46.1 percent from the field and 37.6 percent from three on 4.4 attempts, plus 2.8 rebounds in 19.2 minutes per game.

That's the bet: the rookie version is the real Knecht, and the second-year drop was about role and confidence after the rescinded Mark Williams trade — not talent.

## THE TWO GOALS NOBODY COULD SEE AT ONCE

Here's what makes this move so interesting. For weeks, we knew the Knicks wanted two things: another center and another wing. Mitchell Robinson's departure to Boston left a hole in the middle. And the wing depth needed a defensive, versatile body — that's why the Knicks wanted to keep Bruce Brown so badly.

The problem was money. Under James Dolan's second-apron mandate, the Knicks could only afford one more veteran minimum. They tried to create room for both by trading Pacôme Dadiet, but couldn't find a partner. So they made a choice: James Wiseman got the 14th roster spot as the third center, and Bruce Brown was released.

That looked like the end of the wing search. It wasn't. Leon Rose found a second door — the two-way contract — and used it to add a wing without touching the apron at all. Center: Wiseman. Wing: Knecht. Both goals. Zero apron dollars crossed.

## THE 30-DAY TIMELINE

Now let's walk through how we got here, because when you see every move in order, the strategy becomes obvious.

**Mid-September: the camp invites.** The Knicks brought in five veterans on non-guaranteed deals — James Wiseman, Bruce Brown, Drew Eubanks, Tony Bradley, and Ochai Agbaji — plus John Konchar, who later parted ways with the team for personal reasons. The message: let them compete, and let the games decide.

**Late September: the two-way room.** Kevin McCullar Jr. re-signed on a two-way deal, joining Tyler Nickel and Jaden Akins in the three two-way slots.

**Early October: the preseason auditions.** Wiseman became the first camp big off the bench in Philadelphia and the only third center to play against Washington. Bruce Brown earned praise from Mike Brown — "textbook" on the ball. Jaden Akins quietly impressed the organization, according to Begley.

**October 13th: Wiseman's statement.** In Toronto, Wiseman started and put up 18 points and 10 rebounds.

**October 15th: the cuts.** Drew Eubanks suffered a torn pectoral muscle and was released. Tony Bradley and Ochai Agbaji were waived. Only Wiseman and Bruce Brown remained.

**October 15th: the finale.** The Knicks beat Toronto 122–120. Mike Brown started Mohamed Diawara in place of an injured Josh Hart to test him against Kawhi Leonard and Scottie Barnes. Bruce Brown didn't play. Tyler Kolek hit the shot that won the game.

**October 17th: the final spot.** Wiseman officially earned the last roster spot. Bruce Brown was waived. Pacôme Dadiet and Tyler Kolek stayed — preserving the Knicks' trade flexibility before December 15th.

**October 19th: the pipeline.** The Knicks signed Dink Pate to an Exhibit 10 deal after he withdrew from Providence, then waived him so he could rejoin Westchester — keeping a 6-foot-8, 20-year-old playmaker in the system.

**October 19th–20th: the Cissé chapter closes.** Dallas waived Dwight Powell and kept Moussa Cissé, ending any chance of a reunion with the player the Knicks tried to sign in July.

**October 20th: ring night.** The banner went up. The Knicks lost the opener to Philadelphia, 106–100, with a cold shooting night from the wings.

**This week: Knecht.** Knecht cleared waivers, the Knicks moved aggressively, and he agreed to a two-way deal. McCullar was waived.

## WHAT THE TIMELINE REVEALS

Put all of that together, and three things jump out.

**One: the apron shaped everything.** Every decision — Wiseman over Bruce Brown, keeping Dadiet, using a two-way slot for Knecht — was driven by staying under the second apron. The front office didn't fight the rule. It worked around it.

**Two: the Knicks kept their trade tools.** By not dumping Dadiet or Kolek, the Knicks kept three movable non-starters — Dadiet, Kolek, and Deuce McBride — for trades before December 15th. Dadiet's roughly $3 million salary is the key that could unlock a deal for a rotation player down the line.

**Three: the front office kept hunting after the season started.** Most teams lock in their rosters and wait for December. The Knicks lost on opening night, saw their wings shoot poorly, and immediately went after a shooter. That's a front office paying attention.

## THE YOUTH MOVEMENT HIDING IN PLAIN SIGHT

And here's the bigger picture that a lot of fans might be missing. Look at the young, cheap players now in the Knicks' system:

**Dalton Knecht**, 25 — a former first-rounder and rookie-record shooter, on a two-way.
**Jaden Akins** — a two-way guard the organization is "optimistic" about, per Begley.
**Tyler Nickel** — a young shooter on a two-way the Knicks invested in.
**Mohamed Diawara** — the second-rounder who started the preseason finale.
**Tyler Kolek** — the cheap backup point guard who hit the finale's winning shot.
**James Wiseman**, 25 — a former number two overall pick on a minimum.
**Dink Pate**, 20 — the 6-foot-8 playmaker in Westchester.

None of them are stars. But that's not the point. The point is that under this CBA, the Knicks are going to face painful decisions — KAT's extension, Josh Hart's $22 million option, Deuce's expiring deal, and eventually Jalen Brunson's projected massive extension. Brian Windhorst has already warned that paying KAT near what he wants could cost the Knicks Hart and Deuce. The only way to survive that is to have cheap, developing players ready to step in. That's what this list is. Leon Rose is building for right now — and for the version of this team two years from now.

## WHY McCULLAR, AND WHY NOW

Let's talk about the cost, because Kevin McCullar Jr. deserves respect. He's a champion with the Knicks. He was a strong defender and a productive G League player. But he never found his three-point shot — zero-of-six from deep in the preseason — and injuries held back his development over the years. When the Knicks had the chance to swap him for a former first-round pick with a much higher shooting ceiling, they took it.

It's the same lesson we talked about in our "McCullar Crossroads" video: a two-way contract makes a player easy to keep, but also easy to replace. This time, the replacement was too good to pass up. We wish McCullar nothing but the best. A defender who can pass like he did in Westchester will get another chance.

## THE WING THEY ALWAYS WANTED — WITH A CAVEAT

Some fans will ask: do the Knicks really need another shooter? They already have Tyler Nickel. And in the modern NBA, the answer is easy: you can never have too many players who can make threes, especially next to Brunson and KAT. Knecht is a legit 6-foot-6 wing who can shoot, rebound a little, and make simple plays.

The caveat: he's not perfect. His defense has graded out negatively in both NBA seasons, and he shot poorly this preseason with the Lakers. He has real work to do. But on a two-way deal, he'll get reps in Westchester and spot minutes in New York — and if the rookie version shows up, he becomes a steal.

## HOW THE TWO-WAY ROOM STACKS UP NOW

With Knecht in, here's the new two-way picture.

**Dalton Knecht:** The high-upside shooter. Pedigree, NBA experience — 132 games already — and a rookie season that showed real scoring ability. His path: rebuild confidence in Westchester, then earn spot minutes in New York.

**Tyler Nickel:** The young shooter the Knicks invested in with a two-year two-way deal. He got extended minutes in the bench-heavy preseason game in Toronto, scoring 11 points in 21 minutes, though he made only one of nine threes. His path: keep developing the shot and earn trust.

**Jaden Akins:** The defender. Begley reports the organization is impressed with his play and his approach, and optimistic about his future as a cheap contributor under the apron. His path: lock up on defense, improve the jumper.

Remember the rules: each two-way player can be active for up to 50 NBA games, and none of them can play in the playoffs unless they're converted to a standard contract. That makes this a real competition. If one of them clearly separates, the Knicks could look for a way to convert him — and that would be one of the cheapest ways to add playoff-eligible depth under the apron.

## WHAT KNECHT GIVES MIKE BROWN RIGHT NOW

In the short term, Knecht's value is simple: shooting insurance. In the opener against Philadelphia, the Knicks shot 33 percent from three. OG Anunoby went one-of-nine from deep, Mikal Bridges scored just four points, and Landry Shamet went one-of-four. On nights like that, Mike Brown now has a catch-and-shoot wing he can call up who isn't afraid to let it fly.

And the offense is built for him. Brown loves what he calls the "spray three" — a drive collapses the defense, the ball gets kicked out, and the shooter fires. With Brunson attacking and KAT passing from the high post, the corners will be open. Knecht made over 40 percent of his corner threes as a rookie. That's the exact shot this offense creates.

## THE DECEMBER 15TH WINDOW, EXPLAINED

Before we get to the to-do list, one more piece of context. Under NBA rules, players who sign as free agents can't be traded until three months after signing or December 15th, whichever comes later. That's why the Knicks kept Dadiet and Kolek: right now, only Dadiet, Kolek, and Deuce McBride are movable non-starters.

And the math matters. Dadiet earns roughly $3 million — a little more than a minimum. According to Daily Knicks, his salary paired with draft compensation could be enough for a player like Moussa Diabaté, the Charlotte big man linked to New York for months. Combine Dadiet's salary with Deuce's, and the Knicks could bring back a player making just over $6.9 million. Those are the tools for the next move — and the reason the front office protected them instead of using them to keep Bruce Brown.

## WHAT'S STILL ON THE TO-DO LIST

So are the Knicks done? Not quite. Here's what's left.

**A two-way center.** With Knecht joining Nickel and Akins, all three two-way slots are perimeter players. The center depth behind KAT and Drummond is still just Wiseman. After KAT's foul trouble on opening night forced Drummond into heavy minutes, this is still the biggest gap. If injuries hit, the front office will have to revisit it.

**The 15th roster spot.** It's still open, and still blocked by the apron. Filling it with even a veteran minimum would trigger the second apron — unless a trade sends salary out first.

**The Dadiet trade.** The Knicks couldn't find a partner before the season. Once more players become tradable on December 15th, the market opens up — and Dadiet's contract, alone or paired with Deuce's, becomes the tool for a bigger move.

**The KAT negotiation.** KAT told ESPN he's willing to take less — "nowhere in my mind a max was even thought of." But Jake Fischer expects extension talks to resume after the trade deadline. That's the biggest move of all, and it's months away.

## THE ODDS: WHY THE MOVES KEEP COMING

And here's why the front office isn't resting. The latest NBA championship odds show the market cooling on New York. Oklahoma City is on top at plus-200. San Antonio is next at plus-300. Then the 76ers at plus-1000 — and the Knicks right behind them at plus-1100. Then the Celtics at plus-1300, the Raptors at plus-2000, the Timberwolves at plus-2500, and the Cavaliers at plus-2700.

Remember, a couple of weeks ago, the Knicks were tied with Philadelphia at plus-1000. After the opener, Philly has moved ahead. The defending champions are now the fourth choice in the betting market.

That's the reality the Knicks know better than anyone: the Eastern Conference may be better than it's been in years. Philadelphia added LeBron James and Jaylen Brown. Boston has Jayson Tatum back and added Paul George and Mitchell Robinson. Toronto has Kawhi Leonard. You don't win in that environment by standing still. You win by squeezing value out of every roster spot — including the two-way slots.

## THE LEON ROSE PATTERN

And that's the theme of this whole timeline. When the Knicks couldn't keep Mitch, they found Drummond, who showed up in the best shape of his recent career and dominated the first half of the opener. When they couldn't keep both Bruce Brown and Wiseman, they chose size and then found a wing through the two-way door. When they couldn't dump Dadiet, they kept him as a trade chip. When the wings shot poorly on opening night, they went out and got a shooter within days.

Nobody was talking about Knecht a week ago. Then the Knicks were linked to him. Then, a day later, he was signed. That's the pattern: quiet, fast, and cheap. Leon Rose pulled another rabbit out of the hat.

## MY CALL

Here's my take on what comes next. Knecht spends a lot of time in Westchester early, gets spot minutes in New York when the Knicks need shooting, and becomes a real candidate for a standard contract by midseason if his shot comes back. The front office keeps an eye on the two-way center question and revisits it the moment the frontcourt gets thin. And the bigger moves — a Dadiet-based trade, the KAT extension — come after December 15th and after the deadline. The Knicks aren't done. They're just getting started.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. Shams and Begley report the Knicks are signing Dalton Knecht to a two-way deal and waiving Kevin McCullar Jr., after pursuing Knecht aggressively once he cleared waivers. Knecht averaged 9.1 points on 37.6 percent from three as a rookie before slipping to 4.2 points last season. The move completes a 30-day stretch in which the Knicks solved both of their roster needs under the second apron: a center through Wiseman's standard deal, and a wing through Knecht's two-way. Along the way, they kept Dadiet and Kolek as trade tools, brought Dink Pate back to Westchester, and built a young, cheap pipeline for the apron years ahead. Still on the to-do list: a two-way center, the open 15th spot, a possible Dadiet trade, and the KAT extension. And with the Knicks now at plus-1100 to win the title, behind Philadelphia, the front office has every reason to keep making moves.
