# NBA TUNNEL — "KNICKS 0–3! THREE ALARMS, ONE OPPORTUNITY — And a New Center Finally Gets His Big Chance"

Tunnel fam, it's official: the defending champions are 0–3. On Sunday night at Madison Square Garden, the Orlando Magic — playing without Paolo Banchero — beat the Knicks 111–102. It's the first time since the 2011–12 Dallas Mavericks that a defending champion has lost its first three games of a title defense. And the way it happened hurt: the Knicks led by 10 with a little more than nine minutes left and then completely fell apart. But inside the wreckage, there was also a signal from Mike Brown that every Knicks fan should notice. He said only five spots in his rotation are locked in — and then, instead of going to Andre Drummond, he turned to his third-string center, James Wiseman. Today we break down three alarms — Jalen Brunson's jumper, Mikal Bridges' disappearance, and the fourth-quarter collapse — plus the trade talk that's already starting, and the opportunity that just opened for the new center. ## ACCOUNTABILITY AND HONEST FRAMING

First, I predicted a win against a Banchero-less Orlando. I was wrong — again. And to be clear about the title: there's no trade. The "massive" story here is a rotation shake-up and a center getting his chance. We'll also talk about the trade chatter already forming around Mikal Bridges — and why it's way too early. Now, the night itself.

## THE COLLAPSE

For three quarters, this looked like the win the Knicks needed. The defense was smothering Orlando. Brunson had 19 points through three periods, and even though it took him 22 shots, the Knicks won his minutes by 12 over that stretch. New York led 86–76 with a little more than nine minutes to go.

Then everything fell apart. Orlando outscored the Knicks 40–27 in the fourth quarter and closed the game on a 35–16 run over the final nine-plus minutes. The Knicks committed eight of their 14 turnovers in that final period. And Franz Wagner took over — 21 points in the fourth quarter alone.

That's not a talent problem. That's an execution and composure problem. The Knicks had the lead, the crowd, and the defense working — and then the possessions got sloppy, the shots got rushed, and Orlando smelled blood. And for a team that rallied from double-digit deficits four times on the way to a title, blowing a 10-point lead at home is exactly the opposite of who they were last spring.

## ALARM ONE: BRUNSON'S JUMPER

Jalen Brunson led all scorers with 28 points. But the numbers behind it tell a more complicated story: 12-of-28 from the field, one-of-seven from three, three-of-six from the line, four assists, and three turnovers. During his time on the floor in the fourth quarter, the Knicks were outscored by 15.

Now, the encouraging part first. Brunson attacked the basket much more aggressively. He took eight shots at the rim and made seven. Before Sunday, he'd attempted only six shots at the rim all season, making three. That's the version of Brunson that wins games.

The problem is the jump shot. He missed 15 of his 20 jumpers against Orlando. Through three games, he's made just 15 of 50 — 30 percent. Last season, he shot 43.9 percent on those same looks.

And here's the comparison that jumps off the page. Through three games, Brunson has taken 64 shots and scored 68 points. Through the first three games of last season, he also took exactly 64 shots — and scored 91. His true shooting percentage is 46.7 percent, tying the lowest mark through three games of his nine-year career. The only other time he started that badly was 2019–20, his second year in Dallas.

## THE WRIST QUESTION

So the uncomfortable question is out there: is the captain fully himself yet? Brunson had surgery on his wrist after the NBA Finals. From the outside, it's impossible to know whether this is rust, lingering effects from the procedure, or just a bad run of luck. In the preseason, he looked like the least of the Knicks' worries — attacking, hitting floaters, no hesitation. Three games into the real thing, the jumper isn't there yet.

## BUT BRUNSON IS STILL DOING A LOT RIGHT

And here's the important balance. The on-off numbers show the Knicks' offense has been better with him on the bench — about 115 points per 100 possessions without him, compared with roughly 100 with him. But the defense tells the opposite story: with Brunson on the floor, the Knicks have allowed 104.7 points per 100 possessions. When he sits, that balloons to 127.5. He's not being hunted or played off the floor. His defensive effort has been real.

Advanced tracking from Spencer Pearlman's Court Lab backs that up: Brunson has been a negative on offense and a positive on defense so far. The offensive damage comes mostly when he has the ball — which makes sense, because he ranks among the top five players in the league in touches per game. But the same data shows he ranks among the top 10 in "advantages created" per game, averaging 14 — passes that lead to open catches, drives that pull in two defenders, screen assists, passes that set up free throws. He's still bending defenses. The finishing just hasn't followed.

And after the game, Brunson didn't hide. He put the responsibility on himself and said the team has been hearing too much positivity since the championship. When a reporter suggested maybe the Knicks need the New York media to turn up the heat, Brunson said, with a straight face: "Do what you do." That's not a player looking for comfort. That's a player looking for an edge.

How does he get out of it? Exactly what he showed Sunday: get to his spots, stay aggressive at the rim, and trust the jumper to come back. He's been through tough stretches before, and every time, he's found his way out.

## ALARM TWO: MIKAL BRIDGES HAS DISAPPEARED

If Brunson's slow start raised questions, Mikal Bridges' start has raised alarms. Against Orlando, he scored just six points in 35 minutes and finished with a plus-minus of minus-23 — the worst of anyone in the game.

For the season: 10 total points in nearly 90 minutes. Five-of-23 from the field — 21.7 percent. Zero-for-10 from three. He's committed more fouls, seven, than he's made field goals, five. He's gone 14 straight quarters without hitting a jump shot, a drought that goes back to the second quarter of the preseason finale against Toronto on October 15th. It's his lowest-scoring three-game stretch in a single season since December 2019, his second year in the NBA. And he's one of the only players in recent memory to log 89-plus minutes over the first three games of a season while scoring 10 points or fewer.

## THE DEFENSE IS MISSING TOO

What worries people most isn't even the scoring. Bridges built his reputation as one of the most reliable perimeter defenders in the league. Right now, that impact is missing. Against Orlando, his main assignments were Jalen Suggs and Anthony Black. According to NBA tracking data, Magic players shot 12-of-18 when Bridges was the primary defender — including eight-of-10 from three. Suggs was held to four points on eight shots before leaving with a knee injury, but Black had a big night: 18 points on six-of-11 shooting, five-of-seven from deep, with two steals. And late in the game, on an out-of-bounds play, Franz Wagner slipped past Bridges on an easy backdoor cut.

The team numbers are brutal. Against Orlando, the Knicks posted an offensive rating of 94.6 and a defensive rating of 127.4 across Bridges' 74 possessions on the floor. For the season: in 183 possessions with Bridges on the floor, New York has been outscored by 26.1 points per 100 possessions. In 109 possessions with him on the bench, the Knicks have outscored opponents by 26.6. Small sample — but a huge gap.

## INJURY OR SOMETHING ELSE?

That leaves two possible explanations. Either Bridges is playing through an injury that hasn't been disclosed, or he's simply floating through games. Some observers have pointed to his ironman streak — he's never missed a game in his career, a streak now well over 600 straight games — and wondered whether he's pushing through something to keep it alive. Nobody outside the building knows. But the question is fair.

## THE MIKE BROWN MINUTES QUESTION

And it raises questions about how Mike Brown has handled him. In Friday's loss in Boston, Brown played Bridges just under 22 minutes as he went scoreless, missing all eight shots. Two days later, Brown gave him 35 minutes.

That choice is fair to question, because Brown has never been afraid to cut Bridges' minutes when a game calls for it — and it's paid off before. He benched Bridges for key stretches during the Game 1 comeback against Cleveland in the Eastern Conference Finals, and again during the Game 4 comeback against San Antonio in the NBA Finals. Brown knows how to manage this. The question is whether he'll start doing it now.

## IS A BRIDGES TRADE REALISTIC?

Let's address the trade talk, because it's already starting. The Knicks gave up five first-round picks to get Bridges. That price has already been justified — he was a key piece of a championship run, and nothing changes that. But a title doesn't guarantee a permanent home. Bridges is in the first year of a four-year, $150 million extension that includes a $41.5 million player option for 2029–30. Even though the deal just kicked in, he's already eligible to be traded.

But let's be clear: a move after three bad games is very unlikely. Bridges has had cold stretches before. What feels different is how passive he looks on both ends — in the past, when the shot wasn't falling, his defense kept him valuable. If this continues for weeks, the conversation will get louder. Right now, it's noise. He remains a starter and a central part of the plan. The Knicks need the old Mikal Bridges back — not a trade.

And the other wings haven't picked up the slack. Landry Shamet and Deuce McBride have competed hard on defense but haven't found a consistent offensive rhythm. That's why newly signed two-way shooter Dalton Knecht might become relevant sooner than expected.

## ALARM THREE: THE FOURTH QUARTER

The third alarm is the pattern. Against Philadelphia, the Knicks led at halftime and lost. Against Orlando, they led by 10 with nine minutes left and lost. Eight fourth-quarter turnovers. A 35–16 closing run allowed. Franz Wagner scoring 21 in one quarter. Champions close games. Right now, the Knicks aren't.

Part of it is shot-making — Brunson's jumper and Bridges' disappearance mean there's no reliable second option late. Part of it is decision-making — too many turnovers under pressure. And part of it might be legs and lineups — which brings us to the opportunity.

## THE OPPORTUNITY: "ONLY FIVE SPOTS ARE LOCKED"

Before Sunday's game, Mike Brown told reporters at the Garden that only five spots in his rotation are locked in. According to SNY's Ian Begley, he didn't name them, but anyone watching knows who they are: Jalen Brunson, Mikal Bridges, Josh Hart, OG Anunoby, and Karl-Anthony Towns. Everything else, Brown said, is open for competition — no matter how many players he ends up using.

And he backed it up during the game. Instead of leaning on Andre Drummond — the veteran signed this summer to replace Mitchell Robinson — Brown turned to his third-string center, James Wiseman.

## WHY WISEMAN, AND WHY NOW

That's the new center getting his big chance. And it makes sense when you remember what we've seen. In the opener, Drummond was phenomenal in the first half — 10 points, 12 rebounds, three assists, two blocks — and then faded badly in the second half after being asked to carry heavy minutes when KAT got into foul trouble. Embiid blocked him repeatedly, and he lacked the lift to finish.

Wiseman brings what Mike Brown called "that vertical threat at the rim, the vertical threat defensively, how good he runs." He's 25, a former number two overall pick, and a player Brown knows from Golden State. In the preseason, he put up 18 points and 10 rebounds in Toronto. And he gives the Knicks something nobody else on the roster does: a lob threat for Brunson in the pick-and-roll — the weapon the Knicks lost when Mitch went to Boston.

## WHAT WISEMAN HAS TO DO WITH THIS CHANCE

So what does he need to show to keep the door open? Run the floor and give Brunson a target at the rim. Protect the paint without fouling. Rebound in traffic. Make his free throws — he went two-of-six in the preseason opener. And bring energy that lifts a team that's been flat in fourth quarters. He doesn't need to be a star. He needs to make the Knicks a little more athletic and a little more dangerous inside.

And the message to Drummond isn't that he's done. It's that nothing is guaranteed. That's the same message Brown gave the entire camp: last year's ring doesn't buy you minutes. And for a team that's 0–3, that kind of competition might be exactly what the bench needs.

## FIVE FIXES BEFORE THE NEXT GAME

So what has to change? Here are five concrete fixes.

**One: protect the ball late.** Eight fourth-quarter turnovers is unacceptable for a veteran team. Simplify the late-game offense — fewer risky passes, more trips to the rim like Brunson showed on Sunday.

**Two: run more through KAT at the top of the key.** KAT has been fighting foul trouble all season. Using him as the high-post hub — handoffs, passes, pick-and-pop — keeps him out of collisions in the paint and gives Brunson easier looks.

**Three: put Jose Alvarado back next to KAT.** Last season, the Knicks were plus-8.8 per 100 possessions with those two together in the regular season and plus-32.5 in the playoffs. Alvarado's energy has been one of the few bright spots on the bench. Pair him with his best partner.

**Four: manage Bridges' minutes.** Until his shot and his defensive edge return, use the playoff playbook: shorter stints, and let the hot hand close.

**Five: keep the bigs fresh.** Use Wiseman in short bursts so Drummond and KAT have legs in the fourth quarter. The opener showed what happens when one big has to carry the load.

And the schedule doesn't get easier. The Knicks have a road game in Chicago on October 28th — the same Chicago team an anonymous executive mentioned as a possible KAT suitor. Expect plenty of storylines there too.

## THE PERSPECTIVE

Let's keep it in perspective. It's three games. The core that won the title is intact. Brunson is creating advantages at an elite level, and his defense has been strong. Bridges has a long track record. KAT has been fighting foul trouble. Things can turn quickly. But the history is real — the last champion to start 0–2 and still win a title was the 1990–91 Bulls, and now the Knicks are 0–3. The urgency has to start now.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. Brunson's jumper comes back — maybe not overnight, but the rim attacks Sunday were a great sign. Bridges is the bigger worry, and I'd like to see Mike Brown manage his minutes the way he did in the playoffs until the shot returns. Wiseman gets a real look in the rotation over the next week, and if he brings energy and finishes lobs, he keeps it. And Dalton Knecht might get spot minutes sooner than anyone expected if the wing shooting doesn't improve. First win comes soon. But the Knicks have to earn it — one clean fourth quarter at a time.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. The Magic beat the Knicks 111–102, closing on a 35–16 run after New York led 86–76 with nine minutes left — dropping the champs to 0–3, the first defending champion to do that since the 2011–12 Mavericks.
