## THE HONEST HEADLINE

Let's be clear right from the start, the NBA Tunnel way. There is no Mitchell Robinson trade. Nobody has reported talks between the Knicks and the Celtics. What we have is an analysis from Heavy's Sean Deveney, who looked at Robinson's rough start in Boston, speculated that the Celtics could move him by late January if the fit doesn't improve, and listed the Knicks as a natural destination because of their history together. That's it. It's speculation from a respected writer — not a trade, not a rumor of talks, and definitely not a "blockbuster." Now let's actually dig into it.

## WHAT'S HAPPENING IN BOSTON

Here's the situation. When Robinson signed with the Celtics, Joe Mazzulla talked about letting him do more offensively — giving him room to show a wider range. And Boston has followed through. Robinson is playing farther away from the basket.

The problem? That freedom has come at the cost of his superpower.

**Offensive rebounds per game:**
- Last season with the Knicks: 4.2
- This season with the Celtics: 1.8

That's a massive drop. And according to Deveney's analysis, the Celtics have actually played better with Robinson off the floor so far.

Here's the thing — this is not a mystery to Knicks fans. We all knew what made Mitch special. He's arguably the best offensive rebounder on the planet. When he's crashing from the dunker spot, he changes games. When he's standing out on the perimeter trying to be something else, a lot of that value disappears. As Deveney put it, if he's not dominating the offensive glass, he doesn't carry the same value.

## WHY THE KNICKS' NAME COMES UP

The logic for New York is simple: no team knows Robinson better, or knows how to use him better. The Knicks built entire lineups around his rebounding and rim protection. They know his strengths, his limitations, his health history, and exactly what role brings out his best.

And the need is real. Behind Karl-Anthony Towns, the backup center spot has been one of the biggest question marks of the season. Andre Drummond brings rebounding and some offense, but his defense has been inconsistent. James Wiseman has had flashes — including that eight points, eight rebounds, and three blocks night against Chicago — but he hasn't earned steady minutes. Mike Brown has even gone small with OG Anunoby at center. And Brown himself has called out the team's paint defense.

So on the basketball side, the idea makes sense. But basketball isn't the only thing that decides trades — especially for this team.

Let's run the checklist.

## CHECKLIST QUESTION 1: DOES BOSTON ACTUALLY WANT TO MOVE HIM?

**Answer: Not yet.**

Right now, this is about a slow start. It's November. Boston signed Robinson for a reason, and teams don't usually give up on a big free-agent signing after a few weeks. Deveney's speculation is specifically about late January — if the fit still looks bad by then.

And there's an easy fix on Boston's side: let Mitch be Mitch. If Mazzulla moves him back near the rim and lets him crash the glass, his numbers could bounce back fast. If that happens, the Celtics probably don't want to trade him at all.

Now, could Boston deal him to a rival? Some people say no way. But remember — this is the same Celtics front office that traded Jaylen Brown to Philadelphia this summer, a team they've had a long and bitter rivalry with. If the Knicks made the best offer, a trade to New York wouldn't be impossible. But "not impossible" is a long way from "likely."

**Checklist status: ❌ Not yet.**

## CHECKLIST QUESTION 2: CAN THE KNICKS AFFORD HIM?

**Answer: This is where it gets really hard.**

Here's the number that matters more than any other this season: according to Daily Knicks, the Knicks are only about $370,000 below the second apron. James Dolan has made it clear he does not want to go over that line.

Deveney acknowledged that a Robinson trade would likely push the Knicks over the second apron, and suggested they could find a way back under later — maybe around a Karl-Anthony Towns extension in the offseason. But that's a big "maybe," and it goes against everything this front office has done all year.

Think about it this way. If the Knicks were willing to go over the second apron, they probably would have just paid Robinson to stay last summer. They didn't. They chose a different path — Drummond, Wiseman, and flexibility. Why would they suddenly reverse that in January?

And even if they wanted to make the money work without crossing the line, they'd have to send out a lot of salary. The Knicks' tradable pieces are small — Pacôme Dadiet at about $3 million, Deuce McBride at about $3.9 million, and Jose Alvarado at about $4.4 million once he's eligible after December 15th. Matching a starting-level center salary would likely require more than that, or a bigger name.

**Checklist status: ❌ Very difficult.**

## CHECKLIST QUESTION 3: WHAT WOULD THE KNICKS HAVE TO GIVE UP?

**Answer: Probably too much.**

Let's say the Knicks tried. To match salary under the second-apron rules, they'd likely need to combine multiple rotation players. That could mean Deuce, Alvarado, and Dadiet together — and maybe more. And remember, the Knicks only have one tradable first-round pick right now, in 2033. They can't easily sweeten a deal with picks.

So you'd be trading away backcourt depth — the same depth that has helped the bench win its minutes — to bring back a backup center. That's the classic "fix one hole, open another" problem.

Is Mitch worth that? For a playoff run, you could argue his offensive rebounding and rim protection matter more than a backup guard. But giving up two playoff-tested guards for a player who's struggling in a new role is a risky bet.

**Checklist status: 🟡 Possible on paper, painful in reality.**

## CHECKLIST QUESTION 4: DOES HE STILL FIT IN NEW YORK?

**Answer: Yes — this is the easy one.**

This is the strongest part of the case. Robinson's skill set fits the Knicks perfectly, and the Knicks know exactly how to use it.

Picture it. KAT spaces the floor or rests. Mitch comes in, sets bone-crushing screens for Jalen Brunson, rolls hard, and crashes the offensive glass on every miss. On defense, he protects the rim and switches onto guards when needed. That's the role he played during the title run. It worked.

And here's a bonus: the Knicks' biggest problem early this season was offense — the slow starts, the cold shooting, the 9-for-38 night against Orlando. Offensive rebounds are one of the best cures for bad shooting. Every miss becomes a second chance. Robinson would literally give the offense more shots.

The one caveat is health. Robinson has had injuries in the past, and any team trading for him would have to think about durability. But in terms of fit, nobody fits the Knicks' system better.

**Checklist status: ✅ Perfect fit.**

## CHECKLIST QUESTION 5: IS THERE A BETTER OPTION?

**Answer: Probably — at least financially.**

We've spent the last few videos building out the Knicks' trade options. And realistically, the cheaper paths look more doable:

- **The $3 million tier:** Dadiet's expiring contract for a minimum-level big — names like Charles Bassey or Karlo Matković have come up in analysis.
- **The $7.4 million tier:** Dadiet plus Alvarado, if Tyler Kolek keeps holding down the backup point guard job.
- **Developing what they have:** Wiseman's big night against the Bulls showed there might be something there.

And as the video this idea came from pointed out, the type of player the Knicks were looking for all offseason was someone young, athletic, and up-and-coming — a center who helps them get better without triggering the second apron. That's still the target.

**Checklist status: 🟡 Cheaper options exist.**

## THE HONEST TRUTH: WHY THIS HURTS

Let's be real for a second. A lot of Knicks fans miss Mitch. He was a fan favorite. He was the longest-tenured Knick. He was part of the culture change in New York. He was there before Brunson, before the playoff runs, before the championship. And watching him struggle in a Celtics uniform, in a role that doesn't fit him, is honestly a little sad.

But that's also why this rumor spreads so fast. It's emotional. Fans want the band back together. And when a player leaves and struggles, it's natural to want to "rescue" him.

The front office can't make decisions based on nostalgia, though. Leon Rose and Brock Aller have to balance a championship roster, a looming wave of extensions — Towns, Josh Hart, eventually OG Anunoby and Jalen Brunson — and an owner who won't go into the second apron. Every move has to fit inside that box.

## WHAT MITCH MEANT TO THE TITLE RUN

Let's take a minute to remember exactly what the Knicks lost when Robinson left, because it explains why this rumor won't die.

**The extra possessions.** Four-plus offensive rebounds a night is not just a stat. It's four-plus extra chances to score. In tight playoff games, those second chances were often the difference. When the Knicks' shooters went cold, Mitch kept possessions alive.

**The playoff defense.** In the biggest moments, Robinson could switch onto guards, protect the rim, and wall off the paint. He was the kind of big man who made opponents think twice before driving.

**The free-throw strategy.** Teams sometimes tried to put him on the line late in games. But his presence on the glass and on defense often made it worth the risk for the Knicks to keep him out there.

**The bench anchor.** Coming off the bench behind Towns, he gave the Knicks a completely different look. When KAT sat, the defense didn't fall apart. It got tougher.

Compare that to this season. When KAT sits — especially with early foul trouble — the Knicks have struggled to hold the paint. Brooklyn grabbed 18 second-chance points against them. Brown has criticized the paint defense. The drop-off from starter to backup at center is the biggest weakness on this roster.

That's why fans keep dreaming about Mitch. Not because of nostalgia alone — because the hole he left is still there.

## WHAT IF BOSTON FIXES IT?

There's another scenario worth thinking about. What if the Celtics simply adjust? If Mazzulla moves Robinson back to the dunker spot, tells him to crash every miss, and lets him do what he does best, Boston could suddenly have one of the most dangerous rebounding bigs in the East — alongside Jayson Tatum and Paul George.

And that would be bad news for the Knicks on the court, not just the trade market. The Knicks and Celtics play multiple times this season, and they could meet in the playoffs. A fully weaponized Mitch Robinson playing against the Knicks is a scary thought — he knows their sets, their tendencies, and their weaknesses better than almost anyone.

So in a strange way, the best-case scenario for the Knicks might be Robinson continuing to be misused in Boston. Not because anyone wants him to struggle — but because a happy, dominant Mitch in green is a real problem for New York.

## WHAT LEON ROSE WILL PROBABLY DO

So what happens next? Here's my analysis.

**Leon Rose will make the call.** If Robinson becomes available, Rose will at least check in with Boston to see what the price is. That's what good front offices do. You always ask.

**But the price will probably be too high.** Between the second apron, the lack of picks, and the salary matching, the Knicks would almost certainly have to give up more than they want.

**The real move will be smaller.** My expectation is that the Knicks make their first trade after December 15th, using Dadiet's expiring contract — maybe with Deuce or Alvarado if Kolek keeps proving himself — to land a backup big who fits under the apron.

**And there's always a surprise.** With Leon Rose, you can never fully predict it. He's pulled off moves nobody saw coming before — remember Alvarado arriving last February? So don't completely rule out a creative deadline move. Just don't expect it to be Mitch.

## THE TITLE ODDS CHECK

A quick look at the market, because it explains why the Knicks will be aggressive this season. According to prediction market Kalshi, here's where the title odds stand:

- **San Antonio Spurs:** 23%
- **Oklahoma City Thunder:** 16%
- **Philadelphia 76ers:** 14%
- **Boston Celtics:** 7%
- **Houston Rockets:** 7%
- **New York Knicks:** 6%
- **Minnesota Timberwolves:** 4%

So the defending champions are sitting at 6%, behind two Eastern Conference teams — Philadelphia and Boston. That's what a 4–4 start does to your reputation. And it's exactly why the front office will be looking at every possible upgrade. The East got tougher, and the Knicks know they can't stand still.

But remember: odds after eight games aren't destiny. Six months ago, plenty of people didn't believe in this team either.

## WHERE THE KNICKS STAND

The Knicks came into the weekend at 4–4 after back-to-back wins — 103–90 over Brooklyn and that 135–105 demolition of Chicago, with Dalton Knecht's debut, Brunson's 22-point first quarter, and KAT's eighth straight double-double. Next up is the Cleveland trip, then back-to-back home games against Milwaukee and Cleveland.

And here's the funny connection: every night the backup center minutes look shaky, this Mitch rumor will get louder. Every night Wiseman or Drummond plays well, it gets quieter. So keep an eye on those minutes. They might tell you more about the trade market than any rumor will.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. Mitchell Robinson does NOT come back to the Knicks this season. The fit is perfect, but the second apron makes it almost impossible, and Boston isn't selling yet. I think Robinson's numbers improve in Boston once Mazzulla lets him crash the glass again, and the Celtics keep him. The Knicks' first trade comes after December 15th — a smaller move for a backup big using Dadiet's expiring deal. And one more: Mitch gets a huge standing ovation the first time he plays at the Garden in a Celtics uniform. That one I'm confident about.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. There is no Mitchell Robinson trade — just speculation from Heavy's Sean Deveney that Boston could move him by late January if the fit stays bad, with the Knicks listed as a natural destination. Robinson's offensive rebounds have dropped from 4.2 per game with the Knicks to 1.8 in Boston, as the Celtics have pulled him away from the basket. The basketball fit in New York is perfect. But with the Knicks only about $370,000 under the second apron, Dolan unwilling to cross it, and very few trade assets, a reunion looks nearly impossible right now. Cheaper options — Dadiet's expiring deal for a minimum-level big, or a Dadiet-plus-Alvarado package — are more realistic. And on the odds board, the Knicks sit at 6% for the title, behind the Spurs, Thunder, Sixers, and Celtics.
