THE AKINS DECISION: THE LAST TWO-WAY GOES TO... A GUARD

Here's the news: the Knicks have signed JADEN AKINS to their final two-way contract. All three developmental seats are now spoken for — McCullar, Nickel, and Akins. And let's do the honest scoreboard first, because accountability is the house rule: this channel's projected trio was Nickel, McCullar, and Liam ROBBINS — we assumed, like everyone, that with the frontcourt this thin, the last seat HAD to be a big. Wrong. Two out of three on the board, and a lesson in the miss: this front office drafts for TALENT in the development slots, not for position. Robbins' path now runs through Westchester — which is exactly why the affiliate paid a five-team mega trade for his rights, so nothing is lost there — but the two-way big we all penciled in... doesn't exist.

So who is Jaden Akins, beyond the file we opened when he first surfaced? Fill in the new details: the 23-year-old Michigan State combo guard didn't just have a nice G League season with the Motor City Cruise — he made the G League's ALL-STAR team. That's a real credential for a young guard finding his footing. The Vegas numbers — 11.3 a game on 36% shooting — look modest until you remember the context: that Summer League roster had almost no offensive creators, and Akins was asked to manufacture offense out of rubble. What stood out was exactly what the champs lack in the pipeline: explosive BURST creation — a guard who attacks in sudden violent gear-shifts, gets to his spots, and generates shots out of broken possessions. The organization liked it enough to run the classic machinery: Exhibit 10 signing, quick waive — with the little bonus perk Dolan's ownership supports, keeping young players fed and loyal through the Westchester door — and now, four days later, the real contract. Welcome to the family, officially.

And here's the strategic layer that makes this pick bigger than one guard. Remember how this championship was BUILT: the front office shipped an armada of future draft capital to Brooklyn and Minnesota for Bridges and KAT. Worth every pick — there's a banner that says so. But the bill is real: fewer drafts, fewer rookies, fewer cheap young contracts arriving the normal way for years to come. Which means the scrap heap IS the draft now — the Akins waiver-wire finds, the McCullar retentions, the Dante stashes. Every one of these unglamorous signings is this franchise's replacement for a first-round pick it no longer owns. Viewed through that lens, spending the final two-way on the highest-TALENT player available — rather than the most convenient POSITION — is exactly right. You can rent a third center. You cannot rent a 23-year-old shot creator with an All-G-League résumé.

One more note on what a two-way actually buys Akins, because the mechanism matters: he'll split time between Westchester and the big club, developing against real competition while staying one phone call from NBA minutes. And for a guard who already proved himself at the G League level — the All-Star nod says the level is beaten — the assignment is crystal clear: show you can translate the burst against NBA speed and NBA decision-making windows. The precedent for that exact journey is sitting in the locker room wearing a championship ring: three years ago Deuce McBride was a two-way-adjacent afterthought grinding for trust; today he's the most coveted mid-priced asset in the league. The pipeline doesn't just develop players here. It mints them.

Preseason starts October 5th. The proving ground is lit.

THE DANTE FILE: THE CONDOR, FULLY SCOUTED

And now my favorite part of today — the complete scouting file on N'Faly Dante, because the deep dive dropped and the details are delicious. Start with the frame, and the comp that made me sit up: 6'11" with a 7'6" wingspan — the SAME measurements as DeAndre Jordan. That's elite-lob-threat architecture, and the film backs it: a screener who releases early and rolls HARD, gets behind defenses, and uses that condor reach to catch passes most humans can't touch. Around the rim he finishes with power AND surprising finesse — and the numbers are almost comical: 78% in the restricted area across his first full G League season, 81% on close attempts in his final college year, and even 52% on shots OUTSIDE the restricted area — real touch, not just dunks. Short jumpers, traffic handles, simple reads: feel, in a frame that usually doesn't come with any.

The rebounding file is the headline, though: in the 2024-25 G League season, Dante was one of only FIVE players to average four-plus offensive rebounds a game — and he played the most games of that group. College offensive rebound rate: 10%, above average. Defensive glass: a monstrous 24.5% of available boards in college, 21% in the G League. And the defense got trophies: a 3.2% steal rate and a 6.6% block rate in his final college season — disruptive, active, long — earning Pac-12 All-Defensive honors. The honest limits, as always: he can look heavy-footed, projects as a drop-coverage rim protector rather than a switch big, and the offense is rim-runs and rolls, full stop. And the ACL recovery rules everything near-term — but say it with the file: an ACL changes the TIMELINE, not the ceiling. For an Exhibit 10 that costs nothing, the Knicks just secured supervised rehab rights to a DeAndre Jordan-framed glass-eater who was fought over by two NBA front offices eighteen months ago. That's not a flyer. That's a futures contract.

And picture the Westchester frontcourt this pipeline is quietly assembling, because it might be the most loaded affiliate big-man room in the league: Robbins — the 7-footer with the hook shot and the high-post passing, rights secured via five-team trade. Dante — the condor, rehabbing under team supervision, DeAndre Jordan frame and all. Possibly Broome, if the SEC Player of the Year watch pays off. Three completely different big-man archetypes — the skilled passer, the vertical eraser, the college winner — all developing in one system, one call-up away, costing the cap sheet essentially NOTHING. When the inevitable January frontcourt emergency hits — and it hits everyone — the champs won't be scanning the waiver wire in a panic. They'll be choosing which of their own graduates answers the phone. That's the difference between depth and a SUPPLY CHAIN, and this franchise built the latter in one offseason.
