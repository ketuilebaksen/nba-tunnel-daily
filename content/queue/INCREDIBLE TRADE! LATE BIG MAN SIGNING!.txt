## FRIDAY: THE KNICKS PULL THE PLUG

Here's what happened. On Friday, the Knicks made a public statement that they're tabling extension talks until later in the season. That covers Karl-Anthony Towns, and it covers Josh Hart.

Why? According to Shams Charania, this is the Knicks trying to eliminate the distraction — to get rid of the growing cloud around the team before the title defense begins. And he made it clear: the two sides are fully divided.

Think about where this came from. For weeks, every Knicks press conference turned into a contract press conference. KAT said "no comfort to be found." He said "it doesn't look good." He talked about feeling disrespected. Shams reported a trade could become possible if there's no extension. Begley described the January pivot. Bleacher Report floated Cleveland. Every practice, every preseason game, every quote — all of it got pulled back to the same question. So the front office made a decision: stop the noise, focus on basketball, and revisit the money later.

Is that smart? From a basketball standpoint, there's real logic. A defending champion can't spend the first month of the season answering contract questions every night. But from a relationship standpoint? That depends on how the player hears it. And we know exactly how KAT heard it.

## KAT'S RESPONSE: "IT WAS NEWS TO ME"

According to James L. Edwards III of The Athletic, here's KAT's full public response to the Knicks' statement:

"It was news to me. Very disappointed, to say the least. Let's state the obvious: it's business. But there is business on the court for us to handle. After 53 years, we found a way to bring this trophy back to New York, and we intend for it to stay in New York."

Let's decode that, line by line.

**"It was news to me."** That's the line that stings. KAT didn't get a heads-up. He learned about it with everyone else. For a player who just helped win a championship and has been negotiating in good faith, that's a tough way to find out.

**"Very disappointed, to say the least."** He's not hiding it. And honestly, I respect that. KAT has been open about his feelings this entire process, and he's not going to pretend now.

**"It's business. But there is business on the court."** And here's the pivot. In the same breath, he separates the contract from the competition. He's upset about one, and fully committed to the other.

**"We intend for it to stay in New York."** That's a leader's line. "We." Not "I." He's still talking like a Knick, about a Knicks future.

He doubled down on that when asked about his goals: "We're all humans, but what I do say is true. My only philosophy since we won, and the only thing I've had in my mind, is how do we win another one? The same focus I came in this season with is the same focus I'll go into game one with. How do we keep the trophy in New York past just this one summer?"

So the read is clear: disappointed with the front office, locked in with the team. Both things are true at the same time.

## THE DEADLINE NOBODY'S TALKING ABOUT: OCTOBER 19TH

And now the most important part of this video. According to the New York Post, there's a complication. KAT has until October 19th to decline his $61 million player option for next season. If he doesn't decline it, he cannot sign a contract extension with the Knicks during the regular season — even if the franchise reopens talks later, like it said it would.

Here's what KAT said about it: "I wouldn't say I've thought about it, because I never thought it would get to this point. But I think right now, what's best for our team is we just focus on the basketball." And according to Edwards, KAT said he really hasn't thought about declining or picking up the option yet, because he didn't think it would get here — and that he'd have to decline it to be able to legally negotiate during the season.

So in six days, KAT faces a fork in the road. Let's walk through both paths — and to be clear, what follows is NBA Tunnel analysis of how the options work, not a report on what he'll choose.

## PATH A: KAT DECLINES THE OPTION

If KAT declines the $61 million option by October 19th, here's what changes.

**For KAT:** He keeps the door open to signing an extension during the season, if the Knicks reopen talks "later in the season" like their statement said. And if no deal happens, he becomes an unrestricted free agent next summer, free to sign anywhere. That's maximum leverage. It's a bet on himself: play an All-NBA season, chase that rebounding title — remember, he's +500 to lead the league — and make the market prove his value.

**For the Knicks:** This is where it gets dangerous. If KAT declines, he's effectively on an expiring contract. And that's exactly the scenario Ian Begley described: you get to January, and you have to think about the pivot, because you can't lose him in free agency for nothing. Path A turns the January pivot from a theory into a real decision. The Knicks would face the hardest question of the season — keep him for the title run and risk losing him, or trade him before the deadline.

So Path A means more leverage for KAT — and more pressure on everybody.

## PATH B: KAT KEEPS THE OPTION

If KAT doesn't decline, here's the other side of the fork.

**For KAT:** He's locked in for next season at $61 million. That's security — a huge salary, guaranteed, no free agency risk next summer. But he gives up the chance to sign an extension during this regular season. Any new long-term deal would have to wait.

**For the Knicks:** And this is the part people miss. If KAT keeps the option, the "lose him for nothing" fear disappears — at least for this season. He's under contract through next year. That means the January pivot pressure drops dramatically. There's no expiring contract forcing a deadline decision. The front office gets what it said it wanted: no distraction, a full season of basketball, and talks that can resume when the timing is better.

And there's another layer. A player under contract for another season at $61 million is a very different trade asset than a rental. If the relationship ever truly breaks, the Knicks would have more time and more options. But honestly, if Path B happens, I think the most likely outcome is calm — both sides take a breath, the team plays, and the business waits until next summer.

## WHY THIS MATTERS SO MUCH

Put those paths side by side, and you see why October 19th might be the most important date of the Knicks' fall — even more than ring night.

Path A: KAT bets on himself, keeps in-season extension talks alive, and creates real January trade pressure.

Path B: KAT takes security, the Knicks get their quiet season, and the extension conversation moves to the summer.

And KAT says he hasn't even thought about it yet, because he never thought it would get this far. That tells you how sudden Friday's announcement was for him. He now has six days to make a decision that could shape the rest of his career — while preparing for a banner night and a title defense.

## MIKE BROWN'S SPEECH: "YOU CAN CONTROL YOUR PASSION"

So how did the locker room handle all of this? This is where Mike Brown deserves a lot of credit.

According to Zach Braziller of the New York Post, Brown addressed the contract situation with the entire team before practice on Sunday. And here's what he said about what matters to him: "If the passion is not there, the energy is not there, the effort's not there — then that's what drives me crazy, you know? Because you can control your passion. You can control your energy and your effort. But sometimes you can't control the turnover, or sometimes you may blow an assignment, because you're human — whether it's offense or defense."

And KAT loved it. He said the coaches and players talked openly and called it a "mindful practice." He appreciated Brown talking about everyone being human. "I got to give flowers to Mike. It was an amazing job by him."

Quick note for anyone who watched our Voices of Camp video: we covered that "flowers to Mike" quote there, and now we have the full context. That open conversation was about the contract situation. Mike Brown didn't pretend the elephant wasn't in the room. He addressed it, gave the players permission to be human about it, and then told them what he expects: passion, energy, effort. That's elite leadership. A coach can't fix a contract. But he can keep a locker room together while the contract gets figured out. Brown is doing exactly that.

## THE STEIN REPORT: WHY HART AND DEUCE WERE WAITING

Here's another piece of the puzzle that explains a lot. According to Marc Stein, league sources say Karl-Anthony Towns was the only extension-eligible Knick whose camp had been seriously engaged by the Knicks before New York shut down talks on Friday. In other words, the Knicks prioritized KAT's extension over Josh Hart's.

Why does that matter? Because it tells you the front office's strategy. They wanted to solve the biggest piece first. KAT's number determines everything else — how much room is left under the apron for Hart, whether Deuce McBride can be kept, and how this roster looks for the next several years. You can't plan the rest of the puzzle until the biggest piece is in place.

But it also means that with KAT talks frozen, Hart's situation is frozen too. Remember, Hart has a $22 million team option. And Deuce is heading toward free agency. The longer KAT goes unresolved, the longer everybody else waits. One negotiation is holding up three futures.

## THE DOLAN LINE: "MORE DEPARTURES WILL FOLLOW"

Now the reality check. Ian Begley said it plainly: if the Knicks want to keep this core together, they will have to go into the second apron. That's just the reality of the roster and where they are. You can't keep the core together without crossing that line.

And who decided not to cross it? According to The Athletic's Fred Katz, under a mandate from owner James Dolan, New York's front office has been told not to go into the second apron — and that's caused a lot of bumps in negotiations right after the championship. Katz also framed it bluntly: if Dolan won't go over the second apron under any condition — an approach The Athletic reported the front office disagrees with, shortly after Dolan's interview with WFAN — then more departures will follow.

Let that sink in. According to that reporting, this isn't Leon Rose's preference. The front office would reportedly be willing to go further to keep the champions together. The line is coming from the top. And the consequences of that line won't stop with KAT. "More departures will follow." That's the long-term picture for this core if nothing changes.

To be fair to Dolan: the second apron's penalties are severe — the trade restrictions, the lost exceptions, the risk of a frozen first-round pick for a team that already can't trade firsts before 2033. And 28 other teams are staying under it this season too. There's a real financial argument. But there's also a real cost: this might be the last season we see this exact championship roster together.

## THE UNION ANGLE: KAT IS LIVING HIS OWN ISSUE

And here's a twist that makes this story even more interesting. Some fans have argued in the comments that the players' union was fine with the second apron, since it agreed to the CBA. But that's not the full picture.

This summer, after taking over, the union's new executive director said: "We are no fan of the second apron." He explained that when players make the decision to keep their team together and take less money, the player has to bear the brunt of that decision — because ownership has made it clear they're not going to go past the second apron. And he said it shouldn't all rest with the player.

Now here's the twist: Karl-Anthony Towns is a vice president of that union. He's not just living this problem — he's one of the leaders of the organization that's publicly criticizing it. The rule his union opposes is the exact rule shaping his own contract. That's why the union's words hit so close to home here. The burden of keeping this team together is landing on KAT's shoulders, and on Hart's, and on Deuce's. Not on ownership's.

Will that change? Not anytime soon. The rules are the rules until the next CBA. But it tells you that this fight isn't just Knicks versus KAT. It's players versus a system — and the Knicks are the clearest example in the league of what that system does to a champion.

Now think about the timing. October 19th is the option deadline. October 20th is ring night against Philadelphia — the banner, the rings, the 53-year wait finally celebrated in front of the Garden crowd. KAT will make one of the biggest decisions of his career and, less than 24 hours later, walk out to receive a championship ring from the same organization he's "very disappointed" with.

Sometimes the crowd says what the front office can't.
