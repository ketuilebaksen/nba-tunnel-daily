THE CONFIRMATION: TALKS NEVER STOPPED

Via Sleeper Knicks and NBA insider Jake Fischer: the Knicks have contacted SEVERAL teams around the league regarding centers — and the two names to keep an eye on remain exactly who you think: Pelicans center Yves Missi and Hornets center Moussa Diabaté. Weeks of public silence, and behind the curtain the same hunt we've documented all summer never paused for a single day. That's this front office in a nutshell — no leaks, no noise, just phone calls.

And Ian Begley added the piece that frames everything: Deuce McBride, on his expiring contract, is quote — "one of the most valuable trade chips in the LEAGUE. He would garner significant interest if New York made him available via trade, either in-season or in the offseason. We're certainly not there yet, but logic tells you that's where things could be headed." There it is again — the same drumbeat from every insider, every week, all pointing one direction. The chip is real, the market for it is deep, and while nothing is imminent, the logic train has only one destination. Pair Deuce with a young sweetener — a Dadiet, maybe a Kolek — and a few of those restocked second-rounders, and either center on that board becomes gettable ON PAPER. But paper is where our problem begins.

MY PICK — AND WHY IT'S COMPLICATED

Let me lay my cards down first: between the two targets, give me MOUSSA DIABATÉ. Missi is wonderful — we've made his case a dozen times — but Diabaté is the cleaner bet: young, ascending, relentless on the glass, switchable enough for Mike Brown's schemes, and still nowhere near his ceiling. He's the exact profile the doctrine demands, and at this point on the calendar, honestly? I'd sprint to the podium for EITHER of them. Camp is weeks away. Beggars and choosers, folks.

But here's where I did my homework and found the problem. If Deuce McBride is the centerpiece of any package, you have to look at the buyer's roster and ask the only question that matters: do they need what we're selling? So walk through Charlotte's guard room with me. They traded for Dennis Schröder a few weeks back — the exact move that already killed our Kolek-for-Kalkbrenner dreams. They've got Coby White. They've got Kon Knueppel, who we've called the best young shooting guard in basketball on this very channel. And they used a 2026 draft pick on ANOTHER guard, Christian Anderson Jr., a kid who'll be climbing their depth chart for years. That's four names deep before you even reach the end of their bench — and if I kept scrolling the roster, there are MORE. So ask it honestly: does that team need Miles McBride and Tyler Kolek? Probably not. Would Deuce, Kolek, and second-rounders pry away a player they genuinely believe in — a core piece they WANT to grow with? Probably not. That's the wall, stated plainly. Not that Deuce lacks value — Begley just told you he's one of the league's best chips. The wall is that the ONE team with our dream center is the one team already drowning in exactly what we'd be sending back.

And New Orleans? Same story, different verse — we've been rejected so many times at that door that the hinges know our knock. Joe Dumars has stonewalled every construction, every sweetener, every angle since June. The interest on our side has never wavered. The willingness on theirs has never appeared.

So how DOES a Diabaté deal actually get done, if not with guards? Let's think like a front office for a second. Charlotte's real needs aren't backcourt bodies — they're draft capital for the rebuild and wing depth behind their young core. Which reframes the package entirely: maybe the Deuce conversation with Charlotte was always meant to be a THREE-team construction — Deuce rerouted to a guard-hungry contender (and Begley just told you the league is full of interested parties), Charlotte collecting the picks and a wing like Dadiet from the pile, and the young center landing in New York. Three-team trades are brutal to choreograph in September and routine in February — one more reason the calendar, not the desire, is what's really standing between us and this deal. The pieces fit. The middleman just hasn't shown up yet.

THE WAITING GAME — AND WHY IT'S ACTUALLY THE RIGHT PLAY

So is this pursuit dead? No — and here's the part of Begley's quote that matters most: "either in-season or near the trade deadline." Circle that, because it's the whole strategy. Right now, in September, the Hornets and Pelicans are drunk on offseason optimism — every team is undefeated in September, every young core looks untouchable, every GM believes. But seasons have a way of administering reality. Fast-forward to December: maybe Charlotte's playoff push is sputtering. Maybe New Orleans is 8-20 again and the pressure to collect assets becomes unbearable. THAT'S when a stonewalling front office suddenly rediscovers our phone number — and that's when the Deuce chip detonates at full value. Patience isn't passivity. Patience is waiting for the other side's leverage to rot.

And let's restate the non-negotiable, because it's the nightmare scenario that disciplines everything: Deuce McBride CANNOT walk for nothing next summer. We lived this movie with Mitchell Robinson — watched a homegrown piece leave and got nothing back but cap relief and heartache. History repeating itself there would be unforgivable, and this front office knows it. So yes — I don't want to see Deuce go. You know I don't. But between a painful trade that returns a young starting-caliber center and a free-agency goodbye that returns NOTHING? That's not a choice at all. Keep talking, Leon. Keep every line warm.

And spare a thought for what this limbo means for Deuce himself, because the human side deserves airtime. The man is entering a contract year — the most important season of his financial life — while every insider in America publicly debates which package he headlines. That's a brutal backdrop to work against, and how he handles it will tell you everything about his professionalism. My prediction? He handles it like he's handled everything: head down, defense first, threes flying. Because here's the paradox Deuce surely understands — the better he plays through the noise, the more valuable the chip becomes, and the better the center who arrives in the deal that moves him... or the stronger his case to be re-signed if the front office blinks. Either way, his path runs through excellence. Contract-year Deuce McBride might quietly be one of the best bench weapons in basketball this fall. Enjoy every minute of it while it lasts.

THE CASE FOR WIDENING THE NET

Now, my one push: it's time for the range of these talks to expand beyond the same two stubborn sellers. And the board has never been more interesting. The CLIPPERS — a franchise we just watched get stripped of picks and leadership, staring down a mandatory rebuild with young bigs like Niederhauser on the shelf and a desperate need for exactly the draft capital New York can offer. The MAVERICKS — a roster stacked with bigs, some of whom won't crack the rotation, exactly the kind of logjam that produces value trades by November. And around the league, camp cuts are coming in waves over the next month — teams will waive useful bigs they simply can't fit, and the champs should be first in line at every waiver wire and every buyout conversation. Some teams will hold their bigs hostage specifically to keep them AWAY from the defending champs — we've seen that wall all summer. Fine. The answer to a closed door isn't knocking harder; it's finding the building with more doors. Two targets is a wish list. Six targets is a strategy.

One last piece of perspective on the waiting: remember that the roster as constructed already won everything. The urgency around the third center is real, but it's the urgency of perfectionists, not of a broken team. If the season opened tomorrow, New York rolls out the best starting five in the conference, the deepest wing rotation in basketball, a Sixth Man of the Year candidate, and two championship-tested bigs. The missing piece is insurance, not salvation. That's precisely WHY the front office can afford to let Charlotte and New Orleans stew, let the cut wire ripen, let the Clippers' desperation mature — because every alternative to a perfect deal is still... the defending champions. Teams negotiating from need get fleeced. Teams negotiating from strength get their price. Guess which one we are.
