## WHERE THIS STARTED

First, some context on the video itself. It's a long, story-driven piece. The narrator talks about climbing a wall at a gym when he heard the "news," and he uses a dog-walker metaphor to explain the Pelicans' situation. It's creative, and to be fair, the narrator includes some honest caveats of his own. At one point he says nobody has reported that the Knicks and Pelicans have actually talked, and that the deal is "expected, not official."

But then the video keeps returning to one refrain, attributed to Shams: "This trade is going to happen." That line is what people are clipping and sharing. So that's where we start, with the single sentence that's doing all the work.

## TEST ONE: THE SOURCE TEST

**The claim:** Shams Charania said on ESPN's First Take that he "can confirm the Knicks are making the call" for Dejounte Murray, laid out the package, and closed with "this trade is going to happen."

**What we found:** We searched for any report from Shams, or from ESPN, The Athletic, or any national outlet, connecting the Knicks to Dejounte Murray this season. We couldn't find one. No article, no post on X, no NBA Today segment, nothing. What we did find were old stories. The Knicks were linked to Murray back in 2022, when he was leaving San Antonio. And Shams reported on the Lakers' talks for Murray in January 2024, when Murray was with the Atlanta Hawks. Those reports are real, but they're from years ago, about a different team situation.

**Does the quote sound like Shams?** Not really. Shams reports what is happening: "talks are progressing," "teams are discussing," "a deal is being finalized." He doesn't predict outcomes with a line like "this trade is going to happen." And he doesn't usually describe a package as vaguely as "mid-level contracts plus a second-round pick" without names. When Shams has a deal, he has specifics.

**Verdict: UNVERIFIED, and likely not real.** Until you can see the clip from an official ESPN source or the post on Shams' own account, treat this as invented. The rule on this channel is simple: if you can't find it at the source, it didn't happen.

## TEST TWO: THE NUMBERS TEST

Now, this is what makes viral videos convincing. They mix fake claims with real numbers. So let's check the numbers.

**Claim: Murray is averaging 17.8 points, six assists, and two steals in four games.**
**Verdict: VERIFIED.** His season stats match exactly: four games, 71 points, 24 assists, and eight steals. Those are good numbers, but in a small sample.

**Claim: The Pelicans are 1–4 and got their first win this week over Sacramento.**
**Verdict: PLAUSIBLE.** We couldn't confirm the exact box score, but it's consistent with a slow start in New Orleans.

**Claim: Rookie Jeremiah Fears had 22 points, eight rebounds, and 13 assists in that win.**
**Verdict: PLAUSIBLE, BUT UNCONFIRMED.** Fears had several 20-point games against Sacramento as a rookie last season, so a big night is believable. We just couldn't independently confirm this specific line. Quick note: Fears isn't really a rookie anymore. He was a 2025 draft pick, so this is his second season.

**Claim: Murray earns $32.8 million.**
**Verdict: UNCONFIRMED.** Murray is on a big multi-year deal he signed with Atlanta, and his salary is around the $30 million range. We couldn't confirm the exact $32.8 million figure. Either way, the point stands: he's an expensive player.

**Claim: A local reporter wrote that Fears' growing role complicates Murray's place in New Orleans.**
**Verdict: PLAUSIBLE.** That's a reasonable analysis. A young guard breaking out squeezes the veteran's minutes and touches. But "complicates" is a roster observation. It isn't a trade report, and it doesn't involve the Knicks.

**Claim: A website outlined four Murray trades.**
**Verdict: TRUE, BUT IT MEANS NOTHING.** Trade-idea articles are written every day. A writer outlining four ideas is a brainstorm, not a negotiation.

So here's the pattern. The real parts of the video are the stats and the Pelicans' roster situation. The unverified parts are the parts that involve the Knicks. That's exactly how these videos work. The real numbers make you trust the fake quote.

## TEST THREE: THE MONEY TEST

This is where the story really falls apart. Let's do the salary math in plain English.

**Step one: the Knicks can't just absorb salary.** They're over the cap, so to take in a player making around $30 million, they have to send out a lot of salary in return. That's how NBA trades work for teams over the cap.

**Step two: the apron makes it even harder.** The Knicks are only about $3.2 million below the first apron. That's why they still can't fill the 15th roster spot. Bring in Murray's salary without sending out nearly the same amount, and they fly past the first apron. Once a team is over the first apron, the matching rules get much stricter, and you basically have to send out close to what you take in. Owner James Dolan has also shown he doesn't want to go into the second apron. Adding $30 million in salary is the exact opposite of what this front office has been doing.

**Step three: what "mid-level contracts" could the Knicks even send?** Let's go through the roster. Brunson, Towns, Bridges, and OG are the core, and they're not going anywhere in a deal like this. Josh Hart has the only real mid-sized contract, and he's a starter and the heart of this team. After that, the numbers drop off fast. Deuce McBride makes about $3.9 million. Pacôme Dadiet makes about $3 million. Tyler Kolek is on a small deal. Even combined, those three don't come close to $30 million.

**Step four: the calendar.** Many of the Knicks' newly signed or re-signed players, like Clarkson, Drummond, Wiseman, and Alvarado, can't be traded until December 15th at the earliest. So in October, the Knicks' list of tradable salary is even smaller.

**Verdict: DOESN'T ADD UP.** For the Knicks to land Murray, they'd almost certainly have to send out Josh Hart, or a core player, plus more. That's not "mid-level contracts and a second." The package described in this video doesn't exist on the Knicks' current roster.

## TEST FOUR: THE FIT TEST

Let's be fair and ask the basketball question too. If money weren't an issue, would Murray even help?

**The case for:** Murray is a talented guard who can score, pass, and get his hands on the ball defensively. Two steals a game is real. He's been an All-Star, back in 2022 with San Antonio. And yes, the Knicks would love another creator on nights when Brunson needs a break.

**The case against:** The video says Murray "does not need the ball when Brunson plays." I'd push back on that. Murray has spent most of his career as a lead guard. When Atlanta paired him with Trae Young, two ball-dominant guards, it never fully clicked, and that's part of why the Hawks traded him to New Orleans. He's also coming back from a major injury. He tore his Achilles in early 2025 and returned last season. And at around $30 million, he's being paid like a star.

**The Knicks' actual need:** Right now, the Knicks' problems are slow starts, turnovers, and bench consistency. Their backup point guard spot also has an answer in progress. Tyler Kolek had 21 assists and zero turnovers over three games before the Philly loss. The "second playmaker" the video talks about may already be on the roster, and he costs a fraction of Murray's salary.

**Verdict: QUESTIONABLE FIT AT THAT PRICE.** Murray is a good player. But trading Hart for a ball-dominant guard coming off an Achilles injury, while blowing through the apron, isn't the kind of move Leon Rose makes.

## THE BRUNSON WRIST ANGLE

The video leans hard on the idea that Brunson needs help "after wrist surgery." So let's check that too.

Yes, Brunson had surgery on his left wrist in July. And yes, his shooting was off in the first week, when he shot 39 percent from the field heading into the Detroit game. But then he dropped 38 in Chicago on 13-of-25 with six threes, and followed it up with 24 points and a go-ahead three in Philadelphia. The wrist looks like it's coming around.

Is it smart to have extra ball-handling insurance? Sure. But the premise that the Knicks are desperate for a $30 million guard because of Brunson's wrist doesn't match what we've seen over the last week.

## THE LAKERS PART

The video also says the Lakers are in a "Murray auction" with the Knicks. Here's what we can say: the Lakers wanted Murray in January 2024, when he was in Atlanta, and Shams reported on those talks. That's real history. But we found no current report this season about the Lakers and Murray either. It looks like old reporting being recycled into a new story.

## HOW TO SPOT THIS NEXT TIME

Tunnel fam, this is the third or fourth time this fall that we've seen a viral trade "report" built this way. We fact-checked the Cason Wallace rumor and the KAT-for-Anthony Davis story, and now Murray. They share the same DNA. Here's a quick checklist:

- **A big name paired with a dramatic quote.** "This trade is going to happen" is a hook, not reporting.
- **Real stats as camouflage.** Accurate numbers make the fake parts feel trustworthy.
- **A vague package.** "Mid-level contracts plus a second" sounds specific but names nobody, because naming players would expose that the math doesn't work.
- **No link to the source.** Real Shams news is on his X account and on ESPN within minutes. If you can't find it there, it isn't real.
- **A long story before the facts.** When a video spends minutes on a personal story before getting to the news, that's often a sign the news itself is thin.

## WHAT A REALISTIC GUARD MOVE LOOKS LIKE

So what could the Knicks actually do if they want more backcourt help? This is my analysis, not reporting.

**Option one: nothing.** Kolek is producing, Clarkson is giving instant offense, and Alvarado should be back from his ankle sprain soon. The best "addition" might be getting healthy.

**Option two: a small, cheap upgrade after December 15th.** Use Dadiet's now effectively expiring contract, Deuce's expiring deal, and the extra second-round picks from the de Larrea trade to bring in a veteran guard on a modest salary. That fits under the apron. It's the kind of move Rose and Brock Aller have made before: Bogdanović and Burks at the deadline, P.J. Tucker in March, Alvarado last February.

**Option three: a bigger swing.** That would mean moving a starter like Hart, and there's no sign this front office wants to break up the championship core.

Notice what's missing? A $30 million guard for spare parts. That option doesn't exist. And honestly, the smartest version of this front office is the one we've already seen: patient, creative with small contracts, and unwilling to break the core over a hot October rumor. That's how they built a champion, and it's how they'll try to keep one.

## WHERE THE KNICKS ACTUALLY ARE

Let's bring it back to reality. The video was recorded before the Philadelphia game. Since then, the Knicks lost 98–93 to the Sixers and fell to 2–4. LeBron James had 20 points, nine rebounds, and eight assists, Maxey scored 27, and Philly won even without Jaylen Brown. KAT played through a wrist brace with 22 and 12. And on Monday, the winless 0–5 Brooklyn Nets come to the Garden.

That's the real news, and it's plenty to talk about without inventing more. The real problems are slow starts, turnovers, and bench consistency. None of them get solved by a trade that isn't happening.

## WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND

To be fair, I'm not saying a Knicks-Murray connection is impossible forever. NBA rosters change fast. So here are the signs that would make me take this seriously:

- **A real report from a national insider.** A post on Shams' account, an ESPN or The Athletic article, or a clear report from SNY's Ian Begley or Marc Stein. Not a paraphrase in a YouTube video, but the actual source.
- **Murray's role shrinking in New Orleans.** If Fears keeps taking over and Murray's minutes or shots drop for several weeks, the Pelicans' motivation to move him becomes real.
- **The Knicks' backcourt getting hurt.** If Alvarado's ankle becomes a long-term issue, or Brunson's wrist flares up again, the need for a veteran guard goes up.
- **A three-team structure.** The only realistic way to make the money work would be a bigger deal, with a third team absorbing salary. If you hear about a third team, that's when it gets interesting.
- **The calendar.** Most real trades happen after December 15th, when newly signed players become eligible, or close to the February trade deadline. A trade "happening" in October, with nothing reported, is a red flag on its own.

If two or three of those things happen, I'll make a new video and tell you the picture changed. That's a promise. Until then, this stays in the "fun idea, not real news" bucket.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. Dejounte Murray is not becoming a Knick this season. The source can't be found, the salary math doesn't work, and the fit is questionable at that price. If the Knicks make a backcourt move, it'll be a small one after December 15th, using Dadiet, Deuce's expiring contract, or second-round picks, for a role player who fits under the apron. And if you see "Shams said this trade is going to happen" anywhere, check his account before you believe it. On the court, the Knicks beat the Nets on Monday and get to 3–4.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands:

- **The report:** A viral video claims Shams said the Knicks are calling about Dejounte Murray and that "this trade is going to happen." We couldn't find any such report from Shams, ESPN, or any national outlet. The only Knicks-Murray and Lakers-Murray reporting we found is from years ago.
- **What's real:** Murray is averaging 17.8 points, 6 assists, and 2 steals in four games, and the young-guard squeeze in New Orleans is a reasonable storyline.
- **The money:** With the Knicks about $3.2 million under the first apron and Dolan avoiding the second apron, a roughly $30 million guard for "mid-level contracts plus a second" doesn't add up.
- **The fit:** Murray has been a ball-dominant guard, he's coming back from an Achilles injury, and Kolek is already filling the second-playmaker role for a fraction of the cost.
- **The real Knicks news:** They're 2–4 after the Philly loss, with the Nets on Monday.
