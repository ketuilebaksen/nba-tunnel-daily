THE MECHANISM: ARTICLE 11, OR HOW MATCHING MEANS INHERITING

Here's where the entire story actually lives, so let's do the forensics properly. Under Article 11 of the NBA's collective bargaining agreement, when a restricted free agent signs an offer sheet with a new team, his original team has one way to keep him: MATCH the offer sheet — and matching means matching the PRINCIPAL TERMS, exactly as written. Not a similar deal. Not a renegotiated version with friendlier dates. The exact salary structure, the exact guarantee schedule, the exact trigger clauses that the rival team put on paper. The matching team does not get to rewrite a single line. It's a photocopy with a new logo on top.

Now apply that to July. The Knicks built Cissé's two-year offer sheet with a very specific piece of architecture: a first season that was only PARTIALLY guaranteed, with the rest of the money locking in on a trigger date the Knicks chose — October 1st. Think about why a team writes that clause for itself: it's a trapdoor. If the player comes to your camp and disappoints, you can release him before the date and owe only the partial money. Standard, sensible, self-protective contract writing. But then Dallas matched — and here's the beautiful, under-covered consequence: Dallas inherited the trapdoor. The calendar, the trigger, the decision deadline — all of it transferred, unedited, into the Mavericks' books. Which means that this week, right now, a rival front office is making one of its genuinely uncomfortable roster decisions on a schedule designed in New York, for a roster plan that no longer exists, by a front office that lost the player months ago. The team that "lost" the restricted free agency battle still got the last word on the calendar he lives by in his new building. And folks — that is the exact OPPOSITE of what losing usually means in this league. Usually, losing a player means the story ends and the other team writes the epilogue. This time, the epilogue was pre-written in Manhattan, and Dallas is just reading it aloud.
