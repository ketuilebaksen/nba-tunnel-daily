## JALEN BRUNSON OUTSCORED THE BULLS BY HIMSELF

Let's start with the captain, because this was vintage Brunson.

- **First quarter:** 22 points on seven-of-14 shooting. That's more than the entire Bulls team, which scored 21.
- **Halftime:** 26 points.
- **Through three quarters:** 33 points, and then he sat. His 28 minutes were tied for the team high.

Remember the wrist conversation? Through six games, Brunson was shooting 39.5 percent from the field and 30.5 percent from three, the worst marks of his career. Since then, he's had 38 in Chicago, a gritty 24 in Philly, 19 against Brooklyn, and now 33 in 28 minutes against Chicago. The touch is coming back.

## THE OTHER STARS

**OG Anunoby:** Eighteen points at halftime, with three steals in the second quarter alone. And here's the stat that jumps out: he went to the free-throw line 12 times in the first half, one shy of his Knicks career high for a full game. The entire Bulls team had six free-throw attempts at halftime. That's the "catch-and-go, attack closeouts" OG we said he should be. He wasn't running isolation; he was attacking downhill with purpose.

**Karl-Anthony Towns:** A quiet first half with five points, then a big third quarter, finishing with 17 points and 12 rebounds by the end of three. That's eight straight double-doubles to start the season, eight for eight.

**Mikal Bridges:** He was scoreless in the first quarter, with two assists and two rebounds, then scored seven in the second quarter. The reports I saw didn't include his final line, so I won't guess. But he looked a little more involved. Baby steps.

**Josh Hart:** He sat this one out with right ankle soreness. Keep an eye on that before the trip to Cleveland. Landry Shamet started in his place, scored the team's first four points, and played 25 minutes.

## THE MAIN EVENT: DALTON KNECHT'S DEBUT

Now let's talk about the night's real story.

**The context:** Knecht came into the night with Hart out and a game that turned into a blowout early. Brown admitted he had no plan to play him. "He's on my quote-unquote minute sheets. He's down for zero minutes." Then he went with his gut.

**The line:**

- **Minutes:** 16
- **Points:** 10
- **Rebounds:** 7
- **Field goals:** 4-of-6
- **Three-pointers:** 2
- **Blocks:** 1

**The highlights:**

- **The putback slam.** His first points as a Knick came when Shamet missed a wide-open three short, it bounced off the backboard, and Knecht flew in and slammed it down. The crowd went wild.
- **The step-back three.** In the second quarter, he got his defender off balance and drilled a tough step-back three. Chicago wanted a push-off call. It didn't come.
- **The defense.** Late in the third, he blocked a Buddy Hield shot. That's notable, because defense has been the biggest knock on him.
- **The fourth-quarter threes.** He kept shooting with confidence, and it showed.

## KNECHT IN HIS OWN WORDS

After the game, Knecht said he wasn't surprised to get the call. "No, I wasn't surprised. I just came in ready to go. The coaches said 'you're active' — that just means be ready."

That's exactly what you want to hear from a player in his situation. A few weeks ago, the Lakers waived him. The Bucks reportedly asked for draft picks just to take him in a trade. No team claimed him off waivers. He came to New York on a two-way deal and was assigned to Westchester. He used all of that as fuel, stayed ready, and when his name got called, he delivered.

## WHY THIS MATTERS: THE SHOOTING NIGHTMARE CONNECTION

Remember our video about the Orlando loss, the nine-of-38 night from three? We asked where the shooting would come from. We talked about Kolek, Deuce, Shamet, and the "new sharpshooter," Knecht. We said if Knecht found his rhythm, he could become "the kind of bench weapon that turns a 9-for-38 night into a 13-for-38 night."

On Wednesday, we got a first glimpse of that. A 6-foot-6 wing who can catch and shoot, cut backdoor, and finish above the rim gives the Knicks spacing around Brunson and KAT. That's the fit we described.

## THE HONEST CAVEATS ABOUT KNECHT

Let's be fair, because one great night isn't a breakout.

**It was a blowout.** A few successful minutes in a 30-point win aren't the same as closing a tight game against Cleveland.

**Hart was out.** That's part of why there were wing minutes available. When Hart returns, the rotation gets crowded again.

**The defense is still a question.** The block on Hield was great, but his defensive metrics in Los Angeles graded out negatively. One play doesn't change that.

**The two-way limit.** He can appear in up to 50 NBA games this season. The Knicks have to be smart about when they use him.

The fair conclusion: Knecht earned another look, probably in a small role and in matchups where his shooting matters, without being promised closing minutes. That's the right approach.

## WHAT ABOUT THE "BRIDGES BENCHING" TALK?

Some channels are saying Bridges "is being benched" and that Knecht could take his minutes. Let's slow that down. Bridges played in this game and started. His minutes were reduced in Chicago and against Brooklyn, but nobody has reported a benching. Knecht isn't replacing Bridges. He's competing for the 11th-to-14th man minutes, especially at forward, where Mo Diawara hasn't played much and Pacôme Dadiet's option was just declined.

That said, Knecht's emergence does change the forward depth chart. And that brings us to our rankings.

## THE TWO-WAY MATH: CAN KNECHT STICK?

Here's a cap angle most people are missing, and it ties into the correction I made last video.

Knecht is on a two-way contract. That means two things. First, he can appear in up to 50 NBA games this season. Second, his salary doesn't count the same way a standard contract does, which is exactly why the Knicks could add him at all.

Now, if Knecht keeps playing well, fans are going to ask: why not convert him to a standard contract and give him that open 15th roster spot? Here's the problem. As we covered last time, the Knicks reportedly have only about $370,000 of room under the second apron. A standard minimum contract would cost more than that, so converting Knecht would likely push them over the line James Dolan has said he won't cross. That's my read on the cap math, not a report.

So for now, the two-way deal is the vehicle, and the Knicks have to manage his 50 games carefully. Use him in the right matchups and keep him sharp in Westchester in between. If the front office makes a salary-shedding move before the deadline, say a Dadiet trade, that could open the door for a conversion later. Keep that in the back of your mind every time trade talk comes up. It might not just be about who comes in. It might be about making room for Knecht.

## MIKE BROWN'S GUT HAS A TRACK RECORD

Let's give the coach some credit. This wasn't the first time Brown trusted a gut feeling and got rewarded.

- **Tyler Kolek** got his chance when Alvarado went down, and put up 21 assists and zero turnovers over three games.
- **Jordan Clarkson** carried the Chicago comeback when Brown turned away from his starters. Ten players logged at least 13 minutes that night.
- **Landry Shamet** went from bench depth to a 22-point game against Brooklyn, then started against Chicago.
- **Last season,** Diawara, Kevin McCullar Jr., and Kolek all got minutes ahead of more experienced players at different points of the year.

Brown has said only five rotation spots are locked, and he means it. Minutes are earned, and sometimes they're handed out on instinct. On Wednesday, the instinct was Knecht, and he made Brown look like a genius.

## THE OTHER DEEP-BENCH STORIES

Fourteen Knicks played, and 12 logged at least 10 minutes. Here's who else stood out:

- **James Wiseman:** Eight points, eight rebounds, and three blocks in about 10 minutes, all in the fourth quarter. Remember our "hard truth" video, when he had 10 minutes in five games? This was his best stretch as a Knick. To be fair, he also had a couple of sloppy turnovers on passes from Jose Alvarado, so it wasn't perfect. But three blocks in 10 minutes from a team that ranked 24th in blocks coming in? That's what the Knicks need from a backup big.
- **Mo Diawara:** His first points of the season, seven in the first half, and blocks in the second half. A season high in minutes.
- **Deuce McBride:** Seven points in the third quarter, including a transition three and a four-point play. Deuce is starting to look like Deuce again.
- **Tyler Kolek:** Four assists in the second quarter while Brunson rested.
- **Andre Drummond:** Opened the fourth quarter with an alley-oop.
- **Pacôme Dadiet:** Four points in the fourth quarter. A nice moment for a young player who's had a tough week of headlines.

**The team:** Diawara, Wiseman, and Knecht combined for six blocks. And the Knicks won the fast-break battle 19–6.

## A WORD ABOUT THE BULLS

Let's be fair about the opponent. ClutchPoints' Brett Siegel said this Bulls team may be one of the worst defensive teams in NBA history. He pointed out that Chicago has given up more than 124 points in every game this season, and more than 130 in four of them. The Knicks scored 133 there last week and 135 here. So yes, part of this blowout is about the opponent.

But a champion is supposed to crush the teams it should crush, and the Knicks did exactly that. After weeks of slow starts, a 43–21 first quarter is exactly the response we wanted.

## TUNNEL ROTATION POWER RANKINGS: 8 GAMES IN

Here's something new. After eight games, how does the Knicks rotation stack up? These are my rankings based on production, role, and trust so far, not salary or reputation.

**Tier 1: The Engines**

1. **Karl-Anthony Towns:** Eight-for-eight in double-doubles, the most consistent starter, and the anchor of the team.
2. **Jalen Brunson:** The touch is coming back. Thirty-three in 28 minutes, and he's still the closer.

**Tier 2: The Pillars**

3. **OG Anunoby:** Up-and-down, but 18 against Brooklyn and 18 in a half against Chicago with 12 free throws. That's the right version.
4. **Josh Hart:** Seven assists and three steals against Brooklyn despite foul trouble. Healthy Hart is irreplaceable.

**Tier 3: The Bench Heroes**

5. **Landry Shamet:** Twenty-two against Brooklyn and a fill-in starter against Chicago. Hottest bench player in the rotation.
6. **Jordan Clarkson:** The team's best plus-minus. He still deserves more minutes.
7. **Tyler Kolek:** Twenty-one assists and zero turnovers in a three-game stretch. Backup point guard, earned.

**Tier 4: The Ones Who Need a Reset**

8. **Mikal Bridges:** Too talented to stay this low, and I expect him to climb. But right now, the production isn't there.
9. **Deuce McBride:** Turning the corner, with two threes and two steals against Brooklyn and seven points in the third against Chicago.

**Tier 5: Rising**

10. **Dalton Knecht:** Ten and seven in his debut. Earned another look.
11. **James Wiseman:** Eight, eight, and three blocks against Chicago. Back in the conversation.
12. **Andre Drummond:** Solid rebounding, limited elsewhere. The backup center job is still open.

**Tier 6: Waiting**

13. **Jose Alvarado:** Just back from the ankle sprain. Give him time to get his legs, and he could jump up fast.
14. **Mo Diawara and Pacôme Dadiet:** Both had nice moments on Wednesday, but they need more chances to move up.
