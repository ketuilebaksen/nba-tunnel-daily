## THE CLEVELAND LOSS IN BRIEF

Quick recap of Wednesday night.

**The start:** Cleveland jumped out to a 33–23 lead after the first quarter. After that, the Knicks never led by more than two points. Another slow start, and against a good team, it cost them.

**The Cavs' star:** James Harden poured in 32. The Knicks had no answer for him.

**The Knicks' bright spots:**
- **Jalen Brunson:** 27 points
- **Karl-Anthony Towns:** 21 points, 11 rebounds, and eight assists on seven-of-11 shooting — another double-double
- **Deuce McBride:** 19 points off the bench on seven-of-13 shooting and five-of-10 from three
- **Landry Shamet:** 12 points on five-of-eight shooting and a plus-7 — one of the few Knicks in the positive
- **Tyler Kolek:** four points, four assists, and a plus-12
- **The bench:** outscored Cleveland's bench 37–32

**The concern:** Mikal Bridges had two points on one-of-three shooting, with two assists and two turnovers.

So the streak ends at four, and the Knicks head into the road trip at 6–5. Still, after the 0–3 start, six wins in eight games is real progress. This was a good team beating them on a night when the Knicks dug an early hole — something they'll have to stop doing against the East's best.

## STORY ONE: THE KAT MARKET MAP

Now the biggest long-term question in New York. Karl-Anthony Towns has been the Knicks' most valuable player this season, and according to Dan Favale of Daily Knicks, the anchor of one of the best defenses in the league. He's scoring inside, hitting threes, moving the ball when defenses load up on Brunson — eight assists against Cleveland — and rebounding at an elite level.

That naturally raises the question: is KAT building enough leverage to walk away for a bigger payday?

**The numbers:**
- **The Knicks' offer:** somewhere between $45 and $50 million per season, with SNY's Ian Begley reporting the $50 million figure
- **KAT's player option:** about $61 million for next season
- **The open-market max:** if he declines the option and hits free agency next summer, another team could offer up to about $264.9 million over four years

**The "money is out there" side:** Some respected voices believe a team would pay up. Sam Vecenie on the Game Theory podcast and The Athletic's James L. Edwards III have both suggested a rival team would be willing to give Towns more than $200 million over four years.

**The "who, exactly?" side:** Favale isn't convinced, and his argument starts with a simple question: which teams can actually do it?

## WHO COULD ACTUALLY PAY KAT?

Spotrac's Keith Smith projects seven teams with a relatively clean path to the cap space needed for a deal that size. Then Favale goes team by team — and the list shrinks fast.

| Team | The path | The problem |
|---|---|---|
| Atlanta Hawks | Projected 40-plus million in room | Would still need to clear more money once CJ McCollum and Lu Dort come off the books |
| Brooklyn Nets | Could have max space, even after Julius Randle's player option | Favale doubts Towns picks a team that isn't built to compete |
| Chicago Bulls | Would need to decline or move Norman Powell's team option | They're rebuilding — a $200 million-plus deal doesn't fit their timeline |
| Golden State Warriors | The most interesting case | Would need Jimmy Butler and Draymond Green off the books plus a salary dump; Steph Curry turns 39 in March; a Kristaps Porziņģis contract move could knock them out |
| LA Clippers | — | Docked five first-round picks for circumventing the salary cap; Brandon Ingram and Darius Garland on the books |
| Memphis Grizzlies | — | Would lose space if they give Ty Jerome a big raise, and would have to decide Zach Edey isn't their long-term center |
| Milwaukee Bucks | — | Likely out if they re-sign Tyler Herro or Jaime Jaquez Jr. — and far from contention |

**Favale's conclusion:** The number of teams that could realistically offer Towns more than $200 million tops out at about two — and possibly fewer.

## HISTORY IS ON THE KNICKS' SIDE

And here's a fascinating stat. According to Favale, Paul George is the only free agent in the past five years to change teams while earning more than $40 million per season.

Think about that. Players at that salary level almost never leave. Why? Because very few teams have both the money and the roster to make the move worth it. Getting paid and finding the right situation are two very different things.

## BUT IT'S NOT ALL GOOD NEWS FOR NEW YORK

Favale also makes two uncomfortable points.

**The apron is the real bogeyman.** The Knicks' offer is limited in part by the second apron, and Favale puts much of the blame for the financial squeeze on James Dolan.

**The offer might be a signal.** Favale suggests the front office likely understands the market well — and the size of its offer may signal that the Knicks believe Towns could get more elsewhere. In his view, the Knicks may be right about that.

**My take:** This argument shifts the leverage a bit. If only one or two teams can realistically pay KAT more than $200 million, and history says $40 million-plus players rarely move, then the Knicks' risk of losing him for nothing is smaller than many fans think. But that doesn't mean the Knicks should lowball him. He's been their best player, and keeping the locker room happy matters during a title defense. The likely landing spot is still somewhere in the middle. That's my analysis, not a report.

## STORY TWO: DEUCE McBRIDE IS BACK

While KAT's future is the biggest long-term story, one of the most encouraging short-term developments is coming from the bench. As Knicks On SI's Kento Kado put it, Deuce McBride's resurgence has given the Knicks a good problem.

**The rough start:** Through his first five games, McBride averaged about 17.6 minutes while shooting just 35.7% from the field. He lost playing time over the first two weeks, which surprised a lot of people around the team. Kado noted that McBride was still doing the little things that help a team win — but he wasn't getting the minutes that usually come with that effort.

**The turnaround:** Over his last six games, McBride is averaging:
- 9.2 points per game
- 51.3% from the field
- 46.7% from three
- About 20 minutes per game — still below the 26.3 he averaged last season, but trending up

**The chicken-or-egg question:** Kado admits it's hard to say which came first — the extra minutes or the hot shooting. Either way, they're feeding each other now.

**The recent highlights:**
- **Against Milwaukee:** a plus-39 in 25 minutes, and Mike Brown called him the defensive player of the game
- **Against Cleveland:** 19 points, five threes, and the main reason the bench won its matchup

**The fair caveat:** He was also a minus-18 against Cleveland. The scoring has to come with stops on the other end.

## THE MINUTES PUZZLE

So here's the math: any increase in Deuce's minutes most likely comes at the expense of Bridges, Josh Hart, or Landry Shamet.

- **Bridges** is the long, versatile defender — and the one who's struggling.
- **Hart** is the tone-setter, the rebounder, the toughness. He has weaknesses, especially as a starter, but he's central to the team's identity. He's also been dealing with right ankle soreness.
- **Shamet** is the floor spacer, and one of the quiet success stories of the season. He owns the highest plus-minus on the team.

According to Kado, McBride has generally outplayed Bridges. But Brown can't just move on from Bridges. If the Knicks want to repeat, they need him at his best — which means the coaching staff has to keep giving him chances to work through the slump. Brown has to balance short-term results with what this team will need in April, May, and June.

## THE DEUCE TRADE ANGLE

And there's a front office angle too. Deuce has been mentioned in trade conversations all season — we've talked about him as a fit for Oklahoma City, and as a salary-matching piece in the Trade Calculator.

Kado makes an interesting point: Deuce's resurgence makes trading him harder on a sentimental level, since he's a player the franchise drafted and developed. At the same time, his improved play raises his value around the league. Kado even suggests the front office would have had an easier time moving him if he'd kept struggling, because the decision would have felt less painful.

**The bottom line on Deuce:** If the Knicks keep him, he figures to play a key role in another playoff run. If they trade him, his stronger play should bring back a better return. Either way, this hot stretch made life easier for New York.

## STORY THREE: THE BRIDGES-TO-THE-CLIPPERS IDEA

And now the "two deadly weapons."

**The context:** Bridges was a key piece of the championship run. But his slow start has opened the door to trade ideas that would have seemed unthinkable a few months ago. Knicks On SI described the team as dealing with a championship hangover, and Bridges has been one of the players who's struggled the most. Against Cleveland: two points on one-of-three shooting, two assists, and two turnovers.

**What Mike Brown wants from him:** Brown has been clear. He wants Bridges to lean into his strengths, calling him one of the best runners in the NBA and pointing to his cutting, rebounding, and driving as the areas where he can make the biggest impact. In other words: stop forcing things, and get back to the parts of his game that made him so valuable.

**The proposal:** In Grant Hughes' Bleacher Report idea, the Knicks would send Bridges and two 2027 second-round picks to the Clippers for two players: Max Strus and Derrick Jones Jr. That's the "two weapons."

## DOES THE CLIPPERS IDEA MAKE SENSE?

Let's evaluate it honestly.

**The case for:**
- **Two rotation players for one.** The Knicks would add depth on the wing — Strus as a shooter, Jones Jr. as an athletic, defensive-minded forward.
- **Contract relief.** Bridges is owed about $116.5 million over the following three seasons, including a player option for 2029–30. Moving that long-term money could create future flexibility for the Brunson and KAT decisions.
- **The Clippers' situation.** At 1–7 earlier this month, with their draft future badly compromised, the Clippers have reasons to shake things up.

**The case against:**
- **Selling low.** Bridges' value is at its lowest point right now. The Knicks gave up five first-round picks to get him. Trading him for two role players plus your own second-round picks would mean taking a massive loss.
- **The salary math.** With the Knicks right up against the second apron — only about $89,000 of room, per Bobby Marks — any deal has to bring back no more salary than it sends out. The details matter.
- **The ceiling.** A confident Bridges is a far better player than either player in the deal. As one SI analysis put it, the Knicks need Bridges playing at his best to repeat — not traded away.
- **The timing.** It's mid-November. Most of these deals can't happen until December 15th, and trading a core starter this early would be a panic move.

**And remember the pattern.** This is at least the third Bridges trade idea we've covered — the OKC-Hartenstein idea, the New York Post's "escape hatch" column, and now the Clippers. None of them are reported talks.

**My verdict:** Not now. It's a reasonable idea on paper if Bridges' slump drags into January. But today, the smartest move is getting him right — running, cutting, attacking, rebounding — exactly what Brown is asking for.

## WHAT A BRIDGES BOUNCE-BACK LOOKS LIKE

Let's flip it around, because the trade conversation only exists because of the slump. What would it look like if Bridges got back to his best?

**Running.** Brown called him one of the best runners in the league. That means sprinting the floor in transition, beating defenders down the court for layups, and filling lanes. Easy points help shooters find their rhythm.

**Cutting.** With Brunson and KAT drawing so much attention, there are cutting lanes everywhere. Bridges has always been smart off the ball. A few backdoor cuts a night could change his whole confidence.

**Rebounding.** Crashing the glass on both ends. The Knicks already lead the league in rebounding percentage. Bridges adding to that makes them even more dangerous.

**Driving.** He had zero free-throw attempts through his first six games. Attacking the rim and getting to the line is the fastest way out of a shooting slump.

**Defense.** His point-of-attack defense has slipped too. Getting back to locking up the other team's best perimeter player is where his value has always started.

None of that requires a hot jump shot. It's all effort and decision-making. That's why Brown is pointing him there — and why there's real reason to believe he can bounce back.

## HOW IT ALL CONNECTS

Put the three stories together, and you see the Knicks' whole season in one picture.

KAT is the anchor, and his contract is the biggest long-term decision. Deuce's resurgence gives the Knicks more depth — and more trade value — at the exact moment Bridges is struggling. And the Bridges trade ideas are what happen when a five-first-round-pick player hits the worst slump of his career on a team with almost no cap room.

The good news? The Knicks have options. They have depth, they have 12 second-round picks to work with, and they have a coach who will play whoever's producing. The challenge is making the right decision at the right time — not the fastest one.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. KAT stays a Knick long term, and the Market Map is why — the list of teams that can realistically pay him more than New York is tiny. Deuce stays in the rotation at around 20 to 22 minutes and keeps shooting well. Bridges is not traded to the Clippers or anyone else before December 15th — and I still think he has a bounce-back stretch soon, especially if he leans into the running and cutting game Brown wants. And on the road trip — Indiana, then two in Toronto — the Knicks go 2–1 and get back to three games over .500.
