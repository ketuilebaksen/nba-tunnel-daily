## THE LEON ROSE BARGAIN HALL OF FAME

Before we get to Wiseman, let's remember why the phrase "Leon Rose has done it again" even exists. This front office has a track record of finding value where other teams didn't see it — and getting criticized for it before being proven right.

**Inductee: Jalen Brunson.** In 2022, Rose outbid the competition for a guard plenty of people around the league doubted could be a franchise point guard. Four years later: Finals MVP, first championship since 1973.

**Inductee: OG Anunoby.** When Immanuel Quickley's extension talks stalled, Rose didn't let a homegrown guard walk for nothing. He traded him to Toronto and brought back OG — one of the most important two-way players on a title team.

**Inductee: Karl-Anthony Towns.** Same logic. Rose traded Julius Randle and Donte DiVincenzo to Minnesota right before the 2024 season and landed KAT — the offensive hub of the championship run.

**Inductee: Landry Shamet.** Signed to an Exhibit 9 training-camp deal in September 2024 with no promises at all. Two years later, he's a champion on a four-year, $24 million contract and one of the hottest shooters of this preseason.

**Inductee: Mike Brown.** Not a player, but a bold call. After the Knicks reached their first conference finals in a quarter century, Rose fired Tom Thibodeau and handed the team to Brown. Questioned at the time. Championship the next June.

The pattern is clear. Leon Rose doesn't just chase big names. He finds undervalued pieces, trusts his evaluation, and lets results do the talking. And now the question is whether James Wiseman joins that list.

## THE FINALE: 18 AND 10

Let's start with last night. In the final preseason game against Toronto, Wiseman finished with 18 points and 10 rebounds. Now, let's be honest from the jump: those minutes came in the second half, and Toronto didn't have its starters on the floor. Context matters, and we're not going to pretend he dropped 18 on an All-NBA center.

But what you saw was still impressive. A seven-footer with great athleticism, really nice hands, touch around the basket, and the ability to score from multiple spots — lobs, putbacks, little hooks, face-up finishes. And on the other end, he moved his feet and protected the rim. That's the whole package you want from a backup big, all on display in one night.

And look at the offensive glass, because this is where he's quietly been excellent all preseason: two offensive rebounds against the Sixers, one against the Wizards, and four against the Raptors. Remember, the biggest thing the Knicks lost when Mitchell Robinson went to Boston was offensive rebounding and rim-running. Wiseman is giving them both.

Across the preseason, he averaged about 10 points, five rebounds, and a block. In limited minutes. Coming off an Achilles. That's production.

## THE RISE: THE BEST HIGH SCHOOL PLAYER IN AMERICA

To understand why this matters, you have to remember who James Wiseman was supposed to be. Because nobody questions his talent. He was the number one player in the high school class of 2019. He was the Gatorade National Player of the Year. He was the Morgan Wootten National Player of the Year. He was a USA Today All-USA first-teamer and a McDonald's All-American. Some people projected him as the number one overall pick in the draft.

Then came Memphis. In just three college games, he dominated — around 20 points, 11 rebounds, and three blocks per game, making roughly 77 percent of his shots. And then it was over. The NCAA handed him a 12-game suspension, ruling that an $11,500 moving-expense payment was given to his family in 2017 by Penny Hardaway — who was Wiseman's high school coach and a Memphis booster before becoming the university's head coach. This was before the NIL era, when that kind of thing was treated very differently. Rather than serve the suspension, Wiseman withdrew from the university in December 2019, signed with an agent, and focused on the 2020 NBA draft. His words at the time: "I've decided to leave Memphis and get a head start on my next chapter."

That next chapter started with Golden State taking him second overall.

## THE ROOKIE YEAR: BETTER THAN YOU REMEMBER

Here's something a lot of fans forget: Wiseman's rookie season with the Warriors was actually pretty good. In his very first NBA game, he scored 19 points with six rebounds in 24 minutes. Over 39 games that season, he averaged nearly 12 points, close to six rebounds, and almost a block per night. For a 19-year-old center who barely played in college, that's solid.

The problem wasn't production. It was everything that came after.

## THE FALL: THE HONEST INJURY LEDGER

On this channel, we don't hide the hard parts, so here's the full injury history — because this is the real reason Wiseman was available for almost nothing.

**Rookie season:** A wrist injury in January cost him about three weeks. Later that season, he landed awkwardly after a dunk attempt and tore the meniscus in his right knee, ending his year.

**Second season:** He missed the entire season recovering from that knee.

**After that:** Ankle sprains, including a more severe one that limited his availability. A trade from Golden State to Detroit, where he played two seasons on losing teams.

**October 2024:** He signed with Indiana and tore his Achilles in his very first game with the Pacers.

**Since then:** Only five NBA games across the last two seasons.

So when people say nobody wanted James Wiseman this summer — that's why. It was never about talent, never about production, never about ability. It was about the ability to stay on the floor. And that will always be the risk with him.

## THE COMEBACK: WHY NEW YORK MIGHT BE DIFFERENT

So why could it be different in New York? Three reasons.

**Reason one: the role is small.** As the third center behind Karl-Anthony Towns and Andre Drummond, Wiseman doesn't need to play 30 minutes a night. He doesn't need to carry an offense. He needs to be ready when called — rest nights, foul trouble, injuries. Smaller workloads mean less stress on a body that's been through a lot.

**Reason two: the medical and performance staff.** This Knicks organization has been very good at managing player workloads and keeping guys available. A defending champion with a structured plan is a much better environment for a player rebuilding his health than a rebuilding team asking him to play heavy minutes.

**Reason three: Mike Brown.** Brown was an assistant on the Warriors staff when Wiseman was in Golden State. He knows exactly what Wiseman can do and what he needs to improve. And listen to what Brown said about him this camp:

"When you're that long and that athletic with skill — he has skill — there's a place for you in this league. Sometimes it takes the right moment, the right team, the right situation, all that stuff to be able to fit someplace and grow. We have a lot of great guys in camp that have potential of impacting us in a lot of different ways throughout the course of the year. And James Wiseman, he's definitely one of the many guys that we have that we're excited about."

The right moment. The right team. The right situation. That's the whole story right there. For the first time in his career, Wiseman might finally have all three.

## WHAT WISEMAN GIVES THE KNICKS THAT NOBODY ELSE DOES

Let's connect this to the bigger picture we've been covering all fall. When Mitchell Robinson left for Boston, the Knicks didn't just lose rebounding. They lost one of the most productive pick-and-roll partnerships in the NBA. Brunson and Mitch ranked eighth in the entire league in points generated as a screen-and-roll duo over the past five seasons.

Drummond is a great rebounder and a better passer than people expected, but he's no longer an above-the-rim roll man. KAT is too skilled to be boxed into that role — he's the hub. Eubanks screens well but tends to drift into the middle of the floor. Wiseman is the only option in camp who brings the vertical lob threat. In his last healthy season, more than 46 percent of his made baskets came on dunks or alley-oops. And this preseason, Jose Alvarado was already throwing him lobs against Washington.

That's the weapon. A rim-runner who forces defenses to collapse, opens the corners for shooters like Shamet, Bridges, and OG, and gives Brunson the partner he lost.

## THE VERDICT ON THE 14TH SPOT

So where does this leave the roster battle? Let's be precise. Roster cutdown day is Monday, October 19th. Nothing is official until then. But every sign points one way. Ian Begley says the battle came down to Eubanks and Wiseman, with Tony Bradley out of the running. James L. Edwards says Wiseman feels like the frontrunner. Stefan Bondy called him the favorite back at the start of camp. Mike Brown made him the first camp big off the bench in every game where he had the choice. And now, an 18-and-10 finale.

It's very hard to see him not making this team. And if the Knicks structure it the smart way — a standard minimum that doesn't become fully guaranteed until early January — they get a two-and-a-half-month trial with a safety net. If his body holds up and he keeps producing, they've found a rotation-caliber big at the lowest possible price. If it doesn't, they can move on before his salary locks in.

## WHY "UNSTOPPABLE" ISN'T CRAZY

Now, the title says "unstoppable." Let me explain what I mean, because I'm not saying the Knicks will go 82–0.

What makes a champion hard to stop isn't just the top of the roster. It's what happens when things go wrong. When a star gets into foul trouble. When someone tweaks an ankle in January. When you hit a back-to-back on the road. Last season, the Knicks had answers in those moments. This season, with Mitch gone, the question was whether they'd have them again.

Look at the answers that have emerged this preseason. Drummond has been better than expected on defense and as a passer. Shamet has been red hot. OG is living at the free-throw line. KAT is still running the offense as the hub. Bridges is attacking the rim. Bruce Brown has been "textbook" on the ball and could be added if the Knicks make a Dadiet trade. And now Wiseman looks like a legitimate third center with real upside.

That's depth. That's insurance. That's what makes a team hard to beat over a long season. And if Wiseman turns into even a solid backup — the kind of player who can give you 12 good minutes on any given night — this roster has fewer weak spots than people think. The betting market has the Knicks at +1000 to win the title. Depth like this is how you make the market look silly.

## WHAT TO WATCH FROM WISEMAN IN THE FIRST MONTH

If Wiseman makes the team, here are four checkpoints I'll be tracking over the first month of the regular season.

**Checkpoint one: the free-throw line.** In the opener against Philadelphia, he went two-of-six from the line. A rim-runner who catches lobs and crashes the glass is going to get fouled a lot. If he can't convert, teams will hack him instead of giving up dunks. That's the most fixable weakness on his résumé — and the most important one to fix.

**Checkpoint two: fouls per minute.** Backup bigs live and die by staying on the floor. If he picks up two fouls in four minutes, he's not helping. Discipline on contests and verticality will tell us a lot.

**Checkpoint three: the back-to-backs.** How does his body respond on the second night? The Knicks will be careful with him, but if he can handle back-to-back appearances without setbacks, that's a huge sign.

**Checkpoint four: the Mitch game.** Boston faces New York in the Celtics' second game of the season — Mitchell Robinson's first game against his old team. If Wiseman gets minutes in that game, it's the perfect measuring stick: the new rim-runner versus the guy whose role he's trying to fill.

## THE REST OF ROSE'S FINAL-WEEK DESK

And here's the thing — Wiseman might not be the last "Rose did it again" moment of the week. Look at what's still sitting on the president's desk before Tuesday.

**Bruce Brown.** Begley says he's impressed people internally, Mike Brown called his on-ball defense "textbook," and Brunson says he brings a lot that can help. Keeping him requires opening a second roster spot — most likely by moving Pacôme Dadiet's $2.98 million salary with a second-round pick or two. If Rose pulls that off, he adds a champion defender for a minimum.

**A two-way center.** The Knicks currently have no big on a two-way deal. Former Knick Trey Jemison has reportedly been waived by Toronto. Bringing him back would add a fourth center without touching the apron.

**The Cissé watch.** Marc Stein raised the possibility that Dallas could release Moussa Cissé to make room for Dwight Powell. A long shot, but a front office this aggressive will be watching the wire.

**And the big one: Karl-Anthony Towns.** On Monday, KAT has to decide whether to decline his $61 million player option. Rose and the front office shut down extension talks last week, and KAT said it was "news to me." That decision will shape the entire season.

So yes, Wiseman might be the latest bargain. But this week will tell us whether Leon Rose can do it again — and again — and again.

## THE REALITY CHECK

Let's stay grounded, though. It's preseason. Last night's 18 and 10 came against Toronto's reserves. Wiseman isn't a superstar, isn't an All-Star, and may not even be a regular part of the rotation. If Drummond rolls an ankle and is out a week or two, Wiseman will get real regular-season minutes — and that's when we'll really learn what he is. And his health will always be the question mark until he proves otherwise over a full season.

But compare the risk to the reward. The risk: a minimum contract, non-guaranteed until January. The reward: a former number two pick, the best high school player in his class, a 12-point-per-game rookie, and a seven-foot rim-runner who fits exactly what this team lost. That's a bet you make every single time.

## MY CALL

So — has Leon Rose done it again? My answer: he's on his way. It's too early to put Wiseman in the Hall of Fame next to Brunson, OG, KAT, and Shamet. He has to make the roster, stay healthy, and produce in real games. But the process is exactly what we've seen from this front office before: identify undervalued talent, trust the coaching staff's relationship with the player, structure the risk smartly, and let the player prove it. If Wiseman becomes a reliable backup big this season, this will go down as one of the sneakiest good moves of Leon Rose's tenure.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. James Wiseman finished the preseason with 18 points and 10 rebounds against Toronto's second unit, averaging about 10 points, five rebounds, and a block across the preseason, with seven offensive rebounds in three games. He was the number one high school player in America, a Gatorade and Morgan Wootten National Player of the Year, a McDonald's All-American, and the number two overall pick. His rookie year was good — nearly 12 points and six rebounds — before injuries took over: a wrist, a torn meniscus, ankle sprains, and a torn Achilles, with only five NBA games in the last two seasons. Mike Brown says it takes "the right moment, the right team, the right situation." Every insider now points to him winning the 14th spot before Monday's cutdown. And if he does, Leon Rose might have just added another bargain to a long list.
