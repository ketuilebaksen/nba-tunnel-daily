## WHY GAME TWO MATTERS: THE 0–2 HISTORY

Losing on opening night isn't a disaster. One game out of 82 doesn't decide anything, and a team that just won it all has earned patience. But history sends a clear warning about what an 0–2 start usually means.

The Knicks haven't dropped their first two games of a season since 2020–21. Go further back, and the last Knicks team that opened with back-to-back losses and still won a playoff series was the 1998–99 squad — the one that reached the Finals before falling to the San Antonio Spurs in five games. Across the entire league, the last team to lose its first two games and still win a championship was the 1990–91 Chicago Bulls — a 61-win team led by Michael Jordan and coached by Phil Jackson.

That's a short list. The Knicks would rather not test it.

## THE OPENER AUTOPSY

So what went wrong on Tuesday? New York led 58–54 at halftime, but the offense faded badly after the break. The Knicks finished with an offensive rating of 99.0 — snapping a run of 30 straight games, dating back to March 22nd of last season against Washington, with an offensive rating of at least 100. With Joel Embiid on the floor, the Knicks produced just 61 points on 72 possessions. And according to one analytics podcast, the Knicks scored their fewest points per direct pick-and-roll of any game this decade, playoffs included.

The wings went cold too. OG Anunoby went three-of-12 from the field and one-of-nine from three. Mikal Bridges scored only four points on two-of-eight shooting. Tyrese Maxey led Philly with 28, while Jaylen Brown added 19 and LeBron James 14 in their Sixers debuts.

## BOSTON'S OWN COLLAPSE

Here's the good news: Boston arrives with its own frustration. The Celtics opened their season in Detroit and let a 22-point lead slip away against the Pistons.

For more than 27 minutes, Boston looked dominant: an offensive rating of 139, a net rating of plus 41.6, shooting 52.1 percent from the field and 12-of-25 from deep. Then the bottom fell out over the final 20-plus minutes: the offensive rating dropped to 93.2, the net rating sank to minus 71.9, they shot just 37.5 percent, and they missed 12 of their last 17 threes.

Paul George was the bright spot. In his first game with Boston, he scored 38 points — the most ever by a Celtic in a team debut — and added a game-high five steals. For context, George had just four steals total in Philadelphia's four-game playoff series against the Knicks last spring. Jayson Tatum, back from his Achilles injury, handed out 11 assists but managed only 13 points on 23 shots.

So both teams come into tonight wanting to erase a bad night. Somebody's going to start 0–2.

## ANSWER ONE: THE "PERFECT ANSWER" — WISEMAN'S MINUTES

Let's start with the answer from our title. On Tuesday, James Wiseman sat on the bench the entire game. Tonight, that could — and maybe should — change.

Wiseman's road has been anything but simple. The former number two overall pick came to camp fighting for a job. He knows Mike Brown well from Golden State, when Brown was an assistant on Steve Kerr's staff. He made his case in the preseason, including an 18-point, 10-rebound game in 24 minutes in Toronto. And on October 17th, he officially earned the final roster spot as a reserve center.

Now look at what happened on Tuesday. When KAT picked up early fouls, Mike Brown didn't hesitate to go to Andre Drummond. And facing his former team, Drummond delivered — a first-half double-double in one uninterrupted 14-minute stretch. He showed things Knicks fans aren't used to seeing from a backup center: he can put the ball on the floor a little, score without dunking, step out as a shooter defenses can't completely ignore, and he's reliable enough at the free-throw line.

But the second half told a different story. When KAT got into foul trouble again in the fourth quarter and Drummond returned, the energy was gone. Joel Embiid blocked his shot several times, and Drummond lacked the lift to finish over or through him. Leaning on him that heavily may have cost the Knicks the game.

That's where Wiseman comes in. Nobody expects him to play 20 minutes a night. But if he can give Drummond and KAT short breaks — say three minutes in each half — that keeps both of them fresher for the moments that matter most. Wiseman doesn't need to be on the floor in crunch time to help the Knicks win in crunch time. Fresh legs in the fourth quarter can be the difference between a contested finish and a blocked shot.

## WHY THE KAT FOUL STREAK MAKES IT URGENT

And here's the number that makes this even more urgent. KAT finished the opener with four fouls — and that wasn't a one-time thing. Tuesday marked five months to the day since he last committed fewer than four fouls in a game. That stretch covers the Knicks' final 11 playoff games plus the opener. Many of those fouls come in situations that pose little real threat.

As long as that keeps happening, the Knicks need more than one option behind him. Drummond is both too valuable and too old to carry that load alone.

And the opponent helps. Boston doesn't have a center who attacks a defense the way Embiid does, which makes this a friendlier assignment for Wiseman — who isn't known for his defense. His job would be simple: grab rebounds, run the floor, and finish around the rim when the chance comes. If you're ever going to break in your third center, a night without Embiid on the other side is the night to do it.

## THE PICK-AND-ROLL PROBLEM WISEMAN COULD HELP SOLVE

And there's a second reason Wiseman fits as the answer. Remember that stat: the Knicks scored their fewest points per direct pick-and-roll of any game this decade. Why? Part of it is simply a cold shooting night. But part of it is structural. Last season, Brunson and Mitchell Robinson formed one of the most productive screen-and-roll duos in the league — eighth in the NBA over five seasons in points generated above expectation. Mitch rolled hard to the rim and forced defenses to collapse.

Now, KAT mostly pops out as a shooter. Drummond rolls, but doesn't threaten above the rim the way he used to. So defenses can load up on Brunson without fear of a lob over the top. Wiseman is the one big on the roster who brings that vertical threat. Even a few possessions of Brunson-Wiseman pick-and-roll could force Boston to defend the Knicks differently — and open up the shooters.

Mike Brown said it himself when he explained the Wiseman decision: "That vertical threat at the rim, the vertical threat defensively, how good he runs." Tonight is a good night to find out if it translates.

## ANSWER TWO: TRANSITION DEFENSE

One of New York's biggest problems against Philadelphia was transition defense — the Sixers piled up 24 fast-break points. That should be less of an issue tonight. Boston scored only 11 fast-break points in Detroit, and last season the Celtics averaged an NBA-low 11.8 fast-break points per game. With Jaylen Brown now in Philadelphia, that number might drop even further. The Knicks still need to get back. But the Celtics aren't built to punish them the way the Sixers did.

## ANSWER THREE: WIN THE GLASS — AGAINST MITCH

Rebounding is the storyline everyone will be watching. Even in defeat, KAT and Drummond combined for 10 offensive rebounds against Philadelphia. Meanwhile, Boston's center pairing of Mitchell Robinson and Neemias Queta grabbed only two offensive boards against a physical Pistons front line.

Both teams crashed the glass well last season — according to Cleaning the Glass, Boston ranked fifth in offensive rebounding percentage at 31.8, and the Knicks were seventh at 31.3. So second-chance points could swing this game. Add OG Anunoby's new habit of crashing the offensive glass, and the Knicks have a real chance to win the rebounding battle against the man they couldn't afford to keep. That would be a statement.

## ANSWER FOUR: THE THREE-POINT WAR

The biggest battle might happen beyond the arc. Philadelphia launched 40 threes against New York and made only 11 — a 27.5 percent night. The Knicks can't count on that again. In Detroit, Boston fired 42 threes and made 17 — 40.5 percent.

So the Knicks have to run Boston off the line — and, just as important, knock down their own looks. In last season's meetings with Boston, New York attempted close to 40 threes per game and hit nearly 41 percent. Anything close to that would go a long way tonight. That means OG and Bridges bouncing back from cold openers, and Landry Shamet finding the stroke that made him one of the best players in the preseason.

## ANSWER FIVE: THE DEUCE QUESTION

And the fifth answer the Knicks need is about Deuce McBride. His minutes shrank on opening night in a way that caught a lot of people off guard.

First, the honest part. Deuce is important, but he isn't a complete player. He has the size of a point guard and the game of a shooting guard — he doesn't have the ball-handling or shot creation to consistently make plays for himself or others. That can limit him in certain matchups, especially on nights when his defense isn't enough to make him a net positive.

And recent months haven't been kind. He struggled in the Finals. He was one of the most disappointing players in the preseason, when the Knicks lost their first four exhibitions before beating Toronto 122–120. In the opener, he hit a three in the first quarter, but his offense never came together after that. To be fair, he's said he was playing through a core-muscle injury that required surgery. And it's a small sample. Three full seasons of solid work should count for more than a handful of rough games.

But the minutes stand out. Last regular season, Deuce played fewer than 15 minutes in only four of his 41 games, and a couple of those were due to injury. One game into this season, he's already at one. And that's surprising, because Philadelphia's Tyrese Maxey is exactly the kind of fast, downhill guard the Knicks have often used Deuce to slow down. If there was ever a night to lean on his defense, that seemed like it.

There were signs this could happen. Landry Shamet raised his level during the playoff run, and the gap between them was already small last season: Shamet averaged 23 minutes per game, Deuce 26.3.

## IS A DEUCE TRADE IN PLAY?

So here's the bigger question. Could the Knicks be more open to trading Deuce than people assumed? That would be a tough decision. He's on an expiring contract, which means the Knicks could lose him for nothing next summer. And in a world without the second apron, an extension might already be done.

There's also basketball logic to a deal. Deuce is one of the few Knicks trade assets other teams genuinely value, and moving him could be the most direct way to fill other needs — the center rotation is already a talking point after one game, and the uncertain role of young wing Mohamed Diawara suggests the Knicks might still want another proven wing they can trust in big moments.

But let's be clear: it's one game, and any trade talk is speculation. Many believe keeping Deuce gives the Knicks their best chance to win another title — winning teams need depth, and players who can defend at a high level are always in demand in the playoffs. His minutes tonight will tell us a lot about whether Tuesday was a one-night decision or the beginning of a real change.

## THE MITCH HOMECOMING

And then there's the emotional storyline. Tonight is the first time Mitchell Robinson faces the Knicks since leaving. He spent his entire NBA career in New York — the longest-tenured Knick — before signing a three-year, $47.4 million deal with Boston on the first day of free agency. The Knicks, operating under James Dolan's second-apron mandate, couldn't match.

His old teammates have made it clear how they feel. Brunson said he's happy for his close friend — while joking that Mitch won't be his friend on the four nights a year these teams play. Jordan Clarkson called Mitch "my dog," said he was probably the closest guy to him on the team, and added that when they see him, they've got to beat him. And Mitch told OG on social media that he tried to stay.

So expect hugs before the game and elbows during it — and a loud ovation from the traveling Knicks fans who remember everything he gave this franchise. And expect Mitch to play with something to prove — he'll want to show the Knicks exactly what they lost.

## WHAT A WIN WOULD MEAN

A win tonight would do a lot more than avoid 0–2. It would quiet the early noise after a rough ring night. It would show the rest of the league that the champs can respond in a rival building. It would give Drummond, Wiseman, and the bench confidence that the frontcourt plan can work without Mitch. And it would let the Knicks carry momentum into the rest of October instead of answering "what's wrong?" questions for another week.

A loss wouldn't be the end of the world — it's still October. But the history we talked about is real, and the Knicks know it.

## THE KAT CONTEXT

And through all of this, remember the bigger picture from this week. Karl-Anthony Towns told ESPN's Malika Andrews he's willing to take less than the max to keep this team together — "nowhere in my mind a max was even thought of." Jalen Brunson's response: "Sign a contract." The relationship is in a good place. What KAT needs now is a clean game — fewer cheap fouls, more work from the top of the key as the offensive hub. Do that, and a lot of Tuesday's problems disappear on their own.

## MY CALL

Here's my prediction for tonight. KAT stays out of early foul trouble. Wiseman gets his first regular-season minutes — a short stint in each half — and Drummond is fresh in the fourth quarter.
