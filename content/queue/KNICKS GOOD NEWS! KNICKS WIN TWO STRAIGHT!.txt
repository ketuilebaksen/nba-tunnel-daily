## THE PREVIEW CHECK

Let's run through every big pregame storyline, one at a time: what we were told before tip-off, and what actually happened. Each one gets a simple verdict: good news, bad news, or still unclear.

## STORYLINE 1: "KAT IS WEARING A BRACE"

**Before the game:** This was the big worry. After the hard fall in Chicago, when Towns landed on his left wrist, went to the locker room in pain, and came back with it wrapped, he was seen before the Philly game wearing a black brace on his left wrist, tied around his thumb to keep it stable. That kind of brace isn't something you wear for nothing. But the Knicks didn't list him on the injury report.

**What happened:** He played, and he played well. Twenty-two points and 12 rebounds, his sixth straight double-double to start the season. And in the fourth quarter, when the Knicks were chasing, KAT scored eight straight points to drag them back into the game. A player with a serious wrist problem doesn't take over a stretch like that.

**Verdict:** GOOD NEWS. Is the wrist 100 percent? We don't know. The Knicks rarely share injury details, and the brace means they're being careful. But playing through it at this level is the best sign we could have asked for. Keep watching it, but this was the headline that mattered most, and it came out positive.

## STORYLINE 2: THE SIXERS' INJURY REPORT

**Before the game:** Per New York Basketball on X, Jaylen Brown was questionable with left knee soreness. Philadelphia was also without Dominick Barlow (back), Tacko Fall (quad), Jabari Walker (knee), and Ariel Hukporti, who will miss the entire season with a torn Achilles. Yes, that's the same Ariel Hukporti who started his career in New York as a Knicks second-round pick. We wish him a full recovery.

**What happened:** Brown was ruled out with the knee injury. So the Sixers played without one of their stars, and they still won. Joel Embiid did play.

**Verdict:** BAD NEWS for the Knicks. A short-handed Philly team at home was a real opportunity, and New York let it slip.

## STORYLINE 3: "WITHOUT BROWN, THE KNICKS CAN FIND A RHYTHM"

**Before the game:** Roundtable Sports pointed out that Philadelphia ranked fifth in defense, and Brown is a big reason for that. Without him, the thinking went, Kentavious Caldwell-Pope would take on more of the defensive load, Dean Wade might slide to the three, and the Knicks might finally get comfortable on offense.

**What happened:** Not early. The Knicks were sloppy in the first half. Philly led 29–21 after the first quarter and 53–44 at halftime, and the lead reached 14 in the third. The Sixers controlled the game for most of the night, and Caldwell-Pope hit a long three in the third quarter that stretched the lead.

**Verdict:** BAD NEWS. Another slow start. That's now a clear pattern: 38–25 down after one in Detroit, 19 down in Chicago, and 14 down here. You can't keep giving good teams a head start.

## STORYLINE 4: "BRUNSON WILL BE READY"

**Before the game:** After the 38-point night in Chicago, everyone expected Brunson to carry it into Philly, especially after a rough opening night against this same team.

**What happened:** He finished with 24 points, and he delivered the biggest shot of the night. The Knicks' first lead since the first quarter came on a Brunson corner three that put New York up 90–89 with 1:32 left. In the third quarter, Brunson and Bridges also led a 7–0 run that cut the lead to five.

**Verdict:** MOSTLY GOOD NEWS. The closer showed up late again. He just didn't have enough help in the final 90 seconds.

## STORYLINE 5: THE REVENGE GAME

**Before the game:** This was supposed to be the night the Knicks paid Philly back for spoiling ring night. Some voices were calling for a blowout.

**What happened:** It was a battle. After Brunson's three, LeBron answered with a 12-footer. Philly kept scoring, and with 11.9 seconds left, a tip-in off a Maxey miss pushed the lead to 96–91. OG Anunoby had a late look from three that didn't fall. Final: 98–93.

LeBron finished with 20 points, nine rebounds, eight assists, a steal, and a big chase-down block. Nick Nurse called it LeBron's best game since he arrived in Philly. Maxey led everyone with 27, and Anfernee Simons added 21 with five threes.

**Verdict:** BAD NEWS. Philly leads the season series 2–0. But this was not the opening night beatdown. The Knicks were in it until the final seconds. That's progress, even if it doesn't feel like it.

## STORYLINE 6: THE ODDS

**Before the game:** FanDuel had the Sixers as slight favorites, minus-112 to the Knicks' minus-104. Basically a coin flip.

**What happened:** Philly won by five. The books had it right: a close game that could have gone either way, and the home team made more plays at the end.

**Verdict:** NEUTRAL. The odds didn't lie.

## STORYLINE 7: "THE BENCH WILL SHOOT THE LIGHTS OUT"

**Before the game:** After Chicago, when Clarkson, Kolek, and Shamet were the stars of the comeback, the bench was supposed to be New York's edge again.

**What happened:** This one hurts. Anfernee Simons scored 21 points, more than the entire Knicks bench. On a night when the starters were sloppy early, the second unit couldn't bail them out the way it did in Chicago.

**Verdict:** BAD NEWS. And I have to be honest about my own take here. Last video, I called the bench this team's "missing superstar." I still believe in the depth over a long season. But one game after making that case, the bench got outscored by one player. Depth only works if it shows up consistently, and Friday it didn't.

## STORYLINE 8: ALVARADO

**Before the game:** Jose Alvarado was out again with the sprained left ankle, his fourth straight missed game.

**What happened:** Still no timeline and still no grade on the sprain. The Knicks' silence continues.

**Verdict:** UNCLEAR. It's still possible this is minor, and he could return Monday against his hometown Brooklyn Nets. But four games is starting to add up, and on a night when the bench struggled, his energy was missed.

## THE SCORECARD

So let's add it up: two good-news headlines, four bad, one neutral, and one unclear. That sounds rough. But look at which ones are good. KAT's wrist and Brunson's closing ability are the two most important things for this season. The bad ones, slow starts and an off night from the bench, are fixable.

## THE GOOD NEWS — DEEPER

**KAT's six-game run.** Six games, six double-doubles. He's played through foul trouble, a wrist scare, and a brace. He keeps showing up, and he keeps producing. While contract talks sit on the shelf, this is the best possible argument for his number.

**The fight.** Down 14 on the road in the third quarter against a 5–1 team with LeBron and Maxey, a lot of teams fold. The Knicks won the fourth quarter 25–21 and took the lead. Champions don't quit in October, and this group didn't.

**The quarter-by-quarter story.** Look at the line score:

| | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | Final |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Knicks | 21 | 23 | 24 | 25 | 93 |
| 76ers | 29 | 24 | 24 | 21 | 98 |

Philly won the first quarter by eight, and the Knicks lost by five. After that, New York lost the second quarter by one, tied the third, and won the fourth. With even a decent start, this is a win. That's not a talent problem. It's a focus problem, and focus can be fixed.

**Brunson's clutch gene.** A three to take the lead with 1:32 left, two nights after 14 points in the final seven minutes in Chicago. The wrist is getting better, and so is the touch.

**It's six games.** That's about seven percent of the season. Last spring, this team won a title. Nobody remembers October in June.

## THE BAD NEWS — HONESTLY

**The slow starts.** This is the biggest problem right now. Three straight games, three early holes. Mike Brown's lineup data showed the starting five has been losing its minutes this season, and the opening minutes are when they play most. This has to be fixed.

**The sloppiness.** The first half was messy again. We've seen 10 first-half turnovers in Chicago and 18 total against Detroit. Ball security is a recurring theme.

**The bench drop-off.** Chicago showed what the second unit can do. Philadelphia showed what happens when it doesn't. The Knicks need consistency from Clarkson, Kolek, Shamet, and Deuce, especially with Alvarado out.

**The Atlantic standings.** Philadelphia is 5–1, Boston is 4–1, Toronto is 3–2, and the Knicks are 2–4, fourth in the division. That's not panic territory, but it's not comfortable either.

## THE NEW EAST IS NO JOKE

This is worth saying out loud. The East got tougher this summer. Philadelphia added LeBron James and Jaylen Brown to go with Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey. Boston has Jayson Tatum and now Paul George. Giannis is in Miami and just put up a 31-point, 18-rebound, 10-assist triple-double on Friday. Detroit was the last unbeaten team in the East until the Knicks beat them on Tuesday. Orlando is dangerous.

The Knicks are defending a title in a deeper conference than the one they just won. That doesn't excuse a 2–4 start, but it explains why every night feels like a playoff game. And it makes the next game even more important.

## HOW TO FIX THE FIRST QUARTER

If slow starts are the biggest problem, what can Mike Brown actually do? Here are three ideas. This is my analysis, not reporting.

**One: go to KAT early and often.** He's the most consistent player on the roster. Six straight double-doubles. Instead of letting the offense take a few possessions to find its footing, run the first sets through Towns: post touches, pick-and-pop, elbow actions. Get the most reliable scorer going first, and let everyone else feed off it.

**Two: give the starting five a defensive job, not just an offensive one.** The opening minutes have been where opponents land their early punches. Ask Hart and OG to set the physical tone. Pick up full court for a few possessions, make the other team's first sets uncomfortable, and turn that into easy offense.

**Three: be quicker with the hook, again.** Brown already showed in Chicago that he'll pull the starters if they're flat. If the first five minutes go badly, bring in the group that's been winning its minutes earlier. That means Kolek, Clarkson, and Deuce, with a big who's playing well. It sends a message, and it's worked before.

None of these are drastic. The talent is already on the floor in the opening minutes. What's missing is the edge, the urgency this group showed every night last spring. But this team can't keep spotting opponents eight, 13, or 19 points and expect to come back every night.

## LEBRON IN PHILLY: A PLAYOFF PREVIEW?

Let's take a step back and think about the bigger picture. Philadelphia is 5–1 with LeBron, Maxey, Embiid, Simons, VJ Edgecombe, and, once he's healthy, Jaylen Brown. That's a lot of talent. They've beaten the Knicks twice. If these two teams meet in the playoffs, this regular-season series will be used as a measuring stick.

But don't overreact. LeBron's best game in a Sixers uniform came on a night when the Knicks were sloppy early and still had the lead with 90 seconds left. Philly couldn't put New York away until the final possessions, even without Jaylen Brown. A healthy, locked-in Knicks team that starts well wins that game. The real test is the next two meetings. Per the schedule, there are still two left this season, one more in Philadelphia and one at the Garden.

## MONDAY: THE NETS AT THE GARDEN

Brooklyn comes to Madison Square Garden on Monday at 0–5. They lost to Detroit 110–94 on Friday, their fifth straight loss to start the season. On paper, this is the Knicks' most winnable game of the young season.

That's exactly why it's dangerous. Desperate teams play loose, and the Knicks have shown they can start slow against anyone. Here are the keys:

- **Win the first quarter.** That's the whole game plan. No more holes.
- **Protect the ball.** Clean, simple possessions.
- **Watch KAT's wrist.** Does the brace stay on? Does he play his normal minutes?
- **Alvarado's return?** A Brooklyn native coming back against the Nets would be a great storyline. But there's still no update.
- **A bench bounce-back.** After getting outscored by Simons alone, the second unit needs a statement.
- **Bridges.** He helped lead the 7–0 run in Philly. Can he build on it?

## MY CALL

Here's my take. My last few predictions have been shaky, so I'll keep this one simple and own it if I'm wrong. The Knicks beat the Nets on Monday and get to 3–4. KAT keeps his double-double streak alive at seven games, brace or no brace. And I'm calling the end of the slow starts. I think Mike Brown changes something in the first quarter, whether it's the rotation, the pace, or the first few sets, because three straight early holes is a trend he won't tolerate. Bigger picture: the Knicks are .500 or better by the end of November. This team is too good, too deep, and too well-coached to stay below .500 for long.
