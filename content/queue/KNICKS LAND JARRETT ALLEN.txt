## THE SHORT ANSWER FIRST

Let me save you the suspense. There is no Jarrett Allen trade. We could not find any report — from Shams, from ESPN, from The Athletic, from any national or local outlet — linking Allen or the Cavaliers to the Knicks. Even the video itself admits that no reporter and no outlet in its own reporting linked Allen or Cleveland to New York. It calls the whole thing "Shams's read alone." That's not how Shams works. And when you look closer, the story falls apart in a lot of places.

So let's count.

## RED FLAG #1: THE QUOTE DOESN'T SOUND LIKE SHAMS

The video's centerpiece is Shams saying, "I can confirm," and "This trade is going to happen."

Here's the problem. Shams Charania doesn't talk like that. He reports what is happening — "talks are progressing," "teams are finalizing a deal," "a trade is expected." He does not predict the future with a line like "this trade is going to happen" about a deal no one else has reported.

And here's what should really get your attention: this is almost word for word the same quote we fact-checked a couple of weeks ago in the Dejounte Murray video. Same claim, same "I can confirm," same "this trade is going to happen," same "Shams on First Take" setup. Different player. That's not a coincidence. That's a template.

**Red flag count: 1.**

## RED FLAG #2: THE VIDEO ADMITS NOBODY ELSE REPORTED IT

This might be the funniest part. The video itself says — and I'm paraphrasing — that no reporter and no outlet linked Jarrett Allen or the Cavaliers to the Knicks, that nobody has reported Cleveland agreeing to anything, and that it's Shams's read alone.

Think about how strange that is. When Shams breaks a real trade, every outlet in America confirms it within minutes. The Athletic, ESPN, Bleacher Report, every team beat writer, every Knicks podcast. If a Knicks-Cavs blockbuster were real, the internet would be on fire. Instead, there's silence — except for this one video.

**Red flag count: 2.**

## RED FLAG #3: THE RECORD IS WRONG

The video says the Knicks are "5–4" and that they "just beat the Bulls 135 to 105." But it also says this was all happening on November 6th — the day before the Cleveland game.

On November 6th, after that 135–105 win over Chicago, the Knicks were 4–4. Not 5–4. The details don't line up with the timeline the video itself gives. When a video can't keep its own dates and records straight, you should be very careful with its "inside information."

**Red flag count: 3.**

## RED FLAG #4: THE CAP MATH IS WILDLY OFF

Here's where it gets really wrong. The video says the Knicks' payroll "sits about $88 million" from the second apron.

That's not even close. According to Daily Knicks, the Knicks are only about $370,000 below the second apron. That's why they still can't fill their 15th roster spot. Not $88 million. $370,000. If the Knicks had $88 million of room under the second apron, they wouldn't need a four-player package to get anybody — they could practically sign two max players.

Any video that gets the most important number of the Knicks' season wrong by that much isn't working from real reporting.

**Red flag count: 4.**

## RED FLAG #5: THE PACKAGE MAKES NO SENSE FOR THE KNICKS

Now let's look at the trade itself. According to the video, the Knicks would send:

- Josh Hart
- Deuce McBride
- Pacôme Dadiet
- One more player

That adds up to about $30.2 million going out, against Allen's roughly $28 million salary coming in. The video says the Knicks would send out more money so Dolan stays under the limit.

On the surface, sending out more salary than you take in actually would help with the second apron. So the basic idea isn't crazy in a vacuum. But think about what the Knicks would be giving up:

- **Josh Hart** — the heart of the team, a starter, and one of the most important players on a championship roster.
- **Deuce McBride** — a 40% three-point shooter and elite perimeter defender.
- **Dadiet** — a minor piece, fine.
- **Another player** — the video never even clearly says who.

That's a starter and a key rotation guard for a center. You'd be fixing one hole by creating two more. And the Knicks are already thin on the wing — Josh Hart and Deuce are a big part of why the defense works.

**Red flag count: 5.**

## RED FLAG #6: THE INJURY PROBLEM

And here's the detail that makes the package even stranger. Josh Hart is currently out with right ankle soreness. He missed the Chicago game, and the injury report ruled him out against Cleveland too.

Teams almost never build a blockbuster around a player who's injured at that exact moment — the receiving team wants to see him healthy first. The video even admits it doesn't know how Cleveland would view a package that includes a player who's currently out. That's because nobody has reported that Cleveland has looked at any package at all.

**Red flag count: 6.**

## RED FLAG #7: THE "SAME BUILDING" TRICK

The video points out that the Knicks and Cavaliers play on Saturday and says the conversation is happening "in real time."

That's a classic trick. A game on the schedule makes a fake trade feel urgent. "They're in the same building! Something's happening!" But two teams playing each other is not a sign of trade talks. It's just the schedule. The video even admits a game on the calendar isn't a report that anybody has spoken to anybody.

Also — Allen's own team was in the middle of a strong stretch, coming off a convincing win over Philadelphia. Contenders don't usually dump their starting center in early November.

**Red flag count: 7.**

## RED FLAG #8: THE TIMING IS IMPOSSIBLE

Here's the one that ends the conversation. Several of the players the Knicks would need to include, and any player who re-signed or signed this offseason, aren't trade-eligible until December 15th. Most real NBA trades for players on new deals happen from mid-December through the February deadline.

A four-player, $30 million blockbuster in the first week of November, with no other outlet reporting a word? That's not how the NBA calendar works.

**Red flag count: 8.**

## RED FLAG #9: THE LONG STORY BEFORE THE FACTS

And finally — the format. The video spends minutes on a story about polishing shoes, a smeared shoe, walking around in socks in protest, a parking garage metaphor, a friend who's a retired parking attendant. It's creative. It's entertaining. But when a "breaking news" video spends that long on a personal story before getting to any facts, that's often a sign the facts themselves are thin.

Same with the Dejounte Murray video — that one had a rock-climbing wall and a dog-walker metaphor. Same structure, same narrator style, same "I can confirm" quote. If you see that pattern, your guard should go up.

**Final red flag count: 9.**

## WHAT'S ACTUALLY TRUE IN THE VIDEO

To be fair, some things in the video are real. That's what makes these videos convincing — they wrap a fake headline in real facts.

**True:** Jarrett Allen was an All-Star in 2022. He's one of the most reliable centers in the league. He protects the rim, rebounds, and finishes efficiently.

**True:** The Knicks lost Mitchell Robinson to Boston this offseason.

**True:** The Knicks have a backup center problem. Andre Drummond has been inconsistent, and James Wiseman only just broke out with eight points, eight rebounds, and three blocks against Chicago.

**True:** The Knicks beat the Bulls 135–105 with Brunson scoring 33.

**True:** Josh Hart is dealing with right ankle soreness.

**True:** The Knicks and Cavs play each other — twice in a short span, actually.

So the setting is real. The need is real. The player is real. The trade isn't.

## WOULD JARRETT ALLEN EVEN BE A GOOD FIT?

Let's play along for a second, because it's a fair basketball question.

**The case for:** Allen is exactly the type of big the Knicks miss. A rim protector who rebounds, finishes, and doesn't need plays called for him. Next to Karl-Anthony Towns, he'd let KAT drift to the perimeter and use his career-best 42.9% three-point shooting. On paper, a KAT-Allen frontcourt is a nightmare for opponents on the glass.

**The case against:** Allen is on a big, multi-year contract at about $28 million this season — that's a long-term commitment for a team already facing huge decisions on Towns, Hart, OG, and eventually Brunson. You'd be adding salary to the future just as the Knicks are trying to stay under the second apron. And losing Hart and Deuce would make the team smaller, thinner, and less tough on the perimeter.

**The other problem:** Cleveland is a contender. They were swept by the Knicks in last year's Eastern Conference Finals. Why would they hand their rival their starting center? It would make the Knicks much better and themselves much worse, right in the middle of their own push.

So even in a fantasy world, this trade is a stretch.

## WHY THESE VIDEOS KEEP WORKING

Let's be honest about why videos like this spread so fast. It's not because Knicks fans are gullible. It's because they're hungry.

**The need is real.** Every Knicks fan watching this team knows the backup center spot is a problem. When KAT goes to the bench with foul trouble, the paint gets thin. So when a video promises a fix, you want to believe it.

**The name is perfect.** Jarrett Allen isn't a random player. He's exactly the profile the Knicks need: a rim protector, a rebounder, an efficient finisher. That makes the fantasy feel logical.

**The numbers sound precise.** $30.2 million out, $28 million in. 13.3 points, 7.8 rebounds. An All-Star selection in 2022. Precise numbers make a story feel researched — even when the most important number, the apron gap, is completely wrong.

**The source is trusted.** Shams is the most trusted insider in basketball. Putting a fake quote in his mouth borrows all of that credibility.

**The timing feels urgent.** "They play on Saturday!" "It's happening in real time!" Urgency makes people share before they check.

Put all of that together, and you get a video that feels real enough to send to your group chat. That's exactly why we do these breakdowns. Not to make fun of anyone — to protect the fan base from getting their hopes yanked around every week.

## THE CHECKLIST: HOW TO SPOT A FAKE TRADE VIDEO

Here's your toolkit, Tunnel fam. Next time a "Shams confirms" video pops up, run through this:

1. **Check Shams' X account.** Real Shams news is there within minutes. If it's not there, it's not real.
2. **Check if anyone else is reporting it.** A real blockbuster is confirmed everywhere almost instantly.
3. **Listen to the quote.** "This trade is going to happen" is a hook, not reporting.
4. **Check the basic facts.** Wrong record? Wrong cap number? Big red flag.
5. **Check the calendar.** Players signed in the offseason usually can't be traded until December 15th.
6. **Check the package.** Does it make sense for both teams? Would a contender really give up its starter?
7. **Watch out for long stories first.** If the video spends ages on a personal anecdote before the "news," the news is probably thin.

Screenshot that list. You're going to need it again — trust me.

## WHAT THE KNICKS ARE REALLY DOING

So what's the real situation? Here's what we actually know.

**The need:** The Knicks want another dependable big behind Towns. That's been clear all season.

**The constraint:** They're only about $370,000 under the second apron, with only one tradable first-round pick — in 2033 — and James Dolan won't cross that line.

**The realistic options:** Smaller moves. A Pacôme Dadiet-for-a-minimum-big type of deal after December 15th. Young, cheap names like Yanic Konan Niederhäuser of the Clippers have been floated in analysis — though no talks have been reported. Or developing what they have: Wiseman's four blocks in 21 minutes this season is the same total as starters who've played ten times as much.

**The bigger swings:** The Mitchell Robinson reunion idea, the Jaylin Williams idea — all hypothetical, all difficult under the apron.

None of it looks like a four-player blockbuster for Jarrett Allen.

## IF THE KNICKS EVER DID WANT A BIG CENTER LIKE ALLEN

Let's say down the road — maybe at the February deadline, or next summer — the Knicks really did want a starting-caliber center. What would it actually take?

**Salary:** They'd need to send out roughly as much as they take in, and stay under the second apron. That almost always means a starter or a big contract going out — someone like Hart or, in the more extreme scenarios people discuss, Mikal Bridges.

**Assets:** With only the 2033 first-round pick tradable and a few second-rounders, the Knicks can't outbid teams with big pick stashes. That's exactly why a contender like Oklahoma City has the edge in the big-man market right now.

**Timing:** The most realistic window is from mid-December through the deadline, when more players become trade-eligible and struggling teams start selling.

**Willing partner:** It would have to be a team that's falling out of contention and wants to move salary or collect picks — not a contender like Cleveland.

So a big-center trade isn't impossible forever. But it would look very different from this video — and it would show up first in real reporting.
