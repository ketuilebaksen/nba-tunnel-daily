## THE COMEBACK IN FIVE BEATS

Let's walk through how this game swung, because it was a ride.

**Beat one — Brunson sets the tone.** Eleven points in the first quarter. From the opening minutes, he was attacking with a confidence we hadn't seen in the first four games.

**Beat two — the scare.** Early in the first quarter, KAT went down hard after a collision with Chicago forward Noa Essengue, who was fighting through a screen. Towns landed on his left wrist and headed to the locker room. For a few minutes, the whole season felt like it was resting on that wrist.

**Beat three — the hole.** Chicago got rolling, and by the third quarter New York was down 19. On the second night of a back-to-back, on the road, that's when a lot of teams pack it in.

**Beat four — the bench run.** Instead, the second unit flipped the game with a 22–7 run late in the third quarter. Suddenly it was a game again.

**Beat five — Brunson closes.** The Knicks won the fourth quarter 43–31. Brunson scored 14 of his 38 in the final seven minutes. When Chicago cut it to three in the closing seconds, Brunson and OG Anunoby went to the line and closed it out.

## BRUNSON IS BACK

Let's go deeper on the captain. Thirty-eight points on 13-of-25 shooting, six-of-10 from three, and seven assists. It was his highest-scoring game of the young season, and it came at exactly the right time.

One play summed up the night. Brunson hit Norman Powell with a sharp crossover, left him behind, and buried a three. Then came a loud, emotional celebration, and that's rare from a player known for staying calm. You could feel how much he needed that one.

Why does it matter so much? Because before this week, his shot didn't look like his own. He had wrist surgery in the offseason. He entered the week shooting just 39 percent from the field. We spent a whole video on the wrist question, and on whether the touch would come back. Wednesday was a loud answer. When the rhythm returns, Brunson is still one of the most dangerous closers in basketball. One game doesn't prove the wrist is 100 percent, but six-of-10 from deep is a very good sign.

## THE $370 MILLION QUESTION

And that performance pushes us straight into a bigger conversation, one about his next contract and its timing.

Here's how it breaks down, based on the reporting this week.

**Door A: next summer.** Starting July 12th, 2027, Brunson will be eligible to decline his 2028–29 player option and sign a four-year extension with New York. That deal is projected at about $286.5 million. Most people see it as the most likely path.

**Door B: wait one more year.** If Brunson waits until the 2028 offseason, he could re-sign on a five-year deal projected at around $370.8 million. That's more than $84 million in additional guaranteed money.

On paper, Door B is tempting. Brunson already left a huge amount of money on the table when he extended with the Knicks instead of testing free agency. A contract approaching $400 million would be a way to make some of that back. And he has hinted that being paid fairly matters to him. Nobody can blame him for that. He's a Finals MVP.

## WHY WAITING IS A RISK

Waiting another year is a bet on his own health, and right now the league is giving every player a reason to think twice about that bet.

Look around. Jalen Williams in Oklahoma City has a dislocated ankle and fractured fibula and is expected to miss most, if not all, of the season. Reporting this fall also points to injuries for Kawhi Leonard, Anthony Edwards, Jaren Jackson Jr., and Paolo Banchero. Some medical analysts expect that list to keep growing.

What stands out is that age isn't the common thread. Edwards, Williams, Banchero, Jackson, and Tyrese Haliburton are all younger than Brunson. Some of them have longer injury histories, and that matters. But the way the game is played matters too.

Caitlin Cooper of Basketball, She Wrote has pointed out that the NBA is now averaging the most possessions per game and the shortest possessions of the tracking era. It also has the longest average pickup distance, which means defenders are meeting ball handlers farther from the basket. Teams are pushing the pace like never before. That puts heavy stress on primary creators, and that's exactly what Brunson is.

He's also a smaller guard, now 30 years old, who plays an extremely physical style and drives into contact again and again. His durability is usually seen as a reason not to worry. But you can look at it the other way: all those minutes add up. Since Brunson arrived in New York, only Mikal Bridges and Anthony Edwards have logged more combined regular-season and playoff minutes.

## THE WILD CARDS

Could Brunson surprise everyone again? He has before. He went against expectations when he signed with the Knicks, and again when he chose an extension over free agency.

- **He waits for the bigger payday.** He may feel secure enough in his relationship with the franchise to wait.
- **The Knicks pay whatever it takes.** After he led them to their first championship in 53 years, the front office might feel it has to.
- **He takes less than the max.** There's even a chance he does that to help the team keep building around him. New York shouldn't count on it, but it can't rule it out.

My read: with injuries piling up across the league and the physical demands growing every season, the smart move for Brunson is to lock in long-term security as soon as he can. That makes next summer, Door A, the likely moment, not a year later. That's my analysis, not reporting. Either way, Wednesday reminded everyone why he's worth whatever he signs.

## KAT: THE ANCHOR IN THE STORM

While Brunson grabbed the headlines, the Knicks' most consistent player through two weeks has been Karl-Anthony Towns, and Wednesday showed how much he means to this team.

After that early wrist scare, he came back with the wrist taped. He finished with 19 points, 13 rebounds, and five assists in 28 minutes, shooting seven-of-14 from the field and three-of-six from deep. He was a plus-five in a game the Knicks once trailed by 19. That's five straight double-doubles to start the season.

And remember, this came less than 24 hours after his best game of the year: 27 points and 20 rebounds in the 101–97 win over Detroit.

There's been no long-term update on the wrist yet, so keep an eye on it over the next few days. But the fact that he played through it says plenty.

## THE NUMBERS THAT EXPLAIN THE 0–3 START

Here's the context that makes KAT's start so important. Heading into the Detroit game, look at how the starters were shooting:

| Player | Field-goal % |
|---|---|
| Josh Hart | 41% |
| OG Anunoby | 41% |
| Jalen Brunson | 39% |
| Mikal Bridges | 22% (0-for-10 from three over the first three games) |
| Karl-Anthony Towns | 62% |

That gap explains a lot. The Knicks lost their first three games to Philadelphia, Boston, and Orlando, but none of them got out of hand. Boston won by just four. With four starters ice-cold, the team stayed competitive largely because Towns kept producing, and producing efficiently, even while dealing with his usual foul trouble.

Now things are turning. Bridges went four-of-eight from deep against Detroit. OG scored 23 in Chicago. And Brunson found his rhythm. But the Knicks survived the slow start because their big man gave them steady ground to stand on.

## THE SCHEDULE IS ON KAT'S SIDE

This team isn't built to explode out of the gate. Under Mike Brown, the Knicks play a patient, physical style. They wear teams down instead of running them off the floor. A team like that needs a reliable anchor, and right now that's KAT.

The schedule might help him keep it going. For most of the next month, Towns won't face a center at his level. The bigs coming up include Jalen Smith, Jarrett Allen, and Jakob Poeltl. They're solid NBA players, but none of them bring KAT's mix of size and skill. Smith had 17 points and 11 rebounds on Wednesday, and the Knicks still won the battle of the boards 65–52.

The first true heavyweight matchup comes at the end of November, when Towns finally meets Giannis Antetokounmpo. That game carries extra meaning. In the months before last season's trade deadline, KAT's name kept coming up in rumored packages that would have sent Giannis to New York. Instead, Towns stayed, and the Knicks won the title. That's a storyline worth circling.

## THE CONTRACT ANGLE, AGAIN

All of this creates an interesting situation off the court. A lot of people, including many fans and maybe KAT himself, expected him to have a new contract by now. That hasn't happened. Talks were tabled before the season. Instead, he's playing better than any other starter while the team works through its early struggles, and that only strengthens his position.

There's a difference between a good player and a foundational one. A good player raises his team's floor. A great one raises the ceiling. A true star does both, and also becomes the steady piece everything else is built around. Through two weeks, that's been KAT. A player like that wins you a handful of games you probably should have lost. In a crowded East, those extra wins decide playoff seeding in April.

If the other starters keep slipping while KAT keeps rolling, it'll be very hard for James Dolan to justify not paying him what he's asking. And if a deal doesn't get done, the fan reaction could be loud. For now, though, the priority is simple: that left wrist.

## THE BENCH THAT SAVED THE NIGHT

Not every story from Chicago was about the stars. When the Knicks fell behind by 19, the second unit turned it around.

**Jordan Clarkson:** 18 points on seven-of-11 shooting, plus-15 in just 16 minutes. That's the instant offense the Knicks brought him in for.

**Tyler Kolek:** Nine assists again, and two threes during that 22–7 run late in the third. After nine assists and zero turnovers against Orlando, this is becoming a pattern, not a fluke. Two videos ago we asked whether Kolek had earned more minutes. He keeps answering yes.

**Landry Shamet:** 14 points, even after twisting his right ankle on a dunk in the first half. That's toughness. Keep an eye on that ankle too.

**Andre Drummond:** 11 rebounds. On a night when the Knicks won the glass 65–52, Drummond was a big part of it.

As a team, the Knicks finished with 32 assists. That's the ball movement we've been begging for. When the ball moves like that, shots fall like that.

## DALTON KNECHT: THE LEAGUE JUST SENT HIM A MESSAGE

And that deep bench is exactly what Dalton Knecht now has to fight through.

Quick recap: the 25-year-old wing joined New York on a two-way deal last week, days after the Lakers waived him before the regular season. To open the spot, the Knicks released Kevin McCullar Jr., who had received his championship ring only days earlier. Knecht now shares the two-way group with Tyler Nickel and JD Akins, and he can appear in up to 50 NBA games this season.

It's a big fall for the 17th overall pick in the 2024 draft, a player who starred at Tennessee. After two seasons in Los Angeles that left a lot to be desired, he was cut before his third year.

And he just got some extra motivation. According to ClutchPoints' Brett Siegel, the Lakers talked to the Milwaukee Bucks about a Knecht trade before releasing him. Milwaukee's answer was blunt. The Bucks reportedly wanted multiple second-round picks or a pick swap to take Knecht and send back Jericho Sims.

Think about what that means. Milwaukee is rebuilding after trading Giannis and could use all the young talent it can get, and the Bucks still saw Knecht as a negative asset, someone who needed draft compensation attached. Then no team claimed him off waivers. He went through the process untouched and became a free agent.

The release, the empty waiver wire, and the Milwaukee report all send the same message from around the league. Knecht can use every bit of it as fuel. And so far, he's saying the right things. In his first comments as a Knick, he sounded like someone who knows this is a wake-up call and is ready to answer it.

## THE PLAN FOR KNECHT — AND MY CORRECTION

Mike Brown said the team wants Knecht to play as much as possible, which means he'll start in Westchester with the Knicks' G League affiliate. The goal is steady minutes, rebuilt confidence, and a chance to earn his way up. He spent time in the G League with South Bay during his Lakers years, so it won't be new territory.

Accountability time: last video, I predicted Knecht would get his first Knicks minutes sooner than people expected. With this plan, and with Clarkson, Kolek, Shamet, Deuce, and Mo Diawara all ahead of him, that call looks wrong. He's going to have to earn it in Westchester first, and that's the right approach.

## WHY NEW YORK COULD BE THE RIGHT PLACE

A change of scenery has saved plenty of careers. Fit and environment often decide whether a player sinks or swims.

Knecht's Lakers situation wasn't simple. He arrived as a rookie in an organization built around LeBron James. Then, in that first season, the Lakers made the stunning overnight trade that sent Anthony Davis away and brought in Luka Dončić. Later, his own trade to Charlotte was rescinded. For a young player trying to find his footing, that kind of chaos makes it hard to settle in.

New York is different: a stable team coming off a championship, with a clear structure and a coach who has helped several players find their best form. Under Brown, Landry Shamet revived his career, Karl-Anthony Towns kept growing, and James Wiseman got another chance. Knecht could be next on that list.

What he brings is clear. He's a perimeter shooter first, which is a different profile from McCullar, who was used more as a defensive wing. Over a long season, another reliable shooter can become a valuable piece, even on a roster full of scoring options. Just remember the Orlando game, when the Knicks went nine-of-38 from three.

## WHAT THE STREAK TELLS US

Let's step back. What did we learn in 48 hours?

- **The shooting slump was a slump, not an identity.** Bridges hit four threes on Tuesday. Brunson hit six on Wednesday.
- **The depth is real.** On a back-to-back, with a 19-point hole, the bench won the game.
- **KAT is the anchor.** Five straight double-doubles while the other starters searched for their shots.
- **The turnovers and the slow starts are still there.** A 19-point deficit against Chicago and a 38–25 first quarter against Detroit don't disappear just because you won.

This team's identity is coming back into focus: tough, deep, physical, and dangerous late.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. The Knicks are at .500 within the next week. The rhythm is back, the bench is producing, and the schedule over the next month doesn't throw a dominant center at KAT. On the contract front, I think Brunson takes Door A, the four-year extension next summer, because the injury landscape makes security too valuable to gamble. And I think KAT's wrist is fine. He shot three-of-six from deep after the injury, and that doesn't look like a player who can't feel his hand. But if the team holds him out for a game as a precaution, don't panic.
