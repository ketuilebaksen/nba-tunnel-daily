Clarity first, the NBA Tunnel way. There are no "two new stars" coming to New York today. ## WHERE THE KNICKS STAND

Quick context. The Knicks just held off Minnesota 102–99 for their third straight win. They're 9–6, they've won seven of their last nine, and Karl-Anthony Towns just extended his franchise record to 15 straight double-doubles to open the season.

But the problems are real. Before the Minnesota win, the Knicks ranked just 25th in scoring at about 110 points per game. They struggle whenever Towns steps off the floor. After Mitchell Robinson left for Boston, the front office took chances on Andre Drummond and James Wiseman, and neither has fully solved the backup center spot.

And there's the Bridges question. Even ESPN's Stephen A. Smith weighed in on the November 19th episode of First Take, pointing out that the Knicks gave up five first-round picks for Bridges and demanding more from the reigning champs.

That's the backdrop. Now, the report cards.

## PROPOSAL #1: THE MILWAUKEE MOVE

**The deal:** Proposed by Knicks On SI and highlighted by Heavy:
- **Knicks send:** Mikal Bridges and Pacôme Dadiet
- **Bucks send:** Kyle Kuzma and Kel'el Ware

**The pitch:**
- **Kuzma** is in the final year of a four-year, $90 million deal and becomes an unrestricted free agent next summer. That could give the Knicks about $20 million in extra financial flexibility — potentially making it easier to keep Karl-Anthony Towns long-term.
- **Ware** is a young center with the potential to address one of the roster's biggest weaknesses: depth in the paint. He blocks shots, protects the rim, and has shown he can step out and hit threes.
- **For Milwaukee,** Bridges would bring veteran leadership during the rebuild. And if he gets back to his Brooklyn form, they could flip him later for draft picks.

**The fit check:** This connects to recent reporting. ClutchPoints' Brett Siegel reported that the Bucks are open to offers on virtually any player heading into the deadline, and that Kuzma is one of the two players they've been trying to move since the offseason. So Milwaukee wanting to deal Kuzma is real. Ware, though, wasn't mentioned in that report.

## GRADING PROPOSAL #1 — FOR THE KNICKS

**What they'd gain:**
- A young center prospect in Ware
- A versatile forward in Kuzma for this season
- Major cap flexibility next summer, which matters for KAT's contract

**What they'd lose:**
- Bridges' defensive versatility, size on the wing, and scoring
- The player they gave up five first-round picks for — sold at his lowest point

**The timing problem:** Bridges is just starting to come out of his slump. Remember Tuesday in Toronto: 17 points on seven-of-11 shooting and the dagger three with 36 seconds left. Two nights earlier, he led the team in plus-minus in the overtime win with elite defense. Trading him right as he finds his rhythm would be the definition of selling low.

**The immediate-term problem:** As Heavy's breakdown itself noted, the Knicks would lose Bridges' defense and scoring without necessarily getting better right now. Kuzma isn't a perfect replacement on the wing, and Ware hasn't yet lived up to expectations.

**Knicks grade: C-minus.** The cap logic is smart. The basketball logic, for a team trying to repeat, isn't.

## GRADING PROPOSAL #1 — FOR THE BUCKS

**What they'd gain:**
- A proven two-way wing with a championship ring who could steady a young locker room
- A possible future trade chip if Bridges rebounds
- Dadiet, a young wing on what's now effectively an expiring deal

**What they'd lose:**
- Kuzma, whom they're already trying to move
- Ware, a young big — and young pieces are exactly what rebuilding teams usually keep. Remember, Milwaukee has reportedly not been receptive to calls about its other young players, Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Ryan Rollins.

**The long-money issue:** Bridges is owed about $116.5 million over the three seasons after this one. For a rebuilding team, that's a big long-term commitment for a 30-something wing in a slump.

**Bucks grade: C.** It helps them dump Kuzma, but it adds long-term money and costs them a young big.

**Chance it happens: Low.** Both teams have reasons to hesitate, and the Knicks' front office has shown no sign of selling Bridges low.

## PROPOSAL #2: THE ORLANDO MOVE

**The deal:** Proposed by ClutchPoints, which argued the Knicks should act quickly before the frontcourt issues hurt the rest of their season:
- **Knicks send:** Landry Shamet, James Wiseman, and two future second-round picks
- **Magic send:** Goga Bitadze

**The pitch:**
- **Bitadze** is 27, 6-foot-11, and brings physical strength, rim protection, offensive rebounding, and efficient finishing.
- **This season:** In 11 games, he's averaging 4.9 points and 3.9 rebounds in just 12.3 minutes per game, while shooting an impressive 69% from the field.
- **The role:** He could strengthen the second unit and let Mike Brown pair Towns with a defensive-minded center in key playoff matchups.
- **For Orlando,** Wendell Carter Jr. and the return of Nikola Vučević have limited Bitadze's minutes, and he could become an unrestricted free agent in 2027.

**The money:** Shamet and Wiseman's combined contracts come to about $7.81 million, while Bitadze earns about $7.61 million. That lets New York take back slightly less salary than it sends — which is exactly what the second-apron rules require.

## GRADING PROPOSAL #2 — FOR THE KNICKS

**What they'd gain:**
- A legitimate, physical backup center who protects the rim and finishes efficiently
- Exactly the type of big the Knicks have been missing since Robinson left
- A small payroll cut

**What they'd lose — and this is the problem:**
- **Landry Shamet.** Not a throw-in — one of the most important players on the team right now. He's shooting 43.5% from three, he's the team's best point-of-attack defender against quick guards, and the Knicks are plus-16.6 per 100 possessions with him on the floor and minus-7.5 without him — the worst off-court mark on the roster. Mike Brown has even said it's hard to find minutes for Bridges and Hart because Shamet is playing so well. And he just re-signed this summer on a four-year, $24 million deal.
- **James Wiseman.** The young big whose upside the Knicks haven't even fully tested yet.

**The trade-eligibility issue:** There's also a practical question. Shamet re-signed this offseason, and players who sign new deals in the summer generally can't be traded until mid-December at the earliest. So this deal couldn't happen today anyway.

**The "solve one problem, create another" issue:** You'd fix the backup center spot by opening a hole in the backcourt. And the guard depth is one of this team's biggest strengths.

**Knicks grade: D-plus.** Bitadze is the right type of player. Shamet is the wrong price.

## GRADING PROPOSAL #2 — FOR THE MAGIC

**What they'd gain:**
- Shamet, an elite shooter on a reasonable long-term deal
- Wiseman, a young big with upside
- Two future second-round picks

**What they'd lose:**
- A backup center who isn't playing much behind Carter and Vučević, and who could leave in 2027 anyway

**Magic grade: A-minus.** Orlando would love this deal.

**Chance it happens: Very low.** When one side grades an A and the other a D, it usually doesn't happen.

## THE BETTER VERSION OF THE BITADZE IDEA

Here's the thing: I actually like Bitadze for the Knicks. The profile is right. So let's fix the proposal.

**The problem with the original:** Shamet is too valuable to include.

**A better structure — my idea, not a report:** Something built around **Pacôme Dadiet** (about $3 million, effectively expiring), a smaller salary like **Wiseman's**, and the **second-round picks** the Knicks have stockpiled (they have 12 to work with). The challenge is getting close enough to Bitadze's roughly $7.61 million. That might require adding another mid-sized salary, like **Jose Alvarado's** (about $4.4 million, trade-eligible after December 15th), now that Tyler Kolek has emerged as a reliable backup point guard.

**Why that's better:**
- It keeps Shamet, the team's hottest bench weapon.
- It uses Dadiet's expiring deal, which is exactly what it's for.
- It uses picks the Knicks have plenty of.
- It turns the guard depth surplus into a frontcourt solution without gutting the shooting.

**The emotional cost:** Alvarado is a fan favorite and played a big role in last year's Finals. But Kolek's rise has made this kind of move at least possible.

**Revised Knicks grade: B.** That's a deal worth a phone call after December 15th.

## HOW BITADZE WOULD FIT ON THE COURT

Let's imagine it for a second, because the fit is genuinely interesting.

**Next to KAT:** In big playoff matchups, Brown could pair Towns with Bitadze. KAT slides to the perimeter and uses his career-best three-point shooting, while Bitadze handles the rim protection and the physical battles inside. That's similar to how the Knicks used Mitchell Robinson next to Towns in key moments last spring.

**Behind KAT:** When Towns sits, Bitadze holds the paint. That's been the biggest weakness all season — the Knicks bleed points when KAT goes to the bench, especially with early foul trouble.

**On the glass:** He crashes the offensive boards, which fits the Knicks' identity as one of the best rebounding teams in the league.

**In the pick-and-roll:** A 6-foot-11 roll man who finishes at 69% gives Brunson, Kolek, and Alvarado an easy target.

**The limits:** He's not a floor spacer, and his minutes in Orlando have been limited, so there's a small-sample question. But for a backup center, the job description is simple: rebound, protect the rim, finish, and don't make mistakes. Bitadze checks those boxes.

## THE KUZMA FIT QUESTION

And for fairness, let's do the same for Kuzma. He's a versatile, 6-foot-9 forward who can score, rebound, and handle the ball a bit. On a contender, he'd likely come off the bench as a scoring forward. The concern is defense: he isn't the kind of two-way wing Bridges is, and the Knicks' championship identity was built on long, switchable defenders. Kuzma would add scoring punch but take away some of that defensive versatility. That's why he's not a like-for-like replacement for Bridges, and why the main appeal of that proposal is really the expiring contract, not the player.

## THE BIGGER QUESTION: SHOULD THE KNICKS TRADE FOR A CENTER AT ALL?

Let's step back. Before any trade, there's a strong argument that the Knicks should first find out what they have in-house.

**Andre Drummond:** One of the best rebounders in the game — he leads the NBA in offensive rebounds per 36 minutes among players with at least 100 minutes. But outside rebounding, his impact has been limited. Still, he showed a new wrinkle in Minnesota, knocking down a 10-foot jumper off a Bridges feed.

**James Wiseman:** He's barely played — about 26 minutes through the first 12 games — but per 36 minutes, he grabs more offensive rebounds than anyone on the roster, and he flashed real shot-blocking with eight points, eight rebounds, and three blocks against Chicago.

**The smart order:** Give Wiseman a real look now. If it works, the Knicks keep their guard depth and save their trade assets. If it doesn't, they trade in January with full information.

And here's a funny twist: in Proposal #2, Wiseman is one of the trade pieces. If the Knicks trade him before ever finding out what he can do, that could be a mistake they regret.

## THE MINNESOTA WIN, QUICKLY

Before the final grades, let's give the latest win its due. According to Knicks On SI's Matthew Miranda, there were five big highlights from the 102–99 win in Minnesota — and one controversial moment.

**The highlights:**
- **KAT:** 22 points and a dominant first half, flirting with a double-double before the break. He finished with 22, 12, four assists, and three blocks for his record 15th straight double-double.
- **Brunson:** Just seven points in the first half after taking a hit to the face early, then 14 of the Knicks' 25 points in the third quarter. He finished with 21 points, seven assists, and four-of-five from three.
- **The rest:** OG Anunoby added 15, and five Knicks scored in double figures.

**The controversy:** Late in the game, with the Knicks up 11, Towns appeared to sell contact from Rudy Gobert. The offensive foul call was overturned on a Minnesota challenge, and the Wolves turned it into three points. After building a 19-point lead early in the third, the Knicks nearly let Minnesota pull off a stunning comeback. They scored just 12 points in the fourth quarter.

That's exactly why the frontcourt and offense questions aren't going away, even during a winning streak.

## WHY THESE PROPOSALS KEEP COMING

One more thought before the grades. Notice how many trade proposals we've covered this season that involve Bridges, Shamet, Deuce, Kolek, Alvarado, or Dadiet? There's a reason. With only about $89,000 of room under their hard spending line, the Knicks can't take back more salary than they send out. So every trade idea has to start with which players' salaries go out the door. That's why writers keep building proposals around the same small group of names. The real question isn't "who can the Knicks get?" It's "who can the Knicks afford to give up?"

## MY CALL

Here's my take. Neither proposal happens as written. Bridges stays — he's just starting to turn the corner, and selling him now would be selling at the bottom. Shamet stays — he's too important, and the Knicks just re-signed him. But the Bitadze idea is the right kind of target: a physical, efficient, rim-protecting backup center on a mid-sized deal. If the Knicks make a frontcourt trade after December 15th, it'll look more like my revised version — Dadiet, picks, and maybe Alvarado — than either proposal in the headlines. And before any of that, I expect Mike Brown to give Wiseman a real audition. The Knicks beat Portland at the Garden and make it four straight.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. The Knicks aren't signing "two new stars" — these are trade proposals, not reported deals. Knicks On SI floated Bridges and Dadiet to Milwaukee for Kyle Kuzma and Kel'el Ware, which offers cap flexibility through Kuzma's expiring $90 million deal but sells Bridges low just as he's breaking out of his slump — a C-minus for New York. ClutchPoints floated Shamet, Wiseman, and two second-rounders to Orlando for 6-foot-11 Goga Bitadze, who's shooting 69% in limited minutes — the right type of big, but Shamet is far too valuable to include, so that's a D-plus. A revised Bitadze package built around Dadiet's expiring deal, picks, and possibly Alvarado makes much more sense. And with Drummond flashing a jumper and Wiseman barely tested, the smartest first move might be internal.
