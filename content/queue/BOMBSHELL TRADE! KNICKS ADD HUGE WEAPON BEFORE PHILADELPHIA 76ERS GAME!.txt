THE SHAMET REALITY CHECK

The 2026 Finals turned Landry Shamet into a New York legend — that's simply fact. The 91.7% three-point series against Cleveland that set an NBA playoff record. The sixth-man takeover in the Finals. The team-friendly return on four years, $24 million that the whole city celebrated. Number 44 earned every ounce of the love. But before the praise gets deafening, let's rewind the tape — because the full story makes this coming preseason far more important than people realize.

Who was Landry Shamet before the Garden adopted him? A JOURNEYMAN, in the truest sense. Two seasons with the Clippers. One with Brooklyn. Two in Phoenix. A year in Washington. Six stops, one label following him everywhere: useful bench shooter, decent enough defender, spot-starter in a pinch... and never quite enough for any franchise to fight to keep him. Here's the stat that says everything: this upcoming season will be the FIRST time in his entire eight-year career that Shamet spends three consecutive years with the same organization. Read that again. Eight years in the league, and no team — not one — ever committed to him past two seasons. Even his calling card, the three-ball, ran hot and cold across those stops. He was valuable... and replaceable. That was the book on Landry Shamet.

Then last season happened, and he burned the book. Career-high scoring tied. Second-best three-point percentage of his career. A real, noticeable leap in defensive reliability. A championship. A contract. A legacy. But now come the questions that a single great season cannot answer: can the body survive a full 82 after years of durability wobbles? Was the volume-plus-efficiency shooting a new baseline... or a peak? Were the improved assist and free-throw numbers real growth, or the product of perfect circumstances inside a perfect team? He's 29. One extraordinary year sits on top of seven ordinary ones. And the competition beneath him is VERY real: McBride hungry to erase a rough postseason, Diawara expected to leap, Kolek pushing with his new aggression mission, and rookie Nickel bringing directly overlapping shooting skills. Mike Brown's trust — earned in June — buys Shamet a generous leash. But leashes have ends. Preseason is where number 44 tells us which Shamet is the real one: the beginning of a new chapter, or the highest point of an old one. Knowing how hungry this man has stayed his whole career, I know which way I'm betting — but the receipts have to be printed all over again. Champion's rules.

And understand why this reality check is an act of respect, not disrespect. Shamet's whole story is the reason we love him: nobody handed this man anything, ever. Undrafted-energy career arc, six cities, a hundred nights proving himself to coaches who'd already made up their minds — and when the biggest stage of his life finally arrived, he answered with a RECORD. That history is exactly why the questions don't scare me. Players who've survived the journeyman grind don't relax after one payday; the insecurity is baked into their bones. The four-year deal didn't buy Shamet comfort. It bought the Knicks four years of a man still playing like he's on a ten-day contract. That's the bet — and it's a good one.
