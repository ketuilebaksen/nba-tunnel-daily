## DISPATCH ONE: THE HART SITUATION

**The update:** Hart is out against Cleveland with right ankle soreness. That's his second consecutive absence.

**How it started:** The issue showed up after the 103–90 win over Brooklyn on Monday — and look at what he did in that game. Eight points, six rebounds, seven assists, and three steals in just 27 minutes, despite battling foul trouble for most of the first three quarters. He was a huge part of the energy behind that 28–11 fourth quarter.

**What happened next:** He was listed as questionable for the Chicago game on Wednesday and was ultimately ruled out. Then, even with two days of rest before Saturday, he still wasn't ready.

**The concern:** The Knicks haven't given an official timeline for his return. And as we've talked about all season, this team is famously quiet about injuries. When Jose Alvarado's ankle sprain dragged on for four games with no timeline, the silence got loud. Now it's Hart's ankle, and we're getting the same quiet treatment.

To be fair — "soreness" is usually the mildest kind of label. This may well be the Knicks being cautious. But two straight absences with no timeline is worth watching.

## WHY HART MATTERS MORE THAN HIS NUMBERS

Through the first seven games, Hart was averaging 8.6 points, 7.3 rebounds, and 3.9 assists. On paper, that looks modest. But anyone who has watched this team knows those numbers don't tell the story.

**He's the glue.** Hart rebounds like a big man, guards everyone from point guards to forwards, pushes the pace in transition, and does the dirty work that makes everyone else look better.

**He's the voice.** He's the one who stood at the podium after the Philly loss and said, "We all feel the fatigue. We have to fight through it." Then he went out against Brooklyn and fought through it.

**He's the matchup piece.** Against a backcourt like Cleveland's, Hart is exactly the type of defender you want — physical, relentless, and willing to take on anybody.

So losing him for Cleveland isn't just losing eight points. It's losing a defensive tone-setter against two of the best scoring guards in the East.

## HOW BROWN REPLACED HIM LAST TIME

Against Chicago, Brown slid Landry Shamet into the starting lineup alongside Brunson, Mikal Bridges, OG Anunoby, and Karl-Anthony Towns. Shamet finished with seven points, two rebounds, and two assists in 25 minutes. Not a huge line, but he scored the team's first four points and the starting group set the tone for that historic first half.

And the results speak for themselves:
- **Brunson:** 33 points, 22 in the first quarter alone
- **Towns:** 17 points and 12 rebounds — eight straight double-doubles to start the season
- **Anunoby:** 18 points
- **The bench:** 53 points total, with Jordan Clarkson and Dalton Knecht scoring 10 each — Knecht in his Knicks debut

But let's be honest: Chicago is not Cleveland. The Bulls have been one of the worst defensive teams in the league, giving up 124-plus in every game this season. Cleveland is a totally different test. Brown hasn't officially confirmed his lineup, but Shamet getting another start seems like the most likely move.

## THE LINEUP OPTIONS WITHOUT HART

Here's how I see Brown's choices — my analysis, not a report.

**Option one: Shamet starts again.** It's the continuity choice. It worked against Chicago, and Shamet's shooting spaces the floor for Brunson and Towns. The downside is size and physicality on defense against Mitchell and Harden.

**Option two: Deuce McBride starts.** If Brown wants defense first, Deuce is the natural pick. He's been finding his rhythm — two threes and two steals against Brooklyn, a transition three and a four-point play against Chicago. And he's one of the best point-of-attack defenders on the roster.

**Option three: go big.** Slide OG to the four, play a bigger wing at the three, and lean on rebounding to replace what Hart does on the glass. Less likely, but possible.

My guess? Shamet starts, and Deuce gets the big defensive minutes in the second half — especially when Mitchell or Harden heat up.

## DISPATCH TWO: BRUNSON'S MINUTES

Now let's talk about the captain, because Mike Brown said something this week that changes how we should think about his workload.

**The numbers:** Brunson — the MVP of both the 2026 Eastern Conference Finals and the NBA Finals — is averaging 25.6 points and 5.1 assists per game. He's shooting 41.4 percent from the field, still below his usual standard after the offseason surgery on his left wrist. And he's playing about 34.1 minutes per game, the most on the team.

**The bounce-back:** But the trend is clearly heading up. Against Chicago, he hit five three-pointers and scored 33 in only 28 minutes, sitting the entire fourth quarter. After shooting 39.5 percent through the first six games, the touch is coming back.

**The two-minute data:** As we covered recently, Brown explained that the Knicks' analysts track players in two-minute intervals — shooting efficiency, movement, and signs of fatigue. With most players, the staff sees a drop-off after a certain point. With Brunson, the data shows the opposite. His performance doesn't fall off the way you'd expect as the minutes pile up.

**The new detail:** Brown said Brunson is capable of playing 36 to 38 minutes a night — but emphasized that the goal is to preserve him for the moments that matter most.

## WHAT THAT MEANS AGAINST CLEVELAND

This is where it gets interesting. Without Hart, the temptation will be to lean on Brunson heavily. And the data says he can handle it inside a game.

But here's the balance:

- **Short term:** Against a tough opponent on the road, with a key starter out, Brunson might push toward that 36-minute range. That's fine — it's one game, and it's a statement game.
- **Long term:** Brown is clearly thinking about April, May, and June. Hart already said the team is feeling fatigue after the short offseason. Brunson has more combined regular-season and playoff minutes than almost anyone since he arrived in New York. The goal isn't to max him out in November.

My view: managing Brunson's minutes this season is just as important as winning any single November game. We need the captain healthy for the whole ride. If the Knicks build a lead in Cleveland, he should sit. If it's close late, let him cook — that's what the data says he does best.

## DISPATCH THREE: THE CLEVELAND THREAT

Let's talk about the opponent.

**The momentum:** Cleveland comes in off a convincing win over Philadelphia — the same Sixers team that beat the Knicks twice already this season. Donovan Mitchell had 23 points, seven rebounds, and eight assists in that one.

**The stars:** Mitchell is averaging 22.6 points per game and looking to get back to his peak scoring form. And alongside him is James Harden, a veteran playmaker who can pick apart a defense that's missing its best point-of-attack hustle player.

**The history:** This is the first meeting in Cleveland since the Knicks swept the Cavs 4–0 in the 2026 Eastern Conference Finals — including that wild Game 1, when the Knicks came back from 22 points down in the fourth quarter to win in overtime. You better believe that crowd remembers. And you better believe that Cavs locker room remembers.

**The schedule twist:** After tonight, the Knicks host Milwaukee and then Cleveland again — on back-to-back nights. So these two teams will see each other twice in a short span. Whatever happens tonight will carry right into the rematch at the Garden.

## THE KEYS TO WINNING WITHOUT HART

Here's what the Knicks need tonight:

1. **Start strong.** The slow starts against Detroit, Chicago the first time, and Philadelphia can't happen against a team this good. Against the Bulls, they opened 43–21. Bring that energy.
2. **Win the glass.** Hart's rebounding has to be replaced by committee. Towns, Drummond, OG, and the wings all have to crash.
3. **Contain the backcourt.** Mitchell and Harden will attack. Deuce's defense and OG's length will be critical.
4. **Protect the ball.** Turnovers have been a theme all season. Harden and Mitchell will punish every one in transition.
5. **Trust the bench.** Clarkson, Kolek, Shamet, Deuce — the second unit has won its minutes most nights. Tonight, they have to again.

## BONUS DISPATCH: THE CLIPPERS BIGS

And finally, a frontcourt idea that's getting some attention. According to a Hoopswire piece by Sam Amico published on November 7th — building on an idea first raised by Matthew Miranda of Knicks On SI — the Knicks have been linked to two young Clippers big men as possible depth behind Towns.

Again, let's be crystal clear: there are no reports of active negotiations, and no indication that the Clippers are shopping either player. This is analysis.

**Yanic Konan Niederhäuser:**
- **Age:** 23
- **Size:** About 6-foot-11
- **Background:** A former first-round pick
- **Contract:** Earning less than $3 million this season, with team options for the next two years
- **Strengths:** Rim protection, finishing around the basket, and contributing without needing plays called for him

**Baba Miller:**
- **Age:** 22
- **Size:** A similar frame
- **Strengths:** Upside, plus the potential to develop his jump shot, which could give Mike Brown new lineup options

## DO THE CLIPPERS BIGS MAKE SENSE?

Let's run the quick test.

**The cap fit:** ✅ Niederhäuser's salary is under $3 million. That's the kind of number that could match with Pacôme Dadiet's roughly $3 million expiring contract — important for a team only about $370,000 below the second apron. That's the exact "Tier 1" type of move we've been talking about.

**The age and contract:** ✅ Young, cheap, and controllable for multiple seasons. That's exactly the kind of player the Knicks were looking for all offseason — young, athletic, and up-and-coming.

**The availability:** ❌ The Clippers have had a rough start — one win and seven losses — but that doesn't mean they want to give up young players on team-friendly deals. In fact, a struggling team usually wants to keep its cheap young talent, not sell it.

**The immediate impact:** 🟡 This is the real question. Is a young big man on a rookie-type deal ready to help a championship team right now? Rim protection and simple finishing translate quickly. Developing skills don't.

My view: the Knicks should only consider this if the price is truly favorable. This roster needs players who can contribute right now — not just prospects for the future. But if a cheap, young rim protector is available for Dadiet and a second-round pick, that's a deal worth looking at after December 15th.

## THE BIGGER FRONTCOURT PICTURE

Let's zoom out on why these names even come up. The backup center spot has been the Knicks' biggest question mark since Mitchell Robinson left for Boston this summer.

**Andre Drummond** has had moments — 11 rebounds in Chicago the first time, nine against Brooklyn, and an alley-oop to open the fourth quarter of the Bulls blowout. But his defense has been inconsistent, and he hasn't locked down the job.

**James Wiseman** finally broke out against the Bulls with eight points, eight rebounds, and three blocks in about 10 minutes. That was easily his best night as a Knick. But it came in garbage time of a blowout, and he's still fighting for regular minutes.

**Small ball** with OG Anunoby at center has been Brown's fallback in tight spots, but it costs you size and rebounding — exactly what you can't afford against a big Cleveland frontcourt.

And this connects to the other rumors we've been covering: the long-shot Mitch Robinson reunion talk, the Charles Bassey and Karlo Matković ideas, and the bigger Jaylin Williams package. Every one of them points to the same need. The Knicks want one more dependable big. The question is just how much they're willing — and able — to pay under the second apron.

That's why young, cheap, controllable bigs like Niederhäuser keep popping up in these conversations. They're the kind of player that fits the Knicks' financial box. Whether they fit the Knicks' championship timeline is a different question.

## WHAT TO WATCH TONIGHT, BEYOND THE SCORE

Even if you're just watching the final score, keep an eye on a few smaller things tonight. They'll tell you a lot about where this team is headed:

- **Bridges' aggressiveness.** He had zero free-throw attempts through his first six games. Does he attack the rim against Cleveland? With Hart out, the Knicks need more from him on both ends.
- **Kolek vs. Alvarado.** Kolek has held the backup point guard job since Alvarado returned. Does that continue on the road against a strong backcourt?
- **Knecht's role.** After his 10-point debut, does Brown's "gut feel" call his number again?
- **The minutes distribution.** How many players get 10-plus minutes? Brown's two-minute data suggests he'll keep rotating aggressively — especially without Hart.
