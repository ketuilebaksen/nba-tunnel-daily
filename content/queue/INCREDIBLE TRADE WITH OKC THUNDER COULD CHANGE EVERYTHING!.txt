## PART TWO: WHAT HAPPENED IN OKLAHOMA CITY

Now the trade talk. On Sunday night against the Clippers, the Thunder suffered two brutal injuries. Shams Charania reported that Jalen Williams has a dislocated left ankle and a fractured fibula. On the basketball side, the defending West powerhouse suddenly has two big holes at guard and wing. And that's where the Knicks come into the conversation.

## THE KEY: THE DISABLED PLAYER EXCEPTION

To understand why this idea keeps coming up, you need to know one tool: the Disabled Player Exception, or DPE. Here's the plain version.

Normally, a team over the salary cap can't simply take on a player's salary. It has to send out salary that roughly matches what it brings in. The DPE changes that. When a player suffers an injury that's expected to keep him out for the rest of the season, his team can apply to the league for an exception. If it's granted, the team can add a replacement without sending any salary back.

There are rules. You can use the DPE to sign a free agent, or you can use it to trade for a player who is in the final year of his contract. The player's salary has to fit inside the exception amount.

According to Daily Knicks, the Thunder are expected to get two exceptions: one worth about $15.4 million and another worth about $3.7 million. Those are estimates, and the league would have to approve them.

Now here's the connection. Deuce McBride earns roughly $3.9 million this season, and he's on an expiring contract. That fits easily inside the larger exception. On paper, he checks every box.

## THE SCENARIO, STEP BY STEP

Here's how the proposed deal would work. This is the idea being discussed, not a report of real talks.

- **Step one:** Oklahoma City uses the larger DPE to take in McBride's full salary.
- **Step two:** The Thunder send no players back, so New York doesn't take on any salary.
- **Step three:** The Knicks ask for draft capital in return. OKC has a huge stockpile of future picks, more than it can realistically use. In the boldest version of the idea, that could even mean a future first-round pick.

For the Thunder, they get a proven, playoff-tested guard who defends at the point of attack and hits threes, which is exactly the type of player they just lost. For the Knicks, they clear salary this season and add a future asset. For Deuce, he lands on a contender with a big role and a shot at a strong contract next summer.

That's the pitch. Now let's test it.

## WHY THE KNICKS WOULD EVEN CONSIDER IT

**The money is real.** The Knicks have major decisions ahead with players at the center of their core. Karl-Anthony Towns has a player option and an unresolved extension. Josh Hart has a team option. And next summer, Jalen Brunson becomes eligible for a giant new deal. On top of that, owner James Dolan has shown he doesn't want to go into the second apron, the strictest level of the cap system. Teams above it face heavy penalties and lose key roster-building tools. The Knicks are already operating close to the line.

**The extension isn't happening.** Deuce's name came up in trade rumors all summer as extension talks stalled. Before the season, there were reports that the Knicks were active in conversations about him. And SNY's Ian Begley has said publicly that he finds it hard to believe McBride and the Knicks will land on a new contract that leaves everyone happy. At 26, in his sixth season with the team, Deuce has earned a real payday. The Knicks may not be the team that can give it to him.

**The tool is rare.** Most trades for a player like Deuce require the Knicks to take salary back. The DPE route doesn't. Getting value for an expiring contract without adding a dollar is exactly the kind of move this front office loves.

## THE CASE AGAINST IT

**Deuce is really good, and he's a winner.** Last season was the best of his career: 12 points, 2.6 assists, and 2.4 rebounds per game, while shooting 40.3 percent from three. He was a key piece of the team that won New York's first championship in 53 years. He's one of the best perimeter defenders on the roster. And just last week, he scored 20 in Boston.

**The shooting problem.** We spent our last video on a 9-of-38 night and a team shooting 32 percent from three. Trading away a 40 percent shooter in the middle of that slump would be a strange way to fix spacing.

**The guard depth is thin right now.** Jose Alvarado is sidelined with an ankle injury. Without Deuce, more pressure falls on Tyler Kolek and Jordan Clarkson. Kolek just showed he can run the offense with nine assists and zero turnovers against Orlando. But asking him to take on Deuce's defensive load is a different job.

**You'd be helping a rival.** This is the one nobody talks about. Before the season, Oklahoma City was the betting favorite to win the title. The Knicks were a long shot. If the Thunder and Knicks meet in June, do you want Deuce on the other side, guarding Brunson? A first-round pick is nice. A second ring is nicer.

## THE MIDDLE GROUND

The most realistic version of this probably isn't "trade Deuce now." It's more like "keep the option open." Here's my analysis:

- **Timing matters.** There are trade-window rules about when certain players can be moved, and nothing is imminent. The Knicks can wait to see how the season unfolds.
- **The Thunder have choices.** The DPE can also sign a free agent. They could chase several guards, and the Knicks aren't the only team with an expiring contract to offer.
- **Deuce's value is high.** If a contender is desperate for a 3-and-D guard, the price goes up. The Knicks don't have to take the first offer. They can make OKC, or anyone else, pay full value.

In other words, this is leverage for the Knicks, not a fire sale.

## PART THREE: OG ANUNOBY, THE CONNECTOR

Now, one player whose growth could help fix this offense: OG Anunoby.

Much of the preseason talk around OG was about whether he could become an All-Star in 2026–27. So far, his scoring hasn't matched what he did during last year's playoff run. But he's showing something different. He's passing more than ever.

In last Friday's 107–103 loss in Boston, OG came close to a triple-double with 20 points, 10 rebounds, and eight assists. Analyst Shaz Ali tracks a stat called passing rate. It combines assists, potential assists, passes that lead to free throws, and passes that lead to scoring chances. By that measure, the Boston game was OG's best passing performance in the last two years.

That's a surprise. OG has never been known as a playmaker. Last season, his passing turnover rate ranked in only the 14th percentile among wings. So what changed? Mostly, how the Knicks are using him.

## THE PLAY THAT UNLOCKS HIM

Against Boston, the Knicks ran one specific set three times to get OG the ball, and they scored all three times. Here's how it works:

- **Step one:** KAT sets a down screen, and OG cuts toward the left side of the floor. That angle lets him attack going right, toward his stronger hand.
- **Step two:** KAT immediately comes up to set a ball screen. That makes it hard for the defense to push OG toward the sideline.
- **Step three:** As OG gets into the gap, Brunson cuts through to clear the driving lane, and Bridges sets a pin-down screen to stop a defender from rotating over to help.

On one of those plays, the defense forced OG to his left. He still made the right read and found KAT popping open on the perimeter.

The design works because it keeps the decisions simple. If the opposing center drops back, OG has an easy choice between driving and passing. If the defense rotates, he can attack the space off the catch and draw fouls, which is another area where he's improved.

Nobody expects OG to become an elite passer, and he doesn't need to. If he can be a reliable connector, making quick, simple reads inside the system, the Knicks gain a third creator next to Brunson and KAT. For an offense that has struggled all month, that's a big deal.

## PART FOUR: THE DRAFT-NIGHT MOVE NOBODY NOTICED

Now let's talk about the front office, because one of its quiet summer moves is getting fresh attention, and it ties right back into the Deuce conversation.

Under Leon Rose, the Knicks have built a reputation for bold decisions that pay off:

- They gave Jalen Brunson a contract worth more than $100 million when plenty of people still saw him as unproven.
- They sent five first-round picks to land Mikal Bridges.
- They traded fan-favorite young players to bring in OG.
- They made a major deal for Karl-Anthony Towns.

Every one of those players is now part of a championship core. And with salary cap expert Brock Aller alongside him, Rose keeps finding creative angles in an era when the aprons limit what teams can do.

Here's the newest example. On draft night, the Knicks moved back one spot, from No. 24 to No. 25, picking up cash from the Lakers along the way. At 25 they selected Sergio de Larrea, a 20-year-old Spanish guard from Valencia, and immediately sent his rights to Dallas. At the time, it looked like a typical Knicks draft night: trade out, collect assets, don't add a rookie salary.

One quick note: some coverage this week said the Knicks took him at No. 38. Draft-night reports from outlets like Eurohoops and NBC Sports list him at No. 25, so that's the number we're using.

## WHAT THE NEW REPORTING SAYS

On the latest episode of Brian Windhorst's Hoop Collective, ESPN's Tim MacMahon, who is very well connected to the Mavericks, added an inside detail. According to MacMahon, word had spread around the league that Dallas wanted de Larrea. The Knicks heard about that interest, picked him themselves, and then charged the Mavericks to get him. The return included two future second-round picks.

There was risk. If Dallas had backed off, New York would have been stuck with the rights to a player who was never in its own plans. So why were the Knicks so confident? Windhorst pointed to the Mavericks' front office. Their general manager, Mike Schmitz, a former ESPN draft analyst, has a deep background in international scouting, and so does team president Masai Ujiri. That doesn't mean every overseas prospect is their target. But it helps explain why the Knicks felt sure Dallas was serious.

And de Larrea wasn't a random name. Before the draft, he was playing in Spain's Liga ACB, and just days after being selected, he helped Valencia beat Barcelona to win the league championship. Now he's made a strong home debut for Dallas.

## HOW IT ALL CONNECTS

Here's why the de Larrea move and the Deuce idea are really the same story. Second-round picks don't sound exciting, but for a team in New York's spot they're valuable. They help balance trades, sweeten offers, and move salary, all without adding long-term money. As defending champions with one of the most expensive rosters in the league, every pick, every dollar, and every roster spot matters more.

So picture what the Knicks have in their toolbox now: extra second-round picks from the de Larrea deal, Deuce's expiring contract, Kolek's rising value, and Pacôme Dadiet's small salary. Those are the pieces Rose and Aller can use when the trade window opens after December 15. They could keep this group together, or they could add the piece that pushes it over the top. The Rose era has never been about flashy headlines. It's about reading the market better than anyone else.

## MY CALL

Here's my take. Deuce McBride is NOT getting traded to Oklahoma City in the next few weeks. The fit makes sense on paper, but the Knicks are fighting a shooting slump, Alvarado is hurt, and Deuce is one of their best two-way guards. I don't see Leon Rose sending a 40 percent shooter to the title favorite in October. Where I could be wrong: if the extension gap stays wide and the second-apron pressure grows, Deuce becomes a real deadline conversation, and OKC, with all those picks, would be a natural partner. I'd say a 25 percent chance he's moved by the deadline. And I think OG's playmaking is the real story of the week. If it keeps growing, it changes this team's ceiling more than any trade could.
