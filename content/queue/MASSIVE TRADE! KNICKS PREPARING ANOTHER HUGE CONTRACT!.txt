## THE HONEST PART FIRST

Let's be clear, the NBA Tunnel way. No trade has happened. Nobody has reported that the Knicks are actively shopping Deuce McBride. And no new contract has been signed. "Massive trade" in this video means the trade conversation that's building around this roster — and "another huge contract" means the one the Knicks are preparing for down the road: Jalen Brunson's. Everything about what the Knicks might do is analysis, clearly labeled.

## THE PAYDAY LINE

On October 9th, the Knicks released a statement saying they won't negotiate contract extensions until later in the season so the team can focus on the title defense. That pause affects four players: KAT, Hart, Deuce, and Kolek. The Knicks have until June 30th to get deals done with the extension-eligible players outside of KAT's special case. So let's line them up the way the front office has to see them.

**At the front: Jalen Brunson.** Not extension-eligible yet, but every decision the front office makes should start with him. The captain and Finals MVP already took less money to help build this team, and he's not expected to keep taking pay cuts. Fred Katz has projected an extension around $290 million over four years, starting in 2028–29. When that moment comes, the Knicks have to be ready to pay him. That's the "huge contract" this whole roster is being built around.

**Second: Karl-Anthony Towns.** The team's clear number two option. He's earning $57 million this season and holds a $61.1 million player option for 2027–28 — which means he could reach free agency next summer. He's eligible for an extension worth up to about $276 million, while the Knicks reportedly offered about $200 million over four years. And his option deadline is today.

**Third: Josh Hart.** He's making about $21 million this season and has a team option worth around $22 million for 2027–28. A full-time starter, one of the league's best rebounding guards, the guy who does the dirty work. Publicly, he's been calm — he says he believes everything will work out.

**Fourth: Deuce McBride.** And this is where it gets painful.

## DEUCE: FOURTH IN LINE

Here's the hard reality. Deuce McBride could have the best season of his career and still find himself fourth in line among the players waiting for new deals. That's not a knock on him. It's just how the Knicks have to build under the apron. Brunson first. Then KAT and Hart. Then Deuce.

And he's still delivering. In the preseason finale — a 122–120 win over Toronto — Mike Brown used him sparingly, but his impact was obvious. In just 13 minutes, he went three-of-six from three and finished with nine points, a rebound, and two assists, including three straight threes to close the first quarter. That's the sharp shooting that's defined his career in New York, and a sign he's ready for the regular season.

But there was also a telling detail. Mike Brown leaned more heavily on Landry Shamet off the bench in that game. Shamet played 24 minutes — almost twice as many as Deuce. That doesn't mean Deuce has lost his place. Nobody in New York doubts what he can do. The real issue isn't his production. It's his contract.

## WHAT DEUCE'S AGENT JUST SAID

And his camp is sending a clear message. Deuce's agent, Mark Bartelstein, spoke in an interview on SiriusXM on Saturday, as reported by Jared Schwartz of the New York Post. Bartelstein said the two sides had been talking throughout the summer, right up until the team shut down all extension discussions. He said that, like any player in his position, Deuce would love to sign an extension. And he made the case that his client has earned it — pointing out that Deuce has been a huge part of the organization's rise over the past three or four years and has served as the team's sixth man.

All of that is true. Deuce wants to stay in New York, and he's made that clear. But wanting to stay and actually staying are two very different things. And right now, the numbers may not be on his side.

## THE TWO PATHS FOR DEUCE

So that leaves Leon Rose and the front office with two realistic paths.

**Path one: keep him all season.** Give the team its best chance to win back-to-back championships, then let Deuce test the market next summer — much like the Knicks handled Mitchell Robinson's final year. The benefit: you keep one of your most reliable shooters and perimeter defenders for a title run. The cost: you could lose him for nothing.

**Path two: trade him during the season.** Most likely in a package that brings back draft picks or other assets that fit more easily under the team's financial limits. The benefit: future value instead of an empty spot next summer. The cost: it weakens a bench that already has less margin for error than last year — especially after losing Robinson.

And here's the connection to our last couple of videos. Remember the trade window: until December 15th, Deuce, Pacôme Dadiet, and Tyler Kolek are the only non-starters the Knicks can trade. Combining Deuce's salary with Dadiet's could bring back a player making just over $6.9 million. That's why keeping Dadiet over Bruce Brown mattered. If the front office ever decides a Deuce trade makes sense, the tools are already in place.

**My take on Deuce:** I keep him for the title run unless a deal brings back a rotation big who clearly makes this team better now. Shamet's surge makes a Deuce trade easier to accept — but Deuce's defense is still one of this team's most valuable bench skills. You don't give that away for picks in the middle of a title defense.

## WHAT A DEUCE TRADE COULD ACTUALLY LOOK LIKE

Let's sketch it, clearly labeled as NBA Tunnel analysis — not a report. If the Knicks ever moved Deuce, the most logical version wouldn't be a pure dump for picks. It would be a consolidation: Deuce plus Dadiet, maybe with second-round picks from the Knicks' stash of at least 11, for one player making up to about $6.9 million who fills a real need. And the biggest need on this roster is obvious: size behind KAT and Drummond.

That's why names like Moussa Diabaté in Charlotte keep coming up. A young, athletic big on a reasonable contract who could slot in as a rotation center — exactly what the Knicks lost when Mitch left. You'd be giving up a great backup guard, but with Shamet, Clarkson, Alvarado, and Kolek in the backcourt, guard depth is the one thing this team has plenty of. Center depth is the thing it doesn't.

Would I do that deal? If the big man is a real rotation player and the Knicks can't extend Deuce anyway — maybe. But not before December, and not for anything less than a clear upgrade at a position of need.

## WHY THE LINE EXISTS: THE OWNER'S CALL

Now let's talk about why this line exists at all. Because the reporting is clear: it starts at the very top.

Before the championship parade even started, James Dolan announced the Knicks would not spend into the second apron. That decision effectively meant goodbye to Mitchell Robinson — the team's longest-tenured player, one of its most popular, its only true rim protector, and one of the best offensive rebounders the league has ever seen. And he left for Boston, the Knicks' biggest rival.

According to the reporting, that wasn't because the Knicks had a replacement ready, and it wasn't because the front office believed it was the right basketball move. It was Dolan's call. And here's the part that frustrates fans: going over the apron doesn't trigger immediate punishment. The Denver Nuggets and Cleveland Cavaliers have both been above it without putting stars like Jamal Murray or Evan Mobley on the trade block. Robinson became the Knicks' first apron casualty — and without him, the Knicks' title chances are clearly lower.

And as Fred Katz reported, the front office disagrees with the "never under any condition" approach — and if it doesn't change, more departures will follow. Deuce could be the next player caught in that squeeze.

## THE LOCKER ROOM QUESTION

None of the key players are pretending to be happy about the pause. KAT has been the most direct. He said the news caught him by surprise, that he was very disappointed, and when asked about his chances of signing before the season, he answered simply: "It don't look good." Hart has kept a calmer tone. Deuce's agent is making his case publicly.

And here's the bigger concern. Modern title windows are short by design, and one of the best ways to attract new talent is to treat your own players well. If the only players the Knicks seem to value are the ones willing to accept less than they believe they deserve, morale could take a hit. And what message does that send to players around the league?

There's also the question of the White House visit. After the title, Dolan announced the Knicks had accepted an invitation. But this preseason, Mike Brown said the matter was still being discussed internally — and that whatever the team decides, everyone will do it together. When an owner makes announcements without full buy-in from the people affected, it points to a gap between the top of the organization and the locker room. That's not where you want to be when 29 other teams are trying to knock you off the throne.

The Knicks became champions for a lot of reasons. An underrated one: nobody on the roster tried to do what he couldn't do. Everyone knew their limits. The best thing the organization can do now is make sure that applies at the top, too.

## WHY IT MATTERS NOW: THE ATLANTIC GAUNTLET

And here's why every one of these decisions matters more this season than ever. The hardest part of the road back to the Finals might be in the Knicks' own backyard.

Think about last season's other finalist. The Spurs played in a division where Memphis, Dallas, and New Orleans missed the playoffs comfortably, and Houston went out in the first round. The Knicks won't have that luxury. Every defending champion gets every opponent's best shot — but New York also has to survive a loaded division twice: first for seeding, then possibly in the playoffs.

Last season, counting the playoffs, the Knicks went 14–3 against Boston, Toronto, and Philadelphia. This year, each of them has a real argument that it can end New York's run.

**Toronto.** The Raptors landed Kawhi Leonard, replacing Brandon Ingram — an upgrade across the board. Health is always the question: he hasn't played 60 or more games in back-to-back seasons in a decade. But when he plays, he hasn't slowed down. Last season, he logged his second-highest minutes total since his first stint in Toronto, and it's been more than 10 years since he played at least 50 games without making one of the first two All-NBA teams. And Toronto can throw three elite defenders at KAT or Brunson: Kawhi, a two-time Defensive Player of the Year; Scottie Barnes, second-team All-Defense last season; and Collin Murray-Boyles, a young player with DPOY potential. Nobody wants to see the Raptors in a best-of-seven.

**Boston.** Two seasons ago, the Knicks knocked the Celtics out in the second round, with KAT and Mitch combining for about 25 points and 20 rebounds a game. Last spring, Boston lost a seven-game first-round series to Philadelphia — winning two of the three games Joel Embiid missed, but losing three of the four he played. The lesson: get better at center. They did. Mitch now shares the position with Neemias Queta, giving Boston two starter-level rim protectors. Jayson Tatum is back from his 2025 Achilles tear. Paul George plays a supporting role. Baylor Scheierman and Jordan Walsh earned real minutes last season, the organization is high on Hugo González, and about half a dozen players remain from the 2024 title team. Boston could chase a seventh straight division title.

**Philadelphia.** The Sixers added two former Finals MVPs — LeBron James and Jaylen Brown — major upgrades over Paul George and Kelly Oubre. Brown, a 6-foot-8 wing with explosive athleticism, gives Tyrese Maxey — who averaged 28.3 points and 6.6 assists last season — a true co-star. LeBron, entering his 24th season and turning 42 in December, doesn't need to carry the team; he can be a Swiss Army knife. He was the league's second-leading transition scorer last season, behind only Maxey. The biggest question is Embiid: few teams can handle him when he plays, but he struggled badly on defense against New York in last year's playoffs, when the Knicks swept the series.

And here's a piece of history: the last time the Knicks defended a title, their biggest threat came from inside the division — Boston won it all in 1974. In 2026, New York didn't face a single top-10 player in the playoffs until the Finals. Now there are three in the Atlantic alone — four if you count Brunson. As long as the Knicks have their captain, they have a real shot.

## WHY THE PAYDAY LINE AND THE GAUNTLET ARE CONNECTED

Put it together. The Atlantic has never been tougher, and the Knicks' margin for error has never been thinner. Mitch is in Boston. The center depth behind KAT and Drummond is a 25-year-old Wiseman. And the bench's best defender, Deuce, is fourth in a payday line with no guarantee of staying. Every decision Leon Rose makes — keep Deuce or trade him, pay KAT or pivot, prepare for Brunson's mega-deal — will ripple through a division where one bad week can cost you home-court advantage.
