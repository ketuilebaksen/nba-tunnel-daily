## THE ROOT: ONE SPOT, ONE RULE

Let's remind everyone of the foundation, because every branch grows from it. The Knicks have 13 guaranteed contracts. They're required to carry at least 14 players during the season, so the 14th spot must be filled. And because they're only about $3.2 million below the second apron — the line James Dolan won't let them cross — they can only fit one more veteran minimum contract. One spot. That's the root of the tree.

And this week, the insiders agreed on what grows from that root.

## THE INSIDER CONSENSUS: A BIG GETS IT

**Stefan Bondy, New York Post:** He expressed confidence that the Knicks will ultimately keep a center as the 14th man. That means the battle comes down to James Wiseman, Tony Bradley, and Drew Eubanks — unless the front office makes a trade to open up more room below the second apron.

**Ian Begley, SNY:** He confirmed it — the Knicks will add a big with the 14th spot. But he narrowed it further. According to Begley, the two centers battling for that spot are Drew Eubanks and James Wiseman. Tony Bradley does not appear to be in the running anymore.

**James L. Edwards III, The Athletic:** Wiseman still feels like the frontrunner. New York needs a third center and will likely use the 14th spot to address it. And Wiseman has been the first big man on a training camp deal that Mike Brown has tapped to check into games, with Eubanks and Bradley falling behind.

Three different reporters, three different sets of sources, and one shared conclusion: the 14th spot goes to a center — and Wiseman is in the lead.

## BRANCH CUT: TONY BRADLEY

Let's take a moment for Tony Bradley, because if Begley is right, his chances are essentially over. And that's a little surprising when you remember how this camp started. Bradley was the player Mike Brown spoke about most warmly when the Knicks signed him — the veteran who had hurt the Knicks in the past, the box-out specialist. But the games told a different story. About six minutes in Philadelphia, one shot made, not much else. No minutes against Washington. And when Brown gave the veterans their shot in the Minnesota game, Bradley didn't do enough to change the picture.

That's how fast camp moves. A coach's warm words in September don't guarantee anything in October. Bradley will almost certainly land somewhere — he's a legitimate NBA big — but it looks like it won't be in New York.

## MIKE BROWN'S DIPLOMATIC ANSWER

So what does the head coach say? Begley asked Mike Brown about his impressions of Eubanks, Wiseman, and Bradley. Brown said he believes all three are legitimate NBA players. None of them are projects, so to speak. Each brings a different skill set. And it's going to be a tough decision.

**What it really means:** That's a coach protecting all three players publicly — which is exactly what a good coach does. You don't embarrass veterans in the final week of camp, because one of them might be the guy you call in January when injuries hit. But read the second part carefully: "none of them are projects." That's actually an important line for Wiseman. The biggest knock on him has always been that he's a reclamation project — a former number two pick still trying to figure it out. Brown publicly putting him in the same "legitimate NBA player" category as the veterans removes that label. And the actions back it up: Wiseman has been the first camp big to check into games every time Brown has had the choice.

## A QUICK ACCURACY NOTE

One correction worth making, because some coverage this week got it wrong. You may have seen Wiseman described as coming off a torn ACL. That's not accurate. Wiseman's major injury was a torn Achilles tendon, suffered in his very first game with the Indiana Pacers in October 2024. He also had knee surgery earlier in his career. Small detail, but on this channel, we get it right.

## THE CASE AGAINST WISEMAN — STILL WORTH HEARING

Now, fairness. Even with the consensus forming, there's a real argument against Wiseman, and it deserves a hearing. He's 25 and was the number two pick in the 2020 draft, but he's never found his footing in the NBA. He hasn't been consistent. He's played very little since the Achilles. And you could argue that the third center should be a veteran the coaches KNOW they can rely on — because the third center's job is to be ready when Towns or Drummond gets into early foul trouble or suffers an injury. In those moments, you want certainty, not upside.

That's Begley's long-running argument for Eubanks, and it's not wrong. But the counterargument is just as strong: Wiseman gives the Knicks the frontcourt athleticism they lost when Mitchell Robinson left for Boston. He's the only candidate who brings a real vertical lob threat — the rim-running weapon that made Brunson and Mitch one of the league's best pick-and-roll duos. And as we covered in our trial video, if the Knicks sign him to a standard minimum that doesn't become fully guaranteed until early January, they get a two-and-a-half-month trial period with a safety net. Upside AND caution. That's why the insiders keep landing on him.

## THE SECOND DOOR: BRUCE BROWN

Now let's climb to the more interesting branch of the tree. Because while the 14th spot is almost certainly going to a big, there's a growing push for a second addition — Bruce Brown.

Edwards wrote that the Knicks should strongly consider adding Bruce Brown to the main roster, given his championship experience and positional versatility. And Begley went even further. When he was asked which bubble player stood out in the fourth quarter against Minnesota and could actually be on the court on opening night, his answer was clear: "I want to say that's Bruce Brown. I think he's put himself in a good position to make this roster. He's impressed people internally with his play in training camp and preseason."

And it's not just reporters. Jalen Brunson said earlier this week that Bruce Brown "brings a lot of things that can help us." When the captain and Finals MVP vouches for a camp invite, that's not nothing.

This lines up with everything we've seen. Mike Brown called his on-ball defense "textbook." BBall Index ranked him as the second-best screen navigator on the roster last season, behind only Jose Alvarado. He's played more minutes than any other newcomer in the preseason. And he's a champion who's been on eight teams in nine years and knows exactly what a winning role looks like.

## THE PROBLEM: THE MATH SAYS ONE

But here's the issue Begley spelled out: the Knicks have to keep a third center, and they only have one roster spot open. So to keep Bruce Brown, they'd have to make a trade to open room. And Begley asked the question out loud: is that Pacôme Dadiet? Is that Tyler Kolek? Is that someone else?

Edwards added that it's possible the Knicks keep both a center and Bruce Brown — if they can pull off a trade between now and the start of the season.

So this is where the decision tree splits.

## BRANCH A: NO TRADE

If the Knicks don't make a trade, the tree is simple. The 14th spot goes to a big — most likely Wiseman. Eubanks and Bradley are released. And Bruce Brown, despite impressing everyone, is released too. He'd become one of the best free agents available, and some team would probably scoop him up quickly. That would be a tough loss — but it would be the cost of Dolan's apron line.

## BRANCH B: THE DADIET SALARY DUMP

If the Knicks trade Pacôme Dadiet, the tree opens up. Here's why it works. Dadiet makes about $2.98 million this season. Moving him without taking salary back would open a roster spot AND create more room under the second apron — enough, combined with the existing cushion, to fit a second veteran minimum. That means Wiseman at 14 AND Bruce Brown at 15.

How would it work? As we covered in our Buy, Sell, Hold video: Dadiet plus a second-round pick or two — the Knicks have at least 11 between 2027 and 2033 — to a team with cap space or a trade exception. Memphis has a little cap space. Charlotte, New Orleans, the Clippers, and Sacramento all have exceptions that fit his salary.

And there's a telling detail about where Dadiet stands in this organization. The Knicks gave a contract to Mohamed Diawara, a second-round pick, while Dadiet — a first-round pick — has fallen behind. When a team invests in a second-rounder over a former first-rounder at a similar position, that tells you who the front office believes in more. Add the October 31st decision on his roughly $5.37 million option for 2027–28, which the Knicks are very unlikely to pick up, and Dadiet looks like the obvious piece to move.

And honestly, it might be best for him too. He's only 21. On this roster, there are no minutes for him. Somewhere else — with more playing time and lower expectations — he could finally show what made him a first-round pick. A change of scenery could help everyone involved.

## BRANCH C: THE KOLEK QUESTION — DON'T DO IT

Begley also mentioned Tyler Kolek as a possible name. Let me be very clear about where this channel stands: I would NOT trade Kolek to make room for Bruce Brown.

Kolek has already shown he's an NBA player. In the preseason opener, he had five points and five assists without a single turnover — one of the best nights of any reserve. He's a young, cheap backup point guard who sees the floor and takes care of the ball. On a team that will need affordable contributors more than ever as the apron squeeze tightens, Kolek is exactly the kind of player you keep. Trading him to open a spot for a 30-year-old veteran on a one-year deal would be short-term thinking. Dadiet is the move. Kolek stays.

## BRANCH D: THE WILDCARDS

And then there are the branches nobody can fully predict.

**The Cissé branch:** As we covered in our last video, Marc Stein raised the possibility that Dallas could release Moussa Cissé to make room for Dwight Powell. If that happens and Cissé clears waivers — where the Knicks pick last — it could shake up the center decision completely. Still a long shot, but worth watching this week.

**The KAT branch:** On Monday, October 19th, Karl-Anthony Towns has to decide whether to decline his $61 million player option. That decision won't change the 14th spot directly, but it will shape the front office's mindset heading into the season — especially if he declines and becomes an expiring contract. Every roster decision this week is happening in the shadow of that deadline.

## THE PRESEASON FINALE: THE DRESS REHEARSAL

Now let's talk about the last two preseason games, because they're part of the decision.

The first Toronto game was a bench showcase. The starters played Minnesota on the first night of a back-to-back, so they sat in Toronto. And the betting market made that clear: the Raptors were huge favorites, around minus-430, with the Knicks at plus-320. That had less to do with New York's winless preseason and more to do with who was on the floor — bench players and roster hopefuls only. For the bubble guys, that game was a showcase, not a scoreboard.

Then comes Thursday, October 15th — the final preseason game, again against Toronto. And this one is different. Mike Brown is treating it as a dress rehearsal for the regular season. Expect the starters to play real minutes, expect the rotation to look a lot like opening night, and expect it to be the clearest preview yet of how this team will look on Tuesday.

## WHAT I'M WATCHING THURSDAY

Here's my watch list for the dress rehearsal.

**Who's the first center off the bench after Towns and Drummond?** If it's Wiseman again, that's your answer on the 14th spot.

**Does Bruce Brown get rotation minutes alongside the regulars?** If Brown is playing with the main bench unit in a dress rehearsal, the front office might be preparing for the Dadiet move.

**Tyler Kolek's role.** Does he get backup point guard minutes, or is he behind Alvarado and Deuce? His usage will tell you how the coaches see him.

**Mohamed Diawara.** Remember the chase-down block on Kentavious Caldwell-Pope in the opener? I want to see if he can get back to that level and earn a spot in the rotation.

**Tyler Nickel.** One of the two-way guys who's been getting minutes. Can he shoot his way into the conversation?

**The starters' rhythm.** Brunson's wrist, KAT as the hub, OG at the free-throw line, Bridges attacking. The dress rehearsal should show us whether everything we've talked about is carrying into the regular season.

## DON'T FORGET DREW EUBANKS

Before we close the tree, let's give Drew Eubanks his due, because he's not out of this yet — and even if he loses, he might not be gone for long. Eubanks has eight years in the NBA. He's physical, he rebounds, and he screens more like Mitchell Robinson than Wiseman does. Begley, one of the best-sourced reporters on this team, has backed him as the safe option all camp. And remember Begley's deeper logic: if the Knicks end up trading for a big man before the February deadline anyway, the 14th man is really just a bridge — and a bridge should be reliable.

Here's the other thing. If Wiseman wins the spot on a non-guaranteed deal and struggles or gets hurt before the January guarantee date, who's the first phone call? Probably the veteran who already knows the system, the coaches, and the locker room. Eubanks spent a full camp learning Mike Brown's schemes. That has value. Sometimes losing a camp battle in October just means you're the first name on the list in December. So if Eubanks gets released, don't be surprised if his name comes back around this season.

## WHAT I'D DO

If I'm Leon Rose, here's my decision tree. Trade Dadiet with a second-round pick or two to a team with room. Sign Wiseman to a non-guaranteed standard minimum for the 14th spot. Sign Bruce Brown for the 15th spot. Keep Tyler Kolek. Thank Eubanks and Bradley for their professionalism, and keep their phone numbers for January. That roster is deeper, tougher, more experienced on the wing, and more athletic at center than any version that keeps Dadiet.

And it does all of that without crossing James Dolan's second-apron line. That's the art of team-building in this era: not one big swing, but a series of small, smart moves at the end of the bench.
