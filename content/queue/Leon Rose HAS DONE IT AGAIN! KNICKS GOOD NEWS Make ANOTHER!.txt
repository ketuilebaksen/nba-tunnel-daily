## WHAT "ROSE DID IT AGAIN" REALLY MEANS

Let's define the phrase, because it gets thrown around a lot. When people say Leon Rose has "done it again," they're talking about a specific formula this front office keeps using:

**One: buy low.** Find a player the market undervalues — for injury reasons, for fit reasons, for reputation reasons.

**Two: keep the cost tiny.** Don't spend assets or blow up the cap to get him.

**Three: put him in the right situation.** A clear role, a structured environment, a coaching staff that knows him.

**Four: protect yourself.** Structure the deal so that if it doesn't work, it doesn't hurt.

Landry Shamet came in on an Exhibit 9 deal and became a $24 million champion. Andre Drummond, a signing fans were split on, showed up in the best shape of his recent career after one phone call from Mike Brown. And now Wiseman — a player nobody else wanted this summer — wins the last roster spot on a title team. Same formula. Different player.

## THE DECISION ITSELF: SIZE OVER EXPERIENCE

Here's the context. The Knicks brought five veterans into camp on non-guaranteed deals. Drew Eubanks suffered a torn pectoral muscle and was waived. Tony Bradley and Ochai Agbaji were released. That left Wiseman and Bruce Brown. With James Dolan refusing to cross the second apron, the Knicks only had room for one more veteran minimum. Keeping both would've required a trade. The Knicks chose size.

And notice the cost of the choice. Bruce Brown is a champion who earned praise from the coach and the captain. Letting him go wasn't easy. The front office decided the hole in the middle — the one Mitchell Robinson left when he signed with Boston — mattered more than another veteran wing. That's a clear statement about priorities.

## MIKE BROWN'S TWO QUOTES THAT EXPLAIN EVERYTHING

Mike Brown has given us two quotes about Wiseman, and when you put them side by side, you understand exactly why this happened.

**Quote one, from camp:** "When you're that long and that athletic with skill — he has skill — there's a place for you in this league. Sometimes it takes the right moment, the right team, the right situation, all that stuff to be able to fit someplace and grow."

**Quote two, after the decision:** When you have a 25-year-old with the length, athleticism, and talent of James Wiseman — because KAT isn't athletic like that, and Andre once was and still has some of it — you look for guys who could have, should have, and at his age, maybe still can. "That vertical threat at the rim, the vertical threat defensively, how good he runs. You welcome that at his size and age and athleticism."

Read them together. The first quote is about belief: Wiseman never had the right situation. The second quote is about need: the Knicks don't have anyone else who brings that vertical dimension. Belief plus need. That's the whole decision in two sentences.

And there's history behind the belief. Brown was an assistant on Steve Kerr's staff in Golden State during Wiseman's first two seasons. He saw the talent up close before the injuries took over.

## THE SCOUTING REPORT IN THREE WORDS: CATCH, FINISH, PROTECT

So what exactly did the Knicks get? Let's simplify it to three words.

**Catch.** Wiseman is a natural lob target. In his last healthy season with a regular role, more than 46 percent of his made baskets came on dunks or alley-oops. This preseason, Jose Alvarado was already feeding him lobs against Washington.

**Finish.** He has real touch around the basket — the kind of soft hands that let him score from multiple spots, not just on dunks. His best preseason night came in Toronto, where he started and put up 18 points and 10 rebounds. Across four preseason games, he averaged about 8 points and a little over 4 rebounds in limited minutes.

**Protect.** He moved his feet on defense and protected the rim, blocking shots in multiple games. And he crashed the offensive glass — two offensive rebounds against Philadelphia, one against Washington, and four in Toronto. For a team that lost one of the best offensive rebounders in the league, that matters.

Catch, finish, protect. That's the job description of a third center on a contender. And that's what Wiseman showed.

## THE LEDGER: THE UPSIDE COLUMN

Now let's do the honest accounting. Start with the upside.

**The pedigree.** Wiseman was the number one player in the high school class of 2019. He was the Gatorade National Player of the Year and the Morgan Wootten National Player of the Year. He made the USA Today All-USA first team and was a McDonald's All-American. Nobody questions the talent.

**The college flash.** At Memphis, he played just three games — and in those three games he averaged about 20 points, 11 rebounds, and three blocks while making roughly 77 percent of his shots.

**The rookie year.** In his very first NBA game with Golden State, he scored 19 points with six rebounds in 24 minutes. Over 39 games that season, he averaged almost 12 points, nearly six rebounds, and close to a block per game. For a 19-year-old who barely played in college, that's legitimate production.

**The age.** He's still only 25. Big men often develop late. He has years of runway left.

**The price.** A veteran minimum. For the Knicks, under the apron, that's the most efficient possible use of their last roster spot.

## THE LEDGER: THE RISK COLUMN

Now the other side, because a ledger has to balance.

**The rookie-year injuries.** A wrist injury in January cost him about three weeks. Later that season, he landed awkwardly on a dunk attempt and tore the meniscus in his right knee, ending his year.

**The lost season.** He missed his entire second season recovering.

**The ankles.** Sprains in later seasons, including a more severe one that limited his availability.

**The Achilles.** In October 2024, he tore his left Achilles in his first game with Indiana.

**The inactivity.** Only five NBA games over the last two seasons combined. Across five NBA seasons, he's averaged about 9.0 points and 5.5 rebounds.

**The weaknesses.** He's never been much of a passer, and his defense hasn't always matched his size and athleticism.

So it's never been about talent. It's been about staying on the floor. That's the risk, and it's real.

## THE CAREER THAT NEVER GOT A NORMAL START

And there's one more part of the story worth remembering, because it explains a lot. Wiseman's college career ended almost before it started. The NCAA handed him a 12-game suspension after ruling that an $11,500 moving-expense payment was given to his family in 2017 by Penny Hardaway — his high school coach, a Memphis booster at the time, and later the university's head coach. This was before the NIL era, when that kind of thing was treated completely differently. Rather than serve the suspension, Wiseman withdrew in December 2019 and prepared for the draft. His words: "I've decided to leave Memphis and get a head start on my next chapter."

Then Golden State — a championship-chasing team — drafted him second overall and asked a raw teenager to develop on the fly. Then a trade to a struggling Detroit team. Then the Achilles in Indiana. He's never had a normal developmental path. New York, with a clear role, a stable structure, and a coach who knows him, might be the first truly normal situation of his career.

## WHY THE MATH FAVORS THE KNICKS

Put both columns side by side and the logic becomes obvious. The downside is limited: a minimum salary for a third center who may not play every night. The upside is a 25-year-old former number two pick who could become a legitimate rotation big — exactly the type of cheap contributor a team stuck under the second apron desperately needs.

And it's not just about this season. Drummond is on a one-year deal. The Knicks didn't draft a center this summer. They don't have first-round picks in two of the next three drafts. And the KAT contract situation is unresolved. If Wiseman develops, the Knicks don't just have a third center — they might have part of their frontcourt future.

That's what makes it a "Rose did it again" move. Small bet. Big potential payoff.

And think about what the alternative would have been. The Knicks could have kept a veteran like Eubanks, the safe choice Ian Begley backed for much of camp — before the torn pec ended that option. Or they could have chased a bigger name on the trade market and spent assets they can't afford to lose. Instead, they took the cheapest high-ceiling option available and let the preseason confirm it.

## HOW MIKE BROWN COULD USE HIM

So what does Wiseman actually look like inside this offense? Here's how I'd expect the coaching staff to deploy him — clearly my projection, not a report.

**The lob unit.** Picture Jose Alvarado pushing the pace with Wiseman rim-running. Alvarado was already throwing him lobs in the preseason. Add shooters like Landry Shamet and Jordan Clarkson spacing the floor, and the defense has a choice: collapse on the lob or stay home on the shooters. That's exactly the two-way pressure Brunson and Mitch created together.

**The Brunson pick-and-roll.** In the minutes when Brunson plays with the second unit, a Wiseman screen-and-dive gives him the vertical partner he lost. Brunson's footwork and pull-up force the big to step up; Wiseman diving hard forces the help to come over. One of them is going to be open.

**The emergency minutes.** When KAT picks up early fouls or Drummond needs a rest night, Wiseman steps in to protect the rim and rebound. That's the bread-and-butter job, and it's where most of his minutes will probably come.

**The matchup minutes.** Against long, athletic frontcourts — think Minnesota or Cleveland — the coaching staff might want an extra vertical body. Wiseman gives Mike Brown an option that Drummond's bruising style doesn't.

## THE SECOND-CHANCE BIG MAN PATH

And the history of the league says this can work. Plenty of former top picks have stumbled early and then found a home as rotation players later. Marvin Bagley III — another former number two overall pick — has carved out a role as a backup center in recent seasons after a rough start to his career. Many "second draft" players never reach their original promise. But some find the right situation, accept a smaller role, and become genuinely useful.

That's the realistic goal for Wiseman. Not superstar. Not All-Star. A reliable backup big on a contending team. If he gets there, the bet pays off many times over.

## WHAT THE BRUCE BROWN TRADE-OFF COST

Let's be fair about what the Knicks gave up to make this choice. Bruce Brown would have added a proven perimeter defender and a backup ball-handler with championship experience. Mike Brown called his on-ball defense "textbook." The bench lost that option.

But look at the wing depth that's left: Mikal Bridges, OG Anunoby, Josh Hart, Deuce McBride, Landry Shamet, Jordan Clarkson, and young Mohamed Diawara, who Mike Brown trusted enough to start in the preseason finale against Kawhi Leonard and Scottie Barnes. The Knicks are deep on the perimeter. They were thin in the middle. The decision followed the need.

## WHAT SUCCESS LOOKS LIKE BY JANUARY

There's also a natural checkpoint ahead. Players who make a roster after camp usually sign standard minimum contracts that don't become fully guaranteed until early January, when the league-wide guarantee date arrives. If the Knicks structured Wiseman's deal that way, then the first two and a half months are effectively his audition.

Success by January would look like this: he's been available, he's made his free throws at a respectable rate, he's given the Knicks solid minutes when called on, and the coaching staff trusts him in real games. If he checks those boxes, the guarantee becomes an easy decision. If he doesn't, the Knicks have flexibility. Either way, the bet stays smart.

## THE 30-DAY CHECKLIST

So what does Wiseman need to prove? Here's my checklist for his first month.

**Free throws.** He went two-of-six at the line in the preseason opener. A rim-runner gets fouled a lot. If he can't convert, opponents will foul him instead of giving up dunks. Fixing this is priority one.

**Fouls.** Backup bigs have to stay on the floor. Picking up two fouls in four minutes makes you unplayable. Discipline on contests matters.

**Availability.** Can he handle back-to-backs and stay healthy through the first month? Every week he's available builds trust — and builds the case for the front office.

**The moments.** When KAT gets into foul trouble or Drummond needs rest, can Wiseman hold his own in meaningful minutes against real NBA centers?

**The Boston game.** The Celtics play the Knicks in their second game of the season — Mitchell Robinson's first game against his old team. If Wiseman gets minutes there, it's the perfect measuring stick.

## WHAT THE DECISION MEANS FOR THE REST OF THE ROSTER

And this decision closes some doors while leaving others open. Bruce Brown is now a free agent. The Moussa Cissé storyline is over — Dallas waived Dwight Powell and kept Cissé. Pacôme Dadiet and Tyler Kolek stay, preserving the Knicks' trade flexibility before December 15th.

But one question remains: the two-way spots. The Knicks still have no center on a two-way contract — Tyler Nickel, Kevin McCullar Jr., and Jaden Akins are all perimeter players. Even with Wiseman in, a fourth center for insurance would make sense over an 82-game season.

## MY CALL

Here's my verdict. Yes — Leon Rose has done it again. Not because Wiseman is guaranteed to succeed, but because the process is exactly right: buy low, keep the cost tiny, put him in the right situation, and protect the downside. If Wiseman stays healthy, I think he becomes a useful third center who gives the Knicks 10 to 15 strong minutes on the nights they need him, especially in lob-heavy lineups with Brunson and Alvarado. And if he takes the next step? This could be one of the sneakiest good moves of the entire offseason.

## THE BOTTOM LINE

Here's where it stands. James Wiseman is officially on the Knicks' roster as the third center behind KAT and Drummond, chosen over Bruce Brown after Eubanks, Bradley, and Agbaji were released. Mike Brown says Wiseman brings a vertical threat at the rim and on defense that KAT and Drummond don't — and that the right moment, team, and situation can unlock a talented player. The upside: the top high school player in his class, a dominant three-game college stint, a solid rookie year, age 25, and a minimum salary. The risk: a long injury history and just five games over the last two seasons. The verdict: a smart, low-cost bet — and another example of the Leon Rose formula.
